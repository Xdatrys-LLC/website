The 3 free photo spots

1Shirley Heights

Shirley Heights is a popular high viewpoint in Antigua, and it works for a phone camera because the land drops away so far below you. The classic picture is from the Shirley Heights Lookout, with English Harbour and Falmouth Harbour spread out beneath you, yacht masts in the water and the coastline curling round. For something a little different, go to The Blockhouse, a viewpoint at the largest fortress on the site. It faces the east coast, so it suits an early start: you can catch the sunrise over the water, usually with fewer people around than at the main lookout. Shirley Heights gets very busy on Sunday evenings, when the parties are crowded, and weekday mornings are calmer for photos. If you want a sunset instead, Dow's Hill Interpretation Centre gives a view of the sun going down close to the line of English Harbour. One guide says it closes around 5pm and the fence view is hard to reach, so it may not be available at sunset. Bring a light layer for an early start, keep your lens clean, and take a few frames both wide and zoomed in, because the coast looks different at each setting.

Where it is: Shirley Heights overlooks English Harbour and Falmouth Harbour in Antigua. The Blockhouse is part of the same fortress site as the main lookout.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the sweep of coast and sky, then use the default lens (1x) for a natural view. Try the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the fortress walls, boats or a stretch of coastline.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (viewpoint at the largest fortress, facing the east coast). Antigua's east coast and the sunrise over it, as a quieter alternative to the main lookout. From here your phone sees Antigua's east coast and the sunrise over it, a quieter alternative to the main lookout. The Shirley Heights Lookout is elsewhere on the same site and can be reached on foot, so walk over there for the harbour view.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and set the horizon on the top or bottom line so the sea stays level. Include a bit of the fortress stonework in the foreground to give the view some depth. If you can, also take the classic shot from Shirley Heights Lookout, with English Harbour and Falmouth Harbour and the yacht masts below.

Settings tip: Tap on the brightest part of the sky to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sunrise colours do not wash out. Take a Live Photo too: moving clouds or flags can make a nice short clip. If the light is still dim before sunrise, let night mode work and hold the phone steady against a wall.

Best time: Sunrise at The Blockhouse. Weekday mornings are calmer than Sunday evenings. For sunset, the Dow's Hill Interpretation Centre view is an option, but check it is open first.

2Sir Vivian Richards Stadium

Sir Vivian Richards Stadium is Antigua's international cricket ground, named after the former West Indies captain Viv Richards. For a phone photographer the best picture here is not the whole stadium but the bronze statue of Viv Richards raising his bat, which stands outside the main entrance in front of the pavilion. It gives you a person, a pose and a recognisable building in a single frame. The statue stands outside the pavilion entrance and is easy to frame without going inside, but it is unclear whether an entry fee applies, so check on the day.

The stadium was built for the 2007 Cricket World Cup and usually holds about 10,000 people, though temporary seating doubled that for the tournament. It has two main stands, the Northern Stand and the five-storey South Stand, and it has since hosted Test matches and, in 2024, matches of the ICC Men's T20 World Cup. It sits in the North Sound area of Saint George parish, roughly a 10-20 minute drive from St. John's.

The ground is large, so keep your framing simple. Stand back far enough to fit the whole statue, with the raised bat clear against the sky or the pavilion behind. Keep the statue in the centre or on one third of the frame, and try a second shot from a little lower down so the figure looks taller. Check for people walking through the picture before you press the shutter. Entrance rules and match-day restrictions can change, so check the official site before you go.

Where it is: In North Sound, in the parish of Saint George, Antigua. It is roughly a 10-20 minute drive from St. John's and the airport.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the statue with the entrance and pavilion behind it. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want more of the building, and keep the statue near the middle so it is not stretched at the edges. The telephoto (2x or more) is good for a tighter shot of the raised bat and the face.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Then stand back far enough to fit the whole statue, with the stadium entrance and pavilion behind it.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the statue on one of the vertical lines, leaving the pavilion visible behind it. Crouch slightly and hold the phone low and level so the raised bat sits against the sky. Wait for a gap in passers-by.

Settings tip: Tap on the statue to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the bronze keeps its detail instead of turning into a dark silhouette. Portrait mode is not needed for a statue, and may blur the edges oddly.

Best time: Choose a time when the sun is behind you or off to one side so the statue is lit rather than a silhouette. Check the stadium's official site for events, as match days may be busier and have different rules.

3Nelson's Dockyard

Nelson's Dockyard sits around English Harbour on Antigua's south coast, and it is the only Georgian-era dockyard in the world that still works as a dockyard. The Royal Navy built it up in the 18th century and left in 1889. It is named after Admiral Horatio Nelson, who lived there from 1784 to 1787. Restoration began in 1951 and the site reopened to the public in 1961. Today the old storehouses, workshops and officers' quarters share the waterfront with a marina, shops, restaurants and a museum. It is part of Nelson's Dockyard National Park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The best overall photo is not taken inside the dockyard but from above it, on Shirley Heights. From the lookout you see the dockyard below, English Harbour in the foreground, and Falmouth Harbour and the coastline stretching away beyond. Guides call it the classic panoramic view, and it works well on a phone because the scene is so wide. Look for the line of old buildings along the water, the yachts at anchor, and the layers of hills and sea behind them.

If you want a different angle, Dow's Hill Interpretation Centre gives a wide view over the whole national park. Fort Berkeley has old stone walls and cannon positions that frame the coast, with fewer people around. On Sunday evenings the Shirley Heights barbecue parties are lively and photogenic but crowded, so arrive early if you want a clear spot at the railing.

Where it is: The dockyard is at English Harbour on the south coast of Antigua, in the parish of Saint Paul.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the dockyard, both harbours and the coastline in one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a tidier view of the dockyard and harbour. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the old buildings and yachts.

Where to stand: Stand on Shirley Heights (lookout above English Harbour, looking down on the dockyard). Nelson's Dockyard below with English Harbour in the foreground and Falmouth Harbour and the coastline beyond. From the lookout, Nelson's Dockyard is below you to the north-west, with English Harbour in the foreground and Falmouth Harbour and the coast beyond.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the horizon on the top third line so the harbour and dockyard fill most of the frame. Use the railing or a foreground edge to add depth, and keep the horizon level. For a different view, try Dow's Hill Interpretation Centre for the whole national park, or Fort Berkeley for old stone walls and cannon positions framing the coast.

Settings tip: Tap on the dockyard to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the bright sky keeps its colour. Try panorama mode for the full sweep of the two harbours, moving slowly and steadily.

Best time: Late afternoon into sunset gives the best light, according to the photography guides. Sunday evenings are lively but crowded.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Antigua?

The first three on this map are Shirley Heights, Sir Vivian Richards Stadium and Nelson's Dockyard. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 11.

Where should I stand to photograph the Shirley Heights?

Stand right at the Shirley Heights (viewpoint at the largest fortress, facing the east coast). Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the sweep of coast and sky, then use the default lens (1x) for a natural view.

When is the best time to photograph Sir Vivian Richards Stadium?

Choose a time when the sun is behind you or off to one side so the statue is lit rather than a silhouette. Check the stadium's official site for events, as match days may be busier and have different rules.

How do I get to Nelson's Dockyard?

There are no stations or tram stops here, so start from English Harbour and go on foot (a short hike) or by taxi to Shirley Heights.
