The 3 free photo spots

1Český Krumlov Castle

Český Krumlov Castle is a huge castle complex that spreads along a steep, long rocky headland, with the Vltava running along its southern side. Its colourful tower and the layers of courtyards, bridges and walls make it easy to photograph from many angles, and because everything is stacked up the hillside, even a simple shot has plenty of depth. The first castle here dates back to 1253, built by the Vítkovci family, and the complex later passed through the Rosenbergs, the Eggenbergs and the Schwarzenbergs. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992 and is the second largest castle and chateau complex in the Czech Republic, after Prague Castle. Look out for the five-storey Cloak Bridge, dating from 1764, which joins the Upper Castle to the theatre building and gardens. The Baroque theatre from 1767 is counted among the best preserved in the world. Start with the whole castle and the tower, then move closer for details such as the bridges and the courtyard. Work at a relaxed pace: tap the screen on the part you want sharp, and check the edges of the frame for people and railings before you press the shutter. Right next to it: Castle Tower. Right next to it: Cloak Bridge (Plášťový most).

Where it is: In the town of Český Krumlov, on a steep, long rocky headland with the Vltava running along its southern side.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the castle and its setting. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are close and the walls and tower will not fit. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the tower, rooftops or the Cloak Bridge from a distance.

Where to stand: Stand about 30 m west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. Face east, keep the phone level and let the castle fill the upper part of the frame.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the tower or the main mass of buildings on one of the vertical lines, with the rock and walls leading the eye up to it. Include a little foreground, such as a wall or the rooftops, to give a sense of scale. For close-up detail, use the telephoto instead of walking right up to the walls.

Settings tip: Tap on the castle walls to set focus, then slide the exposure control down slightly if the sky is bright, so the colours of the buildings stay rich. Take a few frames with the default lens (1x) and the ultra-wide (0.5x) so you can choose later.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people in the shot. Midday can be harsh and bright, so check how the light looks on the walls before you shoot.

5Castle Gardens

The Castle Gardens are part of the castle complex in Český Krumlov, and the best way to photograph them is from the bridge that leads in. From the garden end of Plášťový most (the Cloak Bridge), where the upper covered passage runs into the garden, you see the arcaded bridge framing the entrance. Beyond it are the castle buildings and the red roofs of the town. The arches give your photo a natural frame, and the roofs and towers behind them show where you are. Guides describe the Cloak Bridge as one of the most typical Krumlov views and a route into the garden. Walking across the outside of the bridge is free, but the bridge interior is only on the guided castle tour route. Opening hours for the garden vary by season, so check before you go. If you want the garden itself, step inside to Zámecká zahrada. There the stepped terrace and cascade fountain sit in the foreground, with the castle and garden walls behind. For a different angle, Seminární zahrada looks across the Vltava to the castle and its towers, with the terracotta roofs of the Old Town beside them. The garden is not clearly visible from there. Keep the bridge arches straight in your frame and let the roofs fill the background.

Where it is: The Castle Gardens are on the castle side of Český Krumlov, beside the castle complex and reached over the Plášťový most (Cloak Bridge).

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to frame the arcaded bridge with the castle buildings and red roofs behind it. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the bridge and more of the garden entrance in one frame. Use the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) to pick out the roofs and castle details beyond the arches.

Where to stand: Stand on Plášťový most (garden end of the bridge, where the upper covered passage leads into the garden). The arcaded bridge framing the entrance to the garden, with the castle buildings and red roofs of the town visible beyond. Where the upper covered passage leads into the garden. From here your photo shows the arcaded bridge framing the entrance to the garden, with the castle buildings and the town's red roofs beyond.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the bridge arches with the vertical lines so the arcade looks straight. Keep the castle buildings and red roofs in the upper part of the frame. For a close-up of the garden, go inside to Zámecká zahrada and shoot the terrace and cascade fountain with the castle behind. Seminární zahrada across the Vltava is an alternative for a view of the castle and towers with the Old Town roofs.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the sky or the roofs to focus, then lower the exposure slider a little so the covered passage does not wash out the sky. If the arches are in shade, check that the roofs behind still look bright.

Best time: Visit when the garden is open, since hours vary by season.

6Barber's Bridge (Lazebnický most)

Barber's Bridge (Lazebnický most) crosses the river in the old part of Český Krumlov. The classic views are looking out from the bridge, but this spot is about photographing the bridge itself. The best way to do that is from Latrán, the street guides describe as the main walking route to the bridge. As you walk down towards the river, the bridge closes the far end of the street, with the castle rising beyond it. That layering of street, bridge and castle is what makes the picture work on a phone. The street leads the eye straight to the bridge, and the castle gives the frame a clear background.

Stand on Latrán at the Spičák end and look south-west along the street. The bridge is a short walk ahead of you. Keep the bridge in the centre and let the street edges frame it. If you want a second picture, walk onto the bridge for the view of the castle and river. The bridge isn't in that frame, so it only works if you want it as a foreground element.

The bridge has been closed for reconstruction in the past, so check it is open before you plan your walk. Whatever you find, the street view of the bridge from Latrán is worth taking.

Where it is: In the old town of Český Krumlov, where the street Latrán runs down towards the river and ends at the bridge. The castle rises behind the bridge when seen from the Spičák end of the street.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the bridge, the street edges and the castle all fit in one frame. If the street is crowded or you can't step back, the ultra-wide (0.5x) fits more in. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the castle and bridge closer together.

Where to stand: Stand on Latrán (approach from Spičák end, where the bridge closes the street). The bridge seen at the far end of the street as you walk toward the river, with the castle rising beyond it. On Latrán, at the Spičák end, where the bridge closes the street. From here you see the bridge with the castle rising beyond it.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and put the bridge on the centre line, with the street's building edges on both sides as a frame. Keep the camera level so the street doesn't lean. For a second shot, walk onto the bridge to photograph the castle and river, though the bridge itself won't be in that frame.

Settings tip: Tap on the bridge to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky and castle keep their detail. Take a few frames while people walk through, and keep the one with the clearest view of the bridge.

Best time: Early morning or later in the evening is likely to leave fewer people in the street, so the bridge is easier to frame cleanly.
