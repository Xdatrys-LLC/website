This map shows 13 of the most-searched sights in Český Krumlov as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Castle, St. Vitus Church, Castle Tower, Cloak Bridge and Svornosti Square lead the list. The 3 free photo spots

1Český Krumlov Castle

Český Krumlov Castle is a huge castle complex that spreads along a steep, long rocky headland, with the Vltava running along its southern side. Its colourful tower and the layers of courtyards, bridges and walls make it easy to photograph from many angles, and because everything is stacked up the hillside, even a simple shot has plenty of depth. The first castle here dates back to 1253, built by the Vítkovci family, and the complex later passed through the Rosenbergs, the Eggenbergs and the Schwarzenbergs. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992 and is the second largest castle and chateau complex in the Czech Republic, after Prague Castle. Look out for the five-storey Cloak Bridge, dating from 1764, which joins the Upper Castle to the theatre building and gardens. The Baroque theatre from 1767 is counted among the best preserved in the world. Start with the whole castle and the tower, then move closer for details such as the bridges and the courtyard. Work at a relaxed pace: tap the screen on the part you want sharp, and check the edges of the frame for people and railings before you press the shutter.

Where it is: In the town of Český Krumlov, on a steep, long rocky headland with the Vltava running along its southern side.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the castle and its setting. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are close and the walls and tower will not fit. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the tower, rooftops or the Cloak Bridge from a distance.

Where to stand: Stand about 30 m west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. Face east, keep the phone level and let the castle fill the upper part of the frame.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the tower or the main mass of buildings on one of the vertical lines, with the rock and walls leading the eye up to it. Include a little foreground, such as a wall or the rooftops, to give a sense of scale. For close-up detail, use the telephoto instead of walking right up to the walls.

Settings tip: Tap on the castle walls to set focus, then slide the exposure control down slightly if the sky is bright, so the colours of the buildings stay rich. Take a few frames with the default lens (1x) and the ultra-wide (0.5x) so you can choose later.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people in the shot. Midday can be harsh and bright, so check how the light looks on the walls before you shoot.

3Castle Tower

The Castle Tower is a spot on the Český Krumlov map, ranked third on this map. If buildings sit between you and the tower, use them as a foreground layer, with the tower behind. That layering is what makes the picture work on a phone.

Start with the whole scene on the default lens (1x) and use the gridlines to keep the tower upright and slightly off-centre. If other buildings or people crowd the edges, step a little to one side rather than cropping later. The telephoto lens (2x to 5x, depending on your phone) is good for isolating the tower and its upper details, and it flattens the view so the tower looks close to the buildings in front of it. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you move closer and want to fit the full height in, and keep the phone level so the walls do not lean.

Tap the tower to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky looks washed out. Take a few frames, as passers-by often drift through the shot. Check the official site before you go if you plan to go up or into the grounds, as access can vary.

Where it is: The Castle Tower is on the Český Krumlov map.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Begin with the default lens (1x) for the full view of the tower and its surroundings. Switch to telephoto (2x to 5x, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the tower itself. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only when you are close and need to fit in the full height.

Where to stand: Stand about 240 m south-east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-west. Turn that way and keep the phone upright and level.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the tower on one of the vertical lines, with any foreground buildings filling the lower third. Take one wide frame and one zoomed frame so you have both the setting and the detail. If people or signs intrude, shift a step to one side.

Settings tip: Tap the tower to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the sky is bright. Try Live Photos if people are moving through the scene, so you can pick the cleanest moment.

Best time: Soft morning or late-afternoon light is usually kinder to the walls and sky than midday sun. Early in the day it is often quieter too.

12Barber's Bridge (Lazebnický most)

Barber's Bridge (Lazebnický most) crosses the river in the old part of Český Krumlov. The classic views are looking out from the bridge, but this spot is about photographing the bridge itself. The best way to do that is from Latrán, the street guides describe as the main walking route to the bridge. As you walk down towards the river, the bridge closes the far end of the street, with the castle rising beyond it. That layering of street, bridge and castle is what makes the picture work on a phone. The street leads the eye straight to the bridge, and the castle gives the frame a clear background.

Stand on Latrán at the Spičák end, at the phone icon on the map, and look south-west along the street. The bridge is a short walk ahead of you. Keep the bridge in the centre and let the street edges frame it. If you want a second picture, walk onto the bridge for the view of the castle and river. The bridge isn't in that frame, so it only works if you want it as a foreground element.

The bridge has been closed for reconstruction in the past, so check it is open before you plan your walk. Whatever you find, the street view of the bridge from Latrán is worth taking.

Where it is: In the old town of Český Krumlov, where the street Latrán runs down towards the river and ends at the bridge. The castle rises behind the bridge when seen from the Spičák end of the street.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the bridge, the street edges and the castle all fit in one frame. If the street is crowded or you can't step back, the ultra-wide (0.5x) fits more in. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the castle and bridge closer together.

Where to stand: Stand about 150 m north-east of the subject, on Latrán (the phone icon on the map), and face south-west. On Latrán, at the Spičák end, where the bridge closes the street. From here you see the bridge with the castle rising beyond it.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and put the bridge on the centre line, with the street's building edges on both sides as a frame. Keep the camera level so the street doesn't lean. For a second shot, walk onto the bridge to photograph the castle and river, though the bridge itself won't be in that frame.

Settings tip: Tap on the bridge to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky and castle keep their detail. Take a few frames while people walk through, and keep the one with the clearest view of the bridge.

Best time: Early morning or later in the evening is likely to leave fewer people in the street, so the bridge is easier to frame cleanly.
