The 3 free photo spots

1Castillo San Felipe del Morro

El Morro is the great stone fortress at the north-western tip of Old San Juan, standing on a steep headland where the Atlantic meets the entrance to San Juan Bay. Building began in 1539 as a fortified tower, and by 1595 it had grown into a larger bastion fort. Work carried on for roughly two more centuries, and it reached its present form in 1787. The walls rise about 43 metres from the shoreline and are several metres thick, which is why the fort looks so solid in a photo. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1983.

It photographs well because the fort, the sea wall, the surf and the green slope can all sit in one frame. From here the fort is just to the east, close up and seen from the water side. Guides describe it as quieter than the main lawn, with clean backgrounds and good light towards sunset. The walkway can be reached on foot from the Paseo de la Princesa end. If you want a second view, the lawn in front of the fort shows the stone facade and lighthouse above the grass, with the horizon behind. Plaza del Quinto Centenario gives a higher angle back towards the castle from the city side.

Where it is: In the Old San Juan historic quarter, on the north-western point of the old city's islet, where the headland guards the entrance to San Juan Bay. The Paseo del Morro walkway runs along the sea wall below the fort.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for a natural view of the fort. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the wall, surf and green slope into one frame. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as the lighthouse or the stonework.

Where to stand: Stand on Paseo del Morro (the paved walkway that runs along the sea wall from the Raíces side up to the fort). The fort seen close up from the water side, with the wall, the surf and the lush slope in one frame. The fort is to the east of you. From here you see it close up from the water side, with the wall, the surf and the lush slope together.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the sea wall along a lower line so the fort rises above it. Keep the horizon level. For a different view, try the lawn in front of the fort, which shows the facade and lighthouse above the grass, or Plaza del Quinto Centenario for a higher angle looking back at the castle.

Settings tip: Tap on the fort's stone to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. Take a few Live Photos or short bursts where the surf hits the wall, then pick the best frame. Wipe your lens first, as sea air can leave a film on it.

Best time: Sunset, according to several guides, when the walkway and lawn are the best places to be.

3Isla Verde Beach

Isla Verde Beach is the long stretch of sand in front of one of the main tourist and resort areas of the San Juan metropolitan area. The name means "green island" in Spanish, after a small cay and reef a little way off the shore. The district is packed with hotels and condominiums, so you get a wide curve of golden sand and turquoise water with a skyline of buildings behind it. The beach also sits right next to Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport, so planes come in low over the water on their final approach. That means a passing aircraft can be your subject. Isla Verde is officially part of the municipality of Carolina, although it shares its coastline with Ocean Park and Condado, which are part of San Juan. For photos, stand on the sand near the waterline and decide first whether you want the planes or the beach and hotels. For the planes, wait for one to appear and keep plenty of sky above the sand. For the beach itself, keep the waves in the foreground and the hotel line on the far side. Parking nearby is reported to be difficult, so plan how you will arrive.

Where it is: Isla Verde lies on the Atlantic coast east of Ocean Park and Condado, between the San Juan side of the metropolitan area and Piñones. It is technically in the municipality of Carolina, and the airport sits just to its south.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the long beach and the hotel skyline, and the default lens (1x) for a planes-over-the-sand view. If your phone has telephoto/zoom (2x-5x), it brings an approaching aircraft closer, but keep some sky around it.

Where to stand: Stand about 490 m south-east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-west. Stand on Playa Isla Verde, on the sand facing northeast toward the airport approach path, with the sea and the beach in front of you. From the shoreline you can frame low aircraft on final approach to Luis Muñoz Marín Airport against the sky above the beach.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the horizon on the lower line, leaving two thirds of the frame for sky, so a plane has room to cross it. Tap on the plane or the sky to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the aircraft stays crisp and the sky keeps its colour. For a different view, shoot along the sand with the ultra-wide (0.5x), keeping the turquoise water in the foreground and the hotels on the far side. Balneario del Escambrón is an alternative viewpoint from further along the coast, showing the Condado/Ocean Park and Isla Verde waterfront from a distance.

Settings tip: Take several shots as each plane passes, because the moment goes quickly. Check your first photo and adjust the exposure slider if the sky is washed out or the aircraft looks too dark.

Best time: Afternoon is best for the planes over the beach. For the beach and hotel skyline lit by early sunlight, go in the early morning.

4Castillo San Cristóbal

Castillo San Cristóbal is the big stone fort on the Atlantic side of Old San Juan. For a phone photo, the best subject here is the Garita del Diablo, a small sentry box that juts out over the cliff at the sea's edge. It is the most photographed feature on the ocean side of the fort. It works well because it is a simple, strong shape with nothing behind it but the Atlantic, so there is little clutter to manage. The usual shot is taken from the rampart, looking back at the sentry box, with the sea filling the background. The grounds, ramparts and sentry box are part of a ticketed National Park Service site, so plan to go in rather than shoot from outside. Opening times can vary, so check the official site for current hours and tickets before you go. Start with the default lens (1x) so you can see the whole sentry box against the sea, then step back and try 2x for a tighter, flatter view. Tap the sentry box to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is washing out. Keep well back from the edge, and keep one hand firmly on the phone. If you want a second picture, the fort also gives a wide view over the rooftops of Old San Juan, the harbour and the Atlantic horizon. Plaza de Colón, which is free, shows the fort as a large stone mass at the end of the streets.

Where it is: Castillo San Cristóbal is in Old San Juan, on the Atlantic-facing side of the old town. The Garita del Diablo shot is described separately: it is taken from the rampart, looking back at the sentry box.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the whole sentry box and the sea behind it. Switch to 2x for a tighter shot that takes in less of the wall around it. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want the whole rampart and sweep of coast in the frame.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (outside the seaward wall, shot from the rampart looking back at the sentry box). The small sentry box jutting out over the cliff at the sea's edge, with the Atlantic filling the background. For the Garita del Diablo shot, stand on the rampart and look back at the sentry box, which sits out over the cliff, outside the seaward wall. The box should fill part of the frame, with the Atlantic filling the background behind it.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and place the sentry box on one of the thirds, leaving the open sea and sky on the other side. Keep the horizon level. For a different view, the fort also gives a wide panorama over the Old San Juan rooftops and harbour, or you can shoot the fort from Plaza de Colón.

Settings tip: Tap the sentry box to lock focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky keeps its colour and the stone keeps its detail. Take a few frames, because sea wind can make a handheld phone shake.

Best time: Early morning, before the crowds arrive, gives the best chance of empty ramparts. Check the current opening time on the official site first.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in San Juan?

The first three on this map are El Morro, La Fortaleza and Isla Verde Beach. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 18.

Where should I stand to photograph El Morro?

Stand on Paseo del Morro (the paved walkway that runs along the sea wall from the Raíces side up to the fort). Start with the default lens (1x) for a natural view of the fort.

When is the best time to photograph Isla Verde Beach?

Afternoon is best for the planes over the beach. For the beach and hotel skyline lit by early sunlight, go in the early morning.

How do I get to San Cristóbal Fort?

No station or tram stop is listed for this spot, so start from Old San Juan on foot.
