The 3 free photo spots

1Canton Tower

Canton Tower is a 600-metre observation tower in Haizhu District, and it looks like nothing else on the skyline. Its lattice of columns, rings and braces twists as it rises and pinches in at about the halfway point, which is why locals nicknamed it "Slim Waist". The tower came into use in September 2010, just before the Asian Games that Guangzhou hosted that year. For a short while it was the tallest tower in the world. It was designed by Dutch architects working with the engineering firm Arup, and the twisting shape comes from rotating two ovals, one at the base and one near the top, against each other.

That shape is what makes it so photogenic. The lines of the lattice curve and cross, and the narrow waist gives your picture a natural focal point. You will probably need to tilt the phone upwards and accept that the sides lean inwards a little, which can look quite dramatic. If you want it to look straighter, step back a few paces and zoom in slightly rather than tilting more. Keep a person or a railing in the lower corner to give a sense of scale. If the tower fills the frame, try a vertical (portrait-orientation) shot.

Where it is: Canton Tower stands in Haizhu District, close to Haixinsha Island.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) to take in as much of the tower's height as you can from this close. Use the default lens (1x) for a tighter, less stretched view of the waist. Telephoto (2x-5x) suits the detail of the lattice and the top section.

Where to stand: Stand about 170 m south-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east. Hold the phone upright and tilt it up gently.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the tower up with a vertical grid line so it sits centred, or place it on one third line with space beside it. Include a little foreground, such as paving, trees or a railing, to give it depth. For a different view, try a close crop on the waist at 2x or more, where the twist is easiest to see.

Settings tip: Tap the tower to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky does not wash out and the structure stays detailed. If you shoot after dark, use night mode and brace your elbows against your body or a wall to keep the picture sharp.

Best time: Late afternoon to dusk is a good time, as the sky colours can contrast well with the tower's shape. The tower is probably lit up in the evening, which gives night shots a different look.

2Shamian Island

Shamian Island is a small sandbank island in Guangzhou's Liwan District, and it feels very different from the busy streets around it. It covers about 0.3 square kilometres, roughly 900 m from east to west and 300 m from north to south. The Pearl River runs along its southern edge and a canal separates it from the mainland. In 1859 the island was split between Britain and France, and the old trading mansions, churches and quiet tree-lined pedestrian avenues are still there. That mix is why it photographs so well: long shaded walks, verandahed buildings in various states of repair, and bronze statues dotted around that show scenes from the island's past. Two buildings are worth looking for. Christ Church Shameen dates from 1865, and Our Lady of Lourdes Chapel was completed in 1892. The chapel stands at the French end of the island. Today the island also has hotels, restaurants and souvenir shops, so there is somewhere to stop between pictures. Look for tree-framed avenues, building facades with their verandahs, and the statues, which make good foreground subjects. Take your time, as the best shots are often found by following the quiet side streets.

Where it is: Shamian Island is in Liwan District, Guangzhou. It is bordered by the Pearl River to the south and separated from the mainland by a narrow canal.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for street scenes and building fronts. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) for the long tree-lined avenues, and use telephoto/zoom (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for architectural details and the statues.

Where to stand: Stand about 290 m east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face west.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up an avenue so the trees on each side frame the view and the buildings lead the eye into the picture. Use the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) to take in the whole avenue, and keep your phone level so the buildings do not lean. For the bronze statues, get close and use portrait mode to separate them from the background. Use telephoto (2x-5x) to pick out details such as verandahs and church facades.

Settings tip: Tap to focus on the main subject, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the bright sky is washing out the buildings. In shaded avenues, hold your phone steady, and use night mode if you are shooting in dim light.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon is usually pleasant, as the light is softer and the island is often quieter.

3Guangzhou Opera House

Guangzhou Opera House was designed by Zaha Hadid and opened on 9 May 2010. It was conceived as two rocks washed away by the Pearl River, and the building does look like a pair of large pebbles, with sloping stone-clad sides and no obvious straight lines. That makes it a good subject for a phone. The shapes are bold and the outline reads clearly even in a small picture. The auditorium is concrete, set inside a granite and glass-clad steel frame, and the building took over five years to build. It is the biggest performing centre in southern China.

The best view for most visitors is from the square, 花城广场, rather than the building's own forecourt. From there the opera house sits in a row with the library and the museum, and the Canton Tower rises behind. The square is spacious, so you can fit in the whole group of buildings and often keep crowds out of the frame. If you want a closer, more abstract picture, walk over to the building itself. The double-curved stone form is reflected in the water pool in front of it, which is worth a few frames of its own.

Take a wide shot first to show the setting, then move in for the curves and the reflection. Keep the phone level so the big curves look natural rather than leaning.

Where it is: In Guangzhou, with the Pearl River to the south, beside the open square of 花城广场 and its neighbouring library and museum.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole row of buildings. Then use the default lens (1x) for a cleaner view of the opera house alone. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the Canton Tower behind or for the stone details.

Where to stand: Stand in 花城广场. The opera house as one part of a row with the library and museum, with the Canton Tower in the background. The opera house is to the west of you. From here you see it as one part of a row with the library and the museum, and the Canton Tower in the background.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the base of the buildings up with a horizontal line so the curves look natural. Leave open space in the foreground to show how big the square is. If you want a different view, go to the building's own forecourt and shoot the curved stone form with its reflection in the water pool.

Settings tip: Tap the opera house to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the pale stone and sky keep their detail. After dark, use night mode and brace your arms against your body, or rest the phone on a ledge, to keep it steady.

Best time: Late afternoon to dusk is a good choice, as the light is softer on the stone. Early morning is likely to be quieter.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Guangzhou?

The first three on this map are Canton Tower, Shamian Island and Guangzhou Opera House. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 18.

Where should I stand to photograph Canton Tower?

Stand about 170 m south-west of Canton Tower, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east. Start with the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) to take in as much of the tower's height as you can from this close.

When is the best time to photograph Shamian Island?

Early morning or late afternoon is usually pleasant, as the light is softer and the island is often quieter.

How do I get to Guangzhou Opera House?

Go by taxi or use a map app from central Guangzhou, and search for or ask for 花城广场 or 广州大剧院 (show the Chinese names on your phone).
