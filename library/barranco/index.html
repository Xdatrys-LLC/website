This map shows 9 of the most-searched sights in Barranco as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Pedro de Osma Museum, Bridge of Sighs, Bajada de Baños, Main Square and MATE Museum lead the list. The 3 free photo spots

1Pedro de Osma Museum

The Pedro de Osma Museum is a museum in Barranco. It is a good first stop on this map because you can photograph the building from a short, easy distance without needing to go inside. You can frame the whole frontage with very little walking, and there is room to step a little left or right to change the angle.

The building works well as a subject because its entrance, walls and any garden greenery give clear shapes, textures and colour. These show up well on a phone screen. Start with a wide, straight-on shot to record the whole building, then move closer for details such as the doorway, windows or decorative features. Details make a good second picture and help your set of photos feel varied.

Keep the phone level, using the gridlines so that vertical lines stay straight. If people walk through the scene, wait a moment for a gap, or include one person to give a sense of scale. Check the official site before you go if you also want to visit the inside, as access to museums can be ticketed or restricted.

Where it is: The Pedro de Osma Museum is in the Barranco district.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for a natural view of the building. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you cannot fit the whole frontage in, and use telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for details.

Where to stand: Stand about 20 m south-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east. The building will be in front of you with room to spare, so you can step sideways to find the angle you like.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the edges of the building with the vertical lines. Take one wide shot of the whole frontage, then a closer one of the entrance or a detail. Keep the phone upright to avoid leaning walls.

Settings tip: Tap the building to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright so that the walls keep their colour. Use portrait mode only if you want a person in front of the building.

Best time: Morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and gentler shadows on the walls, and it is often quieter.

3Bajada de Baños

Bajada de Baños is a stepped pedestrian path that drops from the edge of Barranco down a ravine toward the sea. Its walls are covered in colourful murals, which makes it a good place for a phone camera: there is colour everywhere, and the steps give the picture a strong line that leads the eye downhill.

The recommended vantage point is the upper, Barranco-side end of the wooden footbridge, the Puente de los Suspiros. The raised deck looks down the ravine, so you get the mural-lined steps falling away toward the sea with part of the bridge in the frame.

The Bajada is a narrow path rather than a wide square, so there is no single big viewpoint. Every angle gives a different picture, and it is worth walking a little way down to shoot the murals, steps and path from within. Take a few frames from the bridge first, then go down for close-ups.

If you want a wider view out to sea, there is a lookout beside the bridge that looks over the coast, with the bridge and the top of the Bajada at the edge of the frame.

The stairs are steep in places, so allow time and energy for the walk back up. The lower section near the coast is fenced and roofed, which may limit open-air views down there.

Where it is: Bajada de Baños is in Barranco. It runs down the ravine from beside the Puente de los Suspiros toward the coast.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) to fit the bridge, the steps and the murals into one frame. Use the default lens (1x) for a calmer view of the path, and 2x if your phone has it to pick out a single mural or a section of steps.

Where to stand: Stand on Puente de los Suspiros (upper (Barranco-side) end, looking down the Bajada). The stepped, mural-lined Bajada falling away below the wooden footbridge toward the sea, with the bridge itself in the frame. The recommended vantage point is the upper (Barranco-side) end of the Puente de los Suspiros, looking down the Bajada. You should see the stepped, mural-lined path falling away below the wooden footbridge toward the sea, with the bridge in the frame. Then walk a little way down the steps for views from within the Bajada.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and use the line of the steps as a leading line, with the bridge at one side. Keep the phone level so the steps do not lean. For a different picture, go down onto the Bajada and shoot along the path with murals on both sides. For a seaward view, try the lookout next to the bridge, with the bridge and the top of the Bajada at the edge of the frame.

Settings tip: Tap on the mid-tone of the steps to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky looks washed out. Wait a moment for people to clear the steps, or use a Live Photo and pick the cleanest frame.

Best time: Earlier or later in the day usually gives softer light and fewer people on the narrow steps, but check the light on the day.

5MATE Museo Mario Testino

MATE is the Museo Mario Testino, a museum named after the photographer, and it sits in Barranco. It is a good stop for a phone photographer because the building and its street give you simple, clean frames. The museum's name and purpose also make it a natural 'I was here' picture for a trip album.

The phone icon on the map is about 50 m north-east of the subject. That is a short, easy distance, so you can start with a wider view of the frontage and its surroundings, then walk closer for details. Try the entrance, signs, doors and any textures or colours that catch your eye.

Keep the phone level, switch on the gridlines, and watch for passers-by and parked vehicles at the edges of the frame. If the street is busy, wait a moment for a gap or shoot slightly from the side. Take one wide picture to show where you are, one at the default lens (1x) for the main view, and one zoomed in for a detail. Opening arrangements and any rules about photography inside can change, so check the official site before you go.

Where it is: In Barranco, a short walk south-west of the stand-here point marked on the map.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the main view. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) if the street is narrow and you cannot step back far enough. Try 2x telephoto, if your phone has it, for details of the entrance or signs.

Where to stand: Stand about 50 m north-east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face south-west.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the building up with the vertical lines so it does not lean. Take a wide shot first, then a closer one of the entrance or a feature that catches your eye. Keep people and vehicles at the edges rather than in the middle.

Settings tip: Tap on the building to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the sky is bright, so the façade does not turn dark. Take a few frames and keep the sharpest.

Best time: Daytime is easiest for outside pictures. Early morning or late afternoon often gives softer light and fewer people.
