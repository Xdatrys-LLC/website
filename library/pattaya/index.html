The 3 free photo spots

1Walking Street, Pattaya

Walking Street is Pattaya's best-known evening strip: a long, neon-lit road of restaurants, bars, music venues and clubs that fills with people once the sun goes down. It began as a road serving a fishing village. In the 1960s and 70s it changed to cater for visiting American soldiers on leave, and it was closed to traffic in the evening after a run of car accidents in the 1990s. A large video sign went up at the Beach Road entrance in March 2010.

It is near Soi 13/4, a narrow side lane. Please check the exact spot on the day. The lane has bare-bulb string lights overhead, which give warm light and a good backdrop for portraits, and usually fewer people in frame than on the main strip. Most visitors walk straight past it.

For the wide neon scene, two other places work well. One is under the illuminated entrance arch, with the crowd moving beneath it. The other is Bali Hai Pier, where a zoomed view back up the street squeezes the signs together. Keep your phone out of the venues themselves and avoid photographing bar staff. Street performers and animals usually expect a tip if you photograph them. Please confirm the lane on the map before you set off.

Where it is: Walking Street is in Pattaya, a pedestrianised evening strip running from the Beach Road entrance down towards the Bali Hai pier.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the lane and the lights overhead. Switch to 2x for portraits, as it flatters faces and keeps the background softer. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want to show the whole line of bulbs stretching above you.

Where to stand: Stand on Soi 13/4 (Side alley branching off Walking Street about a third of the way down from the arch). A narrow side lane with overhead bare-bulb string lights, giving warm light and a portrait-friendly backdrop with fewer people in frame. This is on Soi 13/4, so check the exact spot on the day. From here the main Walking Street lies to the south-west. Face along the lane with the string lights above and behind your subject, so the bulbs glow in the background.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place your subject on one of the vertical lines, with the strings of bulbs running away behind them. Hold the phone at chest height and tilt up slightly so the lights fill the top of the frame. For the full neon street, try the entrance arch with the crowd passing under it, or a zoomed view from Bali Hai Pier looking back up the street.

Settings tip: Use portrait mode for people, and tap on your subject's face to focus. Then slide the exposure down a little so the bulbs stay warm and don't burn out. Let night mode do its work and hold still for a couple of seconds. Ask people to stay still as well, because moving figures blur in low light.

Best time: After dark, when the string lights are lit and the street is open to pedestrians. Earlier in the evening the lane is likely to be quieter.

2Sanctuary of Truth

The Sanctuary of Truth is a huge temple-like building in Pattaya, carved almost entirely from wood. It is still unfinished: work began in 1981 and carries on today, which is why visitors are given hard hats to wear inside. The grounds cover 13 hectares, and the tallest spire reaches 105 metres. The design draws on the temples of Ayutthaya and on Hindu and Buddhist ideas, so the building is richly decorated with hand-carved wooden figures, gods and ornament.

It photographs well because of the detail and the setting. From the sea-facing waterfront platform at the water's edge you can look back up at the whole structure with open water and coastline behind it, which the front view cannot give you. Late afternoon is the time guides recommend for this angle, and it also suits a walking shot or a photo of two people with the building above them.

If you want a second angle, the View Point of the Sanctuary of Truth gives one wide frame of the wooden façade and spires with the sea behind. For the front of the building, stand further back so the full height fits without the sideways-tilting look you get close up. Go earlier in the day if you want fewer people in the frame. Take a moment to step in close for the carvings too: the wood textures are the real character of the place.

Where it is: In Pattaya, on the coast, where the building stands at the water's edge with the open sea behind it.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) on the waterfront platform when you want the whole structure and the sea in one frame. Use the default lens (1x) for a calmer, more natural view of the building. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for carved figures and spire details.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. The sea-side viewpoint is the waterfront platform at the water's edge, inside the ticketed grounds. From there your photo shows the whole structure rising above you with the open sea and coastline as the backdrop.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and keep the building's vertical lines straight on them. Tilting the phone upwards makes the spires lean inwards, so hold it level and step back if you can. The View Point of the Sanctuary of Truth is a good alternative: it gives one wide frame of the wooden façade and spires with the sea behind. For the front of the building, shoot from a distance so the full height fits without the upward distortion you get up close.

Settings tip: Tap the building to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the bright sky does not wash out the carved wood. Take a few frames of each view, because other visitors move through the shot.

Best time: Late afternoon is the time guides recommend for the sea-side view. Early morning is said to be quieter.

3Pattaya Beach

Pattaya Beach is the long curve of sand and sea that gives the city its postcard look, and on this map the best way to photograph it is from the Pattaya City Sign. The large lettering sits on the hillside, so you can put the words in the foreground and let the beach and the sea fill the background. That makes a bolder, more graphic picture than a plain beach snap, and it tells everyone looking at it exactly where you were. Photo guides describe the sign as a popular photo spot, with a view over both the city and the sea. For a different take, the hilltop viewpoint on Khao Phra Tamnak gives a wide panorama of the whole bay, with the hotel strip following the shoreline and Koh Larn offshore. Tour groups crowd that hilltop viewpoint, so arrive early there. Down on the sand itself, a low shot of the shoreline works well when the sun sets over the water to the west. Keep your phone steady, clean the lens with your sleeve before you start, and take a few frames with and without people in them. Leave a little space around the lettering so nothing is cut off at the edges.

Where it is: Pattaya Beach is the main city beach in Pattaya, on the curve of the bay beside Beach Road. The City Sign stands on the hillside towards the north end of Beach Road, looking over the city and the sea.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the sign and the bay both fit in the frame. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are close to the lettering and want to keep all of it in. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out details such as boats on the water or Koh Larn offshore.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (north end of Beach Road, on the hillside). The large city lettering in the foreground with the beach and sea behind it. For the sign shot, make your way to the Pattaya City Sign towards the north end of Beach Road, where the large city lettering is in the foreground and the beach and sea spread out behind it.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the lettering along the lower third, with the sea and sky filling the rest. Crouch slightly so the letters rise against the water. If the sign is busy, wait a moment for a gap rather than zooming in. For a different view, the hilltop viewpoint on Khao Phra Tamnak gives a wide bay panorama, and the sand at the beach is good for a low shot at sunset.

Settings tip: Tap on the sea or the sign to focus, then use the exposure slider to stop the sky from turning white. If the lettering looks dark against a bright background, raise the exposure a little. Panorama mode works well on the hilltop viewpoint if you hold the phone steady and sweep slowly.

Best time: Tour groups crowd the hilltop viewpoint, so arriving early is advised there. For the beach shot, late afternoon is best because the sun sets over the water to the west.

What are the top photo spots in Pattaya?

Where should I stand to photograph Walking Street?

Stand on Soi 13/4 (Side alley branching off Walking Street about a third of the way down from the arch). Start with the default lens (1x) for the lane and the lights overhead.

When is the best time to photograph the Sanctuary of Truth?

How do I get to Pattaya Beach?
