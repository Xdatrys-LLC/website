This map shows 15 of the most-searched sights in Cebu City as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Taoist Temple, Ayala Center Cebu, Magellan's Cross, SM Seaside City and Santo Niño Basilica lead the list. The 3 free photo spots

1Cebu Taoist Temple

Cebu Taoist Temple is a bright red-and-gold temple on a hillside in the Beverly Hills subdivision. It was built in 1972 by Cebu's Chinese Filipino community and sits about 110 metres above sea level. It photographs well because the pagoda roofs, the long flight of steps and the wide view over the city all fit into one frame. The temple's stairs and pagoda rise above the road in the same view. This spot is said to be a quieter alternative to the crowded staircase, though the route comes from a 2011 report and may have changed since, so don't rely on it. The temple is open to worshippers and visitors alike, and devotees climb its 81 steps, one for each chapter of the Taoist scriptures. The entrance was built as a replica of the Great Wall of China. Once you are up at the temple, the dragon winding along the hillside and the tiered pagoda roofs make good foregrounds, with the coast and Mactan Island spread out below. Keep your phone outside the buildings: photography is not allowed inside the main hall and prayer halls, or of the saint statues. Visitors enter through a guardhouse where you need to show ID, so bring it with you. Opening hours vary, so check opening hours before you go.

Where it is: The temple is in the Beverly Hills subdivision of Cebu City, on a hillside above the downtown area.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the stairs, the pagoda and the city view in one frame. Use the default lens (1x) for a cleaner, less stretched view of the temple. Switch to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the pagoda roofs.

Where to stand: Stand at the corner of Canyon Road and Woolbright Drive (sidewalk on the left at the turn into Woolbright Drive, on the approach to the temple). An open view across the sprawling city, Lapu-Lapu City and Mactan Island, with the temple's stairs and pagoda rising above the road. Stand on Canyon Road, on the sidewalk on the left at the turn into Woolbright Drive. From here the temple lies to the north-west, and you can see the stairs and pagoda rising above the road, with the city, Lapu-Lapu City and Mactan Island opening out beside it.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the pagoda on one of the upper lines, leaving the city and sea view in the lower part of the frame. Keep the road edge as a leading line. If you go up to the temple itself, a second shot is the green dragon in the foreground with the red-and-gold rooflines behind it.

Settings tip: Tap the temple to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the red and gold stay rich and the sky isn't washed out. Take a Live Photo if people are on the stairs, so you can choose the best frame afterwards. Remember that photography is not allowed inside the halls.

Best time: Morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and gentler shadows on the colourful roofs than midday does. Check the opening times first, as they differ between listings.

2Ayala Center Cebu

Ayala Center Cebu is the big shopping mall at the heart of the Cebu Business Park. For a phone photo, the best part is The Terraces, its open-air garden area, where a large tree and lots of greenery sit among modern buildings. It reads as a small green pocket in the city, and that contrast is what makes the picture. The upper balcony looks over the landscaped garden and the tree, and the view is wide and open, so it works at most times of day. The mall opened in November 1994 and was the first Ayala mall outside Metro Manila. The Terraces opened on 30 October 2008, on the site of the mall's old lagoon area, and now holds food and drink places. The mall is very busy, with more than 100,000 visitors on an average day and up to 160,000 at weekends. Expect people in your frame, and go early if you want it calmer. A major redevelopment of the mall, including its parks and gardens, was planned from 2024 to 2026, so the layout may differ from older photos. Stalls and events at The Terraces can block parts of the view, so look around for a clear angle before you shoot. If you want a second picture, the wide stairs and entrance, or the fountain against the greenery, make good alternatives.

Where it is: Ayala Center Cebu is in the Cebu Business Park in Cebu City, and The Terraces is the open-air garden area within the mall. The subject is the mall itself.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole garden, the tree and the buildings around it. Use the default lens (1x) for a more natural look. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out details such as the tree canopy or people on the stairs.

Where to stand: Stand on The Terraces, Ayala Center Cebu (upper balcony overlooking the landscaped garden and the large tree). The mall's open-air garden with its large tree and greenery, framed by the surrounding modern architecture. From there you look out over the landscaped garden and the large tree, framed by the modern architecture around it.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the big tree on one of the third lines, with the buildings forming a frame around it. Keep the balcony rail low in the frame or out of it altogether. For a different shot, the wide stairs and entrance or the fountain against the greenery make good alternatives, and people add scale.

Settings tip: Tap on the tree to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright so the greenery keeps its colour. In the evening, switch on night mode and hold the phone steady against the rail.

Best time: Golden hour (the hour after sunrise or before sunset) and after dark, when the decorative lights come on, are the best times. The wide, open view also works at other times of day.

3Magellan's Cross

Magellan's Cross sits inside a small eight-sided kiosk of coral stone, built in 1834 under Spanish rule, on Plaza Sugbo beside the Basilica del Santo Niño. The pavilion houses the original wooden cross planted by Magellan's Spanish expedition when it reached Cebu on 21 April 1521, encased inside a tindalo-wood cross. The tindalo-wood case was placed around the original in 1835 to protect it from people chipping off pieces. Together with the basilica, the pavilion was declared a National Cultural Treasure in 2021.

It photographs well because the arches work like picture frames. Stand just outside one and you get the cross in the middle, with the painted dome ceiling above it. The mural shows the baptism of Rajah Humabon and his household and the planting of the cross. The pavilion is small, so stepping back outside gives you a fuller picture than squeezing in. For a wider shot, move back across the plaza and include the whole pavilion with the basilica towers behind it. This puts the cross in its historic setting.

The pavilion has been closed to the general public since cracks appeared in the 2025 earthquake. People can still pray outside and see the cross through the entrances and windows. There may be scaffolding around the outside, so check the current situation before you go. Keep your phone steady, take a few frames, and be quiet and respectful around people who are praying.

Where it is: Magellan's Cross is on Plaza Sugbo in Cebu City, right beside the Basilica del Santo Niño. Magallanes Street runs close by.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) from just outside an arch. It frames the cross and the ceiling together. If you want the whole pavilion with the basilica towers behind, step back across the plaza and switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x). If railings, scaffolding or people are in the way, try 2x zoom to pick out the cross through the opening.

Where to stand: Stand in Plaza Sugbo (just outside one of the pavilion's arches, facing the cross). The wooden cross inside the octagonal coral-stone kiosk, framed by its arches with the painted dome ceiling visible above. Walk about a minute south-east to just outside one of the pavilion's arches and face the cross. From there the arch frames the wooden cross inside the coral-stone kiosk, with the painted dome ceiling visible above it.

Framing: Centre the cross inside the arch and let the stonework make a natural frame. Turn on the gridlines and keep the verticals straight so the pavilion doesn't lean. For a different view, the wider shot from across Plaza Sugbo shows the whole facade with the basilica behind. Another option is Magallanes Street, which gives a street-level angle of the pavilion and the small chapel with the basilica close by.

Settings tip: Tap on the cross to focus. Then slide the exposure down a little if the bright sky or plaza is washing out the stonework. Take a few frames in case people walk through the shot.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon, when the light is softer.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Cebu City?

The first three on this map are Taoist Temple, Ayala Center Cebu and Magellan's Cross. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 15.

Where should I stand to photograph Taoist Temple?

Stand at the corner of Canyon Road and Woolbright Drive (sidewalk on the left at the turn into Woolbright Drive, on the approach to the temple). Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the stairs, the pagoda and the city view in one frame.

When is the best time to photograph Ayala Center Cebu?

Golden hour (the hour after sunrise or before sunset) and after dark, when the decorative lights come on, are the best times. The wide, open view also works at other times of day.

How do I get to Magellan's Cross?

There is no nearby station or tram stop for this spot, so head for the Basilica del Santo Niño in Cebu City.
