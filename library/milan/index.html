The 3 free photo spots

1Milan Cathedral (the Duomo)

Milan Cathedral is the largest church in Italy and one of the largest in the world. It is a huge Gothic building of pale marble, crowded with spires and statues. Work started in 1386 and the final details were not finished until 1965, so it has been built, added to and tidied up over nearly six centuries. Look high on the exterior for Amadeo's Little Spire, a Renaissance addition from 1507 to 1510 that sits happily among the Gothic points around it. The cathedral stands on the site of Milan's old Roman centre, and the streets around the square still radiate from it or circle it.

It photographs well because the façade is so wide and detailed, and because the open square gives you room to step back. The classic picture is the full width of the façade filling the frame, with the main spire centred above. Early morning is the usual advice: the square is emptier and the low sun lights the marble. By day it is very busy, so expect people in the foreground. For a variation, move to another spot in the square for an angled view with the Galleria entrance in front, which shows the cathedral's depth as well as its width. Via Torino is another option, with the cathedral rising above the rooflines. Tripods and selfie sticks are not allowed in the cathedral complex, so plan on handheld shots.

Where it is: In the very centre of Milan, on Piazza del Duomo. The Duomo Metro station is a short walk away, and the Galleria sits at the edge of the same square.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole façade in. Use the default lens (1x) for a tighter crop of the central section. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the spires and statues.

Where to stand: Stand about 230 m west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. From here you see the full width of the cathedral façade filling the frame, with the main spire centred above.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line up the central spire with the middle vertical line. Keep the phone level so the building doesn't lean backwards. For a second picture, move along the square for an angled view with the Galleria entrance in the foreground. Via Torino gives a more street-level view of the cathedral above the rooflines.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the marble to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the white stone keeps its detail. Zoom in on the spires with the telephoto and hold your elbows against your body to keep the shot steady.

Best time: Early morning, when the square is empty and the low sun catches the marble.

2Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

The Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II is a glass-roofed shopping arcade linking Piazza del Duomo and Piazza della Scala. It is the oldest active shopping arcade in Italy and a major Milan landmark. It was designed in 1861 and built by Giuseppe Mengoni between 1865 and 1877. Two arcades cross in an octagon, topped by a glass dome roughly 37.5 m across inside. Four barrel vaults run out from it, each crowned with a lantern. The ironwork was made by a French firm and the glass plates by Saint-Gobain.

It photographs well because everything leads the eye upward and along the length of the arcade. You get the curved glass-and-iron roof, long perspective lines and shopfronts on both sides. Under the dome, the floor has four mosaics showing the coats of arms of Turin, Florence, Rome and Milan. The Turin bull is worn into a hole by visitors who spin on it for luck. The arcade is also known as Milan's drawing room, and some of the city's oldest names are here, such as Biffi Caffè (1867) and the Borsalino hat shop (1883).

For a phone, work in layers. Take one wide shot of the dome, then a few closer shots of details such as mosaics, ironwork and shop signs. Keep the phone level, because the strong vertical and horizontal lines will show any tilt. Wait a moment for gaps in the crowd before you press the shutter.

Where it is: In the centre of Milan, between Piazza del Duomo and Piazza della Scala. It sits right beside the Duomo and across from Teatro alla Scala.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the dome and the curve of the glass roof. Use the default lens (1x) for the long view down the arcade. Switch to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for mosaics, statues and shop details.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Stand here and let the arcade open out around you, then turn slowly to compare the views in each direction.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and line them up with the shopfront edges so the verticals stay straight. Hold the phone level and tilt it up gently for the dome. Tilting too far makes the walls lean inwards. For a symmetrical shot, centre the arcade between the two rows of shops and let the roof lines converge. For a floor detail, use the telephoto on one of the mosaics.

Settings tip: Tap on a bright part of the roof or on a shopfront and slide the exposure slider down a little if the glass looks washed out. Turn on night mode for evening shots, but hold the phone against a wall or rail or brace your elbows to keep it steady. A Live Photo can help if people walk through your frame, because you can pick a cleaner moment afterwards.

Best time: Early in the morning usually means fewer people. Daylight comes through the glass roof, so it is bright in the middle of the day. In the evening the shop windows glow, which gives warmer, moodier shots.

3Sforza Castle

Sforza Castle is the big brick fortress at the edge of Milan's historic centre. Francesco Sforza built it in the 15th century on the remains of an earlier 14th-century fortification. Today it houses several of the city's museums. It photographs well because the front is wide, symmetrical and easy to read. The tall central Filarete Tower sits over the main gate, flanked by round corner towers and long red walls. The tower was rebuilt between 1900 and 1905 from 16th-century drawings, after the original was lost. In front, the semi-circular Piazza Castello was laid out in the Napoleonic period, and it leaves enough open space to step back and fit the whole front into the frame. The fountain in the middle of the square gives you a foreground, which stops the picture looking flat. Behind the castle, Parco Sempione now covers the old parade ground, and it offers a second, calmer view. From the pond you can catch the castle rising behind the water, with trees reflected in it. If you only have time for one picture, make it the classic postcard view of the tower and gate with the fountain in front. Take a few steps left or right until the tower sits centrally above the gate and the fountain.

Where it is: Sforza Castle sits at the edge of Milan's historic centre, between Piazza Castello and Parco Sempione. It lies at the end of Via Dante, on the way from the Duomo, and is a short walk from Cairoli.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole front and the fountain in one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural look at the tower and gate. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as the top of the Filarete Tower.

Where to stand: Stand about 120 m south-east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-west. The castle lies to the north-west of you. From here you see the Filarete Tower and the main gate, with the fountain in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the Filarete Tower up with a vertical line so it does not lean. Keep the fountain low in the frame as a foreground and leave the tower and gate in the middle. If you want a quieter alternative, walk into Parco Sempione and photograph the castle behind the pond, with the trees reflected in the water.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the brickwork to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky does not wash out. Keep your phone level, as tilting it makes the tower look as if it is falling backwards. Use the ultra-wide only if you need it, because it can stretch things at the edges of the frame.

Best time: Golden hour, according to one photo guide, when the light on the brick is warm.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Milan?

The first three on this map are Duomo, Galleria and Sforza Castle. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph the Duomo?

Stand about 230 m west of the Duomo, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole façade in.

When is the best time to photograph the Galleria?

Early in the morning usually means fewer people. Daylight comes through the glass roof, so it is bright in the middle of the day.

How do I get to Sforza Castle?

From Cairoli (Metro line M1), the castle is to the north-west and about 4 minutes on foot.
