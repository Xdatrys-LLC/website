This map shows 10 of the most-searched sights in Cancún as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Punta Nizuc, Playa Delfines, Playa Tortugas, Mercado 28 and Playa Gaviota Azul lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. Directions and Navigate buttons take you to the standing point on foot or by public transport. 3 spots are free to view; the full map is for members.

The 3 free photo spots

1Punta Nizuc

Punta Nizuc is a point within the Parque Nacional Costa Occidental de Isla Mujeres, Punta Cancún y Punta Nizuc, in the Nizuc area of Cancún. The photo works because of its simplicity: a strip of pale sand, clear shallow water and a long horizon, with very little clutter.

The tip itself is largely behind a resort, and outside visitors may no longer be able to walk out to it. For that reason the map sends you to the public beach at Playa Coral (El Mirador II), where the shoreline and the open water around the point frame the picture. A second option is the bridge at Puente Canal Nizuc. From the bridge you look across the channel to the clear water and tropical vegetation of the Punta Nizuc area. It makes a good second photo with a different feel, because the water leads your eye into the green.

For a phone, keep the picture simple. Put the sea and sand in the frame, let the shoreline curve through it, and keep the horizon level. Do not try to reach the resort side or any private pier or pool, as those are not public. Access can change, so check local access rules before you go.

Where it is: Punta Nizuc is in the Nizuc area of Cancún, near the airport road. The public beach at Playa Coral is close by.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the sand, sea and sky together. Use the default lens (1x) for a cleaner, more natural view of the shoreline. If your phone has a telephoto (2x-5x), use it to pick out the point across the water.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. From here the photo shows the Punta Nizuc shoreline and the open water on the point, with sand and sea framing the view. If you want a different angle, the bridge at Puente Canal Nizuc looks across the channel to the clear water and tropical vegetation.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the horizon on the upper or lower third line. Let the curve of the beach lead the eye towards the point. Keep a little wet sand in the foreground for interest. For a second shot, go to the bridge at Puente Canal Nizuc and photograph across the channel.

Settings tip: Tap on the point or the water to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the sky does not wash out and the water keeps its colour. Wipe the lens first, as sea spray and sunscreen leave a haze on phone cameras.

Best time: Morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and more comfortable conditions on the beach than midday.

2Playa Delfines

Playa Delfines is a beach in Cancún, and its classic photograph is taken from El Mirador, the lookout platform. From the south end of the platform, the colourful CANCÚN letters sit in the foreground. Behind them are the turquoise sea and the curve of the bay. The letters frame the water nicely, and the sun is behind you, so the colours look rich and the sea looks bright.

The main viewpoint is the south end of the El Mirador platform. Start with the default lens (1x) and hold the phone level, using the gridlines, so the horizon stays straight and the bay's curve is clear. Then try the 2x zoom for a tighter, more natural-looking view of the letters and the sea. For a close, head-on shot of the letters with just the ocean behind, use the Letrero CANCÚN viewpoint instead. Come back at sunset for the sky and sea seen from the lookout.

Two practical points. The letters may be at the beach entrance or beside the lookout, so check on the day exactly where they stand before you walk. The lookout and the sign also get queues from mid-morning, and the sign is busiest around midday. Seaweed and strong surf can affect the beach from summer to autumn, and swimming currents are strong, so stay at the shoreline.

Where it is: Playa Delfines is on the Caribbean coast of Cancún, and El Mirador is the lookout platform at Playa Delfines. The CANCÚN letters are at or near the lookout.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to capture the letters, the sea and the curve of the bay together. Switch to 2x for a tighter, more natural-looking frame. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want a dramatic close-up of the letters, and keep the horizon level.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (south end of the lookout platform, sun behind the photographer). The CANCÚN letters in the foreground with the turquoise Caribbean and the curve of the bay behind them. At the south end of the El Mirador platform, with the sun behind you. From the platform the photo shows the CANCÚN letters in the foreground with the turquoise Caribbean and the curve of the bay behind them. For a closer, head-on shot of the letters, try the Letrero CANCÚN viewpoint as an alternative.

Framing: Put the letters in the foreground and let the sea fill the space behind them. Turn on the gridlines and place the horizon on the upper or lower line so it stays straight. Keep a little sky and the curve of the bay in view. At sunset, shoot from the lookout towards the water to capture the setting sun over the sea.

Settings tip: Tap the sea or the letters to focus, then slide the exposure down slightly if the sky or the white sand looks too bright. Use Live Photos if people are walking through the frame, so you can choose the best moment afterwards. In the evening, use night mode only after the sun has gone, and hold the phone steady.

Best time: Early morning, roughly 7:30 to 9:30, when the light comes from the side and crowds are small. Sunset is the alternative for a colourful sky.

8Punta Cancún

Punta Cancún is the northern tip of the hotel zone, where the land bends round and the coast changes character. The focus here is Faro Punta Cancún, a red-and-white lighthouse on a rocky point, at Blvd. Kukulcán km 9, behind the Hyatt Ziva. On one side is the open Caribbean. On the other, the lagoon and the hotel-zone coastline stretch back behind it. Guides call it the classic photo subject of this area, and the sea views are wide. On a clear day Isla Mujeres shows up in the distance, which gives you a good second layer in the frame. The red and white of the tower stands out well against blue water and sky, so even a quick snap looks tidy. Reviewers say it works well for a short photo stop rather than a long visit. The rocks are sharp and the wind can be strong, so wear proper shoes and hold your phone firmly with both hands. Try a wide shot that includes the rocks in the foreground, then a closer one that fills the frame with the tower. Access is the one thing to plan for. The lighthouse sits on Hyatt Ziva property, and you reach it by walking over the rocky shoreline from the public beach at Playa Caracol or through the hotel grounds. Guards sometimes restrict passage, so it is not guaranteed.

Where it is: At the northern tip of the hotel zone (Zona Hotelera) in Cancún, on Blvd. Kukulcán at km 9, behind the Hyatt Ziva and not far from the public beach at Playa Caracol.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the lighthouse, the rocks and the open sea in one frame. The default lens (1x) is better for a cleaner shot of the tower alone. If your phone has a telephoto (2x-5x), use it to pick out Isla Mujeres in the distance or to crop in on the lighthouse.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Kukulcán km 9, behind the Hyatt Ziva. Frame the red-and-white tower from the nearby rocks, with the Caribbean on one side and the lagoon and hotel-zone coastline behind it.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the lighthouse on one of the vertical lines, with the sea filling the other two-thirds. Keep a few rocks in the foreground for depth. For a second shot, aim out to sea and let Isla Mujeres sit on the horizon, if it is visible.

Settings tip: Tap on the lighthouse to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the white paint keeps its detail against the bright sky. Hold the phone with both hands in the wind, and wipe the lens if it gets salty spray.

Best time: Sunrise or the golden hour before sunset, which is when guides describe the best light here.
