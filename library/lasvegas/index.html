The 3 free photo spots

1Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas sign

The Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas sign is one of the best-known landmarks in the city, and it is easy to see why people queue for it. It is a 25-foot, diamond-shaped roadside sign in 1950s style, edged with flashing yellow bulbs. Silver-dollar circles spell out "Welcome" across the top, and a red eight-pointed star crowns it. Designer Betty Willis created it for Clark County, and it went up in 1959. It stands in the median of Las Vegas Boulevard South, and some consider it to be the southern end of the Strip. Many people photograph the south face, which carries the "Las Vegas" lettering. The back, which reads "Drive Carefully, Come Back Soon", is photographed far less often and usually has no queue. Since 2008 there has been official access with a small car park and a paved walkway, and since 2015 marked crosswalks, so please use those rather than crossing the road. For a phone photo, the straight-on shot is the classic, but it often has a line of people in front. Stand a little to one side, off the central line, and the sign appears at an angle with its bulbs and poles in the foreground and the sky or the Strip behind. The default lens (1x) gives the most natural look, while the ultra-wide (0.5x) fits the whole sign when the plaza is crowded. Check the edges of the frame for strangers before you press the shutter.

Where it is: The sign sits in the middle of Las Vegas Boulevard South, at what some consider the southern end of the Strip. It is in Paradise, roughly 4 miles (6.4 km) south of the city limits of Las Vegas. It is north of the old stone pillars of the former McCarran Field and level with the Bali Hai Golf Club.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for a natural view of the sign. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are close or want the poles, star and sky all in the frame. A little telephoto zoom (2x) can help you crop out other visitors.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. For a quieter, more dynamic picture, move to either side of the sign, off the central line, towards the north or south edge of the platform. From there you see the sign at an angle, with its bulbs and poles in the foreground and the Strip or sky behind, and fewer people queuing in front of you.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and tilt the phone slightly upwards so the sign sits in the upper two thirds of the frame, with the poles running parallel to the lines. The straight-on view of the full face, with its starburst and lettering, is the classic shot if you can wait for a gap. For something different, walk round to the back of the sign for the "Drive Carefully, Come Back Soon" lettering, which rarely has a queue.

Settings tip: Tap the sign to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the bulbs and neon keep their colour instead of washing out. After dark, use night mode and hold the phone steady against your body or a railing. Try a Live Photo to catch the bulbs flashing.

Best time: The lit bulbs and neon show up better as the light fades.

2Caesars Palace

Caesars Palace is one of the Strip's best-known landmarks, a big Roman-themed resort on the west side of Las Vegas Boulevard, next to Bellagio. It opened in 1966 and was built to make guests feel like emperors. That is why the name has no apostrophe: every guest is a Caesar. Statues, columns and classical details run through the property, including a 20-foot (6.1 m) statue of Augustus Caesar near the entrance. By 2016 it had 3,960 rooms in six towers, so it is far too big to fit into one frame.

For a phone photo, the easiest approach is to stay outside on the Strip sidewalk. The corner with the Roman-style gazebo gives you a framed view of the pavement and Las Vegas Boulevard, with the hotel's front grounds behind you. It is calmer than the busy lobby. The fountains out front also have a bit of history: in 1967 the stunt rider Evel Knievel tried to jump them on a motorbike.

The gazebo corner can fall into shade quickly, so check your shadows and brighten them when you edit. Inside, the Forum Shops are crowded and the painted ceilings are hard to photograph. Ask staff before taking photos in the casino. Keep your phone close to your body on the busy pavement.

Where it is: Caesars Palace is on the west side of the Las Vegas Strip in Paradise, Nevada, between Bellagio and the Hard Rock site.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) at the gazebo corner, so you can fit the gazebo, the sidewalk and the boulevard into one frame. Use the default lens (1x) for a tidier, less stretched view. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as statues or the fountains.

Where to stand: Stand at the corner of Flamingo Road and Las Vegas Boulevard (gazebo in the far corner, out front of the hotel on the Strip sidewalk). The Strip sidewalk and Las Vegas Boulevard, framed by the gazebo, with the hotel's front grounds behind you. Stand on Las Vegas Boulevard, at the gazebo in the far corner out front of the hotel on the Strip sidewalk. From here your photo shows the Strip sidewalk and the boulevard framed by the gazebo, with the hotel's front grounds behind you. The main hotel complex lies to the north-west of this point.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the gazebo up on one side of the frame, with the pavement leading into the picture. Take a second shot turned round to catch the hotel's front grounds. For another view, try the Caesars Palace Fountains, where the three lit fountains sit in front of the casino with the hotel facade rising behind them.

Settings tip: Tap to focus on the gazebo or a person, then use the exposure slider to stop the sky washing out. This corner can get shady quickly, so brighten the shadows when you edit. In the evening, switch on night mode and hold still.

Best time: Choose a time when the pavement is quieter if you want a clean frame. Come after dark if you want the lights and fountains in the picture.

3Sphere (venue)

Sphere is a huge domed entertainment venue just off the Las Vegas Strip, and its outside is the draw for photographers. The dome is covered in LED panels, so it can look like a giant moon, an eyeball or a glowing planet, depending on what is playing. It stands 366 feet (112 m) high and 516 feet (157 m) wide, and its exterior carries 580,000 sq ft of LED displays, the largest in the world. It opened on 29 September 2023 with a U2 residency, and the lit outer shell can be seen from several miles away.

From across Las Vegas Boulevard from LINQ Promenade you get a wide view of the whole sphere with neon signs in the foreground. That helps the picture feel like Las Vegas rather than just a glowing ball. Viewing from here is free and open at all hours. Sunset to about 9 PM is the best window, when the sky still has some colour and the screen is bright. The outside programming changes through the day and night, so the display you hope for may not appear. If you have time, the Sands Avenue pedestrian bridge gives a nearly head-on silhouette of the whole dome with sky behind it. The Sands Avenue bridge and its corner are on busy pavements, so step aside from the flow of people before you stop to shoot.

Where it is: Sphere is in Paradise, Nevada, on the east side of the Las Vegas Strip, just east of the Venetian resort.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the whole dome with the neon signs in front. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if the crowd or buildings force you to stand close. Try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the screen, keeping your arms steady.

Where to stand: Stand on LINQ Promenade (across Las Vegas Boulevard from the promenade, facing the Sphere). A wide shot of the full spherical Exosphere with neon signage in the foreground for context. From here the photo shows the full spherical dome with neon signage in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the dome in the upper-middle of the frame, with the neon signs along the bottom. Keep the phone level so the horizon and buildings stay straight. For a different angle, the Sands Avenue pedestrian bridge gives a clear head-on silhouette of the dome with the sky behind it.

Settings tip: Tap on the dome to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the bright LED picture keeps its colours and detail. In low light, night mode can help with the street and signs. Hold the phone very still, since moving images on the dome can blur. Take a few shots, because the display changes.

Best time: Sunset to about 9 PM is the best window. The sky still has some colour and the dome's display shows up well.
