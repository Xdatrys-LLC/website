The 3 free photo spots

1Kuranda Scenic Railway

The Kuranda Scenic Railway is a heritage-listed line that climbs from Cairns up the Great Dividing Range to the town of Kuranda. Work began in 1886 and passenger services started on 25 June 1891. Builders cut 15 hand-made tunnels and built 37 bridges to lift the track from sea level to 328 metres. The whole line is 37 km long, and a one-way trip takes about one hour and 55 minutes.

For a phone photo, the easiest place to start is Freshwater Station at the foot of the range. The old station buildings, the carriages and the locomotive all sit together, with green hills behind. It is a simple, friendly scene, and the train gives you a strong subject whether it is standing or pulling away. The station also has an information centre, a gift shop and a café in an old train carriage, so there is plenty to look at while you wait.

Stand on the platform, roughly beside the carriages where the train leaves for Kuranda. Try one wide shot of the whole train with the hills behind, then move closer for details such as the carriage doors, the locomotive and the station signs. If you ride up, Barron Falls Station is a second photo stop. From its platform you see Barron Falls dropping over the rock cliffs across the gorge, with the heritage train beside you. Stoney Creek Falls is best photographed from inside the train.

Where it is: The railway runs through the northern suburbs of Cairns, including Stratford, Freshwater and Redlynch, before it climbs the Macalister Range.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) for the carriages and station buildings together. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if the platform is narrow and you want the whole length of the train. Use 2x telephoto, if your phone has it, for the locomotive and small details.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (the platform, roughly beside the carriages where the train departs for Kuranda). The train with its carriages and locomotive leaving the historic station platform, with the surrounding hills behind. Roughly beside the carriages where the train leaves for Kuranda.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the carriages up along one of the horizontal lines, so the train looks straight. Keep the hills as a green band behind it. Take a second shot closer in on the locomotive or a carriage door. If you ride up, Barron Falls Station is a good alternative: from its platform you can shoot the falls across the gorge with the train standing beside you. Stay on the platform and behind the barriers there.

Settings tip: Tap the train to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the carriage colours stay rich. Try a Live Photo as the train pulls out, but hold the phone steady.

Best time: Choose a departure time and arrive a little early, so you can take photos before boarding. For Barron Falls, the falls flow strongest after rain, so January to February is better than the drier months. Only some services stop at Barron Falls Station, so check your timetable.

2Cairns Esplanade Lagoon

Cairns Esplanade Lagoon sits on the city's waterfront, with the sea beyond it and the town behind. It photographs well because several things line up in one frame: still water, palms, the fish sculptures and the skyline. From here the lagoon and waterfront sit against the city skyline, which gives a different view from the one at the water's edge. If you also want the classic close-up, walk down to the lagoon afterwards. There the water fills the foreground, the ocean sits behind it, and the fish sculptures and palms frame the scene. Coconut Grove is a third option. It is a palm-lined walkway along the Esplanade, good for framing sky and sea rather than the lagoon. The best moment varies. Some favour the lagoon at high tide, others sunrise along the boardwalk, so it is worth taking a few frames at different times if you are staying nearby. Keep your phone level and use the gridlines so the waterline stays straight. Look for people, palms or sculptures to put in the foreground, which makes the scene feel bigger. Check current opening hours before you go. Drones are typically restricted near the lagoon, so check local rules if you travel with one.

Where it is: On the Esplanade waterfront in central Cairns, about 210 m north-west of the stand-here point in Fogarty Park, with the city skyline behind.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the lagoon against the skyline. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more water and sky, and use 2x if your phone has it to pick out the buildings behind.

Where to stand: Stand in Fogarty Park. The lagoon and waterfront set against the city skyline behind it. From here the lagoon and waterfront sit in front of the city skyline. The lagoon itself lies to the north-west.

Framing: Put the water and waterfront in the lower third and let the skyline fill the middle, using the gridlines to keep the horizon level. For an alternative, walk to the lagoon's edge and shoot across the water with the ocean behind and the fish sculptures and palms framing the scene. Coconut Grove, the palm-lined walkway, gives a frame of sky and sea.

Settings tip: Tap on the skyline to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. If the water is moving, try a Live Photo and pick the best frame afterwards.

Best time: Some favour the lagoon at high tide, others sunrise on the boardwalk. Early morning is a sensible first try, but check tide times if you want high water.

4Cairns Night Markets

Cairns Night Markets is a covered market where the photography is all about the evening atmosphere. Once the lights come on, the stalls glow, the food court adds warm colour, and the crowd drifting between souvenir tables gives you plenty of candid, street-style scenes. The market is fully undercover, so it gives shelter from rain or sun.

There is no outdoor viewpoint for the market's exterior, so the phone icon on the map sits inside the market itself, along the stall lanes. Walk the lanes slowly and look for rows of lit stalls, colourful souvenir displays and the busy glow around the food court. Entrances are at 73-75 Esplanade and on Abbott Street, so you can loop through from one to the other.

Photo guides say the market looks best in the evening after dark, once the lights are on. One also mentions the golden hour just before sunset as a pleasant time to arrive. Photography is generally fine for personal use, but be polite to stallholders and ask permission before taking close-up photos of people.

Where it is: Cairns Night Markets has entrances at 73-75 Esplanade and on Abbott Street, Cairns.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for stalls and the lit food court. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) in the wider lanes to fit in rows of stalls and the crowd. Use telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for details on souvenir tables or for candid shots from a polite distance.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. From here the photo shows the lit stalls and the busy evening crowd under the covered roof, with the glow of the food court nearby.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line up a lane so the rows of stalls lead towards the middle of the frame. Put a lit stall or a colourful display in the foreground and let the crowd fill the background. Wait for a gap in the crowd, or let people walk through the frame for a street-style feel. Ask before taking close-up photos of people.

Settings tip: Tap to focus on a lit stall, then drag the exposure slider down a little so bright lights do not wash out. In dimmer corners, use night mode and hold the phone steady against your body or a rail. Ask people to stay still for a moment, as night mode can blur anyone who moves. Live Photos can capture the movement of the crowd.

Best time: Evening after dark, when the lights are on. One guide also suggests golden hour just before sunset.
