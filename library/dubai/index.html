The 3 free photo spots

1Burj Khalifa

The Burj Khalifa is the world's tallest structure, at 829.8 m, and it opened in January 2010 as the centrepiece of Downtown Dubai. Skidmore, Owings & Merrill designed it with a Y-shaped floor plan, and its three wings step upwards to a slim spire. That tapering shape is what makes it so rewarding to photograph. From a good spot, the tower reads as one clean vertical line against the sky.

The best-known frame is from the Souk Al Bahar Bridge, a small footbridge over Burj Khalifa Lake. From here the tower rises behind the Dubai Fountain, with the water in the foreground and the Souk Al Bahar archways if you want a frame within the frame. It is free to stand there and works by day or during the evening fountain shows. It does get very crowded at show times, so arrive early and check the fountain schedule before you go.

If the bridge is too busy, Burj Park gives a wide, uncluttered view of the whole tower from base to tip. The Dubai Mall Waterfront Promenade shows the tower above the fountain, with Souk Al Bahar and the Downtown skyline around it.

The tower is so tall that you will need to step back or tilt your phone upwards to fit it in. Take a few frames at different distances, and keep the phone level so the sides do not lean inwards too much.

Where it is: The Burj Khalifa stands in Downtown Dubai, beside the Dubai Mall and Burj Khalifa Lake, close to Sheikh Zayed Road. The Souk Al Bahar Bridge crosses the lake on the Souk Al Bahar side.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the tower, the fountain and the lake in one frame. Then try the default lens (1x) for a more natural view. If your phone has a telephoto (2x-5x), use it to pick out the upper part of the tower or a tighter fountain-and-tower shot.

Where to stand: Stand on Souk Al Bahar Bridge (small footbridge over Burj Khalifa Lake, near the Souk Al Bahar side). The tower rising behind the Dubai Fountain, with the lake in the foreground and the Souk Al Bahar archways optionally in frame. The small footbridge over Burj Khalifa Lake near the Souk Al Bahar side. The tower rises behind the Dubai Fountain, with the lake in the foreground. If you like, include the Souk Al Bahar archways at the edge of the frame. For a wider, cleaner view of the whole tower, Burj Park is a good alternative.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the tower with a vertical line so it stands straight. Keep the lake in the lower third of the frame so the water and the fountain lead the eye upwards. If the bridge is crowded, hold the phone slightly higher than the heads in front of you. For a very different view, Burj Park or the Dubai Mall Waterfront Promenade are alternatives.

Settings tip: Tap the tower to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky and the bright fountain lights do not wash out. In the evening, use night mode and hold the phone steady against the railing. Live Photos can capture the movement of the fountain.

Best time: Arrive early for the evening fountain shows, as the bridge is very crowded at show times. Check the fountain schedule first. In daylight, early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light on the tower.

2Palm Jumeirah

Palm Jumeirah is an island built from the sea in the shape of a palm tree: a trunk, a set of fronds and an outer crescent that curves around the top. Building began in June 2001, and the Dutch dredging company Van Oord carried out the work. Today the trunk is mixed shops and homes, the fronds are villas, and the crescent holds the big resorts, with around 30 hotels on the trunk and crescent as of 2024. The shape is the whole point, and it is easiest to read from height. The best way to photograph it is from above. The View at The Palm is the open-air terrace and glass barrier on the upper levels of the Palm Tower. From about 240 m up you can see the crescent, the fronds and the trunk laid out below, with Dubai Marina and the downtown skyline in the distance. Guides often treat it as the standard Palm photo spot, and the shot at the glass barrier is the one most people come for. Two ground-level alternatives are Palm Jumeirah Corniche, for the Marina and Jumeirah Beach Residence skyline across the water, and The Pointe, for Atlantis The Palm against the sea and sky. Drone flying over the Palm is heavily restricted, so don't plan an aerial shot from the ground. The terrace is the realistic way to get one.

Where it is: Palm Jumeirah is the palm-shaped island off the Jumeirah coast, on the Gulf side of Dubai. Dubai Marina is visible across the water from the viewpoint. The viewpoint is in the Palm Tower on the island itself.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole palm shape, crescent included, into one frame. Then switch to the default lens (1x) for a tidier view of the skyline. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out Dubai Marina or the towers in the distance.

Where to stand: Stand at The View at The Palm (open-air terrace and glass barrier on the upper observation levels of the Palm Tower). The whole palm-shaped island from about 240 m up, with the crescent, fronds and trunk laid out below and the Dubai Marina and downtown skyline in the distance. At the glass barrier on the open-air terrace of the upper observation levels of the Palm Tower. From here the island's trunk, fronds and crescent lie below you. Dubai Marina and the downtown skyline sit in the distance.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the trunk with a vertical line so the fronds spread evenly either side. Keep the whole crescent in the frame, because that is what makes the shot recognisable. If you want something different, Palm Jumeirah Corniche gives a view of the Marina skyline across the water, with the Palm's shoreline in the foreground.

Settings tip: Tap on the island to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. Hold the phone close to the glass, without pressing hard, to cut reflections. Try a Live Photo or a panorama if you want to take in the whole sweep of the skyline.

Best time: Sunset is the time guides recommend, but sunset slots cost more and sell out, so book ahead. After the light fades, use night mode and brace your elbows on the barrier to keep the phone steady.

3Burj Al Arab

Burj Al Arab is the sail-shaped hotel that stands on its own artificial island off the Jumeirah coast. It opened on 1 December 1999 and is about 321 m tall. The shape is meant to recall the sail of a dhow, and the translucent fibreglass facade is lit up at night, so the hotel looks different at dusk than at midday. The island sits roughly 280 m offshore and is linked to the land by a private curving bridge, so you photograph it from a distance rather than from its doorstep. From the outdoor areas along the canals and the open-air dining terraces, the hotel appears in the distance, framed by the souk's arches, waterways and traditional Arabian architecture. The old-style foreground against the modern sail makes the picture. If you want a clean side profile instead, Umm Suqeim Beach and Umm Suqeim Park are the alternatives. The hotel is currently closed for renovation, due to run until 31 October 2027, so check what the exterior looks like before planning a special trip. Take a few frames at different zoom levels and pick the one where the arches and the tower balance best.

Where it is: Burj Al Arab sits on its own island just off Jumeirah Beach, on Dubai's Gulf coast.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the arches, the water and the hotel together. Switch to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the sail. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you are standing close to an arch and want it to frame the scene.

Where to stand: Stand at Souk Madinat Jumeirah (outdoor areas along the canals and open-air dining terraces). The hotel seen in the distance, framed by the souk's arches, waterways and traditional Arabian architecture. On the outdoor walkways by the canals or on the open-air dining terraces.

Framing: Use an arch as a natural frame and place the hotel in the opening, with the canal as a leading line below it. Turn on gridlines and keep the tower on one of the vertical lines so it stays upright. If you want a plain side view of the sail above the sea, Umm Suqeim Beach is the alternative, and Umm Suqeim Park gives the hotel behind lawns and paths.

Settings tip: Tap the hotel to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the bright sky doesn't wash out the sail. After dark, use night mode and rest your elbows on a wall or rail to keep the shot steady.

Best time: Late afternoon into dusk usually works well, when the sky softens and the hotel's lighting starts to show. Check on the day, as conditions vary.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Dubai?

The first three on this map are Burj Khalifa, Palm Jumeirah and Burj Al Arab. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 19.

Where should I stand to photograph the Burj Khalifa?

Stand on Souk Al Bahar Bridge (small footbridge over Burj Khalifa Lake, near the Souk Al Bahar side). Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the tower, the fountain and the lake in one frame.

When is the best time to photograph Palm Jumeirah?

Sunset is the time guides recommend, but sunset slots cost more and sell out, so book ahead. After the light fades, use night mode and brace your elbows on the barrier to keep the phone steady.

How do I get to Burj Al Arab?

There is no Metro or tram stop at this spot, so the easiest way is by taxi or a navigation app to Souk Madinat Jumeirah, on the Jumeirah coast.
