The 3 free photo spots

1Table Mountain

Table Mountain is the flat-topped mountain that rises behind Cape Town, and it is a prominent landmark overlooking the city. The level plateau is roughly three kilometres from side to side, edged by steep cliffs, with Devil's Peak to the east and Lion's Head to the west. The top stands about 1,000 metres above the streets below, so even from a distance it fills a good part of the frame. It also holds around 2,285 plant species, about 80% of them fynbos, which is why the low bushes near your feet make a natural foreground. From Signal Hill you see the mountain side-on, so the cliffs and the long flat edge show up clearly, with Lion's Head alongside. The mountain is often covered by a layer of cloud known as the 'table cloth', which can hide the plateau or spill over it, so look at the forecast and be ready to wait a little. Keep the horizon level and leave a strip of fynbos along the bottom of the picture to give a sense of scale. If you want a different view, Kloof Corner frames the mountain against the sky and city, but it is a steep walk. Bloubergstrand shows the whole front of the mountain across the bay, about 20 minutes' drive north of the city.

Where it is: Table Mountain rises directly above Cape Town's City Bowl, with Lion's Head to its west and Devil's Peak to its east.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start on the default lens (1x) to see the whole scene. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want fynbos close to the lens and Lion's Head in the same frame. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the cliffs and the straight edge of the plateau.

Where to stand: Stand in Signal Hill (the quieter small car park near the top and the hiking path leading from it, away from the main upper car park). Table Mountain seen from the side, with its cliffs and flat plateau, alongside Lion's Head, with fynbos in the foreground. From here Table Mountain is seen from the side, with its cliffs and flat plateau next to Lion's Head and fynbos in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the plateau's top edge on one of the horizontal lines so it stays straight. Keep roughly the bottom third for the fynbos and the mountain in the upper two thirds. A panorama works if you sweep slowly from Lion's Head across to the plateau. Hold the phone steady and keep it level while it sweeps.

Settings tip: Tap on the cliffs to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright so the cloud keeps its detail. If cloud is moving over the top, take a Live Photo or a few quick shots and choose the best one.

Best time: Sunrise is recommended for this view, and the small car park is quieter than the main one. If you go for sunset, arrive early because parking fills up.

2Clifton

Clifton is a string of four small beaches on the Atlantic side of Cape Town, split from one another by big rounded granite boulders. The beaches are numbered 1st to 4th from north to south, and the sand is almost pure white. That gives you a clean pale curve of sand, dark boulders and blue water in the same frame, which is why it works so well on a phone. Houses climb the slope behind, and Lion's Head rises above them.

The beaches are well sheltered from the strong south-easter, which is part of their popularity. The sand moves with the seasons. In winter it is stripped away and the boulders stand out more. By late summer the beaches are at their widest. Fourth Beach is the busiest and most glamorous of the four, and it has Blue Flag status.

For this photo, go to the point near The Rock, at the end of Ocean View Drive. From this higher angle you look down onto the beaches and the boulders between them. It is a quieter view than the beach itself. Stay well back from the edge of the boulders. For a second angle, walk down to Clifton 4th Beach for the white sand and turquoise shallows, with the Twelve Apostles ridge and Lion's Head behind. Kloof Road is another option, with a high view over the coves towards Camps Bay and Table Mountain. It is a busy winding road, so keep to the pavement or a safe verge.

Where it is: Clifton is a seaside suburb on Cape Town's Atlantic Seaboard, between Bantry Bay and Camps Bay, with Lion's Head mountain behind it. The viewpoint is at the end of Ocean View Drive, on the high ground above the beaches.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the beaches and boulders together. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the coast and sky. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out one beach or the pattern of boulders.

Where to stand: Stand on The Rock (Ocean View Drive, at the end of the road; walk from the parking spots to the point near The Rock). An elevated view looking south down onto the Clifton beaches and the boulders between them. From there you look down onto the Clifton beaches and the boulders between them. Stay on the walking path from the parking spots and do not climb onto the boulders or out to their edge.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and put the line of the beaches along a lower third, with the sea and sky above. Use the boulders in the foreground to lead the eye down the coast. For a different angle, try Clifton 4th Beach, where Lion's Head and the Twelve Apostles sit behind the sand. Kloof Road gives a high view towards Camps Bay and Table Mountain.

Settings tip: Tap on the sand to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the white sand does not wash out. A short panorama along the coast also works well if you hold the phone steady.

Best time: Choose a clear day, and check the weather before you set off, because haze and cloud flatten the colours of the sea.

3V&A Waterfront

The V&A Waterfront is a working harbour area on Table Bay, with shops, restaurants, hotels and marina basins, and Table Mountain rising behind it. It stands on the oldest working harbour in the Southern Hemisphere. The story goes back to 1654, when Jan van Riebeeck built a small jetty here to supply passing ships. The two harbour basins were built between 1860 and 1920, and several buildings from that era are still standing.

For a phone photo, the easiest classic shot is at the large yellow picture frame on the harbour side, near the Den Anker restaurant. Table Mountain sits neatly inside the frame, so the picture is composed for you, and the harbour and city skyline are behind you. It works well for a photo of the mountain on its own, or of a friend standing in the frame.

If you have time for a second picture, Nobel Square puts four bronze statues in front of the mountain. The Clock Tower, built in 1882 and now leaning very slightly, gives a quieter, more architectural view with reflections on the water. Expect other visitors to be taking the same photo, so be patient and take a few frames while people move through.

Where it is: The V&A Waterfront is a district of Cape Town on the Table Bay harbour edge, just north of the city centre and with Table Mountain as its backdrop. The Yellow Frame is on the harbour side, near the Den Anker restaurant.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the mountain fills the frame naturally. Try the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the frame and sky, and the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) to bring the mountain closer.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (on the harbour side, near the Den Anker restaurant; stand inside the frame and face Table Mountain). Table Mountain framed by the large yellow picture frame, with the harbour and city skyline behind the photographer. On the harbour side near the Den Anker restaurant, and face Table Mountain. The mountain appears framed by the big yellow edges, with the harbour and city skyline behind you.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the mountain up with the centre of the frame, keeping the horizon level. Hold the phone steady and square on, and keep the frame edges even on both sides. For a different picture, Nobel Square gives the bronze statues with the mountain behind, and the Clock Tower is a quieter option.

Settings tip: Tap on the mountain to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky looks washed out. Use portrait mode if you are photographing a person in the frame.

Best time: Choose a clear day when Table Mountain is not covered by cloud. Go early if you want fewer people in the frame.
