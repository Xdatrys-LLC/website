The 3 free photo spots

1Temple of the Tooth

The Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic (Sri Dalada Maligawa) is the Buddhist temple in Kandy that holds the tooth relic of the Buddha. It stands in the old Royal Palace Complex and is part of a World Heritage Site. The temple you see today was built under Vira Narendra Sinha. The octagonal Paththirippuwa and the moat came later, in the reign of Sri Vikrama Rajasinha. The Kandy Esala Perahera is held every year to pay homage to the Sacred Tooth Relic.

The best all-round photo is from above, not at the gates. Guides call it the classic postcard view. The viewpoint is free and open to the public. The temple is small in the frame, so take one wide shot for the setting and one zoomed-in shot for the temple itself.

If you want a different angle, walk the lakeside path at Kandy Lake. From there the white roofs and spires are reflected in the water, and you stand at ground level. Photography is restricted inside the temple, so plan on exterior pictures. Take a few shots with people in them if you can, as they show the scale of the place.

Where it is: The temple is in the centre of Kandy, in the former Royal Palace Complex beside Kandy Lake. The viewpoint is on the hillside to the south-east, on Rajapihilla Mawatha, looking down over the city.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) for the wide view of lake, hills and rooftops. Switch to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the temple. Use ultra-wide (0.5x) only if people crowd the platform and you want to step back.

Where to stand: Stand on Kandy View Point (built platform on Rajapihilla Mawatha, uphill above the city). Kandy Lake, the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic and surrounding green hills in one panoramic frame, with the temple among the rooftops below. The built platform on Rajapihilla Mawatha, uphill above the city. From here the temple sits to the north-west among the rooftops, with Kandy Lake and the green hills around it in the same frame.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the horizon on the top third line, so the lake and rooftops fill the lower part of the frame. Then take a second, zoomed shot with the temple roofs on a third line. If the platform is busy, the lakeside walk at Kandy Lake gives a different view, with the spires reflected in the water.

Settings tip: Tap the temple to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky keeps its colour and the roofs are not too bright. At dusk, try night mode and rest your elbows on a solid ledge or wall, if there is one, to keep the phone steady.

Best time: Go early in the morning or just before sunset, when crowds are smaller and the light is softer. At sunset you may also catch the city lights coming on.

2Royal Botanic Gardens, Peradeniya

Royal Palm Avenue is the classic postcard view of the Royal Botanic Gardens at Peradeniya. Tall royal palms stand in two straight rows and seem to meet in the distance, so the avenue works as a natural leading line. It is a very easy picture to take on a phone: the symmetry does most of the work for you.

The gardens cover 147 acres beside the Mahaweli River and hold more than 4,000 species of plants, including orchids, spices, medicinal plants and many kinds of palm. The garden was formally established in 1843, and the avenues along River Drive are among its best-known features. Elsewhere in the grounds you can find the Cannonball Tree planted by King George V and Queen Mary in 1901.

Walk to the entrance end of the avenue and shoot down its length. Keep yourself in the middle of the path so both rows of palms look evenly balanced. Wait for a gap in the walkers if you can, or let one or two people stand in the distance to show how tall the trees are. Other good spots nearby are the Suspension Bridge, for river and bank views, and the Great Lawn, where the giant Java fig spreads over the grass. The gardens are a big site, so wear comfortable shoes and allow plenty of time.

Where it is: The gardens are at Peradeniya, about 5.5 km west of Kandy city, close to the Mahaweli River. Royal Palm Avenue is one of the avenues along River Drive inside the grounds.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) for the main shot down the avenue. Try ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the palms to look even taller, but keep the phone level so the rows don't lean. The telephoto (2x or more) squeezes the palms closer together, which can look striking from further back.

Where to stand: Stand on Royal Palm Avenue (main palm avenue, shoot down the length of the avenue from the entrance end). A symmetrical view of tall royal palms converging along a straight path, with the avenue as a leading line. Face along the path so the palms run away from you in two straight rows.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and put the middle of the path on the centre line, so both rows of palms mirror each other. Hold the phone at chest height and keep it level. A portrait (upright) frame suits the tall trunks, and a landscape frame shows more of the path. If you want a different view, the Suspension Bridge frames the river and its banks, and the Great Lawn shows the full spread of the giant fig.

Settings tip: Tap on the far end of the avenue to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky looks washed out. Take a few frames, as walkers will change the picture from one second to the next.

Best time: The light along the avenue is best in the first hour after the gardens open, when the path is also likely to be quieter.

3Queen's Hotel, Kandy

Queen's Hotel is a 90-room British Colonial style hotel at the end of Kandy's main street, and it is one of the oldest hotels in Sri Lanka, with more than 160 years of history. The building began as a royal residence, the Dullawe Walauwa, built for King Sri Wickrama Rajasinha. After the British defeated the Kandyan Kingdom in 1815 it became a mansion for the Governor of Ceylon. Later it served as barracks, then a hostel and boarding house, and it took the name Queens Hotel in 1869.

It is an elevated terrace with a wide view over the town, where the hotel sits alongside Kandy Lake and the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic. From here the hotel is one part of a bigger scene, not a close-up, so think of it as a town portrait with the hotel as one landmark in it. If you want the building's frontage itself, D S Senanayake Veediya, formerly Malabar Street, is the alternative. Keep your phone steady against a railing or wall, and wipe the lens first, as hazy hill air and fingerprints soften distant shots quickly.

Where it is: Queen's Hotel is in the centre of Kandy, at the end of the main street, close to Kandy Lake and the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic. The viewpoint is on higher ground to the south-east of the centre, looking back over the town.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick the hotel out of the town. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) or panorama for the whole scene, and the default lens (1x) for a balanced frame.

Where to stand: Stand on Kandy View Point. An elevated panorama in which Queen's Hotel appears alongside Kandy Lake, the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic and the town. On the terrace, with the hotel to the north-west. From here you see Queen's Hotel alongside Kandy Lake, the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic and the rooftops of the town. If you want the hotel's frontage and the temple area nearby, walk to D S Senanayake Veediya instead.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the hotel on a third line, with the lake and temple area filling the rest of the frame. Take one wide shot first for the scene, then zoom in for a second frame where the hotel is the main subject. In the second frame, keep the horizon level. For a variation, take a panorama and sweep slowly from the lake across to the hotel.

Settings tip: Tap on the hotel to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky does not wash out. If you use zoom above 2x, brace your elbows on the rail and take two or three frames, then keep the sharpest. Switch on night mode for the lights after dusk.

Best time: Reviewers say the view is especially good around sunset, when the city lights come on. Arrive a little before, so you can try your frames while the light is still soft.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Kandy?

The first three on this map are Temple of the Tooth, Peradeniya Gardens and Queen's Hotel. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 10.

Where should I stand to photograph Temple of the Tooth?

Stand on Kandy View Point (built platform on Rajapihilla Mawatha, uphill above the city). Use the default lens (1x) for the wide view of lake, hills and rooftops.

When is the best time to photograph Peradeniya Gardens?

The light along the avenue is best in the first hour after the gardens open, when the path is also likely to be quieter.

How do I get to Queen's Hotel?

No station or tram stop is listed for this spot, so you will need your own way to reach Kandy View Point from the middle of Kandy.
