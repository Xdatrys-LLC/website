The 3 free photo spots

1Jeju Dongmun Traditional Market

Jeju Dongmun Traditional Market is a covered market in Jeju City, and it is the largest and oldest permanent traditional market still standing on the island. It started after the end of the Japanese colonial period. A fire destroyed it in March 1954, and it was rebuilt in its current location under the same name. Today it is known for fresh seafood, street food, souvenirs and hallabong mandarins, and there is a night market of food-truck-style vendors at the back.

It photographs well because there is so much going on: bright produce, hanging signs, steaming food and a long covered frontage. The best overall picture is taken from outside, across the stream, where you can fit the market's frontage and roofline into one frame with the lit walkway in front. That also makes a good evening shot. Inside, the aisles are busy and close, so you will mostly be taking details and people-at-work shots rather than wide views.

For a second idea, Dongmun Market Gate 8 has a large entrance signboard with lanterns and stalls behind it, which suits group photos. A few footbridges on the stream north of the market are also worth a look for a different angle. Whatever you shoot, keep the lens clean, as market air can be steamy and greasy, and be polite when pointing your phone at stallholders and their produce.

Where it is: In Jeju City, Jeju Province, right beside the Sanjicheon stream. The stream's park and promenade lie directly across from the market.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the market frontage across the stream. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the footbridges or when you want more of the promenade in the foreground. Inside the market, a telephoto (2x or more) helps you capture stall details without leaning over the food.

Where to stand: Stand in 산지천 (opposite bank of the stream, facing the market). The covered market's frontage and roofline, framed across the stream, with the lit promenade in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the market's roofline up with a horizontal line so the building sits straight. Keep some of the walkway or stream edge in the lower part of the frame to give depth. For a different picture, try Dongmun Market Gate 8 for its big entrance sign, with the lanterns and stalls behind it, which suits group portraits.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest sign or lit area to focus, then lower the exposure slider a little so the lights do not burn out. After dark, use night mode and hold the phone still against a railing or your body for the few seconds it needs. Inside the market, use tap-to-focus on the food and a Live Photo to catch steam and movement.

Best time: Evening is the best time for the lit promenade and the night market, but it gets busy, so arrive early. The covered market also runs by day, so daytime pictures are possible.

2Jeju National Museum

Jeju National Museum is an archaeology and local history museum in Jeju. It opened in June 2001, after construction that ran from December 1992 to the end of December 2000. The displays focus on Jeju's past, including Tamna and the island's maritime culture, so it is a good place to see how Jeju's story fits together before you head out to the coast and the volcanic sights.

For photographers, the appeal is the sheer scale of the site. The outline of the museum is around 343 metres across, so there is room to step back and let the building and its surroundings fill the frame, rather than shooting one close-up of a wall. Wide, calm frames work best: clean lines, open sky and a few people for scale.

On the map, the phone icon on the map sits at the centre of the museum outline, so you are standing at the subject itself. Walk a little way along the outside of the building until you find an angle where its full width fits in the frame, and use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for that. Then switch to the default lens (1x) for a tidier, less stretched view of one section. If you go inside, remember that photography rules for galleries can differ, so look for signs or ask staff.

Where it is: The museum is in Jeju, South Korea. It is a large site rather than a single small building.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole width of the building, and the default lens (1x) for a more natural view of one section. If your phone has a telephoto (2x-5x), use it for roof lines, entrances and architectural details.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and line up the building's edges with them so verticals stay straight. Keep your phone level; tilting it up makes the walls lean inwards. Include some open ground or sky around the building to show its scale, and wait for a gap in passing visitors if you want a clean shot.

Settings tip: Tap the building on screen to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky looks washed out. Try a panorama for the full width, moving slowly and keeping the phone upright.

Best time: Choose a bright but not harsh part of the day, such as the morning or late afternoon, when light is softer and shadows are gentler. Overcast days also work well for even colour on the building.

3Samseonghyeol

Samseonghyeol is a shrine site in the middle of Jeju City, tied to the island's founding legend. The story says three demigods, Go Eulna, Yang Eulna and Bu Eulna, rose from holes in the ground here about 4,300 years ago and became the first people of Tamna, the old kingdom of Jeju. Three families still bear those names and claim descent from them. The grounds are best known for the three holes framed by a large cherry tree, and that view is the busy one. For a quieter picture, try the small stream on the edge between Sinsan Park and the shrine. A Jeju photo guide describes it as a secret photo spot, and the cherry branches hang low over the water, with the shrine grounds close by. The result is a soft, layered scene of blossom, water and old trees, without the crowd around the main tree. If you want the classic views too, the holes with the big cherry tree and the red-pillared Samseongmun gate, with its signboard and tiled eaves, are both a short stroll away. Blossom season in spring is the busiest time, so go early. Keep your phone low and close to the water, and let the branches fill the top of the frame.

Where it is: Samseonghyeol is in the city centre of Jeju City, in Jeju Province. The stream sits on the edge between Sinsan Park and the shrine grounds.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the stream and branches together. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if the branches are overhead and you want the water and the shrine in the same frame. Use telephoto (2x or more) to pick out a single blossom cluster.

Where to stand: Stand in 신산공원 (the small stream on the edge between Sinsan Park and Samseonghyeol). Cherry branches hanging down over a small stream, with the shrine grounds nearby.

Framing: Crouch a little and hold the phone low so the branches arch over the top of the picture and the water fills the bottom. Turn on the gridlines and place the stream along a lower line so the frame stays balanced. For the classic view instead, the holes framed by the large cherry tree, or the Samseongmun gate, are a short walk into the shrine grounds.

Settings tip: Tap on the blossom to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the pale petals keep their detail against the sky. A Live Photo can catch the branches moving over the water. Wipe the lens before you start.

Best time: Go early in the morning in the spring cherry blossom season, when it is quieter. Outside blossom time the stream is calmer, but the branches will be bare or in leaf.
