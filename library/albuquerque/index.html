This map shows 13 of the most-searched sights in Albuquerque as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Explora, University of New Mexico, KiMo Theater, Old Town and Frontier Restaurant lead the list. The 3 free photo spots

1Explora

Explora is a spot on the Albuquerque map, and the building itself is the main thing to photograph here. The phone icon on the map sits at the mapped position of the subject, so you can take your first shots as soon as you arrive. Start with a wide view that shows the whole frontage and a little of the sky, then move closer for details such as signs, entrance doors, shapes and colours. These details make good second and third photos and help your set tell the story of the visit. Pick a moment when few people are in front of the building so the frontage stays clear. If visitors do walk through the frame, you can use them to show scale. Walk a few steps left or right to avoid reflections in glass and to keep the lines of the walls straight. Take a quick test shot, check the edges for stray bins, signs or cars, and retake if needed. Stay out of any restricted areas to get a better angle. If you plan to go inside, check the official site before you go, because rules on photography and entry can change.

Where it is: Explora is in Albuquerque, New Mexico.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Begin with the default lens (1x) for a natural view of the frontage. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you cannot step back far enough to fit the whole building in. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for signs and architectural details.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Step back as far as is safe and allowed to fit the whole building in, or move in close for details.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the edges of the walls with the vertical lines so the building does not lean. Leave a band of sky above the roofline. Then take a closer shot of one detail, such as the entrance or the signage.

Settings tip: Tap on the building to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the sky is bright, so it stays blue and the walls keep their detail. Keep the ultra-wide shots level, because tilting the phone makes edges look distorted.

Best time: Soft light in the morning or late afternoon usually gives the nicest colour on the walls. Midday sun can create harsh shadows and glare on glass.

2University of New Mexico

The University of New Mexico is Albuquerque's flagship university, and its main campus covers about 800 acres in the middle of the city. For a visitor with a phone, the draw is the look of the place: low, earth-toned Pueblo Revival buildings with stucco walls, wooden beams and towers, set among trees and desert planting. The style goes back to the early 1900s, when president William G. Tight introduced it, and it was later developed across the campus by the Santa Fe architect John Gaw Meem. The university itself was founded in 1889. A good place to start is the UNM Duck Pond, a shaded, landscaped pond with footbridges. Water, shade and buildings together give you a calm picture with plenty of layers. Two other good frames are Mesa Vista Hall, for its tower and courtyard, and Zimmerman Library, where desert plants and stone steps make a natural foreground. Keep your phone low and level, let the foreground plants or the bridge rails lead the eye into the picture, and take a few frames from slightly different heights.

Where it is: The University of New Mexico's main campus sits in central Albuquerque, New Mexico. The UNM Duck Pond is within the campus greenery, west of the central academic buildings.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the pond, the bridges and the trees. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) when you want the footbridge and buildings in one frame. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for tower tops, wooden beams and other architectural details.

Where to stand: Stand in UNM Duck Pond. A shaded, landscaped pond with footbridges and the surrounding campus greenery, framed with some of the Pueblo Revival buildings. From here you can photograph the shaded pond, the footbridges and the surrounding campus greenery, with some of the Pueblo Revival buildings in the frame. The main campus point is to the east.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the water or a footbridge in the lower third, with the buildings and trees above. If you want a second view, Mesa Vista Hall shows the tower, courtyard, stucco walls and beams, and Zimmerman Library works well with the desert plants and stone steps in the foreground.

Settings tip: Tap on the brightest building wall to set focus, then lower the exposure slider a little so the sky and stucco do not wash out. Dark water and shade can look heavy, so check the result and nudge the slider back up if needed.

Best time: Late afternoon or golden hour is recommended for the pond, and it is also described as especially beautiful in the evening.

13The Pit (arena)

The Pit is the University of New Mexico's basketball arena, and from the outside it is a building with a surprise. It opened in 1966 as University Arena, but students were calling it "The Pit" before the first game. The playing floor sits 37 feet (11 m) below street level, so most of the arena is underground. Its 338-by-300-foot (103 m × 91 m) roof was built first, just above street level, and the bowl was dug out beneath it. That is why you see a low, wide building from the street rather than a tall stadium.

For a phone photo, the best view is the east side, looking west across University Blvd SE. From University Stadium you can fit in the glass tower and the curved roofline of the east facade, with the sky above. It is the classic outside view of the building. The main entrance has a street-level glass front with two wolf statues in the foreground, which makes a good second shot, more of a close-up with a bit of fun in it.

The arena holds 15,411 for basketball, but you will not see that from outside, so concentrate on the shapes: the roof curve, the glass and the straight lines of the facade. Take a wide shot first, then move closer for details. On event days there may be crowds or restrictions, so check before you go.

Where it is: The Pit is on the University of New Mexico's South Campus in Albuquerque, on University Blvd SE.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole east facade, the glass tower and the curved roof from across the street. Then switch to the default lens (1x) for a tidier crop. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as the glass tower or the roof edge.

Where to stand: Stand at University Stadium (east side of The Pit, across University Blvd SE, looking west). The arena's east facade with its glass tower and curved roof, as shown in a 2011 photo on Wikipedia. On the east side of The Pit, across University Blvd SE, and look west. From there you see the arena's east facade with its glass tower and curved roof.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line up the glass tower against a vertical line so the facade does not lean. Leave some sky above the curved roof. Keep traffic and signs on University Blvd SE out of the lower edge, or let the road lead the eye towards the building. For a different shot, walk to the main entrance and photograph the glass front with the two wolf statues in the foreground.

Settings tip: Tap on the glass facade to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the building keeps its detail. Take a few frames at slightly different zoom levels, and hold the phone level to keep the lines straight.

Best time: As an assumption, the east-facing front will generally be lit by morning sun. Event days may be busy, so check the arena's schedule first.
