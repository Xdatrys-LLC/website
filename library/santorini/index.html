The 3 free photo spots

1Oia

Oia is a picture-perfect village on Santorini: whitewashed houses stacked along the top of the caldera cliff, blue-domed churches and traditional cave houses cut into the rock. It stretches for almost two kilometres along the caldera edge, roughly 70 to 100 metres above the sea, so almost every turn gives a new angle. A classic photo is taken from Agios Nikolaos Castle, the ruined walls and lookout terrace at the northern tip of the village, often called Oia Castle. From the castle you can fit the cliff-top houses, the two windmills and the caldera into one frame, with the sun going down over the sea behind the village. The village was rich in the late 19th century. In 1890 it had about 2,500 residents and around 130 sailing ships, trading across the Eastern Mediterranean. The earthquake of 9 July 1956 then badly damaged it, and it was rebuilt as the tourist village you see today. The population was 1,087 at the 2021 census. The village is known for being crowded in summer, and the castle wall fills up early, so plan to arrive well ahead of sunset. If the castle is too busy, the viewpoint over the three blue domes of the church is a good alternative, with the sea and caldera behind the domes. Ammoudi Bay, reached by about 300 steps, shows Oia's cliffs from sea level.

Where it is: Oia is on the north-west edge of Santorini, along the northern rim of the caldera, with Finikia just to the east and Ammoudi Bay below the village. Fira, the island's main town, is about 11 km away.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the full postcard view of houses, windmills and caldera. Switch to the telephoto lens (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the windmills or a single cluster of houses. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) in the narrow lanes and for the blue domes.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (northern tip of Oia, on the ruined walls and the lookout terrace; often called Oia Castle). Oia's whitewashed cliff-top houses, the two windmills and the caldera, with the sun setting over the sea behind the village. From the castle the photo shows the whitewashed cliff-top houses, the two windmills and the caldera, with the sun setting over the sea behind the village. As an alternative, the viewpoint over the 3 Dome Church puts the three blue domes in the foreground, with the sea and caldera behind them.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the horizon on the upper or lower line, not the middle. Keep the windmills off-centre so the houses lead the eye along the cliff. Keep your phone level so the white walls do not lean. If the wall is crowded, use the telephoto lens (2x-5x) to frame between people, or try the blue-dome viewpoint instead.

Settings tip: Tap on the sky or the brightest houses to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the whitewash keeps its detail and the sunset colours stay rich. After the sun drops, night mode can help, but rest your elbows on the wall to keep the phone steady.

Best time: Arrive 1.5 to 2 hours before sunset, because the wall fills up in summer. Sunset times and crowds change with the season, so check locally.

2Fira

Fira is the modern capital of Santorini, a town of whitewashed houses built along the very edge of the caldera on the island's western side. The caldera is a huge flooded volcanic crater, and Fira's name comes from a local way of saying "Thira", the island's ancient name. The town photographs well because it clings to the cliff in layers, with the water far below and the dark volcanic island of Nea Kameni out in the bay. On a clear day the view takes in the whole caldera, about 18 km from end to end, plus Thirassia. Cruise ships often anchor in the water between Nea Kameni and the town, which gives a useful sense of scale.

The best frame of the town itself is from just outside it. Walk out on the caldera path toward Firostefani and look back, so the stacked buildings fill the picture with the caldera behind. Inside Fira the streets are busy and the buildings crowd in too closely to show the whole town. If you also want a church shot, the Three Bells of Fira are further along the path in Firostefani. Remember that frame is of the church, not the town. Keep your phone steady against the wall or rail beside the path, and watch your footing near the edge.

Where it is: Fira is on the western edge of Santorini, on the rim of the caldera. The viewpoint is on the path just north-west of the town, on the way toward Firostefani.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole stacked town with the caldera behind it. Then try the default lens (1x) for a calmer, less stretched view. If your phone has a telephoto (2x-5x), use it to pick out the rooftops and church domes on the cliff.

Where to stand: Stand at Volkan on the Rocks (on the caldera path leaving Fira toward Firostefani, just before the restaurant, looking back at Fira). Fira stacked on its cliff, seen from the rim path just outside the town, with the caldera behind it. Turn back toward Fira, which lies to the south-east. From here you see the town stacked on its cliff with the caldera behind. You do not need to go into the restaurant.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the line of the cliff edge on a lower third line, with the sea and sky above. Include a bit of the path or wall in the foreground to give depth. If you want a church picture instead, the Three Bells of Fira are further along in Firostefani. There the blue dome and three-arched bell tower sit in front of the caldera and sea.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the sky or the white walls, then lower the exposure slider a little so the white buildings keep their detail. Keep the phone level using the gridlines so the horizon stays straight.

Best time: The rim path can be crowded at sunset, so go earlier in the evening or in the morning for more room to stand and frame the shot.

3Perissa

Perissa is a village on the south-east coast of Santorini, and its beach of black volcanic sand is the reason most visitors come. The dark shoreline, the blue Aegean and the rocky bulk of Mesa Vouno rising straight up behind the beach make a simple, strong picture, and the colours stand out clearly on a phone screen. Perissa is about 13 km south-east of Fira. At the 2021 census it had 725 permanent residents, and it belongs to the community of Emporio. The name is tied to an old story: the remains of an Early Christian church of Saint Irene lie here, and a shortened form of her name gave the island its present name, Santorini. The best photo spot is the north end of the beach, near the small whitewashed Church of the Holy Cross. A boulder there helps you frame the dark sand and the sea, with Mesa Vouno behind. If you have time for a second shot, the blue-domed church and its bell tower look good with the village and beach around them. Go early in the morning for the calmest scene, and check the sea and sky before you choose how much of each to include.

Where it is: Perissa is on the south-east coast of Santorini, about 13 km south-east of Fira, at the foot of Mesa Vouno. The photo spot is at the north end of its beach, close to the Church of the Holy Cross.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the sand, sea and mountain together. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the whole sweep of shoreline and Mesa Vouno in one frame. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the church and its bell tower.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. From here you see the black volcanic sand meeting the blue sea, the whitewashed church close by, and Mesa Vouno rising behind the beach.

Framing: Use the boulder at the north end to anchor the foreground, with the dark sand and the sea beyond it. Turn on gridlines and keep the horizon level, with Mesa Vouno filling the background. For a different view, try the Church of the Holy Cross with its blue dome and bell tower, framed against the village and the beach.

Settings tip: Tap on the sand to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the black sand keeps its detail and the sky and sea do not wash out. Try a Live Photo if waves are rolling in.

Best time: Early morning, from just before sunrise until about 10 AM, when the light is gentler and there are fewer people on the beach.
