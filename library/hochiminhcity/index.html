The 3 free photo spots

1Bến Thành Market

Bến Thành Market is one of the best-known landmarks in central Saigon, and it is easy to see why people photograph it. The entrance block is topped by a three-faced clock tower, with a long, low market hall behind it. It was built in a French Indochinese style, and the roof lines were designed to give shade and natural airflow. The market moved into this building in 1912. It covers about 13,056 square metres and has nearly 1,500 booths, so there is plenty of colour and movement around it.

The classic picture is taken from Quảng trường Quách Thị Trang, the open square opposite the South Gate. From there you look straight at the clock tower, with the main facade spreading out behind it. Guides call this the standard postcard angle, and it gives the clearest view of the tower.

For a busier, more everyday scene, walk round to Phan Bội Châu on the east side. Stalls and street life fill the frame there, and it feels more candid than the formal frontage. Go early if you can, because the facade looks cleaner and there are fewer people crossing your view. The exterior has had a refresh, so it may look different from older travel photos. Traffic is heavy around the square, so take care when stepping back to fit the whole building in.

Where it is: Bến Thành Market stands in Bến Thành Ward, District 1, in the centre of Ho Chi Minh City, at a crossing of four busy streets. The classic viewing area is Quảng trường Quách Thị Trang, the square facing the market's South Gate on the Lê Lợi side.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) from the square for the clock tower with the facade behind it. If you are close or the square is crowded, switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole frontage in. Use telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the clock face and tower.

Where to stand: Stand in Quảng trường Quách Thị Trang (opposite the South Gate, facing the clock tower). From here you see the three-faced clock tower above the gate, framed by the open square, with the market's main facade behind it. For a second, busier view, try Phan Bội Châu on the east side, where stalls and street life fill the scene.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the clock tower up with the centre column so the facade stays straight. Keep the tower centred and leave some square in the foreground for scale. Wait for a gap in the pedestrians, or let a few people walk through to add life. On Phan Bội Châu, shoot along the row of stalls rather than straight on.

Settings tip: Tap on the clock tower to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky does not wash out the facade. Hold the phone steady with both hands and keep it level so the vertical lines stay straight. Use night mode in the evening for the lit stalls, and ask vendors before taking close-up photos of them or their goods.

Best time: Early morning, before about 8 AM, when the facade looks cleaner and fewer people are crossing the square. In the evening, the street stalls on Phan Bội Châu and Phan Châu Trinh are lit up and make a lively night scene.

2Landmark 81

Landmark 81 is the tallest building in Vietnam, rising 461.2 metres over 81 storeys on the western bank of the Saigon River. Its outline is partly inspired by a bundle of bamboo sticks, a symbol of strength in Vietnamese culture. That slim, stepped shape is why it photographs so well: it makes a clean vertical line against the sky, with the river and open green space around its foot. The tower was finished in April 2018 and stands at the centre of the Vinhomes Central Park development. Its lower floors hold a shopping mall, and an observation deck sits near the top.

The best free view for a full-height shot is the riverside central lawn in Vinhomes Central Park, where the tower rises above the grass, water features and walking paths. Get low and tilt the phone up to make the tower look even taller. Some visitors go up to the upper level of the riverside observation houses in the park for a better frame. The pedestrian bridge inside the park is a second option. If you have time, two other views are worth a look. Bach Dang Wharf Park shows the tower across the river with Thu Thiem 2 Bridge in the same frame. Ong Cay Bridge gives a lit-up foreground at dusk. Early morning is the quietest time in the park, and the light is soft.

Where it is: Landmark 81 is in Thạnh Mỹ Tây ward, on the western bank of the Saigon River, just south of Saigon Bridge. It stands at the heart of the Vinhomes Central Park development, to the north-east of the historic centre.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole tower in from the lawn. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, less stretched look. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the crown and upper floors.

Where to stand: Stand in Vinhomes Central Park (riverside central lawn facing the tower; the pedestrian bridge inside the park is a second option). The tower rising above open lawns, lake or water features and walking paths, with the Saigon River nearby. The riverside central lawn in Vinhomes Central Park is the recommended viewing area, where the tower rises above open lawns, water features and walking paths, with the Saigon River nearby. For a different angle, try the pedestrian bridge inside the park.

Framing: Crouch and hold the phone low so the tower stretches upwards, with lawn or water in the foreground. Turn on gridlines and line up the tower with a vertical line so it does not lean. Tilt the phone up as little as you can. If you go to Ong Cay Bridge at dusk, use the lit bridge as a foreground. At Bach Dang Wharf Park, include Thu Thiem 2 Bridge across the river.

Settings tip: Tap the tower to focus, then slide the exposure down slightly so the sky stays blue and the top of the tower keeps its detail. At dusk, use night mode and rest your elbows on a railing or your phone against something solid to keep the shot sharp.

Best time: Early morning, when the park is quiet and the light is soft. Dusk works well at Ong Cay Bridge, where the bridge and nearby towers glow.

3War Remnants Museum

The War Remnants Museum is one of the most visited museums in Vietnam, and the open-air yard is where most visitors take their photos. Old military hardware stands inside a walled courtyard, with the museum buildings behind. The collection includes a Huey helicopter, an F-5A fighter, an M48 Patton tank and several other aircraft. The aircraft and tank are large, solid shapes that read clearly even on a small phone screen, and the building behind them gives each shot a sense of place.

The building was once the US military's intelligence headquarters. An earlier version of the museum opened in September 1975 under a different name, and it has been called the War Remnants Museum since 1995. The museum counts about half a million visitors a year, roughly two-thirds of them from abroad, so it can get busy.

For the best photos, start in the front courtyard. Equipment is largely not fenced off, so you can walk right up to it. Use the ultra-wide lens for the whole scene, then switch to the default lens (1x) or a little zoom for details such as the rotor, the cockpit or the tank tracks. This is a serious place, so keep your photography respectful. Indoors, flash is not allowed, and the Agent Orange section on the third floor does not allow photos. Skip the most graphic displays out of respect.

Where it is: The museum is at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street in Xuân Hòa ward, Ho Chi Minh City. The courtyard with the aircraft is the first outdoor area you reach.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit an aircraft and the museum building in one frame. Use the default lens (1x) for a more natural look. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for close details such as the helicopter, the tank and the markings.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. From here you see the captured US aircraft, including a helicopter, a fighter jet and a tank, with the museum building behind them.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place an aircraft on one side of the frame, with the building filling the background. Move in close with the ultra-wide for a dramatic angle. Step back and use 2x for a calmer, tidier picture. Keep your shots respectful and avoid the most graphic displays indoors.

Settings tip: Tap on the aircraft to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the sky looks washed out. Switch your flash off, because it is not allowed inside the galleries.

Best time: Early morning, before the tour groups arrive, when the light on the aircraft is better. Check the official site for current opening times.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Ho Chi Minh City?

The first three on this map are Ben Thanh Market, Landmark 81 and War Remnants Museum. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 16.

Where should I stand to photograph Ben Thanh Market?

Stand in Quảng trường Quách Thị Trang (opposite the South Gate, facing the clock tower). Use the default lens (1x) from the square for the clock tower with the facade behind it.

When is the best time to photograph Landmark 81?

Early morning, when the park is quiet and the light is soft. Dusk works well at Ong Cay Bridge, where the bridge and nearby towers glow.

How do I get to the War Remnants Museum?

No Underground, tram or railway stop is listed for this spot, so go by taxi or hired scooter and ask for 28 Võ Văn Tần Street, Xuân Hòa.
