This map shows 20 of the most-searched sights in Amsterdam as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Anne Frank House, Rijksmuseum, Vondelpark, Dam Square and NEMO lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. Directions and Navigate buttons take you to the standing point on foot or by public transport. 3 spots are free to view; the full map is for members.

The 3 free photo spots

1Anne Frank House and the Prinsengracht

The Anne Frank House is the canal house on the Prinsengracht where Anne Frank and seven others hid from Nazi persecution during the Second World War. Today it is a museum. From the street it looks like a tall, narrow canal house with a plain front, which is part of why it photographs so well. It fits the row of neighbouring houses, the water and the bridges along the canal, so a single frame can show both the building and the street it belongs to.

The house was built in 1635 by Dirk van Delft, and the canal-side front dates from a renovation in 1740. The hiding place, the Secret Annex, is in the rear building and cannot be seen from the street. The museum opened on 3 May 1960 and now takes in the neighbouring houses at numbers 263 to 267. In 2017 it had about 1.27 million visitors, so the street outside may be busy at times.

The phone icon on the map is about 25 m south-west of the subject. From there you can include the canal edge, the neighbouring gables and some sky without pointing the phone steeply upwards. Keep the phone level and use the gridlines so the vertical lines of the building stay straight. If people are in the foreground, wait a moment for a gap, or include them on purpose to show the scale of the place. The Westerkerk is close by if you want a second frame. Please be quiet and respectful around the entrance, and check the official site before you go for entry rules and any photography rules inside.

Where it is: On the Prinsengracht at number 263 to 267, close to the Westerkerk, in central Amsterdam (about 52.37515 N, 4.88409 E).

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the house and a bit of the canal. If the pavement is narrow and you cannot step back, try the ultra-wide (0.5x), but keep the phone level so the house does not lean. Use the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for details such as the windows, shutters and the doorway area.

Where to stand: Stand about 25 m south-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and line up the edges of the house with the vertical lines. Put the canal edge or the water along the bottom third and leave a little sky at the top. For a second shot, step a little closer and use 2x to fill the frame with the front of the house.

Settings tip: Tap the front of the house to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the sky is bright, so the brickwork stays rich and the sky is not washed out. Live Photos can help you pick a frame with fewer passers-by.

Best time: Early in the day usually means fewer people on the pavement. Soft, even light on an overcast day suits the dark front of the house, and evening gives a quieter mood by the water, where night mode can help.

2Rijksmuseum and Museumplein

The Rijksmuseum is the national museum of the Netherlands, and its main building was designed by Pierre Cuypers. The architect mixed gothic and renaissance ideas, so the red-brick front has pointed roofs, towers, decorative bands and lots of detail. It opened in 1885. After a ten-year renovation, it reopened in April 2013. The museum shows around 8,000 objects, including works by Rembrandt, Frans Hals and Vermeer. It sits on Museumplein, beside the Van Gogh Museum, the Stedelijk Museum and the Concertgebouw, so the open square gives you room to step back and fit the whole building in.

The phone icon on the map is about 140 m west of the subject. From there you can take in the full width of the façade and its towers, with the surrounding space in the frame. The building is big, so a wide shot works best, then move closer and pick out details such as the roofline, the windows and the brickwork. Walking around the square also gives you different foregrounds, such as people, trees and the open paving. Keep your phone level so the tall vertical lines don't lean inwards.

Where it is: The Rijksmuseum stands on Museumplein in the Amsterdam South borough. It is close to the Van Gogh Museum, the Stedelijk Museum and the Royal Concertgebouw.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole façade and the open space around it. Then switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural view, and use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for towers, rooflines and decorative details.

Where to stand: Stand about 140 m west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. From there you can see the full front of the museum without turning.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the building up so the verticals stay straight. Put the museum in the centre or slightly to one side, and leave some empty space in front of it so it doesn't feel crowded. For variety, try a closer shot with the 2x telephoto, focusing on the towers and rooflines.

Settings tip: Tap on the brickwork to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the sky looks too bright. If it is cloudy, keep the sky small in the frame so it doesn't look washed out. Use panorama only if you can keep your phone steady, as it can distort straight lines.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people. At dusk, night mode can capture the building with its lights on, but hold the phone very still.

19Westerkerk

The Westerkerk is a big Protestant church on the western edge of the canal ring, just beside the Jordaan, between the Prinsengracht and the Keizersgracht. It was built between 1620 and 1631 to designs by Hendrick de Keyser and finished by his son Pieter. At about 58 metres long and 29 metres wide, it is still the largest church in the Netherlands built for Protestants. The real draw for a photo is the Westertoren, the tower, which at 87 metres is the tallest church tower in Amsterdam. It ends in a spire topped with the Imperial Crown of Austria of Maximilian I, a shape that is easy to recognise even in a small phone frame.

Because the tower is so tall, the main challenge is fitting it in without it looking squashed. Include a little of the street or canal edge in the foreground to give a sense of scale, and keep the phone upright and level so the walls do not lean inwards too much. Once you have the full view, switch to a zoom lens and take a tighter shot of the crown and upper tower. The brickwork and stone details look best in soft, even light. The church is still a working place of worship, so be considerate of anyone going in or out while you take your pictures.

Where it is: The Westerkerk is in central Amsterdam, in the westernmost part of the Grachtengordel (canal ring) in the Centrum district. It sits between the Prinsengracht and the Keizersgracht, right next to the Jordaan. Its position on the map is the middle of the church's outline, which is about 76 m across.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to get the whole church and tower into one frame from close range. Then use the default lens (1x) for a calmer, less stretched view, and the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the crown and upper tower.

Where to stand: Stand about 50 m south-east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-west.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the tower up with a vertical line. Keep the phone as upright as you can, because tilting it upwards makes the tower lean. Leave a little foreground at the bottom for scale, then take a second, tighter shot of the spire with the telephoto.

Settings tip: Tap on the tower to set focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the sky stays blue and the brick keeps its detail. If you take a Live Photo, it can catch passing cyclists or boats.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light on the brick and stone, and tends to be calmer around the church.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Amsterdam?

The first three on this map are Anne Frank House, Rijksmuseum and Vondelpark. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph the Anne Frank House?

Stand about 25 m south-west of the Anne Frank House, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east. Start with the default lens (1x) for the house and a bit of the canal.

When is the best time to photograph the Rijksmuseum?

Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people. At dusk, night mode can capture the building with its lights on, but hold the phone very still.

How do I get to the Westerkerk?

The easiest approach is by tram: from the Marnixplein tram stop (tram 13 or tram 5) the Westerkerk lies to the south-east, about 8 minutes on foot.
