The 3 free photo spots

1Ljubljana Castle

Ljubljana Castle sits on Castle Hill above the Old Town and is one of the city's key landmarks. It is also the easiest shot to get wrong: from close up, the hill and walls are too big to fit in a phone frame. The best answer is to step back and use the town as part of the picture. From the Gallusovo nabrežje side of the river, the row of riverside Old Town facades fills the foreground and the castle rises on its hill behind them. That gives you depth and a clear sense of place in one frame.

The castle has a long history. It was probably built in the 11th century as a medieval fortress and rebuilt in the 12th. It took on its present outline in a near-complete overhaul in the 15th century, and most of the buildings that stand today date from the 16th and 17th centuries. In the 19th century it was used as a prison, and today it is a major cultural venue. A funicular has run up the hill since 2006, so you can also photograph the town from the top.

For the classic frame, stand across the river so the castle sits above the rooftops. Keep the facades along the bottom edge and let the hill fill the upper part. If you want a wider skyline, Tivoli Park is a good alternative. Take a moment to check for people and bicycles on the riverside before you shoot.

Where it is: The castle stands on Castle Hill above the Old Town, the historic centre of Ljubljana. The best photo spot is on Gallusovo nabrežje, on the opposite bank of the Ljubljanica from the castle side, looking across the river at the riverside Old Town facades.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to get the riverside facades and the castle hill in one frame. Switch to 2x or 3x if your phone has telephoto, to bring the castle closer and keep the hill from looking small. Use ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want a lot of foreground, because it shrinks the castle.

Where to stand: Stand on Gallusovo nabrežje (opposite bank of the Ljubljanica from the castle side). The row of riverside Old Town facades in the foreground, with the castle rising on the hill behind them. From there you look east across the water. The Old Town facades fill the foreground and the castle rises on its hill behind them.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines. Put the facades on the lower third and the castle on the upper third, with the hill fully visible. Keep the phone level so the buildings do not lean. If you want a wider skyline with the castle on top of its hill, Tivoli Park is an alternative viewpoint.

Settings tip: Tap the castle to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky does not wash out and the walls keep their detail. Take a couple of shots in case someone walks through the frame.

Best time: This is a general lighting suggestion: because you are looking east, afternoon light may be behind you and fall on the facades and the castle, while morning light may come from behind the castle and make it look darker. Check the light on the day.

2Tivoli Park

Tivoli is the largest park in Ljubljana, and the best way to photograph it is along its central walk, Jakopičevo sprehajališče. This wide, tree-lined promenade runs straight towards the cream-coloured Tivoli Mansion, with Plečnik's lampposts on either side leading the eye along it. That makes an easy, tidy composition for a phone: a strong central line, trees framing both sides, and a clear destination at the far end.

The park was laid out in 1813 to plans by the engineer Jean Blanchard, and the architect Jože Plečnik designed the promenade when he renovated the park in the 1920s. The mansion dates from the 17th century and was given its neoclassical look in the middle of the 19th century. Photo panels are often displayed along the path, which adds interest, though they can block your view, so look at what is on show before choosing your angle.

For a calmer second picture, walk to the fish pond and the old boathouse café, Čolnarna. Trees ring the water, and it makes a quieter scene than the long avenue. If you want an overview of the park's treetops and paths with the city beyond, Mostec gives a high view, but it involves some uphill walking, so confirm the climb before going.

Allow time to wander.

Where it is: Tivoli Park lies on the western side of Ljubljana's Center District, with the Šiška, Vič and Rožnik districts around it. The promenade runs from the edge of the park towards Tivoli Mansion.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the straight view down the promenade. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the tree canopy, or to the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the mansion closer and make the lampposts look more tightly packed.

Where to stand: Stand in Jakopičevo sprehajališče (entrance end, looking east toward Tivoli Mansion). The wide, tree-lined central promenade with Plečnik's lampposts running straight toward the neoclassical Tivoli Mansion, often with photo panels along the path.

Framing: Centre yourself on the middle of the path and turn on gridlines so the lampposts and trees stay level and balanced on both sides. Keep the mansion near the vertical centre line. If photo panels are standing along the walk, include one as foreground or step slightly to one side to avoid it. For a different view, try the pond by Čolnarna for reflections and trees, or Mostec for a high view over the canopy.

Settings tip: Tap on the mansion to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the sky looks washed out. Hold the phone at chest height with both hands for a steadier shot. Take a Live Photo if people are walking along the path, then pick the frame where the path is clearest.

Best time: At the pond, guides recommend sunrise as a good time.

7Ljubljana Castle funicular

The Ljubljana Castle funicular is a short inclined lift that carries visitors from the Old Town up to the castle on the hill above. It was designed as part of the castle's renovation and opened on 28 December 2006. The idea is much older: in 1897 the then mayor, Ivan Hribar, wrote to the Austro-Hungarian authorities to ask for a lift up to the castle. The ride takes about a minute and is popular with tourists. As a photo subject it works best from the outside, because the lower station and the track climbing the wooded hillside make a clean, simple shape. Krekov trg, the square where the ride starts, is the only place where the funicular itself is the subject, and it is near the Central Market. You can photograph the line from the ground before or after a trip. Include a little of the square in the foreground to show where you are, and let the track lead the eye up the hill. Don't plan to shoot from inside the cabin, as the windows are dirty. If you want the castle hill with the whole Old Town beneath it, Čevljarski most (Cobbler's Bridge) gives a wider view across the Ljubljanica, though the funicular is only a small detail there, if you can see it at all.

Where it is: In the centre of Ljubljana, in the Old Town. The lower station is on Krekov trg, close to the Central Market, at the foot of the castle hill.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the station and the track together. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the square around the station. Use 2x or more to pick out the line climbing the hill.

Where to stand: Stand in Krekov trg (east side, by the lower funicular station (Krekov trg 4)). The funicular's lower station and the track climbing the wooded hill toward the castle, with the Old Town square in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line up the track against one of them so it doesn't look tilted. Keep a strip of the square at the bottom of the frame and let the track lead up to the castle hill. For a different view, try Čevljarski most, where the funicular is only a small detail.

Settings tip: Tap the station to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the bright sky doesn't wash out the hillside. Take a few frames, as people often walk through the square.

Best time: Daylight is best, when the wooded hillside and the station are clearly lit. Early in the day the square is likely to be quieter.
