The 3 free photo spots

1Carlisle Bay

Carlisle Bay is a small natural harbour on the southwest coast of Barbados, with the capital, Bridgetown, sitting on its shore. It has been turned into a marine park and is popular with scuba divers. Anchors and cannonballs from old ships still lie on the seabed. The bay is named after James Hay, 1st Earl of Carlisle, who claimed the island in 1627 on behalf of King Charles I. For photographers, the appeal is simple: a long shoreline, calm turquoise water and boats dotted towards the horizon. Pebbles Beach, on the edge of the bay, is the recommended viewpoint for taking it all in. Walk along the water's edge and the shoreline gives you natural leading lines, and the calm sea makes tidy reflections and easy portraits of your travelling companions. You will be shooting from the beach rather than from above, so aim for a low, wide view with a strip of sand in the foreground, then pick out a boat or two on the horizon. If you want somewhere quieter, Brownes Beach is a quieter stretch of white sand and clear water along the same bay. Start early if you can, when the light is fresh and there are fewer people about.

Where it is: Carlisle Bay is the sheltered bay on which Bridgetown stands, on the southwest coast of Barbados.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the sweep of shoreline, then switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural view. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pull boats on the horizon closer.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (edge of the bay, on the shoreline). The bay's shoreline with calm turquoise water and boats on the horizon, best framed toward sunset. The recommended viewpoint. From here you can look out over the calm turquoise water, with boats on the horizon, and the bay opens out to the west.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the horizon on the top or bottom third line. Keep a strip of sand or the line of the shoreline in the foreground to lead the eye into the picture. If you want a quieter alternative, try Brownes Beach along the same bay for white sand and clear water with fewer people.

Settings tip: Tap the sea or a boat to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky keeps its colour and the water stays rich. For portraits on the sand, tap on the face before you shoot. Try a Live Photo if the water is rippling gently.

Best time: Early morning for fresh light and fewer people. Towards sunset the bay also looks good when framed to the west.

2Kensington Oval

Kensington Oval is Barbados's main cricket ground, in the western part of Bridgetown. Cricket has been played here since 1882, when the Pickwick Cricket Club took over the ground. The first Test match was played in January 1930, between the West Indies and England. The stands were largely rebuilt for the 2007 Cricket World Cup, and the final between Australia and Sri Lanka was played here that April. Several of the stands still carry older names, including the Three Ws stand.

It photographs well because the rebuilt stands make strong, curved shapes. Against a Caribbean sky they give you clean lines and plenty of colour. The ground is wide, about 360 m across, so think about what you want to include. For a full view of the stands, use the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) and keep the horizon level with the gridlines switched on. If you would rather show the atmosphere, use the default lens (1x) and look for a frame with seating, floodlights and sky. Use the telephoto lens (2x-5x if your phone has it) to pick out signs, stand names and rooflines.

On match days the area around the ground can be busy, so give yourself time. Whether you can get inside depends on the event, so check the official site before you go. If it is open, a quick look at the field and the big screen is worth a few frames too.

Where it is: Kensington Oval is in the western part of Bridgetown, Barbados. It sits in the area around Fontabelle Road, Prescod Boulevard and Harbour Road, near Holborn Circle.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the sweep of the stands into one frame, then switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural view. If your phone has a telephoto lens (2x-5x), use it for stand names and roof details.

Where to stand: Stand about 130 m north-west of the numbered pin, at the phone icon on the map, and face south-east.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and keep the horizon on a horizontal line so the stands do not lean. Put the stands in the lower two-thirds of the frame and leave the sky above. If people are walking past, wait for a gap or include them to show scale.

Settings tip: Tap on the stands to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the sky is bright, so the colours stay rich. Try a Live Photo to catch movement if there are people around.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer harsh shadows than midday. On match or event days it is busier, so go on a quiet day if you want clear shots.

3Garrison Savannah

Garrison Savannah is the broad green oval at the heart of the Garrison Historic Area, just outside Bridgetown. It is best known as a horse racing venue: a clockwise grass course, with the six-furlong track running around the edge of the green. The annual Barbados Gold Cup is held here, and so is the Barbados Triple Crown series for Thoroughbreds. Racing has gone on at this spot since a British garrison was stationed here, and in 2012 a network of tunnels was found under the area.

For a phone photo, the savannah works best as a wide open stage with something solid in front of it. From the front of the building you can frame the Georgian guardhouse, its domed clock tower and its arched colonnade against the open parade ground, with the savannah stretching away to the east. Guides call this the most photographed building in the Garrison, so it is a good first shot.

On race days the track itself is ticketed, so check the official site before you go. If you would like a second view, walk out onto the grass oval and turn round: from there you can look back towards Bridgetown's church spires. Keep the horizon level and leave some sky above the clock tower, because the dome is the part people notice first.

Where it is: Garrison Savannah lies in the Garrison Historic Area, just outside the centre of Bridgetown. The Main Guard House & Clock Tower stands on its edge, facing the grass.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) for the guardhouse and clock tower, so the building keeps natural proportions. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the colonnade, the tower and the open grass in one frame.

Where to stand: Stand at Main Guard House & Clock Tower (front (main facade) of the building, facing the savannah). The Georgian guardhouse with its domed clock tower and arched colonnade, framed against the open parade ground. On the Main Guard House & Clock Tower side of the savannah, at the front (main facade) of the building, facing the grass. The photo shows the Georgian guardhouse with its domed clock tower and arched colonnade, framed against the open parade ground. The savannah itself is to the east.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the clock tower on one of the vertical lines, with the open grass filling the other side. Hold the phone level so the columns stay straight. For an alternative, walk onto the grass oval and look back towards the church spires of Bridgetown.

Settings tip: Tap the clock tower to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky stays blue and the pale stonework keeps its detail. If a Thursday ceremony is under way, a short Live Photo can capture the movement.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer harsh shadows under the arches. Check the official site for race days, and expect the front view to be busier if a ceremony is taking place.
