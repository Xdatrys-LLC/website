This map shows 20 of the most-searched sights in Dresden as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Altmarkt, Frauenkirche, Zwinger, Dresden Castle and Semperoper lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. The 3 free photo spots

1Altmarkt

Altmarkt is the old market square in the heart of Dresden's Altstadt, and it is a handy first stop for a phone photographer. The open space gives you room to step back and fit a lot into the frame. That is rare in a historic centre, where streets are often too narrow for a wide view. The buildings around the square make a natural frame, and people crossing it give a sense of scale and everyday life.

The phone icon on the map is about 65 m west of the subject. From there you can take in the whole width of the square, with its edges leading the eye into the picture. Walk around a little before you settle. Small changes in position, or crouching a bit lower, change how much sky and paving you include.

Enjoy this square as a place to take in the old town's atmosphere rather than a single landmark. Take a general view first, then step closer for details such as building fronts and street scenes. Keep your phone lens clean, as a smudge shows up badly in bright, open scenes. If the square is busy or has temporary structures, the view may look different, so be ready to adjust your position.

Where it is: Altmarkt is in the Innere Altstadt, the old town centre of Dresden, in the Altstadt district.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole square in, then switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural look. The telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) is useful for picking out details on the far side.

Where to stand: Stand about 65 m west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. The square then opens out in front of you.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and use them to keep the horizon and building edges straight. Put the square's edge or a line of paving in the lower third to lead the eye across. Wait for people to walk through the frame to add scale. If you can, hold the phone at chest height rather than eye level for a steadier, cleaner view.

Settings tip: Tap on the middle of the scene to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright so it keeps its colour. Try a Live Photo to catch people moving, and use panorama only if you want the whole width of the square.

Best time: Early morning usually has fewer people, if you want an emptier square. Evening light can be pleasant, and night mode helps once it gets dark.

2Frauenkirche

The Frauenkirche (Church of Our Lady) is a Lutheran church in the middle of Dresden, and its bell-shaped dome gave the city a distinctive silhouette. The current building is the third church on this site. The Baroque church was built between 1726 and 1743 to a design by the city architect George Bähr. It was destroyed in the bombing of February 1945, and its ruins stood for nearly half a century as a war memorial. Rebuilding began in 1994, the exterior was finished in 2004 and the church was reconsecrated on 30 October 2005. The original dome, called the 'Stone Bell', was about 67 metres high and rested on eight slender supports. The surrounding Neumarkt square was rebuilt in 2004 with its Baroque-style buildings.

It photographs well because the curved dome and the pale, weathered stonework stand out against the surrounding buildings and the sky. From the phone icon on the map you are close enough to fill the frame with the church but still far enough back to see the dome and the lower walls together. Stand still for a moment before you shoot. Square-on shots work well, and so does a slight angle that shows the curves of the walls. People walking past the front add a sense of scale, so you don't need to wait for an empty frame.

Where it is: The Frauenkirche stands in Dresden's old town, in the middle of the rebuilt Neumarkt square, with the Baroque-style buildings of the square around it.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to get the church and its dome together. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the square and the buildings around it. If your phone has telephoto (2x-5x), use it for the dome and the stonework, but keep the phone still.

Where to stand: Stand about 90 m south-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the centre of the dome with a vertical line so the church stands straight. Leave a little sky above the dome, and include some of the square in front for scale. Try one shot at 1x and one at 0.5x with the phone held level, so the walls don't lean.

Settings tip: Tap the church to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch if the sky is bright, so the stonework keeps its detail. Hold the phone steady with both hands and take a few frames in a row.

Best time: Soft morning or late-afternoon light brings out the colour of the stone, and early in the day the square is usually quieter. After dark, night mode can work if you rest your elbows on something steady.

16Neumarkt

Neumarkt is the square around the Frauenkirche in the heart of Dresden's old town. It began as a small settlement on a slight rise above the Elbe, and when the city walls were extended in 1530 it became the 'New Market', the second market square alongside the Altmarkt. Under August II the Strong the square was lined with baroque buildings, and today's houses are mostly reconstructions of them. The area was almost completely destroyed in the firestorm of February 1945. The Frauenkirche was finished again in 2005, and the streets around it have since been rebuilt in stages, mostly to the original structure or with facades close to the old ones.

That history is why it photographs so well. The dome rises above rows of tall burgher-style houses, which close in around it like a frame. The classic picture is taken from the middle of the square, facing east, with the church filling the frame and the houses on either side. Go early in the morning if you can, when the square is empty and you can shoot without people crossing the view. There has been construction work near the square, so look for scaffolding before you set up. If you want a second angle, the Brühlsche Terrasse gives a view down the Münzgasse with the dome at the end of the lane, and Rampische Straße shows the church at the end of the street between old facades.

Where it is: Neumarkt lies in the old town (Altstadt) of central Dresden, on the square that surrounds the Frauenkirche. It is the old town's second market square, alongside the Altmarkt.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x), which shows the dome and the houses on both sides in a natural-looking frame. If you want the whole dome and more of the rooftops, switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x). If you want to pick out the dome or a single facade, use the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone).

Where to stand: Stand about 50 m south-east of the subject, in Neumarkt (the phone icon on the map), and face north-west. From there the dome sits in the centre, with the baroque burgher houses framing the square on either side. Neumarkt itself is a little to the north-west of where you are standing.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the dome on the centre line, with the houses balanced left and right. Keep your phone level so the facades do not lean. For a different angle, try the view down the Münzgasse from the Brühlsche Terrasse, where the dome appears at the end of the lane.

Settings tip: Tap on the stonework of the church to set focus and exposure, then lower the exposure slider a little if the sky is washing out. Take a few frames at 1x and 0.5x so you can pick the best one later.

Best time: Early morning, when the square is empty.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Dresden?

The first three on this map are Altmarkt, Frauenkirche and Zwinger. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph Altmarkt?

Stand about 65 m west of Altmarkt, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole square in, then switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural look.

When is the best time to photograph the Frauenkirche?

Soft morning or late-afternoon light brings out the colour of the stone, and early in the day the square is usually quieter. After dark, night mode can work if you rest your elbows on something steady.

How do I get to Neumarkt?

No tram or railway stops are listed for this spot, so head for the Frauenkirche in the old town, by taxi or hired scooter if you are coming from further out.
