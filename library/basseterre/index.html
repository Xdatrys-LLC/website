The 3 free photo spots

1Port Zante

Port Zante is the cruise port at the heart of Basseterre, the capital of Saint Kitts and Nevis. It sits in the middle of Basseterre Bay on about 15 acres of land reclaimed from the sea in 1995, so it feels like a small waterfront quarter pushed out into the water. Its photo appeal is simple: big ships tied up at the piers, blue Caribbean water, and the island of Nevis as a backdrop. The green hills that wrap around the town add colour behind you when you turn round.

The best view is from the promenade along the berthed piers, looking out over the ships. The port's own guide also points to the entrance arch and the green-roofed plaza buildings, which make good close-up and street-scene shots. Two large ships can berth at once, and the port gets busy on arrival days, so try to shoot between arrivals if you can.

For a wider picture, the waterfront near the Treasury Building gives a view across the harbour that takes in the cruise berths and the town together. It is a good second shot if you have time.

Basseterre itself was founded by the French in 1627, which makes it one of the older towns in the Eastern Caribbean. Give yourself a few minutes to walk the length of the promenade, as the angle on the ships changes as you go.

Where it is: Port Zante is in the centre of Basseterre Bay, on the waterfront of Basseterre, the capital of Saint Kitts. It is the town's cruise port.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit a whole ship and the length of the pier into one frame. Use the default lens (1x) for the entrance arch and the green-roofed plaza buildings. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out Nevis on the horizon behind the ships.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Stand on Port Zante, on the promenade along the berthed cruise piers, and look out over the ships.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and put the ship's waterline along a lower grid line so the sea and sky are balanced. Keep the horizon level. Leave a little water in front of the ship so it does not look cramped against the edge of the picture. For a different view, walk to the waterfront near the Treasury Building for a wider shot across the harbour that takes in the berths and the town together.

Settings tip: Tap on the ship's hull to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright so the white paint keeps its detail. Wipe the lens first, as sea air leaves a film on it.

Best time: Shoot between ship arrivals, when the promenade is quieter and the berths are clearer. Earlier or later in the day usually gives softer light and cooler colour than the middle of the day.

2Independence Square

Independence Square is an open public square in the middle of Basseterre. It was first called Pall Mall Square, and the Government acquired it in 1750. It soon became the administrative, commercial and social centre of the town. It was renamed to mark St Kitts and Nevis becoming independent on 19 September 1983.

The square also has a painful past. It was a place where enslaved men, women and children were sold, on the spot where the fountain now stands. On the south side of the square, a building has a basement with barred windows that is still visible today. It is thought to have been used as an auction house. It is worth taking a quiet moment here before you start photographing.

On a phone, the square works best as a wide, open scene. The fountain gives you a natural centre point, and the buildings on the south side can frame the view. Stand back and let the space show, then move closer for details such as the fountain and the barred basement windows. Walk around a little, because the view changes as you move, and small changes in position can keep passers-by out of the frame. Take your time and be respectful if other visitors are reading or reflecting.

Where it is: Independence Square is in the centre of Basseterre, in Saint George Basseterre Parish, on the island of Saint Kitts. It was the administrative, commercial and social centre of Basseterre.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole square, then switch to the default lens (1x) for the fountain and the buildings on the south side. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details on the south side, such as the barred basement windows.

Where to stand: Stand about 50 m north-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face south-east.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and place the fountain on a crossing point rather than dead centre. Keep the horizon level and the building edges upright. Include a little foreground, such as paving or planting, to give a sense of depth. For a detail shot, use the telephoto to isolate the barred windows of the basement on the south side, and keep the frame simple and unposed.

Settings tip: Tap on the fountain or the buildings on the south side to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a touch if the sky is bright, so the sky keeps its colour and the buildings do not turn too dark. Use the ultra-wide sparingly: keep the phone level, as tilting it will bend the buildings at the edges.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people, but the square can be photographed at any time of day.

5Basseterre Co-Cathedral of Immaculate Conception

The Co-Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception is a Roman Catholic co-cathedral in Basseterre, and the grey stone building closes off the eastern side of Independence Square. That makes it an easy subject for a phone: a wide, open foreground with the square's fountain, and the cathedral filling the far side. Catholic worship in the town goes back to the French period, when the Jesuits built a church in the town. Catholics were later barred from worshipping in public under English rule, and the community grew again after 1829 and with settlers arriving from Madeira from 1835. A church of about 1856 was pulled down in 1927 and replaced on the same site. The present building was designed by Father Claeys and dedicated on 6 December 1928. For the classic view, start on the west side of the square and face east, with the fountain low in the frame and the cathedral behind it. Then walk over to the cathedral for a closer look at the front and the towers, where a tall traveller's palm stands beside the building. Photo guides say the interior, with its altar and stained glass, is worth a picture too. Services are held here, so go outside Mass times, keep your phone quiet and don't use flash.

Where it is: In the centre of Basseterre, on the east side of Independence Square, just south of North Independence Street.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the square, fountain and cathedral together. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the square, or if you are close to the front and need to fit the whole facade in. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the towers and details.

Where to stand: Stand in Independence Square (west side of the square, facing east toward the cathedral). The grey stone cathedral filling the far side of the square, with the square's fountain in the foreground. Facing east toward the cathedral.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the fountain on a lower third line, with the cathedral's towers in the upper part of the frame. Keep your phone level so the building doesn't lean. If you want a second angle, walk over to the cathedral for a closer shot of the facade and towers, with the tall traveller's palm beside the building.

Settings tip: Tap the cathedral to focus, then lower the exposure slider a little if the sky is bright, so the grey stonework keeps its detail. Inside, use night mode only if you can hold the phone still, and keep flash off.

Best time: Choose a time when the sun is not directly behind the cathedral. Outside, mornings and late afternoons are usually easier for light. Visit the interior outside service times.
