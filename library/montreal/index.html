This map shows 20 of the most-searched sights in Montreal as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Notre-Dame Basilica, Kondiaronk Belvedere, Habitat 67, Olympic Stadium and Botanical Garden lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. Directions and Navigate buttons take you to the standing point on foot or by public transport. 3 spots are free to view; the full map is for members.

The 3 free photo spots

1Notre-Dame Basilica

Notre-Dame Basilica is the great Gothic Revival church of Old Montreal, standing at 110 Notre-Dame Street West and facing the Place d'Armes square. It was designed by James O'Donnell, with the main construction between 1824 and 1829. The two towers followed in 1841 and 1843. When it was finished it was the largest church in North America, and it held that title for more than fifty years. It became a minor basilica in 1982 and a National Historic Site of Canada in 1989.

It photographs well because the stone facade is tall and strongly symmetrical, with a pair of towers and three statues (Saint Joseph, the Virgin Mary and John the Baptist) on the facade. The interior is just as striking, with deep blue vaults scattered with golden stars. The stained glass is unusual because it shows scenes from Montreal's own religious history rather than Bible stories.

For phone photos, start outside with the whole facade, then move in close for the statues and stonework. Keep the phone level so the towers don't lean. If you go inside, be ready for dim light and for rules on photography, which the church may set. Restoration work on the stonework began in 2020 and is planned to take over a decade, so you may find some scaffolding in your frame.

Where it is: In the heart of Old Montreal, on the corner of Notre-Dame Street West and Saint Sulpice Street. It faces the Place d'Armes square and stands next to the Saint-Sulpice Seminary.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the facade from close range and for the sweep of the interior. Use the default lens (1x) for natural-looking views of the front. Use telephoto/zoom (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the statues, the tower tops and the carved details.

Where to stand: Stand about 95 m north-west of the subject, on the far side of Place d'Armes near Rue Saint-Jacques (the phone icon on the map), and face south-east across the square. From here the whole facade and both towers fit in the frame; walk closer and tilt up with the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the towers to loom.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and line up the towers with the vertical lines so they stay upright. Centre the main doorway for a symmetrical shot. Then try a tighter frame of the three statues using the telephoto. Wait a moment for people to move out of the foreground.

Settings tip: Tap to focus on the stonework, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky doesn't wash out. Inside, the gold and blue are easy to overexpose, so lower the exposure a little. Use night mode in dim light and hold the phone steady, resting your elbows against your body or a solid surface.

Best time: Visit early in the day for fewer people in front of the facade. Whenever you go, check the official site for visiting times, since the interior may be closed to visitors at some points.

2Mount Royal: Kondiaronk Belvedere

Kondiaronk Belvedere is a viewing terrace on Mount Royal, the green hill that rises above central Montreal. It is third on our Montreal map, and the main reason is the outlook: from the terrace you look out over the city's rooftops and towers, with open sky above them. That gives a phone plenty to work with. You can take a wide scene of the whole skyline, or a tighter shot of one cluster of buildings.

The terrace is open and fairly wide, so you can walk along it and try a few positions before settling. Start with the default lens (1x) for a natural view. Then switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit in more of the city, or to include the terrace edge as a foreground if there is one. Turn on gridlines and line the horizon up with a grid line so the city does not tilt. Use the telephoto/zoom (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a single tower or a distant detail.

People often stop here for a while, so you may have to wait for a clear spot at the front. Keep your phone strap round your wrist if you lean on a railing or low wall, if there is one. If the sky is much brighter than the city, tap on the buildings to focus and then slide the exposure down a little, so the sky keeps its colour.

Where it is: On Mount Royal, above the downtown part of Montreal. The nearest Métro stations, Peel and Guy-Concordia, are to the east and south-east of the viewpoint, about 1 km away.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the main view. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the full spread of the city, and the telephoto/zoom (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for individual buildings.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. The spot is right there, so you only need to move along the terrace to change the angle.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and put the horizon on the upper or lower third line. Keep it level. Use the terrace edge, or a railing or low wall if there is one, as a foreground to add depth, and zoom in for a close shot of a single building.

Settings tip: Tap on the city to set focus, then slide the exposure down slightly if the sky is washing out. For a wide sweep of the view, try panorama mode and turn slowly.

Best time: Clear daylight gives the sharpest view. Late afternoon or early evening often has softer light, and after sunset night mode can capture the city lights. Hold the phone steady with both hands, or rest it against a solid surface if there is one.

20Place d'Armes

Place d'Armes is the square at the heart of Old Montreal, and it is one of the easiest places in the city to get a classic church-and-city photo. The Notre-Dame Basilica fills one side with its facade and twin towers. The bronze Maisonneuve Monument, put up in 1895 for Paul de Chomedey, the founder of Montreal, stands in front of it as a ready-made foreground. The square dates from 1693 and is the second oldest public site in the city. The buildings around it show different periods of Montreal's growth: the Bank of Montreal head office (1859), the New York Life Building (1887, the city's first high-rise) and the Art Deco Aldred Building (1931). Because the stone, bronze and glass are all so different, the square gives you plenty of variety without leaving one spot. From the phone icon on the map, or from the north side of the square, look south and line up the monument with the basilica behind it. A wide frame that includes both tells the story best. Then turn round and try the Aldred Building rising behind the statue. Horse-drawn calèches leave from the square, and one may drift into your picture. Traffic runs close to the pavement, so watch your step when backing up for a wider shot.

Where it is: Place d'Armes is in Old Montreal, between Saint-Jacques and Notre-Dame streets, directly in front of Notre-Dame Basilica.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the monument and the basilica into one frame. Use the default lens (1x) for a tidier shot of the statue. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the basilica towers and the carved details.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. If you want the north-side view, walk a little north before looking south toward the basilica. From here you see the basilica facade and towers across the square, with the Maisonneuve Monument in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and keep the phone level so the towers stand straight. Put the statue low in the frame and let the basilica rise behind it. For a second view, walk to the Maisonneuve Monument and shoot wide, with the bronze figure in front and the Aldred Building and the basilica behind it.

Settings tip: Tap the basilica facade to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the pale stone keeps its detail against the sky. At dusk, switch on night mode and hold the phone steady against your body or a solid ledge.

Best time: Early morning or dusk, when the light rakes across the stone facades and the square is quieter.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Montreal?

The first three on this map are Notre-Dame Basilica, Kondiaronk Belvedere and Habitat 67. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph Notre-Dame Basilica?

Stand about 95 m north-west of Notre-Dame Basilica, on the far side of Place d'Armes near Rue Saint-Jacques (the phone icon on the map), and face south-east across the square. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the facade from close range and for the sweep of the interior.

When is the best time to photograph Kondiaronk Belvedere?

Clear daylight gives the sharpest view. Late afternoon or early evening often has softer light, and after sunset night mode can capture the city lights.

How do I get to Place d'Armes?

From Place-d'Armes Métro station (Métro Orange line), the square is to the south-east, about 4 minutes on foot.
