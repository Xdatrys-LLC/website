The 3 free photo spots

1Atlantis Paradise Island

Atlantis Paradise Island is a big ocean-themed resort on Paradise Island, and its towers rise above the island across Nassau's harbour. The resort covers 154 acres and has five hotels, including the Royal, Coral, Cove and Reef Towers. The Royal Towers opened on 11 December 1998, when the site was rebranded as Atlantis. The resort also has a waterpark, a large open-air marine habitat and a marina for yachts.

The best photo is the long view from the Sir Sidney Poitier Bridge. From the pedestrian sidewalk you look across the harbour, with the towers rising behind the water and the bridge's road span running through the frame. The Nassau skyline adds a sense of place. Guides often call this the classic Atlantis skyline view, and it is a free public walk.

Keep to the sidewalk, because this is the main road bridge to the island and traffic is close by. The resort itself is private, so don't plan on photographing from inside it. If you want a second angle, Marina Village at Atlantis puts docked yachts in the foreground with the towers and pastel buildings behind. It sits inside the resort complex, so access rules may change.

Where it is: Atlantis sits on Paradise Island, the island across the harbour from central Nassau.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the whole resort, the harbour and the bridge span fit in one frame. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the bridge and skyline, and try the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the towers and make them look bigger against the water.

Where to stand: Stand on Sir Sidney Poitier Bridge (pedestrian sidewalk on the Paradise Island side, looking across the harbour toward the resort). The Atlantis towers rising behind the harbour and the bridge's road span, framed by the water and the Nassau skyline. From there the resort lies to the north across the harbour, and your photo shows the Atlantis towers rising behind the water and the bridge's road span, with the Nassau skyline around them.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the towers on one of the upper grid intersections, with the water filling the lower third. Use the bridge's road span as a leading line towards the resort. If you'd like a different view, Marina Village at Atlantis gives a close-up with yachts in front of the towers, but check access first.

Settings tip: Tap the towers to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky and towers keep their detail. Hold your phone steady against the railing or your body, and stay clear of the traffic side.

Best time: Soft light in the morning or late afternoon usually suits the towers and water better than harsh midday sun. If you stay into dusk, night mode can help in low light, as long as you hold the phone very still.

2Paradise Island

Paradise Island is the long, low island that lies just off Nassau's shore, across the harbour. It was called Hog Island until 1962, when the developer Huntington Hartford renamed it. Today it is best known for the Atlantis resort. The island covers about 277 hectares, and two bridges link it to New Providence: the first was built in 1966 and the second in the late 1990s. It is a good subject because the resort towers, the harbour and the boats make a clear layered scene. The best way to photograph it is from height. Fort Fincastle stands on Bennet's Hill, the highest point on New Providence, so from its top landing you look down over Nassau Harbour, with Paradise Island and the eastern approaches spread out below. The fort is a quieter, higher alternative to the usual bridge view, though it is a paid spot. On a phone, the wide view works well. Keep the horizon level, let the harbour fill the lower half of the frame, and place the island in the upper third. Zoom in a little to pick out the resort towers from the surrounding greenery. Fort Fincastle and the Queen's Staircase were listed as temporarily closed during renovation of the nearby Water Tower, so check before you go.

Where it is: Paradise Island sits just off the northern shore of Nassau, across Nassau Harbour from the city centre. The viewpoint, Fort Fincastle, is on Bennet's Hill, the highest point on New Providence, looking north-east towards the island.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the whole harbour scene, then try 2x-5x telephoto to pick out the resort towers. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want a lot of sky and foreground.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (Top landing of the fort on Bennet's Hill, the highest point on New Providence). A broad, elevated panorama over Nassau Harbour, with Paradise Island and the eastern approaches spread out below. On the top landing of the fort on Bennet's Hill. The photo shows a broad, elevated panorama over Nassau Harbour, with the island and the eastern approaches spread out below.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and set the horizon on the upper or lower line so it stays level. Put the harbour water in the foreground and the island across the top of the frame. A little fort wall at one edge adds depth. If you want a closer, lower view with boats passing underneath, the Paradise Island Bridge is a good alternative.

Settings tip: Tap the water or the far shoreline to set focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the sky does not wash out. For a wider sweep of the harbour, try panorama mode and move slowly and steadily.

Best time: Try a clear morning or later in the afternoon, when the light is softer and the harbour colours look richer. Midday sun can flatten the view.

3Cable Beach

Cable Beach is the long sandy shoreline on the northern coast of New Providence, west of Nassau town. It runs for about two and a half miles, which is why it suits wide, simple phone photos: a pale strip of sand, calm shallow water and a line of resort buildings behind. The name is said to come from an 1892 submarine telegraph cable that linked Jupiter, Florida to Goodman's Bay. Tourism here goes back to the 1940s, and by the 1950s the area was a centre for hotel building, later promoted as the "Bahamian Riviera". That history explains the resort frontage you see today.

For the best view of the shore, start at Goodman's Bay Park, along the Cable Beach shoreline to the east of the beach's middle. It has open sand and calm water, with a more relaxed, park-like feel behind you. It is a quieter alternative to the busier resort sands, and there is room to turn and frame the sea behind your subject. Weekdays are calmer, because locals use the park heavily at other times. From here the middle of Cable Beach lies to the west, so you can shoot along the shoreline towards it.

Expect vendors and jet skis on the busier sections, and parking near Goodman's Bay can be tight. Keep the horizon level and leave plenty of sky and water in the frame. The sea and sand do most of the work.

Where it is: Cable Beach is on the northern coast of New Providence Island, west of central Nassau, with resort hotels lining the shore. Goodman's Bay Park sits along the same shoreline, to the east of the beach's middle.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the full sweep of sand, water and sky. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural look, and use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a distant hotel or a person walking at the water's edge.

Where to stand: Stand in Goodman's Bay Park (shoreline stretch along Cable Beach). Open sandy space and calm water along the Cable Beach shore, with a more relaxed, park-like feel behind it. From here you look over open sand and calm water, with the park behind you. The middle of Cable Beach is to the west, so turn that way to shoot along the shoreline.

Framing: Turn the gridlines on and set the horizon on the upper or lower line. Keep a strip of sand in the foreground so the picture has depth. If you want the wider view of the long white-sand strip, with turquoise shallows and resort hotels behind it, Cable Beach itself is the alternative spot.

Settings tip: Tap the water or sand to focus, then pull the exposure slider down a little so the bright sand does not turn white. Try a Live Photo for gentle wave movement, or panorama for the length of the shore. Hold the phone level and sweep slowly.

Best time: Go on a weekday for a calmer park and fewer people in your frame. Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light than midday.
