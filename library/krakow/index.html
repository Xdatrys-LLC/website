The 3 free photo spots

1St. Mary's Basilica

St. Mary's Basilica is the Gothic brick church that rises over the northeastern corner of Kraków's Main Market Square. The basilica stands about 80 m tall, and its two towers differ in height. The church dates from the 14th century, though its foundations go back to the early 13th. It is also known for its carved wooden altarpiece by Veit Stoss, which is inside. Every hour a trumpet call, the Hejnał mariacki, sounds from the taller tower and stops mid-tune. That makes a nice moment to film a short clip of the square.

The basilica photographs best from out in the square, where you can step back far enough to fit the towers in. The classic frame takes in the church and the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) together, with the brick walls, steep roofs and the two towers set against the sky. For a different view, look through the arches of the Cloth Hall and let them frame the tower in the gap. Or walk to Mały Rynek, the smaller square beside the main one, for a quieter angle.

If the facade looks partly covered, that may be building work, as towers were reported under repair in summer 2025. Take a few frames from different spots in case scaffolding gets in the way.

Where it is: In the Old Town of Kraków, at Mariacki Square on the northeastern corner of the Main Market Square (Rynek Główny), close to ul. Floriańska.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the basilica and the Cloth Hall into one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, more natural view of the church. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the tower tops and the gilded crown.

Where to stand: Stand in Rynek Główny (northeastern corner, near ul. Floriańska). The basilica and the Cloth Hall together in one frame, the classic postcard composition of the Main Market Square. The northeastern corner near ul. Floriańska is a separate, closer viewpoint that shows the basilica and the Cloth Hall together in one frame. This is the classic postcard view of the Main Market Square.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the towers up with a vertical grid line so they stand straight. Keep the phone level, since tilting it makes the towers lean. Leave a little sky above the taller tower. For a second shot, frame the tower through the Cloth Hall arches, or try Mały Rynek for a quieter angle.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the brick facade to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky keeps its colour. Try a Live Photo or a short video around the hour to catch the trumpet call.

Best time: Early morning, when the square is nearly empty and the basilica is lit from the east.

3Oskar Schindler's Enamel Factory

This is the former enamel factory at Lipowa 4 in Zabłocie, made widely known by the 1993 film Schindler's List. The building you see from the street is the old administration block. Today the site holds two museums: a branch of the Historical Museum of the City of Kraków, and the Museum of Contemporary Art in Kraków in the former workshops. Oskar Schindler took over the factory in 1939 and renamed it Deutsche Emailwarenfabrik in January 1940. The number of Jewish workers grew from just over 150 in 1940 to around 1,100 by 1944. The city museum's exhibition on Kraków under occupation has been here since 2007. The tour includes the desk and stairs from the film set.

It photographs well because the front is plain and honest, and it still looks much as it did in the 1940s. From the street you can take in the renovated facade, the entrance gate and the area where visitors queue, all in one frame. That is enough for a strong, quiet picture without going inside. Keep the mood in mind: this is a serious place, and a straight, uncluttered shot suits it better than a clever angle.

Stand on the street side, opposite the front of number 4, and keep the whole width of the facade in view. Hold the phone level so the vertical lines stay straight. If people are queuing at the gate, wait a moment for a gap, or include them for a sense of scale, but be considerate of anyone who might be recognisable.

Where it is: In the Zabłocie district of Kraków, at ulica Lipowa 4. The Museum of Contemporary Art in Kraków is on the same site, in the former workshops.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x), which gives the most natural-looking facade. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if the street is too narrow to fit the whole front in, and keep the building centred so the edges do not stretch. Skip the telephoto unless you want a close detail of the gate.

Where to stand: Stand on ulica Lipowa (opposite the Lipowa 4 front facade, on the street side). The renovated front of the former enamel factory administration building at Lipowa 4, with the entrance gate and the museum's queue area in the frame. Opposite the Lipowa 4 front facade, on the street side. You will see the renovated front of the former administration building, with the entrance gate and the museum's queue area in the frame.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the building up with the vertical lines so it does not lean. Put the gate in the centre or on a third, and leave a little street in the foreground so the building has room. Take one wide shot of the whole front, then a tighter one of the gate. Avoid pointing the phone upwards, which makes the walls tilt.

Settings tip: Tap the facade to focus, then lower the exposure slider a touch if the sky is bright so the walls keep their detail. Do not use portrait mode on the building; it works best for people. Keep the phone steady against your body or a wall for a sharp shot.

Best time: Daylight is best for reading the facade clearly. Earlier in the day is likely to be quieter at the gate, but check how the light falls on the front when you arrive.

7Main Square, Kraków

Kraków's Main Square is the great open heart of the Old Town. It dates back to the 13th century, covers about 4 hectares and is often described as one of the largest medieval town squares in Europe. The Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) sits in the middle, rebuilt in the Renaissance style after a fire in 1555, with arcades added in the 19th century. The Gothic towers of St Mary's Basilica rise above the square, and the Adam Mickiewicz Monument of 1898 stands nearby. It was rebuilt after the Second World War, when the original was destroyed. The whole Old Town has been on the UNESCO World Heritage list since 1978.

The square photographs well because everything is on a human scale and the landmarks line up. From the northeast corner, where Floriańska Street meets the square, the basilica and the Cloth Hall fit into one frame. This is the classic postcard composition. The colourful townhouse fronts help to frame it, and the square is large enough that you can step back for a wider view.

A few practical points. The basilica's hourly trumpet call (the hejnal) is hard to photograph from the square, even with zoom, so enjoy it by ear instead. Photography inside the basilica is restricted. Climbing the Town Hall Tower or the basilica tower is a separate, paid visit, so check the official site before you go.

Where it is: In the centre of Kraków's Old Town, on the Royal Road between the Barbican to the north and Wawel Castle to the south.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the basilica and the Cloth Hall together. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural look, and use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as the basilica towers.

Where to stand: Stand at the corner of Rynek Główny and Floriańska (northeast corner of the square, where Floriańska Street meets it). St Mary's Basilica and the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) together in one frame, the classic postcard composition. Stand on Rynek Główny, at the northeast corner of the square where Floriańska Street meets it. From here St Mary's Basilica and the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) sit together in one frame, and the Mickiewicz Monument lies to the south-west.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the Cloth Hall along the lower third, with the basilica towers rising behind it. Keep the phone level so the buildings don't lean. For a second view, stand by the Mickiewicz Monument and shoot past the Cloth Hall arcades towards the basilica, which keeps the café clutter out of the frame. Floriańska Street also makes a good shot, with its colourful townhouse fronts leading towards the basilica towers in the distance.

Settings tip: Tap on the basilica facade to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky doesn't wash out. Try a Live Photo if people are crossing the square, then pick the cleanest frame.

Best time: Early morning, around 7-8 AM, when the light falls well on the basilica facade and the square is quiet.
