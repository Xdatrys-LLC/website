The 3 free photo spots

1Odori Park

Odori Park is a long, green strip through the middle of Sapporo, running east to west for about 1.5 km and splitting the city into its northern and southern halves. It began as a firebreak in the 1870s, was later used as a street and then laid out as a walking area, and today it is a string of flower gardens and event grounds. The Sapporo Snow Festival and the Lilac Festival both take place here. It photographs well because the park is so straight and so long. From above, the whole ribbon of trees and gardens lines up against the city grid, with mountains behind. The classic view is from the observation deck of the Sapporo TV Tower, which was erected in 1957 and stands at the east end of the park. Looking west from the deck, you see the full length of the park, and on a clear day Ishikari Bay beyond. Dusk is the best moment, when the sunset and the first street lights can be shot together. If you would rather stay on the ground, a second option is to stand in the park and photograph the tower rising over the trees, with the subway exit sign bearing the city's name in the foreground. Another is the Sunken Garden, where roses in soft evening light look best, with the quieter western end of the park behind. What you see changes with the seasons, since winter festival structures and flower displays vary through the year.

Where it is: Odori Park lies in the centre of Sapporo, in Hokkaido, running east to west between the northern and southern halves of the city. The Sapporo TV Tower stands at its eastern end, in the Nishi 1 chōme block.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole length of the park, then switch to the default lens (1x) for a cleaner view of the city grid. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the mountains or one flower bed.

Where to stand: Stand at さっぽろテレビ塔 (observation deck, looking west along the park; the tower sits at the east end of Odori Park). A bird's-eye view of the whole length of Odori Park, with the city grid, the surrounding mountains and, on clear days, Ishikari Bay beyond. Face west, along the length of the park. From here the photo shows a bird's-eye view of the whole park, with the city grid, the surrounding mountains and, on clear days, Ishikari Bay beyond.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the park up with a vertical grid line so it runs straight away from you. Keep a little sky and the mountains in the top third. For a different picture, go down to the park and shoot the tower rising over the trees, framed by the subway exit sign bearing the city's name.

Settings tip: Tap on the park to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the sunset sky keeps its colour. When the lights come on, try night mode and hold the phone as still as you can. Hold the lens close to the glass to cut reflections.

Best time: Dusk, when you can shoot the sunset and the first street lights together. Arrive a little early to get a good spot on the deck.

2Sapporo Beer Museum

The Sapporo Beer Museum is a red-brick Meiji-era building in Sapporo Garden Park, and the outside is easy to enjoy even if you never go in. The brick walls, arched windows, red star emblems and the tall chimney behind the roofline give you plenty to work with. The building went up in 1890 as a factory for the Sapporo Sugar Company. The Sapporo Beer Company bought it in 1903 and turned it into a brewery. It opened as a museum in July 1987, and it is described as the only beer museum in Japan. It was registered as a Hokkaidō Heritage site in 2004. The Sapporo Beer Garden is in the south wing of the building. Start at the front entrance, where the facade and the red star give you the classic shot. Then walk round the building and try the side walls for a different angle. Keep your phone level so the vertical lines of the chimney and walls stay straight. If you plan to go inside, check the official site before you go.

Where it is: In Sapporo Garden Park in Higashi-ku, on the east side of Sapporo. The Sapporo Beer Garden is connected to the museum, and the Ario Sapporo shopping mall is also in the park.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the front facade and the red star. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are close and want the whole building and chimney in one frame. Use telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for details such as the star emblems and arched windows.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. From the front the photo shows the red-brick facade with its red star emblems, arched windows and the tall brick chimney rising behind it. For a different angle, walk along the side walls.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the facade with the vertical lines so the walls do not lean. Put the red star in the upper third and let the chimney rise behind it. Then walk round the building and try the side walls for a more graphic shot of brick and windows.

Settings tip: Tap on the brick wall to focus, then pull the exposure slider down a little if the sky is bright, so the red brick keeps its colour. When you use the ultra-wide lens close to the walls, stay square on to the building to limit distortion.

Best time: Late afternoon, which one guide recommends for exterior shots.

3Nijo Market

Nijo Market is a row of seafood stalls in Sapporo, and the stall row is what makes it worth a phone photo. Along the L-shaped stretch of South 2nd Avenue (Minami 2-jo), crab, sea urchin, salmon roe and fish are laid out on display. Colourful signs and fish-shaped signboards hang around them, so almost every frame has colour and a clear subject. Walk the whole row rather than stopping at the first stall. That lets you take wide scenes of the street and then move in on the close-up displays. The roadside mascot statues are a popular photo stop too, and they work well as a foreground for a wider shot. Go in the morning, before about 10:00. If you want a quieter picture, a second option is Sōseigawa Park nearby. From the riverbank you can look back across the Sosei River toward the market buildings, with a calmer foreground. Stall hours vary from shop to shop, so check before making a special trip. Wider shots of the general atmosphere are usually fine, but ask permission before taking close-ups of the people working at the stalls.

Where it is: Nijo Market is in Sapporo, along South 2nd Avenue (Minami 2-jo). The Sosei River and Sōseigawa Park are close by.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the stall displays and signs. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) for the whole street and the mascot statues, and use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for close-ups of crab, urchin and roe without leaning over the stalls.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Stand on the stall row along the L-shaped stretch of South 2nd Avenue (Minami 2-jo).

Framing: Take a wide shot first to show the whole row and its signs. Then move along and frame single displays close up. Use the gridlines to keep the stall fronts straight, and put a mascot statue in the foreground for scale. For a calmer alternative, try the view back across the Sosei River from Sōseigawa Park.

Settings tip: Tap on the display you want to focus on, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the bright signs wash out. Portrait mode can help with a single crab or a bowl of roe, if your phone allows it.

Best time: Morning, before 10:00 AM.
