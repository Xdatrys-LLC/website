This map shows 20 of the most-searched sights in Detroit as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Belle Isle, Eastern Market, Ford Field, Detroit Institute of Arts and Motown Museum lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. The 3 free photo spots

1Belle Isle Park

Belle Isle Park is a 982-acre island park in the Detroit River, reached from the city by the MacArthur Bridge. Frederick Law Olmsted, who designed Central Park in New York, was hired in the 1880s to plan it, though only some of his design was finished. The island has a lot to see: an aquarium and conservatory by Albert Kahn, a marble lighthouse at the east end, beaches and plenty of shoreline. For a phone photo, start at the James Scott Memorial Fountain. Cass Gilbert designed it and it was completed in 1925. Seen from the front, the spray and marble figures sit in the foreground with the Detroit skyline rising behind them, so one frame holds the island and the city. It is a popular place for wedding and portrait photos, and sunset is the favoured hour. Two other spots are worth a look if you have time. Sunset Point shows the skyline and river at dusk, with the Ambassador Bridge and Windsor's shoreline across the water. Belle Isle Front Beach has open river and trees, with Windsor in the distance. Work on the fountain is under way. One guide says the plaza and nearby areas are closed through 2026, but the fountain can still be photographed from outside the closed area. Please check the current position before you travel.

Where it is: Belle Isle is an island in the Detroit River, just off mainland Detroit and joined to it by the MacArthur Bridge. The border with Canada runs along the channel to the south of the island.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the fountain with the skyline behind it. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the whole fountain and more sky, and to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the skyline closer behind the spray.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (view from the front, with the downtown skyline behind the fountain). The fountain's spray and marble figures in the foreground, with the Detroit skyline rising behind it. Walk or drive on to the James Scott Memorial Fountain and stand in front of it, then shoot back towards the downtown skyline, so the spray and marble figures fill the foreground. If the plaza is still closed, shoot from the nearest open spot outside the closed area.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the fountain on one side, with the skyline along the upper line. Keep the horizon level. For a wider, cleaner frame, try 0.5x from low down. If you have time, Sunset Point is a good second spot for the skyline and river at dusk.

Settings tip: Tap the bright part of the sky to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sunset colours are not washed out. Try a Live Photo to catch the movement of the spray. Use portrait mode if you are photographing people in front of the fountain, and night mode once the light fades, holding the phone steady against a wall or railing.

Best time: Sunset, when the sun is low and the skyline glows.

2Eastern Market

Eastern Market is a working market district, and the best phone photo here is not taken inside the sheds. It is taken from the northern end of the Dequindre Cut Greenway, next to the market. The greenway runs sunken below street level, and its walls are covered in murals. People cycle and skate past them, and the market's industrial surroundings sit behind. That mix of colourful street art, movement and industrial background makes a lively, very local picture.

The usual advice is to shoot down onto riders passing a mural. Looking down gives you the painted wall, the path and the people in one frame. If you want the market itself, the shed walls also carry colourful murals, and they make a good backdrop for vendors and shoppers with the open sheds visible. Lincoln Street Art Park is a quieter street-art setting near the market, if the sheds feel crowded.

Saturday is the busiest day, so go early if you want space. Vendors are usually happy to be photographed if you buy something and ask first. Photographing people in public is generally legal, but using a recognisable person's image commercially needs their release.

Where it is: Eastern Market is a market district in Detroit. The Dequindre Cut Greenway meets it at its northern end.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for riders and the mural together. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole painted wall and the cut below. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a single rider from where you stand.

Where to stand: Stand in Dequindre Cut Greenway (northern end near Eastern Market; bridges above the cut). Murals on the sunken greenway walls with cyclists and skaters passing, framed against the market's industrial surroundings. If there is a bridge nearby, shoot down from it onto the mural wall and the path.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the mural along one third of the frame, leaving the path for riders to cross. If you shoot from a bridge, lean slightly over the rail, without dropping your phone, to keep the wall straight. For a second picture, walk to the market sheds and frame a mural behind the stalls, or try Lincoln Street Art Park for a quieter wall.

Settings tip: Tap on the rider's path to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the mural colours stay rich and the bright sky does not wash out. Try a Live Photo so you can pick the best frame as a cyclist passes.

Best time: Late afternoon, when light falls into the cut at an angle. For the market sheds, go early on a Saturday, but expect crowds.

3Ford Field

Ford Field is the indoor stadium of the Detroit Lions, set on the edge of Downtown Detroit next to Comerica Park. It is a big building, and the outline on the map is about 390 m across, so the photo is about how you fit it into the frame. From there the stadium sits on one side of the street, and the long roadway leads your eye along its front. The stadium was built around an old Hudson's warehouse from the 1920s, which is now offices. The playing field is sunk 45 feet (14 m) below street level, which helps keep the stadium from towering over the skyline. The frosted windows near the roofline are meant to echo Detroit's car factories. Super Bowl XL was played here on 5 February 2006. Stand on the pavement, keep the road leading into the picture, and let the stadium fill one side. Brush Street is busy on game days, so a quieter day is easier for photos. If you have time, the corner of Brush Street and Adams Street shows the curved glass entrance.

Where it is: Ford Field is in Downtown Detroit, next to Comerica Park, on the north-east side of the stand-here point on Brush Street.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the street view. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if the stadium front will not fit, and use 2x if you want to pick out a detail such as the marquee.

Where to stand: Stand on Brush Street (the stretch across from Comerica Park, looking toward the stadium). Ford Field on one side of the street, framed down the Brush Street roadway, with Comerica Park across from it. On the stretch across from Comerica Park, looking toward the stadium. Ford Field is to the north-east, on one side of the street, with Comerica Park opposite it. The Brush Street roadway frames the view.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and use the line of the road to lead the eye towards the stadium. Keep your phone level so the building edges stay straight. For a close-up of the curved glass entrance with the Ford Field marquee, try the corner of Brush Street and Adams Street instead.

Settings tip: Tap on the stadium wall to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky looks washed out. Hold the phone still, or lean against a post for steadiness.

Best time: Choose a day with no Lions game or event, in daylight, when the street is quieter and easier to shoot. Check the stadium's event calendar before you go.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Detroit?

The first three on this map are Belle Isle, Eastern Market and Ford Field. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph Belle Isle?

Stand right at Belle Isle (view from the front, with the downtown skyline behind the fountain). Start with the default lens (1x) for the fountain with the skyline behind it.

When is the best time to photograph Eastern Market?

Late afternoon, when light falls into the cut at an angle. For the market sheds, go early on a Saturday, but expect crowds.

How do I get to Ford Field?

Head for Downtown Detroit and walk to Brush Street, on the stretch across from Comerica Park.
