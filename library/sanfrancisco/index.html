The 3 free photo spots

1Golden Gate Bridge

The Golden Gate Bridge is a suspension bridge across the Golden Gate, the strait about a mile wide where San Francisco Bay meets the Pacific. It opened on 27 May 1937 and carries road traffic as well as people on foot and on bikes. Back then it was the longest and tallest suspension bridge in the world. The main span is 1,280 m, and the total height is 227 m. A travel guide has called it the most photographed bridge in the world, and guides call the view from Battery Spencer, on the Marin Headlands side at the north end of the bridge, the classic postcard view. From up here you look at the bridge nearly side-on and from above, with the north tower close by and San Francisco and the bay behind it. That layering of tower, span, water and city is why the view works so well on a phone. It is free, and the views are wide and open. If you want a second angle, Golden Gate Overlook shows the bridge head-on between two cypress trees, and Baker Beach shows it from the ocean side. Fog can hide the bridge completely, so check conditions before you set off.

Where it is: The bridge links San Francisco, at the northern tip of the San Francisco Peninsula, to Marin County across the Golden Gate strait. The viewpoint is on the Marin Headlands side, at the north end of the bridge, looking back towards the city.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the tower, the span and the city behind in one frame. Then try the default lens (1x) for a cleaner, more natural view. If your phone has telephoto (2x-5x), use it to pick out the north tower or to bring the city skyline closer to the bridge.

Where to stand: Stand on Battery Spencer (Marin Headlands side, on the north end of the bridge, reached from Conzelman Road). The bridge seen nearly side-on from above, with the north tower close and San Francisco and the bay behind it. The bridge lies to the south of you, seen nearly side-on from above, with the north tower close and San Francisco and the bay beyond. Stay behind any barriers and keep clear of the road.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line a tower up with a vertical line so the bridge doesn't look tilted. Let the span run diagonally across the frame, with the city in the gap behind it. Keep a bit of the headland in a corner to give a sense of height. For a different angle, Golden Gate Overlook gives a head-on view between two cypress trees.

Settings tip: Tap on the bridge to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky and the paint keep their detail. If fog is rolling through, take a Live Photo and pick the best moment afterwards. Wipe the lens before you start, as sea air can leave it smeary.

Best time: Sunrise or early morning, or sunset, when fog or soft light makes the bridge stand out.

2Alcatraz Island

Alcatraz is a small, flat-topped rock about 1.25 miles offshore in San Francisco Bay, and it is one of the most recognisable shapes on the water. It began as a lighthouse site and a military fort, and from 1934 it was a federal prison. The prison closed on 21 March 1963. Today the island is run by the National Park Service and is a National Historic Landmark. It still has the oldest operating lighthouse on the West Coast, and nearly 1.4 million people visit each year. The island is only a little over 500 metres long, so from the mainland it reads as a compact shape sitting on open water. That makes it easy to frame cleanly, with the sky and bay around it. The best-placed view is from Pier 39. Walk out to the far, back side, past the sea lions, where the angle is most open and the island sits centred across the water. Zoom in rather than shooting wide, because the island looks tiny at normal zoom. Fog can hide it completely, so have a look at the bay before you walk all the way out. If you want a wider view that includes the Golden Gate Bridge, try Marina Green instead.

Where it is: Alcatraz Island sits in San Francisco Bay, north of the city's waterfront and close to the Golden Gate strait.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the telephoto lens at 2x to 3x, whichever your phone offers. The default lens (1x) leaves the island small in a big empty frame, and the ultra-wide (0.5x) makes it smaller still.

Where to stand: Stand on Pier 39 (far (back) side of the pier, past the sea lions). Alcatraz sits centered across open water, with the bay and sky spread behind it, shot best with a 2x to 3x phone zoom or a longer lens. At the far (back) side of the pier, past the sea lions. From there Alcatraz sits centred across open water, with the bay and sky behind it. Face north-west.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the island on the centre or lower-third line, with plenty of sky above. Keep the horizon level. Zoom in until the island fills a good part of the frame without the edges looking cramped. If you want a wider view, Marina Green shows the island with the Golden Gate Bridge and Angel Island in the same scene.

Settings tip: Tap the island to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour and the island does not wash out. Hold the phone steady against a rail or your body at 2x to 3x to avoid blur.

Best time: Golden hour or early morning gives the warmest light. Midday in summer can look harsh. Fog can hide the island at any time, so be ready to try again.

3Fisherman's Wharf

Fisherman's Wharf is the busy stretch of San Francisco's northern waterfront. It began as a working fishing port, and in 1900 the state set this shoreline aside for commercial fishing boats. The fishing docks declined in the 1960s and 1970s as tourism took over, but a few old family-run seafood restaurants are still here. For a phone camera, the best frame is not one single landmark. It is the mix of historic ships, the bay and the waterfront buildings. From here you can look southwest towards Ghirardelli Square and the waterfront, with the old ships moored close by. Across the water you can also pick out the Golden Gate Bridge. The main tourist strip of the Wharf, along Jefferson Street, lies to the east. Pier 39, with its sea lion colony, is further along the same waterfront. The pier has been reported closed for renovation, so check its status before you go. If the entrance is closed, the lettered Fisherman's Wharf sign or Aquatic Park both make good alternatives. Aquatic Park has a sheltered cove, a curved pier and a Streamline Moderne bathhouse, with the bay beyond.

Where it is: Fisherman's Wharf is on San Francisco's northern waterfront, north of Russian Hill, North Beach and Telegraph Hill.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the view towards Ghirardelli Square and the waterfront. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit in the ships' masts and rigging. Use the telephoto lens (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the Golden Gate Bridge across the water.

Where to stand: Stand on Hyde Street Pier (pier entrance, looking southwest). Ghirardelli Square and the waterfront from the pier entrance, with the historic ships of the maritime park nearby. At the pier entrance, looking southwest. From here your photo takes in Ghirardelli Square and the waterfront, with the historic ships of the maritime park close by. The main Wharf is to the east.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the waterfront along the top third of the frame. Keep a ship's hull or rigging in the foreground for depth. Turn to the water for a second shot of the Golden Gate Bridge. For a different view, Aquatic Park gives a calm cove with the curved pier and bathhouse in the foreground. The Fisherman's Wharf sign gives a street-level shot with the busy Wharf behind it.

Settings tip: Tap on the ships to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky and water keep their colour. Wipe the lens first, as sea air can leave a film on it.

Best time: Late afternoon usually gives softer, warmer light on the waterfront. Early morning is typically quieter if you want the ships without people.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in San Francisco?

The first three on this map are Golden Gate Bridge, Alcatraz Island and Fisherman's Wharf. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph the Golden Gate Bridge?

Stand on Battery Spencer (Marin Headlands side, on the north end of the bridge, reached from Conzelman Road). Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the tower, the span and the city behind in one frame.

When is the best time to photograph Alcatraz Island?

Golden hour or early morning gives the warmest light. Midday in summer can look harsh.

How do I get to Fisherman's Wharf?

The Powell / Hyde cable car line ends at Aquatic Park, on the western side of the Wharf.
