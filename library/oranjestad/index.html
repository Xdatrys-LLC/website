This map shows 11 of the most-searched sights in Oranjestad as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Eagle Beach, Palm Beach, Druif Beach, Aruba Aloe and Bubali Bird Sanctuary lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. The 3 free photo spots

1Eagle Beach

Eagle Beach, also known as Arend Beach, is a beach and neighbourhood of Oranjestad, and it is the widest beach in Aruba. The sand is soft and white, and the resorts behind it are low-rise, so the sky and the horizon stay open. It has been rated one of the best beaches in the world. The beach is public, so you can walk along it freely.

The picture most visitors come for is the wind-bent fofoti trees leaning over the white sand, with the turquoise sea behind them. Guides often call the trees the most photographed subject on the island. They make a strong dark shape against a bright sky, which is why the view works so well at sunset.

Walk along the sand until the trees sit clearly against the sea, and keep a little distance so the whole curve of each tree fits in the frame. Include some empty sand in the foreground to give the picture depth. The fofoti trees are protected, so please don't hang anything on them and stay off their roots. Sea turtles nest on this beach from April to October, so don't use any lights near the nests at night. A short walk along the beach will give you plenty of different angles.

Where it is: Eagle Beach is a beach and neighbourhood of Oranjestad, Aruba, lined with low-rise resorts.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the classic view of the trees, sand and sea. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want to take in a whole tree and a big sky from close up. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a single tree against the sunset.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. As you line up the shot, the turquoise sea and the sunset sky come into the frame around the trees.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the horizon on the top or bottom line, so the trees and sky have room. Keep a strip of empty sand in the foreground. For a silhouette, frame the tree against the brightest part of the sky.

Settings tip: Tap on the bright sky to focus and lower the exposure slider a little, so the trees go dark and the sky colour stays rich. Try a Live Photo too, as the leaves and waves move. Keep your phone away from the sea spray and wipe the lens before shooting.

Best time: About 30 minutes before sunset, when the trees stand out against the orange sky. Sunrise also works well, with side light on the sand.

2Palm Beach, Aruba

Palm Beach is a tourism district in Aruba, lined with high-rise hotels around a long, pale beach with clear shallow water. It photographs well because of the colour. Turquoise water runs out to the horizon, and sailboats and seabirds often add a focal point. The best phone view is from De Palm Pier, the easy, quieter alternative to the busy hotel frontage. If you are at the pier, walk to the far end and face the sea. The water fills most of the frame and the pier's lines lead your eye towards the sunset, with the Palm Beach skyline along the shoreline. The name comes from a coconut plantation owned by Captain R.J. Beaujon, who named it after Palm Beach in Florida, and the beach was officially registered under that name in 1960. The first hotel here, opened in the 1950s, was a set of bungalows on bare sand, which is a long way from today's towers. If you want people in the shot, the Palm Beach Boardwalk is good for candid pictures of walkers among palm trees. Eduardo's Beach Shack has swings among palms with the sea behind, which suits a relaxed portrait. Eduardo's is on private business property, so you may only see it during opening hours. Go at sunrise or sunset for softer light and fewer people.

Where it is: Palm Beach is a tourist district in Noord, about 6 km northwest of Oranjestad. It is a strip of beach and high-rise hotels on Aruba's coast, with a shopping district adjacent to the hotels.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the pier, the water and the sky together. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a cleaner, more natural view. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a sailboat or seabirds on the water.

Where to stand: Stand on De Palm Pier (walk to the far end of the pier, facing the sea). Turquoise water running to the horizon, with sailboats and seabirds framing the Palm Beach skyline behind the shoreline. If you are at De Palm Pier, walk to the far end of the pier and face the sea as well. From the water's edge you see turquoise water running to the horizon, with sailboats and seabirds, and the Palm Beach skyline along the shoreline.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the horizon on the upper or lower third line. Let the pier's edge or railing lead the eye towards the sunset or the open water. Wait for a sailboat or a bird to enter the frame, rather than shooting an empty sea. For a different view, try the Palm Beach Boardwalk for people walking among palms.

Settings tip: Tap on the sea or sky to focus. Then drag the exposure slider down a little so the bright sky keeps its colour and the water stays rich. Keep the horizon level using the gridlines. Wipe the lens first, as sea spray and sunscreen smear it.

Best time: Sunrise and sunset, when the light is softer and the crowds are thinner.

3Druif Beach

Druif Beach is a stretch of shoreline in Oranjestad, Aruba, about 300 metres long. That is long enough to give you a good sweep of coast, but short enough to walk end to end in a few minutes. It works well for phone photos because a beach gives you simple, clear layers: sand in the foreground, water in the middle and sky above. Those layers make an easy, uncluttered picture.

The phone icon on the map is about 450 m west of the subject. From there you can take a wider shot of the coast and the beach in its setting. Then walk along to the beach itself for closer pictures, such as a shot along the length of the sand or a low shot with the water's edge leading into the frame.

Whichever you choose, keep the horizon level, because on a long, flat view a tilted horizon is the first thing the eye notices. Turn on gridlines to help. If people are on the beach, wait a moment for a gap, or include them on purpose to show the scale. Conditions change from day to day, so take a few frames from different spots rather than relying on one.

Where it is: Druif Beach is on the coast of Oranjestad, the capital of Aruba.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the overall scene. Use the telephoto lens (2x-5x, depending on your phone) from where you stand to bring the distant beach closer. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) once you are on the sand to fit in the full sweep of the shore.

Where to stand: Stand about 450 m west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face east. From here the beach is a distant feature, so treat this as a scene-setting view.

Framing: From where you stand, use gridlines and place the shoreline on the lower third, with plenty of sky above. Zoom to 2x or 3x if the beach looks too small. On the beach, stand near one end and shoot along the 300 m length so the line of the shore leads the eye through the frame. Keep the horizon level.

Settings tip: Tap on the sand or the sea to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the colours stay rich and the highlights are not washed out. If there is any movement in the water, try a Live Photo.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light than midday. Late in the afternoon the sun is behind you when looking east from where you stand, which helps light the scene evenly.
