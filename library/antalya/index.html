This map shows 12 of the most-searched sights in Antalya as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Lower Düden Falls, Konyaaltı Beach, Hadrian's Gate, Antalya Aquarium and Antalya Museum lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. The 3 free photo spots

1Lower Düden Waterfalls

Here the Düden River drops 40 metres (131 ft) off the cliff into the Mediterranean. That mix of white water, rock and open sea is why it is the first photo most visitors take. The waterfall sits inside Düden Park, and the park's main viewing terrace on the cliff top gives a side-on view of the water leaving the cliff edge, with the sea right beside it. There is no walking route down to the base, so a head-on view from below is only possible from a boat. From the terrace the side view is the one to go for, and it is the classic Düden frame.

The cliff slope can tilt your picture, so turn on the gridlines and line the horizon up with one of them. Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) or the default lens (1x) to get the fall, the cliff and the sea in one frame. Then try a zoom (2x or more) to pick out just the falling water. Take a few shots at each setting, because the light changes quickly on the coast. If you have time, Gençlik Parkı is a second option: it gives a side view of the water crashing into the sea, framed by the park's greenery. Don't confuse this waterfall with Upper Düden, which is a separate, fenced site elsewhere in the province.

Where it is: On Antalya's Mediterranean coast, inside Düden Park, where the Düden River drops over the cliff into the sea.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) or the default lens (1x) to fit the falls, cliff edge and sea into one frame. Use a zoom lens (2x or more, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the falling water.

Where to stand: Stand in Düden Parkı (cliff-top main viewing terrace above the falls, on the side facing the sea). The falls dropping from the cliff edge into the sea, seen from the side, with the sea beside them. From here you see the falls dropping from the cliff edge into the sea from the side, with the sea beside them. The falls lie to the south-west of you.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the horizon on a line, because the slope of the cliff can tilt the picture. Keep the falls on one side and the open sea on the other so the drop reads clearly. For another version of the shot, Gençlik Parkı gives a side view of the water crashing into the sea, framed by greenery.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest water to focus, then lower the exposure slider a little so the white water keeps its detail. Try a Live Photo to catch the movement of the fall, and wipe your lens first if there is any spray.

Best time: Sunset is the time a photo guide recommends, because the light gives strong tones against the sea.

2Konyaaltı Beach

Konyaaltı Beach is one of Antalya's two main beaches, the other being Lara. It runs along the western side of the city for about 13 km, mostly fine pebbles with some sand, and it holds Blue Flag status for water quality. The beach park, bars, cafes and hotels sit along its inland edge, and the Aqualand waterpark on Dumlupınar Bulvarı marks the other end. Turkish pines and hibiscus grow along the front.

For photographs, the best idea is to go up rather than down to the shingle. The park and viewing terrace around the glass pyramid (Cam Piramit) give a wider, higher view than the shoreline. From there you see the curve of the beach, the green park areas, the coast stretching on toward Lara and the mountains in the distance. The glass pyramid also makes a handy landmark to put in the foreground. If you want the classic mountain-and-sea picture, the pebble shore with the Beydağları peaks behind is the other option. For a quieter shoreline view, try the eastern end of the beach near Dumlupınar Bulvarı.

Bring a lens cloth, as sea air can smear the glass. Keep the horizon level using the gridlines. Stay on the public walkways, because swimming and beach-club areas may limit where you can stand down on the sand.

Where it is: Konyaaltı Beach is on the western side of Antalya, with the beach park and hotels along its inland edge.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the park, the pyramid and the whole sweep of beach. Then switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, more natural view. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the mountains or a stretch of coast.

Where to stand: Stand at Cam Piramit (park area and viewing terrace around the glass pyramid, Runtalya village). An elevated panorama over the beach, the park areas and the coastline stretching toward Lara, with the mountains in the distance. From there you look out over the beach, the park areas and the coastline running toward Lara, with the mountains in the distance. Konyaaltı Beach is to the south-west.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the horizon on the upper or lower line, depending on whether you want more sea or more beach. Use the glass pyramid or a park path as a foreground anchor. For a different view, go down to Konyaaltı Plajı for the pebble shore with the Beydağları peaks behind, or to the eastern end by Dumlupınar Bulvarı for a quieter stretch.

Settings tip: Tap on the mountains to set focus, then pull the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. Try panorama mode for the full length of the coast, moving slowly and keeping the phone level.

Best time: Morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and clearer mountains than the middle of a summer day.

3Hadrian's Gate

Hadrian's Gate is a Roman memorial gate, built in honour of the emperor Hadrian, who visited the city in 130 CE. It has three arches, with a tower on each side. It is built of white marble, apart from the granite pillars, and is only about 8 metres high, so it feels close and easy to take in. The arches are decorated with flower and rosette carvings, and the top edge has a band of floral reliefs and a cornice with lion heads. The southern tower is Roman. The upper part of the northern tower was rebuilt in the 13th century and carries an Arabic inscription, so you can see two eras in one frame. Today it is the only surviving entrance gate in the old city walls. The walls hid it for years, which helped protect it. It was restored in 1959. Walk through the central arch and you can look down through a clear panel at the original Roman paving, with deep cart grooves worn into it.

It photographs well because the three arches make a natural frame, with the Old Town lanes opening up behind them. For the classic shot, take the straight-on view from Atatürk Caddesi so the arches sit symmetrically. Switch on gridlines to keep the pillars upright. Then step in close for the carvings and the floor panel. The gate sits on a busy boulevard, so wait for a gap in the traffic before you shoot.

Where it is: Hadrian's Gate is in central Antalya, where the modern boulevard, Atatürk Caddesi, meets the walled Old Town. It stands in the old city wall and is the only entrance gate left there.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the straight-on view of all three arches. Use ultra-wide (0.5x) if the boulevard is crowded and you cannot step back, but keep the gate centred so the edges do not stretch. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the lion-head cornice and the floral carvings.

Where to stand: Stand on Atatürk Caddesi (boulevard side, facing the gate from the modern-city end). A straight-on frontal view of the three arches with the Old Town lanes opening beyond them. From here you get a straight-on view of the three arches, with the Old Town lanes opening up beyond them.

Framing: Centre the middle arch and use gridlines to keep the pillars upright. Wait for a gap in the traffic so the road does not cut across the base. For a different view, shoot from inside the Old Town looking back through the arches to the boulevard, or find a slightly raised spot with the boulevard fountain behind the gate. Guides disagree on which side is best, so try a couple of positions.

Settings tip: Tap on the marble to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the white stone keeps its detail and the arches do not look washed out. If the arches are dark against a bright sky, try the phone's HDR or auto-exposure setting. After dark, use night mode and hold the phone against a wall or railing to keep it steady.

Best time: One guide suggests shooting from the western side in the morning, with the sun behind you, but that side is unconfirmed, so check the light when you arrive. The gate is also lit after dark, which makes a good night-mode shot.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Antalya?

The first three on this map are Lower Düden Falls, Konyaaltı Beach and Hadrian's Gate. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 12.

Where should I stand to photograph Lower Düden Falls?

Stand in Düden Parkı (cliff-top main viewing terrace above the falls, on the side facing the sea). Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) or the default lens (1x) to fit the falls, cliff edge and sea into one frame.

When is the best time to photograph Konyaaltı Beach?

Morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and clearer mountains than the middle of a summer day.

How do I get to Hadrian's Gate?

There are no tram or railway stops nearby, so head on foot to the edge of the Old Town on Atatürk Caddesi and look for Hadrian's Gate (Üçkapılar).
