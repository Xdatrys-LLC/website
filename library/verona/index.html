The 3 free photo spots

1Verona Arena

Verona Arena is a Roman amphitheatre in the historic centre of Verona, and one of the best-preserved ancient amphitheatres anywhere. Restoration work from the 16th century onwards helped it survive in such good shape. Experts still debate exactly when it was built, with suggestions ranging from the 1st to the 3rd century. Scholars' proposals centre on the 1st century, from the late Augustan period to the mid-1st century, which would make it older than the Colosseum in Rome. In summer it hosts an opera festival that has run since 1913, interrupted only by wars, and the rest of the year it works as a concert venue.

It photographs well because the pink-tinted arches fill the frame, with the cafés and arcades of the square around its base giving a sense of scale. For more room for a wide shot, the classic view is from the far side of Piazza Bra. The square is free to stand in, and it is quietest early in the morning. The light is also pleasant in the last twenty minutes before sunset.

For a different view, try the Ponte Scaligero, where the Arena appears in the background across the river Adige. Via Mazzini also offers a view through an archway at the end of the street. Check the framing on the spot, as that last one is less well known.

Where it is: In the historic centre of Verona, on the edge of Piazza Bra, where the Arena fills one side of the square.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the arches without distortion. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if people or café tables crowd the foreground and you want more of the curve of the wall. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details of the arches.

Where to stand: Stand in Piazza Bra (far side of the square, facing the Arena). The Arena's pink-tinted arches filling the frame, with the square's cafés and arcades around its base. From there the pink-tinted arches fill the frame, with the cafés and arcades around its base. For a wider view, the far side of Piazza Bra is the classic viewpoint.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and keep the horizon level, so the arches do not lean. Put the base of the wall on the lower line to include some of the square, or keep it tight to the arches for a calmer picture. For a different angle, try the Ponte Scaligero for the Arena across the river, or Via Mazzini for a shot through an archway.

Settings tip: Tap the arches to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the sky is bright, so the stone keeps its colour. After sunset, use night mode and brace your elbows against your body to keep the shot steady.

Best time: The last twenty minutes before sunset, or early morning when the square is quieter.

2Juliet's House

Juliet's House is the small courtyard that visitors come to see for its famous balcony. Most people shoot from the courtyard floor, where the crowds gather. From there you can frame the balcony above the courtyard, with the bronze statue of Juliet and the ivy-covered walls in the foreground. It is a tight space, so expect other visitors in your shot. A phone's ultra-wide lens (0.5x) helps you fit the balcony, walls and statue into one frame.

There is also a quieter option. Next to the house entrance is an embroidery shop, and a second-floor window there is said to give a raised view down into the courtyard, with the balcony visible from above. You may have the view almost to yourself in the evening when the house is closed. You would be expected to buy a personalised embroidery item to go upstairs. Confirm the shop still offers it.

Access to the courtyard can vary. It may be free, or controlled at busy times, and the courtyard and house may be ticketed through a single entrance at Teatro Nuovo in Piazzetta Navona, with the Via Cappello door reportedly permanently closed. Check the current rules before you go, and allow a little extra time to line up your photo.

Where it is: Juliet's House is in Verona and has a door on Via Cappello, which may be permanently closed.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) in the crowded courtyard to fit the balcony, statue and walls into one frame. From the shop window, use the default lens (1x) for the whole scene, or a telephoto/zoom (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the balcony from above.

Where to stand: Stand at La Casa di Giulietta (second-floor window of the embroidery shop next to the house entrance). A elevated, less crowded view down into the courtyard, with the famous balcony visible from above. From there you have two separate options. One is the courtyard floor, with the balcony framed above the bronze statue of Juliet and the ivy-covered walls. The other is the second-floor window of the embroidery shop next to the house entrance, which is said to look down into the courtyard with the famous balcony visible from above.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and place the balcony on one of the upper intersections. Keep the courtyard below as context. If you are in the courtyard instead, include the statue and ivy at the bottom of the frame so the balcony sits above them.

Settings tip: Tap on the balcony to focus, then lower the exposure slider slightly if the sky or bright walls look washed out. If you shoot through a window, hold the phone close to the glass to cut reflections.

Best time: Evening, when the house is closed. The view from the shop window may be quiet then.

5Ponte Pietra

Ponte Pietra, the "Stone Bridge", is a Roman arch bridge across the Adige and the oldest bridge in Verona. It was completed in 100 BC, and the Via Postumia from Genoa to Aquileia once ran over it. It also gave the city access to the Roman theatre on the opposite bank. The arch nearest the right bank was rebuilt in 1298 under Alberto I della Scala. Four arches were blown up by retreating German troops in World War II and rebuilt in 1957 using the original materials.

The arches fill the foreground, the river runs away in both directions, and the old-town rooftops sit behind them. The hill by the Teatro Romano rises above the roofs and closes the picture.

For a phone, this is an easy spot. Stand look south-west towards the bridge, and let the arches and the river lead the eye. Be considerate of other people nearby, and take your time. Sunset or golden hour suits it best, and early morning is a quieter option. If you have energy left, Piazzale Castel S. Pietro above the river shows the whole city with the bridge in the middle distance, and the riverbank at Lungadige Re Teodorico shows the arches against the warm-coloured buildings.

Where it is: Ponte Pietra crosses the Adige in Verona and links the city on the right bank with the Roman theatre on the east bank.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the arches, river and rooftops all fit naturally. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the arch in the foreground. Try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the hill and rooftops.

Where to stand: Stand on Ponte Pietra (mid-span, facing the old town and Teatro Romano hill). The bridge's arches in the foreground with the historic-centre rooftops and the hill behind them. From here the photo shows the bridge's arches in the foreground, with the historic-centre rooftops and the Teatro Romano hill behind them.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the line of the bridge along the lower third. Let the river lead the eye towards the rooftops and the hill. If you have time, Piazzale Castel S. Pietro gives a wide view of the whole city from above, with the bridge in the middle distance.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the sky or the river to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. At sunset, check the shot is level before you take it. If the light is low, switch on night mode and hold the phone steady against your body or a solid support.

Best time: Sunset or golden hour. Early morning is a quieter option.
