The 3 free photo spots

1Belém Tower

Belém Tower is a small, ornate fortress standing at the edge of the Tagus, and it is one of the easiest buildings in Lisbon to photograph. It was finished in 1519 and built from local lioz limestone. It is made up of a low bastion and a four-storey tower about 30 metres high. The carved stonework, domed turrets and sculpted crosses give it plenty of detail, and the river behind it gives a clean, open background. It was built to guard the mouth of the river and to greet the ships of Portuguese explorers returning home, and today it is listed by UNESCO together with the nearby Jerónimos Monastery. One popular story says the tower once stood in the middle of the river. In fact it was built on a small island close to the shore. For the classic picture, use the riverside promenade north of the tower, which one guide lists as Avenida de Brasília. Standing there keeps most of the tourist clutter out of your frame and lets you include both the tower and the water. Late light gives the stonework a warm glow. It is also at its most photogenic at high tide, when water surrounds the bastion. If you have time, the lawns of Jardim da Torre de Belém give a second view, showing the full river-facing front framed by greenery. Take a few frames from each spot, because the angle changes the whole mood of the picture.

Where it is: Belém Tower stands on the north bank of the Tagus in Belém, in the west of Lisbon, close to the Jerónimos Monastery. It sits at the water's edge, with a riverside promenade and gardens beside it.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the tower and the river together. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want to include more sky and water or are standing close. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the turrets and carved crosses.

Where to stand: Stand about 190 m north of the subject, on Avenida de Brasília (the phone icon on the map), and face south. In late light the tower catches warm, low light. Walk to Jardim da Torre de Belém if you also want the tower's full river-facing front.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the tower on one of the vertical lines, leaving open sky and water on the other side. Keep the horizon level. For a different version, go to Jardim da Torre de Belém and use the lawns and trees to frame the tower.

Settings tip: Tap on the tower to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little in late light so the sky keeps its colour and the stonework does not wash out. In dim light after sunset, use night mode and hold the phone steady against a railing or your body.

Best time: Late in the day, when low light gives the stonework a warm glow. The tower also looks best at high tide, when water surrounds the bastion.

2Jerónimos Monastery

The Jerónimos Monastery is a former monastery of the Order of Saint Jerome in Belém, close to the Tagus. It is one of the best-known examples of Manueline architecture in Lisbon, a richly carved style full of maritime detail. Building began on 6 January 1501 and took about a hundred years. The stone is a gold-coloured limestone, so the long south façade glows when the light is soft. Vasco da Gama's tomb is in the church nave, and the monastery has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983, together with the nearby Tower of Belém.

The classic photo is the whole façade in one frame from the far edge of the Jardim da Praça do Império. The phone icon on the map is there, looking towards the monastery. The gardens' central fountain or reflecting pools can sit in the foreground and give the picture depth. The building is very long, so leave a little space around it rather than cropping tight. If you want the carved detail, walk over to the Portal Sul for a close view of the south portal and its figures.

The site is very busy, so expect people in the foreground. Early morning or late afternoon gives softer light that suits the stone. Photos from inside the monastery are a separate matter: rules vary, so check them on arrival.

Where it is: The monastery is in the Belém district of western Lisbon, beside the Tagus. It stands just north of the Jardim da Praça do Império gardens and not far from the Tower of Belém.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the whole façade. If you cannot fit it all in, switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x). Use telephoto (2x-5x) for carved details and the tops of the towers.

Where to stand: Stand about 160 m south-east of the subject, in Jardim da Praça do Império (the phone icon on the map), and face north-west. From here the full length of the south façade fits in one frame, often with the central fountain or reflecting pools in front.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and line the horizontal ledges of the building up with them so the façade does not lean. Keep the phone level and use the water or fountain as a foreground. For a closer view of the carved portal and its figures, Portal Sul is a good second spot.

Settings tip: Tap the stone façade to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the gold limestone keeps its colour. When you zoom in on the carvings, hold the phone steady with both hands, braced against your body.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon, when the light is softer.

3São Jorge Castle

São Jorge Castle sits on a hill above the old centre, and from across the valley it reads as a long line of pale walls and towers above the rooftops of the Baixa. That is why it photographs so well from outside: the hill gives the castle a natural frame, and the old town fills the middle of the picture. People have lived on this hill since at least the 8th century BC, and the oldest fortifications date from the 2nd century BC. The Portuguese took the castle in 1147, and it was later dedicated to Saint George under King John I. It was also a royal palace, and Vasco da Gama was received here in 1498. Today it is a national monument and museum. For this shot, use the phone icon on the map: the Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara. It gives a side view across the valley to the castle hill. The tiled map on the terrace helps you name the buildings you are photographing. The castle's own walls and esplanade are the best place for views of the city, but they are behind a ticket. The viewpoints around the castle are free, so you can still get a strong picture without going in. Keep the castle on a third of the frame and let the rooftops fill the rest.

Where it is: The castle is on the hill above the Baixa and the Alfama rooftops, in the Santa Maria Maior district of central Lisbon.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the whole scene. Then try telephoto (2x-3x, or up to 5x if your phone has it) to pick out the castle walls across the valley.

Where to stand: Stand about 890 m west of the subject, on Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara (the phone icon on the map), and face east. From the terrace you see the castle hill from the side, with the Baixa and old town rooftops in the middle ground.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the castle on one of the upper thirds, with rooftops filling the lower part. Use the default lens (1x) for the full valley and 2x or more to isolate the walls. For a different angle, the Miradouro da Senhora do Monte gets you much closer to the castle, roughly level with its walls.

Settings tip: Tap the castle to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the pale walls and sky do not wash out. Hold the phone steady against the railing for the zoomed shots.

Best time: Check the light on the day. The sun is in the west in the afternoon, so it will be behind you when you look east towards the castle.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Lisbon?

The first three on this map are Belém Tower, Jerónimos Monastery and São Jorge Castle. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph Belém Tower?

Stand about 190 m north of Belém Tower, on Avenida de Brasília (the phone icon on the map), and face south. Start with the default lens (1x) for the tower and the river together.

When is the best time to photograph the Jerónimos Monastery?

Early morning or late afternoon, when the light is softer.

How do I get to São Jorge Castle?

From Rossio (Metro Green line) the castle hill is about 385 m to the east; from Martim Moniz (Metro Green line, tram 12E, tram 28E) it is about 308 m to the south-east.
