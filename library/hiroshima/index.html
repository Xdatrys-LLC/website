The 3 free photo spots

1Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum

The Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum sits in Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park in central Hiroshima. It documents the atomic bombing of the city in the Second World War. It opened in August 1955, and the main building was designed by Kenzō Tange. Its raised main block on pilotis (supporting legs) has a calm, simple outline that photographs well from a distance. Visitors have come in large numbers: between 1955 and 2005 about 53 million people visited, over a million a year on average. The museum was renovated in 1994 and again from 2014. The East Wing reopened in 2017, and the Main Hall reopened in April 2019 after seismic strengthening. The spot for a phone photo of the exterior is a separate place near the museum, across 平和大通り (Peace Boulevard), opposite the main building. From there you see the whole raised block face-on, with the park entrance in view. The distance suits the museum's quiet, solemn character, and everything fits in one frame without tilting your phone upwards. Two other good angles are inside the park. One is beyond the Cenotaph, where the park's axis leads the eye to the building. The other is beside the Gates of Peace, where the glass gates sit in the foreground with the museum behind. Interior photography is restricted, and rules can vary by exhibit, so treat this as an exterior shot. Keep a respectful distance and avoid blocking the entrance or the pavements.

Where it is: The museum is in Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park in central Hiroshima. The park entrance is in view from across Peace Boulevard.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the whole raised building fits in the frame. If traffic or railings crowd the foreground, try 2x to crop in on the building. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you are too close to fit the whole building in.

Where to stand: Stand on 平和大通り (across the boulevard, opposite the museum's main building). The raised main building on its pilotis, seen frontally from the far side of the road, with the park entrance in view. The spot across the road is separate: stand on 平和大通り (Peace Boulevard), on the far side of the road from the museum's main building. From there you see the raised main building on its pilotis straight on, with the park entrance in view.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the building up so the pilotis (the supporting legs) and the roofline run level with the grid. Leave a little space around the building so it looks calm and uncrowded. For a different angle, try inside the park from beyond the Cenotaph, or by the Gates of Peace with the glass gates in the foreground.

Settings tip: Tap the building to set focus, then lower the exposure slider slightly if the sky is bright, so the building keeps its detail. Take a photo at 1x first, then a second at 2x to compare.

Best time: Daylight is best, and a bright overcast sky or morning light usually shows the building's lines cleanly. Early in the day there is usually less traffic and fewer people in front of the building.

3Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park

Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park is a broad, quiet memorial park in the centre of Hiroshima. It remembers the people who died in the atomic bombing of 6 August 1945, and it was designed by the architect Kenzō Tange. Before the bombing, this was one of the city's busiest shopping and housing districts. Today it is open ground with memorials, museums and lecture halls, and more than a million people visit each year.

It photographs well because the park is made of several clear scenes rather than one view. The Children's Peace Monument shows a girl with outstretched arms and a folded paper crane above her, based on the story of Sadako Sasaki. Folded cranes sent from around the world are placed nearby. The Rest House is one of the buildings that survived the bomb, and its basement is kept nearly as it was. From the central cenotaph there is a long, deliberate view towards the A-Bomb Dome, which joined the UNESCO World Heritage List on 7 December 1996.

For photos, give the place room and take your time. Wide, calm frames suit it better than close-ups of people. Keep voices and movements respectful, and do not photograph visitors who are praying or grieving without asking. Each year a ceremony is held here on 6 August from 8:00 AM, so the park may be busy that morning.

Where it is: The park lies in the centre of Hiroshima, on open ground that was once the busy downtown district. The A-Bomb Dome is visible from the central cenotaph.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the cenotaph-to-Dome view, which looks natural and keeps the distance. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) for the open space of the park. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as the paper cranes or the Dome in the distance.

Where to stand: Stand about 35 m south of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north. Walking the short distance to the subject takes about 1 minute on foot.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line up the long axis of the park with the central vertical line so the view feels balanced. Keep the horizon level, especially with ultra-wide. Leave plenty of sky and open ground, and avoid cutting people off awkwardly at the frame edge.

Settings tip: Tap to focus on your main subject, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the sky is bright so the detail in the monuments is not washed out. Keep Live Photos off in quiet areas to avoid stray shutter sounds or distracting motion.

Best time: Early morning is usually calmer and has fewer people in the frame. The 6 August ceremony begins at 8:00 AM, so the park may be busy that morning.

4Hiroshima Station

Hiroshima Station is a major rail hub, and a good first or last photo of a trip. It is run by JR West, and every San'yō Shinkansen train stops here. The station has two main entrances: the Shinkansen entrance on the north side and the south entrance. Locals still often call the Shinkansen side the "north entrance". The station building was destroyed in the atomic bombing of 6 August 1945, and the building that followed was completed in December 1965. The newest chapter is a new station building called "minamoa", which opened on 24 March 2025. In August 2025 the Hiroden tram stop moved up to the second floor of that building, level with the JR concourse. That makes this a good place to catch old and new Hiroshima in one frame: the big modern façade, the busy entrances and the trams that run from the station. Look for strong straight lines in the building, people crossing the plaza, and the bustle around the entrances. It works as a quick travel-day snapshot or as a more careful architectural shot. Stand back far enough to fit the whole front in, then take a second, closer photo of a detail such as signs, glass or a tram. Parts of the south side have been rebuilt and are sometimes rerouted, so expect barriers and changing walkways.

Where it is: Hiroshima Station is in Minami-ku, Hiroshima, and is a major railway station. A pedestrian tunnel links the Shinkansen entrance to an underground plaza beneath the south entrance, close to Fukuya.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) lens to fit the whole front of the building in, then the default lens (1x) for a more natural look. If your phone has a telephoto (2x-5x), use it for details such as signs, rooflines or a tram.

Where to stand: Stand about 90 m north-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face south-east. The station is to the south-east of you, so face that way and keep the building ahead of you.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the edges of the building up with the vertical lines so they do not lean. Hold the phone level and leave a little space around the front. Add some people or a tram for scale. Then step closer and take a second shot of one detail.

Settings tip: Tap on the building to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if bright sky is washing out the front. If you take a photo after dark, switch on night mode and keep still for a few seconds.

Best time: Daytime gives clear, even detail on the building. At dusk, when the station lights come on, you get a warmer scene with glowing windows. Weekday rush hours are busy, so early morning is calmer.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Hiroshima?

The first three on this map are Peace Memorial Museum, Atomic Bomb Dome and Peace Memorial Park. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 15.

Where should I stand to photograph Peace Memorial Museum?

Stand on 平和大通り (across the boulevard, opposite the museum's main building). Start with the default lens (1x) so the whole raised building fits in the frame.

When is the best time to photograph Peace Memorial Park?

Early morning is usually calmer and has fewer people in the frame. The 6 August ceremony begins at 8:00 AM, so the park may be busy that morning.

How do I get to Hiroshima Station?

The spot is the station itself, so JR trains, the Shinkansen and Hiroden trams all bring you straight to it, and buses also depart from the Shinkansen and south entrances.
