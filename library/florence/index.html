This map shows 20 of the most-searched sights in Florence as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Duomo, Uffizi, Ponte Vecchio, Santa Maria Novella and Piazzale Michelangelo lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. The 3 free photo spots

1Florence Cathedral (the Duomo)

Florence Cathedral, formally Santa Maria del Fiore, is the great marble church whose great dome rises above the city. Work began in 1296 and the dome, engineered by Filippo Brunelleschi, was finished in 1436. It is still the largest masonry dome ever built, and it went up without a temporary wooden frame to support it. The walls are faced in bands of white, green and pink marble, and the ornate west front is a 19th-century addition, completed in 1887. The cathedral stands with the Baptistery and Giotto's Campanile in Piazza del Duomo, part of the UNESCO-listed historic centre.

Up close the building is too big to fit into one frame. The best way to see the whole dome is from across the Arno, so this map sends you to the phone icon on the map, on Piazzale Michelangelo. From the left side of the terrace you get a wide, raised view of the dome rising above the terracotta rooftops, with the river and its bridges in the same shot. The cathedral is the focus, but the city around it makes the picture. It is a free viewpoint and easy to reach, but it fills up fast at sunset, so arrive early and claim a spot at the railing. If you want a higher, clearer view, San Miniato al Monte is a steep walk uphill from the piazzale.

Where it is: The cathedral stands in Piazza del Duomo in the historic centre of Florence. The viewpoint, Piazzale Michelangelo, is on the hill across the Arno to the south-east.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the whole scene, then try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the dome closer. The cathedral is over a kilometre away, so it looks small at 1x. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want a big sweep of sky and rooftops, because the dome will become very small.

Where to stand: Stand about 1.2 km south-east of the subject, in Piazzale Michelangelo (the phone icon on the map), and face north-west. At the left side of the terrace. The cathedral lies to the north-west, and from here you can see the dome above the rooftops, with the Arno and its bridges in the same frame.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the dome on one of the upper intersections, with the river along the lower third. Keep the horizon level. Zoom in a little (2x-3x) so the dome stands out from the rooftops, but leave the river in view. If the terrace is crowded, wait for a gap or hold the phone slightly above the heads in front of you. San Miniato al Monte, on the higher hill, is a good alternative viewpoint with fewer obstructions.

Settings tip: Tap the dome to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. At dusk, switch on night mode and rest your elbows on the wall to keep the phone steady. Panorama mode works well for the full sweep of the skyline if you pan slowly.

Best time: Sunrise or sunset. Arrive early at sunset, as the terrace fills quickly.

2Uffizi

The Uffizi is Florence's great Renaissance art museum, but for a phone photographer the real picture is outside, in the long courtyard in front of it. Two tall, matching wings run down either side of a narrow open square towards the River Arno. At the far end a Doric screen frames the view without blocking it, and the Ponte Vecchio shows in the distance between the arms of the colonnade.

Giorgio Vasari began the building in 1560 for Cosimo I de' Medici, as offices for the city's administration (uffizi means 'offices'). It was finished in 1581. Vasari stressed the perspective by giving both facades continuous roof cornices and unbroken cornices between the storeys, and by adding three long steps along the fronts. That is why the lines pull your eye so strongly towards the river. The niches between the columns were filled with sculptures of famous artists in the 19th century, which give you good detail shots.

The best viewpoint is at the north end of the courtyard, so walk to that end and face south towards the Arno. Keep yourself in the middle so both wings look symmetrical. The square gets very busy, so early morning before the museum opens, or after dark, gives you the clearest view. If you want a wider view of the river, with the Uffizi sitting alongside the Ponte Vecchio and the Palazzo Vecchio tower, Ponte alle Grazie is a good second choice.

Where it is: The Uffizi is in the Historic Centre of Florence, right next to the Piazza della Signoria, with its courtyard opening onto the River Arno.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) so both wings of the colonnade fit in and their lines run towards the river. Then switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, less stretched version. Use telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) to pull the Ponte Vecchio closer.

Where to stand: Stand in Piazzale degli Uffizi, right in front of the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Walk to the north end of the courtyard on Piazzale degli Uffizi and face south towards the Arno. From there the colonnaded courtyard runs away from you towards the river, with the Ponte Vecchio visible in the distance between the arms of the colonnade.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the centre line on the middle of the courtyard so the two sides mirror each other. Hold the phone level, because tilting it makes the walls lean. For a different shot, take a close-up of the statues in the niches between the columns. If you want a wider view of the river, with the Uffizi, the Ponte Vecchio and the Palazzo Vecchio tower together, go to Ponte alle Grazie.

Settings tip: Tap on the far end of the courtyard to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky stays blue and the stone keeps its colour. After dark, use night mode and hold the phone steady against your body or a wall.

Best time: Early morning before the museum opens, or at night, when the courtyard is quietest. At other times it is very busy with visitors.

12Giotto's Campanile

Giotto's Campanile is the slender bell tower standing beside Florence Cathedral, with the Baptistery close by. It photographs well because the walls are covered in patterned marble: white from Carrara, green from Prato and red from Siena. That gives a phone camera plenty of colour and texture even on a grey day. The tower is 84.7 metres tall and square, with sides of 14.45 metres. The first stone was laid on 19 July 1334, when Giotto was in charge of the works. He died in 1337, having finished only the lowest level, and the tower was completed in 1359. The spire he planned, which would have taken it to about 122 metres, was never built. Look at the lowest level for the rows of hexagonal relief panels. The ones you see are copies, as the originals are now in the cathedral museum. The phone icon on the map is on the square, just north-west of the tower. From the square you can fit the cathedral façade, the tower and the Baptistery into one picture. That is the classic view, and it is best early in the morning, before the square fills up. If you want a different angle, try the corner of Via dei Servi for a three-quarter view with the dome, or the entrance to Piazza San Giovanni.

Where it is: In the historic centre of Florence, on Piazza del Duomo, standing next to the cathedral and close to the Baptistery of St. John.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to get the façade, the tower and the Baptistery in one frame. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if the square is crowded and you have to stand close. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the marble patterns and the relief panels.

Where to stand: Stand about 25 m north-west of the subject, in Piazza del Duomo (the phone icon on the map), and face south-east. In the open part of the square facing the cathedral. From there the marble façade, Giotto's Campanile beside it and the Baptistery all fit into one picture. The entrance to Piazza San Giovanni, close by, shows how the tower, cathedral and dome sit together.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and hold the phone upright, not tilted back, so the tower's edges stay straight. Step back as far as the square allows, then use the default lens (1x) to take in the full height. For a different angle, the corner of Via dei Servi gives a three-quarter view with the dome, the Campanile and the cathedral together. Check it on the map before you go.

Settings tip: Tap on the bright marble to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the white stone keeps its detail. For a long row of buildings, panorama mode also works, if you keep the phone level and hold it steady.

Best time: Early morning, when the square is quieter and the light is soft and golden.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Florence?

The first three on this map are Duomo, Uffizi and Ponte Vecchio. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph the Duomo?

Stand about 1.2 km south-east of the Duomo, in Piazzale Michelangelo (the phone icon on the map), and face north-west. Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the whole scene, then try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the dome closer.

When is the best time to photograph the Uffizi?

Early morning before the museum opens, or at night, when the courtyard is quietest. At other times it is very busy with visitors.

How do I get to Giotto's Campanile?

Florence has no metro, and the centre is best covered on foot.
