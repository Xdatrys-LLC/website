The 3 free photo spots

1Chapel Bridge

Chapel Bridge is a covered wooden footbridge that crosses the river Reuss at an angle in the middle of Lucerne, and it is the city's best-known sight. It photographs well because everything comes together in one frame: the long, steep timber roof, the stone water tower standing in the river, and the water below. The bridge was first built around 1365 as part of the town's defences. It is now about 205 metres long, and it is described as the oldest wooden covered bridge in Europe. The octagonal Wasserturm beside it is about 34.5 metres tall and was once used as a prison and a treasury. A fire on 18 August 1993 destroyed much of the bridge and two thirds of its interior paintings. It was rebuilt and reopened on 14 April 1994. Inside the roof are painted panels from the 17th century, showing scenes from Lucerne's history, and some of them have been restored. Switch on the gridlines so the long roofline sits level. Once you have the wide view, walk onto the bridge and photograph the painted panels and the timber beams overhead. Wipe your lens first, as river air and pockets leave smears.

Where it is: In the centre of Lucerne, where the bridge crosses the river Reuss at an angle and links the old town on the right bank with the new town on the left bank. It is named after the nearby St. Peter's Chapel.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the bridge, the tower and some river into one frame, then use the default lens (1x) for a more natural look. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the tower or the roof details.

Where to stand: Stand on Rathaussteg (smaller footbridge downstream (west) of the Chapel Bridge). A wide shot back along the Reuss toward the covered bridge and the octagonal Water Tower, with the curly railings of the footbridge framing the scene. The bridge lies to the north-east, and from here you can see its long roofline and the tower together.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the bridge's roof with a horizontal line so it does not look tilted. Leave a little water in the foreground, and let the tower sit on one side rather than in the centre. Inside the bridge, use the default lens (1x) and aim along the walkway so the painted panels and beams lead the eye.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the sky or the roof to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the sky keeps some colour. Inside the covered part, tap on a painting and lift the exposure slightly. Hold the phone with both hands, as it is darker under the roof.

Best time: Early in the morning is likely to be quieter on the bridge. In the evening, the softer light is kind to the timber, and night mode can help once it gets dark.

2Lion Monument

The Lion Monument (Löwendenkmal) is a huge lion carved straight into a sandstone cliff face, with a quiet pond at its foot. It was designed by Bertel Thorvaldsen and cut in 1820-21 by Lukas Ahorn. It remembers the Swiss Guards killed in Paris in 1792 during the French Revolution. The relief is about ten metres long and six metres high. The lion is wounded by a spear and rests a paw on a shield, with a second shield of the Swiss coat of arms beside it. Mark Twain wrote about the lion mirrored in the pond among the water-lilies, and that reflection is still the picture most people come for.

It works well on a phone because everything sits in one frame: the carved lion, the still water, the ivy-like vines and the trees around it. The best view is from the flat platform on the pond side of the park, straight across from the lion's head. Stepping a little to one side often lets you fit the lion, its reflection and the greenery together without a dead-centre, postcard look. Wait a moment for ripples to settle, as the reflection is much stronger on calm water. If you want a second picture, try the raised part of the park, which gives you the lion from above and to the side with the pond and trees around it. The park has had reconstruction work, so it is worth checking for closures.

Where it is: The monument is in a small park on Denkmalstrasse in Lucerne, set against a rock cliff that was once a sandstone quarry.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to get the whole lion and its reflection. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are too close to fit the carving and the pond, and keep the lion away from the very edge of the frame. Use telephoto (2x or more) for a close shot of the lion's head and spear.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. From here you see the full carved lion in its rock niche with the rectangular pond in front and its reflection in the water. For a different picture, try the raised part of the park, which shows the lion from above and to the side with the pond and trees around it.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the water line on the lower third, so the lion fills the top and its reflection fills the bottom. Step a little to one side to fit the lion, the reflection and the greenery into one frame. Hold the phone low and level so the cliff doesn't lean.

Settings tip: Tap on the lion's face to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so the pale stone doesn't wash out. Wait for the water to go still before you shoot.

Best time: Morning or an overcast day, when the light on the lion is even. Going early or at a quieter time of year also means fewer people on the platform.

8Glacier Garden of Lucerne

The Glacier Garden of Lucerne is an attraction ranked number 8 on the Lucerne map. It suits a phone camera because you will likely find small details close by, so you don't need a big viewpoint to get a good picture. Its ranking makes it a good slower stop between the busier sights.

Walk a little way in before you start. Look for anything with a sense of depth, such as a line leading away from you. Put a person or a bag in the foreground to give a feeling of scale.

Take a few wide shots to show the whole setting, then a few close ones of interesting details. Use anything interesting nearby as foreground. Keep your lens clean, because handling and outdoor air can leave smudges. Take your time, as the best pictures here often come from looking at small things. Check the official site for current opening information before you go.

Where it is: The Glacier Garden is on the Lucerne map. Use the map to see where the Glacier Garden sits among the other spots.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for most shots. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) for a view that takes in a whole area. Use telephoto/zoom (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for close details.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and place a strong line, such as an edge or a path if you find one, along one of the lines to lead the eye into the picture. Keep the phone level, and include something in the foreground for depth. For a close-up, tap the detail to focus and lower the exposure slider slightly so the colours stay rich.

Settings tip: Tap to focus on the main subject, then drag the exposure slider down a little if bright sky is washing out the scene. Portrait mode can work for a person standing in front of the scene.

Best time: Pick a time when the light is soft, such as a bright overcast day or a calm hour outside the middle of the day, which gives gentler shadows and more even detail.
