The 3 free photo spots

2St. Mary's Church

St. Mary's Church is the huge brick church that dominates central Gdańsk. It was completed in 1502 in the Brick Gothic style and is counted among the largest brick churches in the world. Locals call it the Crown of Gdańsk. The building is about 105 metres long, so from close by it is too big to fit in one frame. That is why the street approach works so well. The best classic view is along ulica Mariacka, a cobbled street that runs from the church down towards the Motława river. Looking along it towards the church, the dark brick walls close off the end of the street, with house fronts and porches on either side acting as a natural frame. The church has a long history. Building began on the present church in 1379, and the Baroque Royal Chapel next to it was finished in 1681. For a phone photo, keep the church in the middle of the frame and let the street lead the eye towards it. Hold the phone level, because tilting it upwards makes the tower lean. Wait a moment for people to pass, or include a few of them to show the scale of the street. The tower viewing terrace is a separate, ticketed experience and not part of this ground-level view, so check the official site before you go if you want to go up.

Where it is: In central Gdańsk, at the head of ulica Mariacka, which runs down towards the Motława river.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to get the street and the church together. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the house fronts and the tower. Use 2x or more if your phone has a telephoto lens, for a tighter shot of the church closing off the street.

Where to stand: Stand on ulica Mariacka (looking back up the street from the Motława end, toward the church). The brick church closes the end of the cobbled street, framed by the historic house fronts and porches on either side. The brick church closes the end of the cobbled street, framed by the historic house fronts and porches on either side.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the street in the centre, so the church sits at the vanishing point and the house fronts balance left and right. Keep the phone upright and level so the verticals stay straight. For a different angle, try ulica Piwna, where narrow facades lead the eye towards the tower.

Settings tip: Tap on the church to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the brick keeps its colour. If people are walking through, a Live Photo lets you pick the frame with the fewest passers-by.

Best time: Early in the morning is usually calmer on the street. In the afternoon the church may be backlit, so check the screen and adjust the exposure.

6Artus Court

Artus Court is the grand merchants' meeting house on Długi Targ (the Long Market), in the middle of Gdańsk's historic centre. It was once the place where wealthy traders and visiting foreigners gathered in the evenings, and today it is a branch of the Gdańsk History Museum. It photographs well because the tall, richly decorated front rises straight out of the pedestrian square, and Neptune's Fountain stands just in front of it, which gives you a natural foreground. The façade was rebuilt in 1617 by Abraham van den Blocke in the style of Dutch Mannerism. It carries statues of antique heroes, allegories of strength and justice, and a figure of Fortuna on the gable. Inside is a Gothic hall with an 11-metre tiled stove made in the 1540s. The building was badly damaged in 1945 and rebuilt after the war. For the classic view, stand on the square with the fountain in front of you and the façade rising behind it. Then step closer and use zoom to pick out the statues and portal medallions. The square is busy in peak season, so a patient pause between groups helps. If you plan to go inside, check the official site before you go.

Where it is: Artus Court stands at Długi Targ 44, in the centre of Gdańsk, on the Long Market. Neptune's Fountain is on the same square, directly in front of it.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) for the classic view of fountain and façade together. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if the square is crowded and you cannot step back. Use the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for the statues and gable details.

Where to stand: Stand in Długi Targ (in front of the court, with Neptune's Fountain in the foreground). The classic postcard view: Neptune's Fountain in the foreground with the ornate Artus Court facade rising across the Long Market. Face north-west so Neptune's Fountain sits in the foreground and the ornate Artus Court façade rises across the Long Market behind it.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the fountain on the lower third, with the façade filling the upper part. Keep your phone level so the building's vertical lines stay straight. Then zoom in for a second shot of the statues and the portal. This is the only viewpoint we recommend for the exterior.

Settings tip: Tap on the façade to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky does not wash out and the carved detail stays clear. In the evening, try night mode and hold the phone steady against your body or a solid surface.

Best time: Late afternoon or evening, for golden-hour light and the lit-up façade.

9Main Town Hall

Gdansk's Main Town Hall closes the far end of the Long Market, and its slim tower is the second-highest in the Main City after St Mary's Church. The building is Gothic-Renaissance in style. The oldest fragments date from 1327 to 1336, and the tower went up between 1486 and 1488. After a fire in 1556 the building was rebuilt with Renaissance touches by Dutch architects, and in 1561 a gilded weathervane figure of King Sigismund II Augustus was set on top. The town hall was badly damaged in the Second World War, including the loss of the top of the tower, and was largely rebuilt by 1952. Today it houses the city's history museum.

It photographs well because of the setting. From the Neptune Fountain end of the Long Market you get the tower at the end of the square, the fountain close by and the colourful tenement facades down both sides. That gives you a foreground, a middle and a clear focal point in one frame. Go early if you can. Café parasols and outdoor seating can clutter the scene in summer, and the square is far emptier at sunrise. Two other angles are worth a look: ulica Długa, with the facade at the end of a row of townhouses, and ulica Chlebnicka, which frames the town hall between its buildings. You do not need to climb the tower for any of these ground-level shots.

Where it is: In the Main City (Główne Miasto) part of Gdansk's old town, where ulica Długa meets the Long Market (Długi Targ). It stands on the Royal Route, a short walk from the Neptune Fountain.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the classic view down the square. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the fountain and the facades large in the foreground. A 2x or 3x telephoto lens, if your phone has one, isolates the tower and weathervane.

Where to stand: Stand in Długi Targ (from the Neptune Fountain end of the square, facing the town hall). The town hall tower closing the far end of the Long Market, with the Neptune Fountain and the colourful tenement facades in the foreground. At the Neptune Fountain end of the square, facing the town hall. From here the tower closes the far end of the Long Market, with the fountain and the colourful tenement facades in the foreground. Alternatives are ulica Długa, for the facade at the end of the townhouse row, and ulica Chlebnicka, where the buildings frame the town hall. For Chlebnicka you may need to walk the street to find the framing.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the tower on a vertical line so it stands straight. Keep the fountain low in the frame as a foreground. Hold the phone level, because tilting it up makes the buildings lean inwards. If people or parasols clutter the frame, wait a moment or move a few steps sideways.

Settings tip: Tap on the tower to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour and the facades are not washed out. Take a few frames in a row, as people keep walking through the shot.

Best time: Sunrise, before the visitors and outdoor seating arrive. After rain, ulica Długa gives reflections on the wet paving.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Gdansk?

The first three on this map are Neptune's Fountain, St. Mary's Church and WWII Museum. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 17.

Where should I stand to photograph St. Mary's Church?

Stand on ulica Mariacka (looking back up the street from the Motława end, toward the church). Start with the default lens (1x) to get the street and the church together.

When is the best time to photograph Artus Court?

Late afternoon or evening, for golden-hour light and the lit-up façade.

How do I get to Main Town Hall?

Head for the Main City, the old town centre of Gdansk, and finish the journey on foot, since ulica Długa is pedestrianised all year.
