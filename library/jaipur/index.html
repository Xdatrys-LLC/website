The 3 free photo spots

1Amber Fort

Amber Fort (also spelt Amer) sits high on a hillside above Maota Lake, and the lake is what makes the classic photo. The fort's ramparts, gates and cobbled paths climb the slope, and on a calm morning the whole thing is mirrored in the water below. Guides call that reflection the classic Amber image.

The palace is built of red sandstone and marble and laid out on four levels, each with its own courtyard. It was begun by Raja Man Singh and expanded by Jai Singh I in the early 1600s. Later rulers kept adding to it until the Kachwaha capital moved to Jaipur in 1727. The fort is part of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan, which became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013.

For a phone photo, the best spot is the eastern shore of the lake, below the fort, where you can fit the fort, the slope and the water into one frame. Arrive early, while the lake is still, and take a few frames. Even a light breeze breaks up the reflection. If you want a second angle, the viewpoints at Jaigarh Fort and along the Great Wall of Amer look back over the fort and the valley. Once inside the fort, look for the carved gates and latticed windows, which suit close-up shots.

Amer is about 11 km from Jaipur city, so allow time for the journey and for the climb up to the fort itself.

Where it is: Amber Fort is in the town of Amer, about 11 km from central Jaipur, on a hill that juts into Maota Lake. The lake lies directly below the fort.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the fort and its reflection together. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are close to the water and want more of the shoreline. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the ramparts and gates.

Where to stand: Stand in Maota Lake (eastern shore of the lake, below the fort). The fort rising above the still lake, with its reflection in the water in calm morning light. From here the fort rises above the still lake with its reflection in the water.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the water's edge on a lower line, so the fort and its reflection fill the frame evenly and the horizon stays level. Keep the whole fort in view in one shot, then take a second, tighter shot with the telephoto on the ramparts. If you have time, a guide also suggests the road approaching the fort as another spot for the reflection. Jaigarh Fort and the Great Wall of Amer give high views back over the fort and valley.

Settings tip: Tap the fort to focus, then slide the exposure down slightly if the sky looks too bright. Hold your phone steady for a moment before each shot, and take several frames in case ripples spoil one. A Live Photo can help you pick the calmest moment.

Best time: Early morning, when the water is still. One guide says the winter months, November to February, are best for the reflection.

2Hawa Mahal

The Hawa Mahal, or Palace of Winds, is a five-storey front of red and pink sandstone covered in small carved windows. Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh had it built in 1799, and it has 953 of these windows (called jharokhas). They were designed so that royal ladies could watch street life and festivals without being seen, and they also let cool air move through the building. It rises to about 15 metres, and the top three floors are only one room wide, so the whole thing looks like a pink honeycomb standing above the road. The most famous side is actually the back of the palace, which is part of why it photographs so well from the street: the pattern is bold, repeated and easy to read on a small screen. The best free shot is from the pavement at Badi Chaupar, on the opposite side of the road from the facade. From there you can fit the full front in one frame, with autos and pedestrians in the foreground to give a sense of scale. It is a busy junction, so wait for a gap in the traffic and keep to the pavement. If you want a higher, more head-on view, there is Wind View Cafe, which offers an elevated view, and a terrace by Rang Bihari Temple across the road. Cafe access can change and the temple stair entrance is hard to find, so confirm both on the map before you rely on them. Go early if you can: the facade faces east, so morning light falls directly on it.

Where it is: The Hawa Mahal stands on the edge of the City Palace complex in the old Pink City of Jaipur. The best street-level view is from the Badi Chaupar crossroads, just to its south.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) from the pavement. If traffic or people force you to stand close, switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit all five floors in. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for close-ups of the windows and carving.

Where to stand: Stand in Badi Chaupar (pavement directly opposite the facade, northeast corner of the crossroads). The full honeycomb facade rising above the street, with autos and pedestrians in the foreground. The palace is to the north. From here you see the whole honeycomb front rising above the street, with autos and pedestrians in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and keep the vertical lines straight by holding the phone level. Wait for a gap in traffic so the whole front fits in one frame, and let a few people or autos into the bottom of the picture for scale. For a higher, more head-on view, Wind View Cafe or the terrace by Rang Bihari Temple are alternatives, but check access first.

Settings tip: Tap the facade to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the pink stone keeps its colour against the bright sky. If your phone offers Live Photos or motion, a few seconds of passing traffic can make a lively extra shot.

Best time: Before 8:00 AM, when the streets are quieter and the east-facing facade is lit directly.

15Badi Chaupar

Badi Chaupar, the name means "Large Square", is the big open crossroads in the busy market part of Jaipur. It is also known as Manak Chowk. It is one of the two major squares built by the King of Amer, and Tripolia Bazar, the city's central market, runs between it and its smaller sister, Chhoti Chaupar. The area is still a hub for artisans, jewellers and craftsmen, so there is plenty of street life.

For a phone photo, the draw is the view across the square. From the pavement opposite the Hawa Mahal you see the palace's honeycomb facade rising above the buses, rickshaws and traffic. Guides call this the classic free street-level view. The facade tends to draw the eye more than the square itself, so decide which you want before you raise the phone.

Traffic and overhead wires can get in the way. Be patient and wait for a gap in the vehicles, and keep to the pavement while you do. Early morning, around 8-9am, is the calmest time and the best light on the facade, as the Hawa Mahal closes after sunset. If you want a higher angle, the rooftop Wind View Cafe looks across the square at the full five-storey facade. Photography there may be for customers only and the rules can change, so ask before you go up.

Where it is: Badi Chaupar is in the old market area of Jaipur, at one end of Tripolia Bazar, the city's central market. It sits on the crossroads facing the Hawa Mahal, with Chhoti Chaupar as the other major square nearby.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the Hawa Mahal facade above the traffic. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the square and the street in the frame. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the facade's windows and leave out wires.

Where to stand: Stand in Badi Chaupar (pavement opposite the Hawa Mahal facade, facing north-west). The open crossroads square with the Hawa Mahal's honeycomb facade rising above the traffic, buses and rickshaws in the foreground. On the pavement opposite the Hawa Mahal facade, and face north-west. The middle of the square lies a little to the north.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and keep the facade straight against the vertical lines. Leave the buses and rickshaws in the foreground to give a sense of scale. Wait for a gap in the traffic before you press the shutter. For a higher view across the square, Wind View Cafe is an alternative, but it shows the palace more than the square.

Settings tip: Tap the facade to set focus, then pull the exposure slider down a little so the sky does not wash out. In a busy scene, try Live Photos or motion so you can pick the best frame from the moving traffic.

Best time: Early morning, around 8-9am, before the square gets crowded and while the facade has good light.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Jaipur?

The first three on this map are Amber Fort, Hawa Mahal and Jal Mahal. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 16.

Where should I stand to photograph Amber Fort?

Stand in Maota Lake (eastern shore of the lake, below the fort). Start with the default lens (1x) for the fort and its reflection together.

When is the best time to photograph the Hawa Mahal?

Before 8:00 AM, when the streets are quieter and the east-facing facade is lit directly.

How do I get to Badi Chaupar?

No station or tram stop is listed for this spot, so start from Tripolia Bazar, Jaipur's central market, and head for Badi Chaupar, one of the two squares that mark the bazaar.
