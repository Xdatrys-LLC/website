The 3 free photo spots

1Suomenlinna

Suomenlinna is a sea fortress spread across eight islands, about four kilometres south-east of Helsinki city centre. Building began in 1748 under the Swedish Crown, and it became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991. Today it is as much a picnic spot as a monument, with pastel buildings, grassy ramparts and stone walls meeting open water. That mix is why it photographs so well on a phone.

For the first photo, go to Kuninkaanportti (the King's Gate), on the southern tip of Kustaanmiekka. It is often described as the island's main symbol. From the seaward side, in front of the gate, you can frame the stone gateway against the sea, with the Latin inscription and coat of arms above the archway. It can be quieter here than you might expect for such a well-known view.

If you have time, two other views are worth adding. The dry dock looks down into its stone-walled basin, with the old wooden ship hull and ochre storehouses around it. The Great Courtyard has the pastel barracks and the statue of Augustin Ehrensvärd in the foreground.

The gate is a walk from the ferry, so allow time. Winter snow is not cleared on the outer fortifications, so wear sensible shoes in cold months. Drones are generally restricted on the island, and it is wise to keep to marked paths on the ramparts.

Where it is: Suomenlinna is a district of Helsinki on a group of islands in the sea, about four kilometres south-east of the city centre. Kuninkaanportti stands on the southern tip of Kustaanmiekka, the island with the most fortifications.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the gate against the sea. Switch to the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the inscription and coat of arms above the archway. Use ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want a lot of sea and sky around the gate.

Where to stand: Stand about 500 m south-east of the subject, at Kuninkaanportti (the phone icon on the map), and face north-west. On the seaward side in front of the gate on the southern tip of Kustaanmiekka. From here the stone gateway sits against the sea, with the Latin inscription and coat of arms visible above the arch. The main fortress marker on the map lies to the north-west.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the archway up with the vertical lines so the stonework stands straight. Keep the whole arch and the coat of arms in frame, with the sea behind. If you want an alternative afterwards, the dry dock and the Great Courtyard each make a good second picture.

Settings tip: Tap on the stonework to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sea or sky looks washed out. Take a few frames, as walkers may pass through the arch.

Best time: Sunset is recommended. In summer, morning light is said to light up the inscription.

2Temppeliaukio Church (Rock Church)

Temppeliaukio Church, also called the Rock Church or Church of the Rock, sits in the Töölö district and is built straight into the granite bedrock of Temppeliaukio ("Temple Square"). Timo and Tuomo Suomalainen designed it, and it was completed in 1969. Rather than putting a building on top of the rocky hill, they dug the main hall out of the rock and left much of the stone showing, so the raw rock forms the interior walls. A copper-lined dome sits above, ringed by skylights that let daylight in. That mix of granite, copper and light is why it photographs so well, both as a rough rocky outcrop from outside and as a calm, round hall inside. The church seats about 750 people. It is still a working Lutheran parish church, used for services, weddings and concerts, and it is also one of Helsinki's principal tourist attractions, with a record 938,000 visitors in 2019. If you go inside, remember people are there to worship or listen to music, so keep your phone quiet, skip flash and be considerate when taking pictures of other visitors.

Where it is: In the Töölö district of central Helsinki, on the rocky hill of Temppeliaukio Square, in the Etu-Töölö area.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the dome and the rock walls inside, and for the whole rocky hill from outside. Use the default lens (1x) for natural-looking views, and the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as the copper and the texture of the granite.

Where to stand: Stand about 80 m south-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east. From here you can photograph the rocky setting and the church's surroundings in context, then walk about 1 minute north-east to the church itself.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and keep the horizon level. Outside, include some of the bare rock in the foreground to show how the church grows out of the hill. Inside, stand near the centre if you can and point the ultra-wide (0.5x) upwards so the ring of skylights and the dome form a circle. Hold the phone steady with both hands and keep people out of the edges of the frame.

Settings tip: Inside, the skylights can be much brighter than the rock walls. Tap on the rock to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the bright ring keeps its detail. Portrait mode is not needed here. Panorama works for the curve of the rock wall if you turn slowly.

Best time: Bright daylight lights the interior through the skylights, so the middle of the day is usually a good choice for inside shots. For outside shots, softer morning or late-afternoon light is kinder to the rock. Early in the day is often quieter, but check opening times first.

3Helsinki Cathedral and Senate Square

Helsinki Cathedral stands at Senate Square in Kruununhaka, and it is a major landmark of the city. It has a tall green dome with four smaller domes around it, and a pale neoclassical body with columns and pediments on each arm. Carl Ludvig Engel designed it as the high point of his Senate Square layout, with the smaller buildings around the square also his work. Building began in 1830 and the church was inaugurated on 15 February 1852. It was first a tribute to Emperor Nicholas I of Russia, and it kept the name St Nicholas's Church until 1917. Although it is often called the 'White Cathedral', it is actually painted in three very similar light greys. Larger-than-life zinc statues of the Twelve Apostles stand along the roofline, and they make good close-up subjects. Walk closer for details of the columns and statues. Look left and right of the steps: the left building is the bell tower and the right one is a chapel, and including both gives a balanced, symmetrical picture. Expect other visitors in your frame, especially in summer, so take a few shots and pick the clearest.

Where it is: In the Kruununhaka neighbourhood in the centre of Helsinki, at Senate Square.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the whole cathedral and its steps. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you stand closer and want the dome and steps in one frame. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the dome, the colonnade and the apostle statues on the roofline.

Where to stand: Stand about 120 m south of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and line up the central axis of the building with the middle of the screen, so the symmetry is clear and the columns stay upright. Include the bell tower on the left and the chapel on the right to frame the steps. For a closer picture, zoom to 2x-5x and aim at the apostle statues against the sky.

Settings tip: Tap on the pale façade to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the light grey walls keep their detail and the sky does not turn white. Keep the phone level to avoid leaning verticals.

Best time: Daytime, when the façade is evenly lit, gives clean detail. Early morning is usually quieter on the square. In the evening, try night mode if the building is lit, and hold the phone steady against something solid.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Helsinki?

The first three on this map are Suomenlinna, Rock Church and Helsinki Cathedral. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 18.

Where should I stand to photograph Suomenlinna?

Stand about 500 m south-east of Suomenlinna, at Kuninkaanportti (the phone icon on the map), and face north-west. Start with the default lens (1x) for the gate against the sea.

When is the best time to photograph the Rock Church?

Bright daylight lights the interior through the skylights, so the middle of the day is usually a good choice for inside shots. For outside shots, softer morning or late-afternoon light is kinder to the rock.

How do I get to Helsinki Cathedral?

No Underground or tram stop is listed for this spot, so head for Kruununhaka in the centre of Helsinki, by taxi or hired scooter if you are coming from further away.
