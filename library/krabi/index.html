This map shows 6 of the most-searched sights in Krabi as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Railay Beach, Ao Nang Beach, Tiger Cave Temple, Wat Kaew Korawaram and Phra Nang Beach lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. The 3 free photo spots

1Railay Beach

Railay is a small peninsula between Krabi town and Ao Nang, cut off from the mainland by high limestone cliffs, so you can only reach it by boat. For photographs, the best-known spot is Ao Phra Nang. Pale sand and calm water sit right up against tall cliffs, with a small island, Ko Rang Nok, just offshore and longtail boats moving about nearby. Guides often call it the most photographed beach in the Railay area, and it is easy to see why: the cliffs, sand, sea and island give you several layers in one frame.

Railay's cliffs draw rock climbers from around the world, and the area has been a popular beach destination in Krabi since tourism began there in the 1970s. Ao Phra Nang is one of its four main areas, alongside West Railay, East Railay and Ton Sai.

Stand mid-beach on the sand and face the cliffs and the islet. Keep the horizon level using your gridlines, and let a longtail boat or a person on the sand give the scene some scale. Go early if you can: the light reaches the cliffs before about 9 AM and the beach is quieter. If you are up for a scramble, a route through boulders at the southern end leads to a gap in the cliff with a shallow pool and the sea framed between rock walls.

Where it is: Ao Phra Nang is one of the four main areas of Railay, in Krabi province between Krabi town and Ao Nang. It is reached by a trail from East Railay.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the cliffs, sand and sea together. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural view. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out Ko Rang Nok or a longtail boat.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (mid-beach on the sand, facing the limestone cliffs and the islet offshore). White sand and water framed by towering limestone cliffs, with a small island offshore (Ko Rang Nok) and longtail boats nearby. From there you face the limestone cliffs and the islet offshore, with white sand and water in front and longtail boats nearby.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the horizon on a lower line so the cliffs fill the upper part of the frame. Put a boat or a person in the foreground or midground for scale. For a quieter alternative to the beach-level shot, the Bat Cave opening frames Phra Nang Beach and Ko Rang Nok, and Railay Viewpoint gives a wide panorama. Both involve steep, slippery climbs that guides say become dangerous after rain, so wear proper footwear.

Settings tip: Tap on the cliffs to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the bright sand does not turn the sky and sea pale. Try Live Photos or a short burst when boats pass, so you can pick the best moment.

Best time: Before 9 AM, when the light hits the cliffs and the crowds are thin.

2Ao Nang

Ao Nang is a resort town on the Krabi coast, and this spot is about seeing the whole of it at once. From the Ao Nang Viewpoint you look down on the Ao Nang area, with limestone mountains behind and the sea and beaches stacked in layers below. That is an overview you cannot get from the sand. The town is one of the ten subdistricts of Mueang Krabi District. It consists chiefly of a main street of restaurants, pubs and shops aimed at tourists, and long-tail boats run from the main beach to other beaches and nearby islands. You may spot a few of those boats from above. The viewpoint is a roadside lookout on the road between Ao Nang and Nopparat Thara, about 500 m before Nopparat Thara, and it has roadside parking. One guide calls it the best viewpoint in the Ao Nang area, and it is described as free to visit. Sunset, roughly 5:30 to 6:30 p.m., is the suggested time. For the classic close-up frame, Ao Nang Beach has the sand and rocks in the foreground with the limestone mountain rising behind. Nopparat Thara Beach is a quieter alternative, with a sand bridge and small island at low tide. Check access to the viewpoint locally before you set out.

Where it is: Ao Nang is in Mueang Krabi District, on the coast of Krabi province. The viewpoint sits on the road between Ao Nang and Nopparat Thara, a short way before Nopparat Thara.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit in as much of the mountains, beaches and sea as you can. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, more natural view. Try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the limestone peaks or the boats on the water.

Where to stand: Stand on Ao Nang Viewpoint (roadside lookout on the road between Ao Nang and Nopparat Thara, about 500 m before Nopparat Thara; roadside parking). An elevated view of the whole Ao Nang area, with limestone mountains behind and the sea and beaches stacked in layers below. The Ao Nang Viewpoint is a separate roadside lookout.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the horizon on the upper or lower line. Let the beaches and the water fill the layers, with the mountains as the backdrop. If you want the classic beach-and-rocks frame instead, Ao Nang Beach is the alternative, and Nopparat Thara Beach is a quieter one.

Settings tip: Tap the sea or sky to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sunset colours stay rich. Try a panorama for the full sweep, moving slowly and keeping the phone level.

Best time: Sunset, about 5:30–6:30 p.m., is the suggested time. Arrive a little earlier to find a spot and try a few frames as the light changes.

3Tiger Cave Temple

Tiger Cave Temple (Wat Tham Suea) is a Buddhist meditation temple in the forested Kiriwong Valley, north-north-east of Krabi town. It is known for the tiger paw prints in the cave, the tall Buddha statues and the long climb to the top. The temple dates back to 1975, when a monk went to meditate in the cave and saw tigers roaming around it, which gave the place its name. The stairway to the summit now has 1,260 steps, and the top sits at 278 metres. A large golden Buddha stands up there, and from the shrine you can see the temple buildings and much of Krabi town. For photographs, the temple works at two scales. Down in the grounds, the rainforest, old trees and surrounding mountains make a good backdrop. Higher up, the golden statue and the wide view over the town give you the classic frame. Monkeys live on the lower stairs and around the grounds, so they may well wander into your pictures. Keep a respectful distance and let them get on with their day. Take your time on the stairs, because some risers are over 30 cm high. Stop to shoot on the way up rather than at the top only, when you are tired. Carry water and keep your phone in a pocket or wrist strap on the climb. Check the official site before you go for anything about access.

Where it is: Tiger Cave Temple is in the forested Kiriwong Valley, north-north-east of Krabi town, in the Krabi area of southern Thailand. Mountains and tropical rain forest surround it.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the temple buildings and statues. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) for the full sweep of mountains, trees and buildings. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details and for monkeys from a safe distance.

Where to stand: Stand about 90 m north-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face south-east. Face south-east, towards the temple, and take the first frames from here before walking on.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the golden statue or the main building on one of the thirds. Include a few trees or the mountains behind to show the scale. At the top, the view over Krabi town works best with a strip of foreground, such as a railing or shrine roof, to add depth.

Settings tip: Tap on the brightest part of the scene, such as the golden statue, and slide the exposure down a little to keep detail in the gold. In shaded forest, tap on the subject and raise the exposure slightly. Wipe the lens first, as humidity can fog it.

Best time: Early morning is a sensible choice, as the climb is cooler and the crowds are likely smaller. Check the official site before you go for current opening arrangements.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Krabi?

The first three on this map are Railay Beach, Ao Nang Beach and Tiger Cave Temple. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 6.

Where should I stand to photograph Railay Beach?

Stand right at Railay Beach (mid-beach on the sand, facing the limestone cliffs and the islet offshore). Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the cliffs, sand and sea together.

When is the best time to photograph Ao Nang Beach?

Sunset, about 5:30–6:30 p.m., is the suggested time. Arrive a little earlier to find a spot and try a few frames as the light changes.

How do I get to Tiger Cave Temple?

No railway or tram stops serve this area, so from Krabi town head north-north-east to the temple grounds.
