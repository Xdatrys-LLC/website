This map shows 7 of the most-searched sights in El Yunque as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: El Yunque Forest, La Coca Falls, Yokahú Tower, La Mina Falls and Britton Tower lead the list. The 3 free photo spots

1El Yunque National Forest

El Yunque is a tropical rainforest in the Sierra de Luquillo mountains, in the north-east of Puerto Rico. It is the only tropical rainforest in the US National Forest System, and the U.S. Forest Service has managed it since 1903. The Spanish Crown had already set the land aside in 1876 to protect its soil and water, which makes it one of the oldest forest reserves in the Western Hemisphere. Today it covers about 28,500 acres and gets roughly 1.2 million visitors a year.

For photographs, the easiest high viewpoint is Torre Yokahú, a tower beside the road. From the top you look out over the rainforest canopy, the mountains around it and, on clear days, the coastline and the ocean. Guides describe it as the easiest high viewpoint to reach, with a parking area close by and only a short climb up the stairs. The view is wide and layered, with green ridges fading into haze.

Two other viewpoints are worth knowing about if you are a keen walker. Torre Mount Britton gives a more rugged view with mist and cloud around the peaks, and Los Picachos gives a 360-degree view out to the eastern shore. Both need a hike, so they suit a longer day.

Cloud often hides the high points, so aim for a clear morning. Sunrise and sunset are the best times at the tower.

Where it is: El Yunque National Forest lies in the Sierra de Luquillo in north-east Puerto Rico, mostly in the municipalities of Río Grande, Luquillo and Naguabo.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the full sweep of canopy, ridges and sky. Use the default lens (1x) for a more natural view. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a single peak, a ridge in the mist or a stretch of coast.

Where to stand: Stand at Torre Yokahú (top of the tower, reached by its stairs off PR-191). A panoramic view over the rainforest canopy, the surrounding mountains and, on clear days, the coastline and the ocean.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the horizon on the upper or lower third line. Keep the tower rail out of the frame, or use it at one edge to give a sense of height. Try a slow panorama along the ridgeline, holding the phone upright and moving steadily. If you are fit and the trails are open, Torre Mount Britton and Los Picachos give higher, more dramatic views.

Settings tip: Tap on the green hills to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky keeps its detail. In misty conditions, a Live Photo can capture cloud moving across the peaks. Wipe your lens before shooting, as the air can be damp.

Best time: Sunrise or sunset at the tower. A clear morning is best, as the high points are often hidden by mist and cloud.

2La Coca Falls

La Coca Falls is a waterfall in El Yunque, a rainforest setting where water, rock and thick green leaves fill the frame together. That mix suits a phone camera well. The white water gives a clear focal point, and the dark rock and bright foliage around it give the picture depth.

The falls are close enough to frame without zooming much. Start with the default lens (1x) and turn on the gridlines. Put the main drop on one of the vertical lines rather than dead centre, and leave a little foreground, such as leaves or rocks, to give a sense of depth.

If the view is tighter than you would like, switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in more of the surrounding forest. If something is in the way, try the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) to pick out just the stream of water. Rainforest light can be uneven, with pale sky above and deep shade below, so tap on the water and slide the exposure down a touch to stop the white water turning into a blank patch. Wipe your lens first, as spray and humidity can leave a soft haze on the glass.

Take a few shots from the same place at different zoom levels, then pick the one that best shows the falls and the green around them.

Where it is: La Coca Falls is inside El Yunque, in the rainforest setting that the map covers.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the whole waterfall. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the forest around it, or the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) to isolate the water.

Where to stand: Stand about 50 m north of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face south.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the main drop of water on a vertical line instead of in the middle. Keep a little foreground, such as leaves, at the edge of the frame for depth. Keep the phone level so the rock edges don't tilt.

Settings tip: Tap on the water to focus, then slide the exposure down slightly so the white water keeps its detail. Try a Live Photo to catch the movement and pick the best frame afterwards. Wipe the lens first, as spray can blur it.

Best time: Soft, even light, such as an overcast spell or when the falls are in shade, tends to keep the water and the leaves both looking natural. Harsh sun can create bright patches.

3Yokahú Tower

Yokahú Tower is an observation tower in El Yunque National Forest on the island of Puerto Rico. It was built in 1963 by Frank H. Wadsworth, then Forest Supervisor and Director of the IITF. It stands 69 feet (21 metres) tall at about 1,575 feet (480 m) above sea level, and it is one of only two observation towers in the forest. The other is Mount Britton Tower, which sits higher and is often wrapped in cloud. Yokahú is generally thought to give the better view, and it is easier to reach because there is a car park close by.

For a phone photographer there are two good pictures here. The first is the tower itself: a tall, solid shape against green forest and sky. The second is the view from the top, if you can go up, with ridges of rainforest rolling away below you. Switch to the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) to fit the whole structure in, and keep your phone level so the sides do not lean inwards too much. If the tower is open, take a second set of photos from the top using the default lens (1x) for the wide view and the telephoto lens for details in the valleys. Check the weather before you go, because cloud can hide the distance quickly.

Where it is: Yokahú Tower is inside El Yunque National Forest, on the island of Puerto Rico. There is a car park nearby.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) for the whole tower from close range. Use the default lens (1x) for a more natural look. If you climb the tower, the telephoto lens (2x-5x, depending on your phone) is good for picking out ridges and treetops.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Step back a little if there is room, and angle the phone upwards to catch the full height.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line a vertical edge of the tower up with one of the lines to keep it straight. Leave some sky above the top. For the view from the platform, include a foreground edge of railing or tree canopy to give a sense of depth, and try a panorama across the forest if the air is clear.

Settings tip: Tap on the tower to focus, then slide the exposure down slightly so the sky does not turn white. In misty conditions, tap on a distant ridge and nudge exposure up a little for a softer, clearer layer of hills.

Best time: Check the sky and weather before you go, as a clear spell gives the best distant views.
