This map shows 13 of the most-searched sights in Linz as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Ars Electronica, Pöstlingberg, Lentos, Brucknerhaus and Hauptplatz lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. Directions and Navigate buttons take you to the standing point on foot or by public transport. 3 spots are free to view; the full map is for members.

The 3 free photo spots

1Ars Electronica Center

The Ars Electronica Center is the big modern building on the Danube in Linz, and it is a straightforward subject for a phone camera. Seen from across the river, it gives you a wide, clear view of the whole building with water in front of it, which is what makes the picture work. A dependable view is from the promenade in front of the Lentos Kunstmuseum on the south bank. Guides describe the opposite-bank view from there as the most dependable one. The glass facade of Lentos is also often recommended for river reflections, so you can include the water, or the glass, as part of the shot.

If you want a different angle, two other places are suggested. Nibelungenbrücke gives a straight-on, centred view of the full facade across the Danube. Ars-Electronica-Straße shows the building head-on from the front, and at dusk you can catch car and tram light trails passing in front of it.

People pass along the promenade and the bridge, so take a moment to check the edges of the frame before you press the button. Exact spots can vary, so walk a few steps either way until the building sits neatly in the frame. Photo rules inside the building vary by exhibition, so ask before you take pictures indoors.

Where it is: In Linz, on the banks of the Danube, with the viewing promenade on the opposite bank in front of the Lentos Kunstmuseum.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x), which gives a natural view of the building across the water. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want a lot of river and sky, and keep the building away from the edges so it does not stretch. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for a tighter crop of the facade.

Where to stand: Stand at Lentos Kunstmuseum (south bank of the Danube, on the promenade in front of the museum). The Ars Electronica Center on the opposite bank, with the river and reflections in the foreground. From here the Ars Electronica Center sits on the opposite bank, with the river and its reflections in front of it, to the north-west.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the water in the lower third, with the building across the middle of the frame. Keep the horizon level. If you want a straight-on, centred view of the full facade, try Nibelungenbrücke instead. Ars-Electronica-Straße is the alternative for a head-on shot with light trails at dusk.

Settings tip: Tap the building to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright. At dusk, switch on night mode and rest your elbows on a railing to keep the phone steady, especially for the light trails.

Best time: Dusk is a good time because of the car and tram light trails near the building. In daylight, a calm day gives the clearest reflections on the water.

2Pöstlingberg

Pöstlingberg is Linz's own hill: a 539-metre rise on the left bank of the Danube, topped by the pilgrimage church and a viewing platform over the city. It is also home to the Linz Grottenbahn, and the mountain tramway (line 50 of the Linz tramway, running over the Pöstlingbergbahn) carries visitors up from the city centre. Most people photograph the view from the top. This spot flips that around and shows the hill itself, which makes a calmer picture than the busy tram-side angles.

The Franz-Josef-Warte is a lookout tower in Freinberg park. From the tower's wooden steps you see Pöstlingberg framed across the trees, with the green of Freinberg around it. The trees give you a natural frame, and the hill sits as a calm backdrop. It is a quieter, on-foot alternative to the crowded viewpoints.

For a second angle, try the Nibelungenbrücke over the Danube. It gives a wider frame with the hill across the river and the Urfahr side of the city beneath it. Check on the day for the best standing spot on the bridge. Keep your phone steady against the railing or a post, and wipe the lens before you shoot.

Where it is: Pöstlingberg rises on the left (north) bank of the Danube in Linz. The viewpoint is the Franz-Josef-Warte lookout tower in Freinberg park.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the hill and the trees around it. Then try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the church-topped summit closer.

Where to stand: Stand at Franz-Josef-Warte. The hill of Pöstlingberg framed across the trees from the lookout tower's wooden steps, with the rest of the Freinberg greenery around it.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the hill on one of the upper lines, letting the tree branches form a frame at the edges. If you want a different view, the Nibelungenbrücke shows the hill across the Danube with Urfahr beneath it.

Settings tip: Tap the hill to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky does not wash out. Take a few shots at 1x and at a zoom setting, then pick the best.

Best time: Soft morning or late-afternoon light usually flatters the hill and the trees. Go on a clear day so the hill stands out from the sky.

3Lentos Art Museum

Lentos is Linz's museum of modern art, and the building is as much of a draw for photographers as the paintings inside. It opened in May 2003 and was designed by the Zurich firm Weber & Hofer. It is a long, low block about 130 metres from end to end, wrapped in a transparent glass façade. The glass is lit up after dark. It sits right on the Danube, between the Nibelungen Bridge and the Brucknerhaus, so you can pair the clean lines of the building with open sky and water.

Inside, the collection grew from works by Klimt, Schiele and Kokoschka, among others. That makes it a good stop if you like art as well as pictures of buildings.

From the promenade in front of the main glass façade, the building's repeated lettering and the reflections of sky and river do most of the work for you. Shoot it straight-on for tidy lines, or stand at an angle so the glass picks up more of the surroundings. If you want a wider view, the Nibelungenbrücke gives a long look along the Danube with the museum framed against the river and city. Down on the Donaulände, a lower and quieter angle puts the glass and the water in one frame. Keep your phone level and watch for your own reflection in the glass.

Where it is: Lentos stands directly on the Danube in Linz, on the Ernst-Koref-Promenade, between the Nibelungen Bridge and the Brucknerhaus.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for a straight-on view of the glass front. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you cannot fit the long building in, and use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the lettering and reflections.

Where to stand: Stand on Ernst-Koref-Promenade (in front of the main glass façade). The museum's transparent glass front and its repeated lettering, shot straight-on or at an angle to capture reflections of the sky and river. The museum lies to the north-west of you from here. From this spot the photo shows the transparent glass front and its repeated lettering, either straight-on or at an angle to catch reflections of the sky and river.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line the roof edge up with a horizontal line so the building does not lean. Move a few steps sideways to change what the glass reflects. If you want a wider view, the Nibelungenbrücke gives a longer look along the Danube with the museum, river and skyline together. Check the bridge approach on arrival, as building work for a new walking and cycling ramp has been reported there.

Settings tip: Tap on the glass to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. After dark, use night mode and hold the phone against a railing or your body to keep it steady.

Best time: Go in daylight for sky and river reflections, then stay into the evening when the façade is lit. The night lighting is reported to change colour, so the look varies from night to night.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Linz?

The first three on this map are Ars Electronica, Pöstlingberg and Lentos. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 13.

Where should I stand to photograph the Ars Electronica?

Stand at Lentos Kunstmuseum (south bank of the Danube, on the promenade in front of the museum). Start with the default lens (1x), which gives a natural view of the building across the water.

When is the best time to photograph Pöstlingberg?

Soft morning or late-afternoon light usually flatters the hill and the trees. Go on a clear day so the hill stands out from the sky.

How do I get to Lentos?

No tram or railway stop is listed for this spot, so start from the Nibelungen Bridge or the Brucknerhaus, which are the two landmarks either side of the museum.
