The 3 free photo spots

1University of Colorado Boulder

The University of Colorado Boulder is the town's best-known campus, and its mix of warm sandstone, red-orange tiled roofs and mountain backdrop makes it easy to photograph. The university was founded in 1876, five months before Colorado became a state. Old Main, one of the oldest buildings, had its cornerstone laid in 1875, and the doors opened in 1877. Most of the buildings follow one look, called Tuscan Vernacular Revival, planned by the architect Charles Klauder. It uses rough sandstone walls, limestone trim and sloping tiled roofs. The stone came from quarries across the Front Range, so the buildings sit comfortably against the hills. The campus has been ranked among the most beautiful in the United States by Travel + Leisure and Condé Nast Traveler.

The classic picture is taken from the lawn of Farrand Field. Face the arch at the top of the steps and wait for someone to walk into the opening. The arch acts as a natural frame, and the Flatirons rise behind the campus. Guides suggest spring or autumn, when the trees are in bloom or changing colour. If the lawn is busy, shoot a little wider than you need and crop later. For a different view, Macky Auditorium's front steps show Old Main's red-brick tower. Varsity Bridge gives a calm scene of stone, still water and trees.

Where it is: The main campus lies south of the Pearl Street Mall and east of Chautauqua Auditorium. Farrand Field is the open lawn on the campus.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the arch, the person and the mountains all fit. Try 2x if you want the Flatirons to look larger behind the arch. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only for the whole lawn, and keep the horizon level.

Where to stand: Stand in Farrand Field (from the lawn, facing the arch at the top of the steps, with the Flatirons behind). A passerby or subject standing at the top of the steps, framed by the arch, with the Flatirons rising behind the campus. The campus lies to the north of you. From here the arch frames someone at the top of the steps, with the Flatirons rising behind the campus.

Framing: Put the arch in the middle of the picture and use the gridlines to keep its sides straight. Wait for a person to stand or walk in the opening. Leave some sky above the Flatirons so the ridge isn't cut off. For a second option, Macky Auditorium's front steps give a wider view of Old Main's red-brick tower. Varsity Bridge gives a calm lake scene.

Settings tip: Tap on the person in the arch to set focus, then lower the exposure slider a little so the sky and mountains keep their colour. If you are photographing a friend, portrait mode works well, but check that the arch edges are not blurred oddly.

Best time: Spring or autumn, when the trees are in bloom or the leaves are changing.

2Boulder Creek Path

Boulder Creek Path is the paved trail that follows Boulder Creek through the city, and it is one of the easiest places in Boulder to get a relaxed, scenic photo. The classic picture shows the smooth creekside path in the foreground with the Flatirons, the tilted slabs of rock above the town, rising behind it. That combination of a clear leading line, trees and a big mountain backdrop is why visitors photograph it so often.

If you want something different, Eben G. Fine Park has the creek with rock structures, boulders and reflections, which suits portraits and close creekside shots. Central Park has shaded scenes of cottonwoods, stone bridges and dappled light, close to downtown.

For a phone, keep the path in the lower third of the frame so the Flatirons have room above it. Wait for a gap between cyclists and walkers so the path looks calm. Creek water can run high and fast in spring and early summer, so keep children and bags back from the banks. Conditions near the creek can change, so look at what is open when you arrive.

Where it is: Boulder Creek Path runs alongside Boulder Creek through the city of Boulder, Colorado, with the Flatirons rising to the west.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the path and the Flatirons together. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to bring the rock slabs closer. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you want a lot of sky and trees.

Where to stand: Stand in Scott Carpenter Park. The Flatirons rising behind the paved creek path, the classic Boulder backdrop for the trail. From here the Flatirons rise behind the paved creek path, which is the classic Boulder view of the trail. If the view does not line up, walk a short way along the path and try again.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the path along the lower third so it leads the eye towards the Flatirons. Keep the horizon level. For a different view, try Eben G. Fine Park for the creek, boulders and reflections, or Central Park for shaded creekside scenes.

Settings tip: Tap the Flatirons to set focus, then lower the exposure slider slightly so the sky and rock keep their detail. Wait for a gap in passing walkers and cyclists, or use a Live Photo to pick the best frame afterwards.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light on the rock. Spring and early summer bring fast, high water, so keep well back from the edge.

3The Flatirons

The Flatirons are a row of huge, tilted sandstone slabs on the eastern slope of Green Mountain, just above Boulder. The five numbered ones, First to Fifth, run north to south, and together they are the city's signature backdrop. The rock is roughly 290 to 296 million years old. It was pushed up and tipped on its side long ago, then uncovered by erosion. Settlers named the slabs after old-fashioned clothes irons, and once you see the sloping shapes the name makes sense.

They photograph well because the slabs are big, angled and clearly separate from the green hillside behind. The open meadow in front of the Ranger Cottage at Chautauqua Park gives you the classic postcard view. You get grasses and, in season, wildflowers in the foreground and the full line of slabs rising behind. Because the rock faces east, early morning light is the most flattering, and the slabs fall into shadow later in the day.

Keep the foreground simple, with a strip of meadow along the bottom and the rock filling the rest of the frame. If you want a different angle, NCAR gives a closer, more looming view with a striking building as a second subject, and Flatirons Vista gives a wider, calmer frame of open grassland from farther out. Check current conditions and trail access before you set off, as paths can change.

Where it is: The Flatirons are near Boulder, Colorado, on the east slope of Green Mountain. The Chautauqua Park meadow, at the base of the slabs, is a classic postcard viewpoint for them.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the full line of slabs. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more meadow in the foreground. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a single slab or the ridge detail.

Where to stand: Stand in Chautauqua Park meadow (open meadow in front of the Ranger Cottage, facing the slabs). A green foreground with the full line of tilted sandstone slabs rising behind it, framed wide. In front of the Ranger Cottage, with the Flatirons rising to the west. From here the whole line of tilted slabs sits behind a green foreground of grass and wildflowers, which makes a wide, classic frame.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the horizon of grass on the lower third line, leaving the rock to fill the upper two thirds. Crouch a little so grasses or flowers sit in the foreground, and keep your phone level so the slabs do not look as if they lean. For a different view, NCAR shows the slabs close overhead, and Flatirons Vista gives a wider, quieter frame from farther out.

Settings tip: Tap the rock face to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky and sunlit stone keep their detail. Try a panorama if you want the whole range, and move slowly while you pan.

Best time: Dawn, when the east-facing rock catches the first direct light. The slabs are in shadow by late afternoon, though some people still like the nearby viewpoints at sunset.
