The 3 free photo spots

1Fergburger

Fergburger is a burger restaurant on Shotover Street in the middle of Queenstown. It began in February 2001 in a garage off Cow Lane, where it was hard to find and grew mainly through word of mouth. It now sits on Shotover Street beside its bakery, Ferg Baker, which opened in 2011, and a bar that opened in July 2020. The menu is built around gourmet burgers, including lamb, venison, cod, falafel and tofu versions. For a photo, the appeal is the street scene rather than a grand view: the shopfront, the signs and the people gathering outside give a feel for everyday Queenstown. A simple wide shot of the frontage with the street leading towards it tells the story well. Keep the shop name in the frame and leave a little space around it so the shot does not look cramped. If you want a close-up of a burger or the bakery counter, ask politely and keep out of the way of other customers.

Where it is: Fergburger is on Shotover Street in central Queenstown, next to its own bakery, Ferg Baker, and its bar.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for a natural view of the shopfront. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if the pavement is narrow or you want the whole street. Use telephoto (2x or more) for details such as the signage.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (front entrance, 42 Shotover Street). The shop front with the queue forming outside, framed by the street. Stay on the pavement and out of the doorway.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the shopfront up on one of the vertical lines so it looks straight. Include some of the street in the foreground. Take a second, tighter shot with the telephoto of the sign or the people outside.

Settings tip: Tap the shopfront to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the sky is washing out. In the evening, let night mode work and hold the phone steady against a post or wall.

Best time: Early morning is usually quieter for a clean shot of the street, while dusk gives glowing signs and shopfronts.

2Queenstown Gardens

Queenstown Gardens fills the peninsula beside the town centre, with Lake Wakatipu on several sides. It is a botanical garden with a large pond, tall Douglas firs and a mix of native and exotic trees. The first two oaks were planted in 1866 and the gardens opened officially in 1867. For photographs, the best place is the tip of the peninsula, which looks south across the lake. From there the water fills the foreground and The Remarkables ridge rises on the far side. Late afternoon is the time to aim for, when the peaks pick up golden light. The gardens have other good subjects too. The rose garden dates from the 1960s, when 850 bushes were planted, and the Scott Memorial from 1913 is also worth a look. Trails run through the trees with views of the mountains, the Frankton Arm and the town. For a close-up scene rather than a lake panorama, try the arched bridge framed by trees and reflected in the pond, which looks best in autumn colour. For a wider view, the gardens and lake can also be seen from One Mile Carpark above the town, with The Remarkables and Walter Peak behind. Sunny summer days are busy, so go early if you want the paths to yourself.

Where it is: Queenstown Gardens sits on a peninsula right next to the town centre of Queenstown, with Lake Wakatipu and the Frankton Arm on its sides. The band rotunda stands near the Park Street entrance.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Begin with the default lens (1x) for the lake and ridge together. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more foreground water or the pond and bridge. Try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the peaks of The Remarkables.

Where to stand: Stand in Queenstown Gardens (tip of the peninsula, facing south across the lake). Lake Wakatipu in the foreground with The Remarkables ridge across the water, best framed in late afternoon light. From here Lake Wakatipu fills the foreground and The Remarkables ridge sits across the water.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the horizon on the upper or lower third, depending on whether the water or the mountains are the main subject. Keep a little shoreline in the bottom of the frame for depth. For a different shot, move to the pond and frame the arched bridge between the autumn trees with its reflection below. The view of the gardens and lake from One Mile Carpark is another option.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the sky or the peaks to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the golden light is not washed out. Use panorama mode only if you can pan slowly and keep the phone level.

Best time: Late afternoon or sunset, when the peaks catch golden light. Early morning is quieter, especially on sunny summer days.

3Eichardt's Hotel

Eichardt's Hotel stands on the corner of Marine Parade and Ballarat Street, right on the Lake Wakatipu shore, and is a significant local landmark in Queenstown. The site goes back to the early gold-rush years. William Rees, one of the first European settlers here, had a woolshed on this spot, and after gold turned up in the Shotover River in 1862 he converted it into a hotel called the Queen's Arms. Albert Eichardt joined him in 1866, became sole proprietor by 1869, and his name stuck. The building is listed as a Category Two historic place. It was badly affected by the November 1999 floods, then restored and reopened as Eichardt's Private Hotel in December 2001. It photographs well because it is a solid, characterful building set against open water and mountains, with the rest of the town around it. One suggested viewpoint is the lakefront about 195 m east of the hotel, towards Queenstown Gardens, looking back towards the town. From there you can show the hotel frontage with the lake edge in front of it. A closer, street-level alternative is Marine Parade itself, which gives a straight-on view of the building with the lake and mountains behind. Take a moment to check that trees or passing traffic are not blocking the front of the building, and step a few paces along the shore if they are.

Where it is: Eichardt's Hotel is in central Queenstown, on the lakefront at the corner of Marine Parade and Ballarat Street. The Queenstown Gardens peninsula lies a short walk to the east.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to show the hotel frontage with the lake edge and town around it. If the building looks too small, switch to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick it out. Keep the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the wider lake-and-mountain scene, where the hotel will be a small part of the picture.

Where to stand: Stand in Queenstown Gardens (lakeshore path on the peninsula, looking back toward the town centre). The hotel's lakefront frontage on Marine Parade, seen across the lake edge from the gardens, with the town around it. Queenstown Gardens is the nearby area that suggests this viewpoint. From here the hotel's lakefront frontage on Marine Parade sits among the buildings of the town, with the water in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the hotel on one of the vertical lines so the building stands upright rather than leaning. Use the lake edge as a foreground line leading towards it. If you want a closer, straight-on shot, walk to Marine Parade, where the hotel fills more of the frame with the lake and mountains behind it.

Settings tip: Tap on the hotel to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the sky or water is washing out. If you are there at dusk, try night mode and hold the phone steady against a railing or your body.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer, more even light than midday, and the lakeshore tends to be quieter early on.
