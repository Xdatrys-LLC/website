This map shows 20 of the most-searched sights in Santa Fe as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Loretto Chapel, O'Keeffe Museum, Santa Fe Plaza, Canyon Road and San Miguel Chapel lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. The 3 free photo spots

1Loretto Chapel and its staircase

Loretto Chapel and its staircase is the first stop on the Santa Fe map, and it is a good one for a phone camera. The staircase is the subject, and it rewards a little patience: the staircase itself and the way light falls across it.

If you are in a tight space, the ultra-wide lens (0.5x) helps you fit the whole staircase into one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) when you want a more natural look with less stretching at the edges. If you can zoom to 2x or more, use it for details of the staircase rather than trying to capture everything at once.

Turn on the gridlines so the vertical lines stay straight. Tilting the phone up or down makes the walls lean, so keep it level and step back if you can. Tap the brightest part of the scene to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch so bright areas do not wash out. If other visitors are in the shot, wait a moment for a gap, or include a person to show the scale. Rules on photography and entry can change, so check the official site before you go.

Where it is: In Santa Fe. On the map it is the first numbered spot.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole staircase into a tight space. Use the default lens (1x) for a more natural look. Try the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for close details of the staircase.

Where to stand: Stand about 35 m south of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north. Move a step or two back if you can, so the whole staircase fits in the frame, and keep the phone level.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and line up a vertical edge with a grid line to keep it straight. Take one wide shot of the whole staircase, then a closer one of its details. If visitors are in the shot, wait for a gap, or let one person stand on the stairs to show the scale.

Settings tip: Tap the staircase to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if bright areas are washing out the scene. If the light is dim, try night mode and hold the phone against a wall or rail to keep it steady. Hold still for the full exposure.

Best time: Visit when it is less busy, if you can, and check the official site for current visiting times.

2Georgia O'Keeffe Museum

The Georgia O'Keeffe Museum sits on Johnson Street in the centre of Santa Fe, in an adobe-style building. For a phone photographer the appeal is the street frontage: soft earthy walls, simple lines and the entrance, all of which sit well in a single frame. It is the kind of subject that looks calm and uncluttered, so you don't need clever settings to get a good result.

Indoor photography is restricted at the museum, so plan on getting your pictures outside and don't count on taking any inside.

Start with a straight-on shot of the whole facade with the entrance in view, then move in for details such as the texture of the wall, a doorway or a window. Turn on the gridlines so the building's edges stay level. If people walk through the frame, wait a moment and they will usually pass. The museum is a short walk from the Santa Fe Plaza, so it fits easily into a stroll around the centre.

Where it is: In central Santa Fe, on Johnson Street at number 217, a short walk from the Santa Fe Plaza.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the whole facade. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) only if the pavement is narrow and you can't step back, and keep the building square to the screen so the walls don't lean. Use 2x or more for details such as the doorway or wall texture.

Where to stand: Stand about 40 m south-east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-west. Stand on the pavement at the Georgia O'Keeffe Museum, at the front facade on Johnson Street (217 Johnson St), or just across the street if you can. From there your photo shows the adobe-style street facade with the entrance in view.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and line the edges of the building up with the vertical lines. Put the entrance on one of the thirds rather than dead centre. Then take a closer shot of a detail on the wall or around the door for variety.

Settings tip: Tap on the wall to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a touch if the sky is bright, so the earth-coloured walls keep their texture.

Best time: Visit when the street is quieter and the light is soft, such as early or late in the day, so the facade is not in harsh shadow. Check which side of the street is in sun when you arrive.

8La Fonda on the Plaza

La Fonda on the Plaza is the hotel beside Santa Fe Plaza, the city's central square, and the best way to photograph it is from the square itself. Stand out in the open and the hotel's facade sits across the plaza, with the green benches and the portal (the covered walkway) in the foreground. Those layers give a simple phone photo some depth: benches at the bottom, columns in the middle, the hotel behind. Guides also point to the obelisk and fountains as part of the scene, so a little patience while you choose where to put them in the frame is worthwhile.

You do not need to walk far. Take a few steps either way to line up the columns and keep the benches tidy at the edges of the picture. Go early if you can. At first light the shadows fall across the portal columns and the crowds have not arrived, so you get clean frames without people walking through them. Later in the day the square is busier, so wait for gaps or include people deliberately for a sense of scale. Keep your phone level so the building's vertical lines stay straight.

Where it is: La Fonda on the Plaza stands beside Santa Fe Plaza, the central square of the city. The best photo spot is on the plaza itself, looking across it towards the hotel.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to show the facade across the plaza. If the benches, portal and hotel will not all fit, switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x). Use 2x if you want a closer shot of the building's details without walking over.

Where to stand: Stand about 45 m north of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face south. From there the photo shows La Fonda's facade across the plaza, with the green benches and the portal in the foreground. The obelisk and fountains can also come into the frame.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and use them to keep the building level. Place the benches or the portal columns along the bottom or side of the frame as a foreground, and leave the hotel as the main subject behind them. Take a few steps sideways until the columns line up neatly. Shoot at 1x first, then try 0.5x if you want more of the square.

Settings tip: Tap on the facade to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the building keeps its colour. If you want a lively shot with people in it, try a Live Photo and pick the best frame afterwards.

Best time: First light, before the shops open and the tour groups arrive. The shadows then fall across the portal columns and the plaza is quiet.
