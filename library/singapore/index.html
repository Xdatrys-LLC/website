This map shows 20 of the most-searched sights in Singapore as photo spots, ranked by how often people search for them: Marina Bay Sands, Sentosa, Gardens by the Bay, Raffles Hotel and Sri Mariamman lead the list. Every spot has a pin on the subject and a phone icon showing where to stand, with tips for shooting it on your phone: which lens to use, how to frame it, a settings tip and the best time of day. Directions and Navigate buttons take you to the standing point on foot or by public transport. The first 3 spots are free to view; the full map is for members.

The 3 free photo spots

1Marina Bay Sands

Marina Bay Sands is the three-tower resort on the edge of Marina Bay, and it is probably the easiest building in Singapore to recognise. The three hotel towers are topped by a long platform called the Sands Skypark. That long platform gives you a strong silhouette against the sky, and it is the main reason the building photographs so well from the ground. The architect, Moshe Safdie, has said the design was first inspired by decks of cards.

A few facts help you read what you are looking at. The Skypark is 340 metres long and holds a 150-metre infinity pool. It sits on a cantilevered platform that overhangs the north tower by 66.5 metres. The hotel has 1,850 rooms, and the resort and Skypark officially opened on 23 and 24 June 2010. The ArtScience Museum is part of the same complex.

The towers are huge, so you will not fit everything in from close range unless you use the ultra-wide lens. If you want a tidier composition, step back until the whole building fits, and keep your phone level so the towers do not lean. Walk around a little too, because the building looks quite different from each side.

Where it is: Marina Bay Sands is on the waterfront of Marina Bay in Singapore, right beside Bayfront MRT station.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: From across the bay the hotel is about 740 m away. Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the three towers, the Skypark and their reflection in Marina Bay. Then use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to fill the frame with the towers and the Skypark. The ultra-wide (0.5x) makes the hotel look small from here, so use it only if you want lots of water and sky.

Where to stand: Stand about 740 m north-west of the subject, on Fullerton Promenade at the water's edge (the phone icon on the map), and face south-east across Marina Bay. From here the three towers and the Skypark line up across the water.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and keep the far shoreline level. Put the waterline on the lower third so the reflection fills the bottom of the frame and the towers rise above it. Leave a little space on each side so the Skypark is not cut off.

Settings tip: Tap the towers to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky keeps its colour and the lit windows do not burn out. After dark, use night mode and brace your elbows against your body or a railing to keep the phone steady.

Best time: Early evening, around dusk, is a good bet because the sky and the building lights can both show up. In low light, use night mode and brace your elbows against a wall or rail to keep the shot steady.

2Sentosa

Sentosa is the island resort just off Singapore's southern coast, and it makes an easy, relaxed stop for a few phone photos. The subject here is the island generally rather than one building, so think of this as a scene-setting shot: water, greenery and the feel of a holiday island. The island is separated from the main island by Keppel Harbour, and it once served as a British military base and later a Japanese prisoner-of-war camp. It was renamed Sentosa in the early 1970s, a Malay word meaning peace and tranquility. Today it is a tourist destination with a 2.5 km sheltered beach, Fort Siloso, a cable car network, hotels and Resorts World Sentosa, and it is said to receive up to 25 million visitors a year. That mix gives you plenty of variety: open sea and sky for wide views, and palm trees, paths and people for lively foreground detail. Start at the phone icon on the map, then take a few steps either way to find a clear view without railings or signs cutting across the frame. Keep the horizon level using your gridlines, and include something close by, such as a tree branch or a path edge, to give the picture depth. If the place is busy, wait a moment for a gap in the crowd, or shoot a little higher to keep the sky as the main feature.

Where it is: Sentosa sits off the southern coast of Singapore's main island, across the channel of Keppel Harbour and next to the smaller island of Pulau Brani.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the sweep of water, shoreline and sky. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural view, and use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a single detail such as a boat, a tree or a distant building.

Where to stand: Stand about 160 m north of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face south.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the horizon on the upper or lower line rather than in the middle. Add something close to you, such as leaves or a path edge, to give the picture depth. For a quick extra shot, try a panorama, moving the phone slowly and steadily.

Settings tip: Tap on the main subject to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky looks washed out. Try a Live Photo if there is movement such as waves or passers-by, so you can pick the best moment afterwards.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people. Midday sun can look harsh and flat, so keep the exposure slider handy.

3Gardens by the Bay and the Supertree Grove

Gardens by the Bay is a 105-hectare waterfront park next to Marina Reservoir, and the Supertree Grove sits in its largest section, Bay South Garden. That garden opened on 29 June 2012 and was designed by Grant Associates. Its plan takes its idea from an orchid, with the Supertrees placed as the 'blooms' where paths and links meet. The Supertrees also do a job: they help vent hot air and cool circulated water for the two big glass conservatories, the Flower Dome and the Cloud Forest, nearby.

The grove photographs well because the tall, tree-like structures give you strong vertical shapes against open sky, and the paths around them lead the eye upwards. Gardens by the Bay is a popular attraction, with over 50 million visitors by 2018, so expect other people in your frames and treat them as scale rather than a problem.

Hold the phone upright, keep the grid lines on, and line the tallest trunks up with a vertical grid line so they don't lean. Take one wide shot from the marked point, then walk closer and shoot straight up for a more abstract picture. Check your lens is clean before you start, as humid air can leave a faint film on it.

Where it is: The map's subject point is the middle of the Flower Dome, in Bay South Garden, part of Gardens by the Bay in Singapore's Central Region, next to Marina Reservoir and the Marina South area. The Supertree Grove is in the same garden.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole grove in from the marked point. Then switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer view with less edge stretching. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the tops of individual Supertrees.

Where to stand: Stand about 50 m south-east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-west. You are far enough back to see the Supertrees as a group rather than one at a time.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the tallest trunks up with a vertical line so they stay straight. Keep the phone level and avoid tilting it upwards from the marked point. For a second shot, walk in close, point the phone up, and use the Supertrees' shapes as a pattern. Wait a moment for people to move through the foreground, or include them to show size.

Settings tip: Tap the brightest part of the sky or the structures to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky keeps its colour. If you shoot after dark, use night mode and brace your elbows against your body or a railing to keep the phone still.

Best time: Early morning is usually quieter, and the hour around dusk suits the grove best as the sky darkens. Check the official site for any evening light displays.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Singapore?

The first three on this map are Marina Bay Sands, Sentosa and Gardens by the Bay. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 20.

Where should I stand to photograph Marina Bay Sands?

Stand about 740 m north-west of Marina Bay Sands, on Fullerton Promenade at the water's edge (the phone icon on the map), and face south-east across Marina Bay. From across the bay the hotel is about 740 m away.

When is the best time to photograph Sentosa?

Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people. Midday sun can look harsh and flat, so keep the exposure slider handy.

How do I get to Gardens by the Bay?

From Gardens by the Bay MRT station (MRT Thomson–East Coast Line), the middle of the Flower Dome is about 686 metres away to the north-west, about 11 minutes on foot.
