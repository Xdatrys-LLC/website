The 3 free photo spots

1The Andy Warhol Museum

The Andy Warhol Museum is a big former industrial warehouse that now holds the largest museum collection in North America devoted to a single artist. Warhol was born in Pittsburgh, and the building sits on the city's North Shore. It is one of the four Carnegie Museums of Pittsburgh. Inside there are seven floors and 17 galleries. The museum opened in May 1994, and about 25,000 people came in its first weekend.

From the street, the building photographs well because of its classical, Beaux-Arts-style front. Above the entrance is the old 'Frick & Lindsay Co' inscription, a nice reminder of the building's earlier life. The front is a calm, symmetrical subject, so a simple, well-straightened shot works better than anything fussy.

For the best view, stand across the street from the front. From there you can angle up at the entrance and get the lettering and most of the facade in one frame. Take a few steps back if the top is cut off. If you have time, the Andy Warhol Bridge is only a few blocks away. It gives a wider view of the skyline and river, with the museum as part of the surroundings rather than the main subject.

This guide covers the outside of the building only. If you plan to go in, check the official site before you go.

Where it is: On the North Shore of Pittsburgh, at the corner of Sandusky Street and General Robinson Street. The Andy Warhol Bridge is a few blocks away.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) from across the street. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you cannot step back far enough to fit the whole front in. Use the telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for a closer shot of the 'Frick & Lindsay Co' inscription above the entrance.

Where to stand: Stand at the corner of Sandusky Street and East General Robinson Street (across the street from the front facade, shooting up at the entrance). The museum's classical, Beaux-Arts-style front, including the historic 'Frick & Lindsay Co' inscription above the entrance. Stand on 117 Sandusky Street, across the street from the front facade. From here you look up at the entrance and see the museum's classical, Beaux-Arts-style front, with the 'Frick & Lindsay Co' inscription above the door. The building's middle is to your south-west.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line up the edges of the facade with the vertical lines, so the building does not lean back too much when you tilt the phone upwards. Keep the entrance and inscription in the centre. Take one wide shot of the whole front, then a second closer one of the lettering. For a wider skyline-and-river view with the museum in the background, walk to the Andy Warhol Bridge, which is a short walk away.

Settings tip: Tap on the stonework or the entrance to set focus, then use the exposure slider to bring the brightness down slightly if the sky is washing out. Check the edges of the picture for passing cars and people before you press the button.

Best time: Choose a time when the front is evenly lit and not in harsh glare; early morning or late afternoon on a clear day is a good bet. Look at the facade on the day, as street shadows can fall across it.

2PNC Park

PNC Park is the Pittsburgh Pirates' baseball stadium, on the North Shore beside the Allegheny River. It opened during the 2001 season and was built with steel and limestone, in contrast to the brick bases of other modern stadiums. It was designed to echo classic ballparks such as Pittsburgh's own Forbes Field, which is why the arches and steel trusswork give it a traditional feel.

It photographs well because the stadium, the river, the yellow Roberto Clemente Bridge and the Downtown skyline all sit close together. The bridge was renamed in honour of the Pirates player Roberto Clemente, and a stadium with seating for 38,747 people still looks compact from across the water.

Keep the horizon level using your phone's gridlines, and include some water or railing in the foreground so the stadium doesn't look flat. If you also want the skyline behind the ballpark, the Roberto Clemente Bridge is a good second viewpoint, and the bronze Clemente statue near the stadium makes a good close-up with the bridge behind it.

Where it is: PNC Park is on the North Shore of Pittsburgh, on the north bank of the Allegheny River, directly across from Downtown.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to get the stadium, the Clemente Bridge and the yellow bridges in one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a cleaner, less stretched view of the ballpark, and try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the bridge or the stadium's stonework.

Where to stand: Stand in Allegheny Landing (riverfront side, looking across the Allegheny River). A riverside angle on the stadium with the Clemente Bridge and yellow bridges nearby in the frame. The stadium should be ahead of you, with the Clemente Bridge and the other yellow bridges close by in the frame.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and put the river edge or railing on a lower line so the horizon stays level. Leave the stadium and bridge in the upper two thirds with some water in front. If you want the skyline behind the ballpark, walk to the Roberto Clemente Bridge and shoot from there instead.

Settings tip: Tap the stadium to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright so it does not wash out. If you are shooting after dark, use night mode and rest your elbows on a railing or wall to keep the phone still.

Best time: Golden hour, or after a game, when the riverfront is quieter.

3Acrisure Stadium

Acrisure Stadium, still often called Heinz Field, sits on the North Shore beside the Ohio River. It is home to the Pittsburgh Steelers and the Pittsburgh Panthers, and it opened in 2001. The best phone photo is of its river-facing front: a wide flight of steps leading up to Gate A, with the big scoreboard above. The stadium was designed with Pittsburgh's steel history in mind and uses about 12,000 tons of steel. Steel and glass show clearly on the front, and the glass echoes the buildings Downtown. Near Gate A there is also a statue of a Pitt Panther standing over a paved picture of the Cathedral of Learning. It makes a good detail shot if you have time. The stadium holds 68,400 seats, so it is a very large building. You will need to stand back and use the steps and scoreboard to give a sense of scale. It also works well as a backdrop for people. Ask them to stand on or just below the steps, with the scoreboard above their heads. The Gateway Clipper dock is close by, and a boat passing or moored on the river can add interest if you turn around. On home-game days the steps can fill with fans, so check the fixtures before you go.

Where it is: Acrisure Stadium is in the North Shore neighbourhood on the North Side of Pittsburgh, on the bank of the Ohio River. PNC Park is its neighbour, and Downtown lies across the water.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for people on the steps with the scoreboard behind them. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you are close and the building will not fit. Use the telephoto (2x or more) for details such as the statue near Gate A.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. Your viewpoint is on the south side of the stadium, facing the river, at the steps up to Gate A under the main scoreboard. This is a short walk from the centre of the stadium toward the river side. From here the stadium's river-facing front, its steps and the scoreboard make a close-up backdrop for people. The Gateway Clipper dock is nearby, so a boat can sometimes add to the frame if you turn round.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and keep the stadium front level, so the steps and the lines of the building do not lean. Place your subject on the steps and leave room above them for the scoreboard. For a wider view, the Roberto Clemente Bridge shows the stadium beside PNC Park with the Downtown skyline behind, and the Franco Harris statue gives a close-up with the stadium behind it. Check the statue's exact position on the day.

Settings tip: Tap on your subject's face to set focus and drag the exposure slider down a little, so the bright sky does not wash out the scoreboard. Portrait mode suits a person on the steps. Switch it off if the building edges look smudged.

Best time: Check for home games before you go, as the steps can fill with fans. Check how the light looks on your phone screen when you are there.
