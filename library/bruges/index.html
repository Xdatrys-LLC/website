The 3 free photo spots

1Belfry of Bruges

The Belfry of Bruges is the medieval bell tower that rises over the city centre, and it is one of the best-known sights in Bruges. It was added to the Markt around 1240, when the city was a major centre of the Flemish cloth trade. It stands 83 metres tall and leans about 87 centimetres to the east. It once reached 102 metres, but the wooden spire burned down in 1741 and was never replaced. The octagonal upper stage and the Gothic stone parapet make the top easy to recognise from far away. The tower has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1999.

The quay curves round a bend in the canal, and the brick guild houses are reflected in the water. The Belfry rises above the rooftops at the far end of the view, so the tower becomes the finishing touch to the scene. A few steps along the quay in either direction changes the angle, so try two or three spots. The quay is crowded for much of the day, so be patient and wait for a gap along the quay edge. If you want a different picture, Jan Van Eyckplein may show the Belfry across the canal after dark, though you should verify this view on arrival. The Markt gives a straight-on view of the tower over the guild gables, but it can be cluttered by scaffolding and café umbrellas, especially at night.

Where it is: The Belfry stands on the Markt, the main square in the centre of Bruges, at the front of the old market hall. The Rozenhoedkaai viewpoint is a canal quay a short walk away.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the canal bend, the guild houses and the reflections. Then try telephoto (2x-3x, depending on your phone) to bring the Belfry closer and fill more of the frame. The ultra-wide (0.5x) makes the tower look small and far away, so use it only if you want the whole quay in the picture.

Where to stand: Stand on Rozenhoedkaai (stone quay on the south side of the canal, near the bend where the Dijver meets the Groenerei; a few metres along the quay in either direction changes the angle). The canal bend with brick guild houses and their reflections in the water, with the Belfry rising above the roofs at the end of the view. On the stone quay on the south side of the canal, near the bend where the Dijver meets the Groenerei. From here the photo shows the canal bend, the brick guild houses and their reflections, with the tower closing the scene.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the canal bend in the lower half so the reflections get plenty of space. Keep the tower near a vertical gridline so it looks upright. Move a few metres along the quay to shift the angle. For an alternative, Jan Van Eyckplein may give a canal view of the Belfry after dark (verify this view on arrival), and the Markt gives a straight-on vertical shot over the guild gables, though that view may be cluttered at certain times, especially at night.

Settings tip: Tap the water or the brick fronts to focus, then lower the exposure slider a little so the sky does not wash out and the Belfry keeps its detail. At blue hour, switch on night mode and brace your elbows against your body or a solid surface to keep the phone steady.

Best time: Sunrise and blue hour. The canal reflections work well at both times.

3Rozenhoedkaai

Rozenhoedkaai is the stretch of quay where the canal bends and tall brick gabled houses rise straight out of the water, with the Belfry standing above the rooftops behind them. It is a very busy, popular spot with day visitors and boat trips, and the reason is easy to see on a phone screen: water, old brick, trees and a tall tower all fit in one frame. Because the quay is so busy, the calmest results come at sunrise or around blue hour, when the light is soft and the water is stiller.

For something a little different, this spot's suggested viewpoint is Jan Van Eyckplein, a short walk north along the canal. Guides describe it as quieter and far less photographed than the quay. It shows a calm canal stretch with guild buildings and streetlight reflections, and it works best after dark. One guide says it is largely empty after about 9 PM, which suits slow, steady night shots. A guide also suggests early morning if you want the square to yourself.

If you want the classic view as well, Nepomucenusbrug looks down the canal as it curves towards Rozenhoedkaai, with the quay facades, willows and the Belfry spire together. From the quay itself you can get low, close to the water, and let the gabled houses rise out of the reflection. Take a few frames from each place and compare them later.

Where it is: Rozenhoedkaai lies on the canal in the centre of Bruges, with the Belfry rising above the roofs behind it. Jan Van Eyckplein, the suggested viewpoint, is on the same canal just to the north.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the canal and its reflections. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) on the quay itself when you want the full row of gables, and to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the Belfry above the roofs.

Where to stand: Stand in Jan Van Eyckplein (north of Rozenhoedkaai, along the canal). A quieter canal stretch with guild buildings and streetlight reflections, mainly useful after dark. Which is north of Rozenhoedkaai, along the canal. From the square you see a quieter canal stretch with guild buildings and streetlights reflected in the water. Rozenhoedkaai itself is to the south, so walk down the canal if you also want the close view.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and line the canal edge up with a horizontal line so the buildings sit level. Include the streetlight reflections in the lower half of the frame and keep the buildings in the upper half. For the classic view, try Nepomucenusbrug, where the canal curves towards the quay with the Belfry spire above. On Rozenhoedkaai itself, crouch low so the gabled houses seem to rise out of the water.

Settings tip: After dark, use night mode and rest your elbows on a wall or bridge rail to keep the phone steady. Tap on the brightest lit building to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the lights don't burn out. In daylight, tap on the brick facade to set focus and exposure.

Best time: After dark for Jan Van Eyckplein, when guides say it is largely empty after about 9 PM. Sunrise and blue hour are the practical times for the quay, as it is very busy during the day.

10Markt (Market Square)

The Markt is the central square of Bruges, and it is the easiest place in the city to understand where you are. The Belfry stands on the south side, one of the city's most famous landmarks, and the stepped-gable guild houses and café terraces line the other edges. The square covers about a hectare, and since its renovation in 1995–96 it has been mostly traffic-free, so you can stand back and take in the whole scene without cars in the way.

The Belfry works as a strong vertical anchor in a wide frame, which is why the view facing it is the classic shot. In the middle of the square is a statue of Jan Breydel and Pieter de Coninck, who fought at the Battle of the Golden Spurs in 1302. It was unveiled in 1887 and makes a handy foreground. A diagonal angle lets you add the guild houses and café terraces to the tower.

The square has been a gathering place since the 10th century, with its first international fair around 1200. Early morning gives the cleanest compositions. After dark the Belfry is lit and the cobbles are quieter.

For a different view, turn to face the Gothic civic facade and the colourful houses across the open cobbles. If you want a bird's-eye view of the whole square, the Belfort van Brugge looks straight down on it.

Where it is: The Markt is in the heart of Bruges' city centre, with the Belfry on its south side and the Provincial Court building on its east side.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the Belfry, the guild houses and the cobbles into one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural look, then use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details such as the Belfry's upper tower or the statue.

Where to stand: Stand in Markt (from the square's centre, facing the Belfry). The Belfry rises over the square, framed by the stepped-gable guild houses that line it. From here the tower rises over the square, framed by the stepped-gable guild houses on either side.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the Belfry on a vertical line so the tower stays upright. Keep the phone level and don't tilt it upwards, or the tower will lean. For a variation, step to one side for a diagonal angle that adds the guild houses and café terraces. You could also turn to the Gothic civic facade across the cobbles for a second shot.

Settings tip: Tap the Belfry to focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky and the tower keep their detail. After dark, use night mode and hold the phone steady against your body for a few seconds.

Best time: Early morning for clean compositions with few people; after dark for an illuminated Belfry and empty cobblestones.

What are the top photo spots in Bruges?

The first three on this map are Belfry, Holy Blood Basilica and Rozenhoedkaai. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 18.

Where should I stand to photograph the Belfry?

Stand on Rozenhoedkaai (stone quay on the south side of the canal, near the bend where the Dijver meets the Groenerei; a few metres along the quay in either direction changes the angle). Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the canal bend, the guild houses and the reflections.

When is the best time to photograph Rozenhoedkaai?

After dark for Jan Van Eyckplein, when guides say it is largely empty after about 9 PM. Sunrise and blue hour are the practical times for the quay, as it is very busy during the day.

How do I get to Markt Square?

No station or tram stop is listed for this spot, so start from the centre of Bruges and head for the Markt, the main square.
