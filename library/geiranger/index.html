The 3 free photo spots

1Seven Sisters Waterfall

The Seven Sisters is a waterfall on the north side of Geirangerfjorden, made up of seven separate streams that drop down the mountainside side by side. The whole fall is about 410 metres tall, and the tallest of the seven streams has a free fall of about 250 metres. It is the 39th tallest waterfall in Norway and sits within the West Norwegian Fjords World Heritage Site. It photographs well because the streams make a pattern against the dark rock, with the fjord below as a calm foreground. Straight across the water is a single fall called The Suitor. Legend says the sisters dance down the mountain while the suitor flirts with them from the other shore. The falls lie just south of the old Knivsflå farm, across the fjord from the old Skageflå farm, so a photo can include a sense of the wider landscape. The phone icon on the map is about 450 m east of the subject. That is far enough to fit the whole drop in one frame, but close enough to pick out the separate streams with a little zoom. Take a wide shot first, then a closer one of the streams. Keep your phone level against the mountain edge so the fall does not look tilted, and wipe the lens, as fjord spray and damp air can smear it.

Where it is: The falls are on the northern side of Geirangerfjorden in Stranda Municipality, about 6.5 km west of Geiranger village. They lie just south of the old Knivsflå farm and across the fjord from the old Skageflå farm.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to get the full height of the falls and some fjord. Then try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the seven streams. Ultra-wide (0.5x) will make the falls look small and distant from here.

Where to stand: Stand about 450 m east of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face west. Stay safe and be aware of your surroundings.

Framing: Turn your phone upright so the full height of the fall fits. Switch on gridlines and line a vertical grid line up with the main streams. Leave a strip of fjord at the bottom for scale. For a second shot, zoom in on the streams and the dark rock and skip the sky.

Settings tip: Tap the bright part of the water to focus, then slide the exposure slider down a little so the white streams keep their detail. A Live Photo can capture the movement of the water. Panorama mode may help if you want the fall and the shoreline in one wide image.

Best time: Water flow varies, so check conditions on the day. Check the light and weather on the day as well, since they will change how clearly the seven streams show against the rock.

2Geirangerfjord

Geirangerfjord is a 15-kilometre branch of the Sunnylvsfjorden in Stranda Municipality, and one of Norway's most visited sights. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2005, together with the Nærøyfjorden. This spot shows it from Skageflå, an abandoned mountain farm on the trail above the water. The old farm buildings sit in the foreground, with the fjord and the Seven Sisters waterfall behind them. That gives your photo a sense of scale and a story, which a plain view of water and cliffs does not. The Seven Sisters face another waterfall, the Suitor, across the fjord. Skageflå is one of several farms along the fjord that people have restored in part, and it is the one you can reach on foot from Geiranger. The walk is uphill and takes about 40 to 60 minutes, which tends to keep it quieter than the roadside viewpoints. Guides say the falls look best early in the season, from May to July, when more water is flowing. Let the buildings fill the lower part of the frame and leave room above for the fjord and the falls. If you do not fancy the walk, Flydalsjuvet and Ørnesvingen are two other well-known viewpoints for the same fjord.

Where it is: On the Geirangerfjord, on the trail above the fjord towards the abandoned Skageflå farm, a walk from Geiranger village at the head of the fjord.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to get the farm buildings, the fjord and the waterfall in one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, less stretched view. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the Seven Sisters falls on their own.

Where to stand: Stand at Skageflå (on the trail beside the abandoned farm buildings, the spot right next to the trail). The abandoned mountain farm in the foreground with the fjord and the Seven Sisters waterfall behind it. At Skageflå, look for the view where the old farm buildings are in the foreground, with the fjord and the Seven Sisters waterfall behind them. The fjord lies ahead of you to the north.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the farm buildings on the lower third, with the fjord and falls filling the upper part. Keep the phone level so the water stays flat. For a different view of the same fjord, try Flydalsjuvet or Ørnesvingen, which show the fjord's shape and Geiranger village.

Settings tip: Tap on the fjord to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the sky and the white water of the falls keep their detail. Take a Live Photo too: it can catch the movement of the water.

Best time: Early in the season, May to July, when the falls carry more water. Go in the morning or late afternoon for softer light.

3Geiranger village and harbour

Geiranger is a small village at the head of the Geirangerfjord, in an area of spectacular scenery. Steep mountain sides rise straight from the water, and the harbour, the village buildings and the fjord all fit into one frame. That makes it an easy place to get a good phone photo without any fuss.

The village is small, with a population of 215 in 2023, but it is busy in summer. Between 140 and 180 cruise ships call during the four-month season, and the Geiranger–Hellesylt ferry also stops here. A cruise ship alongside can give you a sense of scale against the mountains, though it can also fill the harbour, so decide whether you want it in the picture. The port has a cruise terminal and a floating Seawalk pier, built in 2013, which is 236 metres long.

Face east and take in the whole scene first, then move in for details. The area is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, so the wider view is worth keeping in a few of your shots, with the mountain walls on both sides of the frame.

Where it is: Geiranger village sits at the end of the Geirangerfjorden in Stranda Municipality, Møre og Romsdal, western Norway. The port has a cruise terminal and a Seawalk.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the whole village, harbour and mountain walls. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a more natural view of the scene. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out details in the distance.

Where to stand: Stand about 150 m west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face east.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and, if water is in your shot, put the waterline on the lower third line, so the mountains and sky get the rest of the frame. Use the shoreline or any strong line in the scene to lead the eye into the picture. For a wide fjord view, try panorama and move slowly and steadily from left to right.

Settings tip: Tap the water or the village to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the sky looks too bright. Take a few frames in case boats or people move.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light. The tourist season runs from May to early September.
