The 3 free photo spots

1Wat Phra That Doi Suthep

Wat Phra That Doi Suthep is a Theravada Buddhist temple on the mountain of Doi Suthep, about 15 km from Chiang Mai city and 1,073 metres above sea level. Its gold-plated chedi stands 24 metres tall and is the most sacred part of the grounds. It photographs well because you get gold spires, ornate roofs, shrines and statues all within a few steps of each other, and views of the city on the far side of the temple. Tradition says the temple was founded in 1383, when the first stupa was built, and the story of a white elephant carrying a relic up the mountain is told here. Start with the chedi, then step back to include the layered roofs and the pediments, which are the most decorated parts of the buildings. Turn away from the temple for the city view. Inside the halls the light is low, so give your phone a moment to adjust. Remember that this is a working place of worship: dress modestly, take your shoes off where asked, and keep other visitors and worshippers out of the way of your shots.

Where it is: The temple is on the slopes of Doi Suthep, the mountain above Chiang Mai city, about 15 km from the city.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) for the golden chedi and the main buildings. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) for the courtyard and to fit the tall chedi in from close range. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for roof details, chofas, Naga figures and the view of the city.

Where to stand: Stand about 10 m south-west of the subject, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the chedi on one of the vertical lines so its spire stays straight. Use a doorway, a shrine roof or a statue in the foreground to add depth. For the city view, face away from the temple and keep some railing or roof edge in the corner to show you are high up.

Settings tip: Tap on the gold surface to focus, then lower the exposure slider a little so the gilding keeps its detail instead of turning pale. In the darker halls, tap on a mural, hold the phone steady and let night mode work if it switches on.

Best time: Early in the morning is usually calmer and gives a clearer view over the city. Late afternoon can give warm light on the gold. Midday is the busiest and the brightest.

2Chiang Mai Night Bazaar

Chiang Mai Night Bazaar is a stretch of market stalls and buildings just east of the old city moat, between the moat and the Ping River. It is known for handicrafts and portrait paintings, with jewellery, toys and clothing alongside. It is described as one of the most popular tourist attractions in Chiang Mai. It began under Chinese merchants, then grew as more commercial buildings went up, and today it has many owners, most of them Thai.

It photographs well after dark, when string lights, neon signs and crowds give you plenty of colour and movement. One visitor guide suggests an elevated walkway beside Kalare Night Bazaar, which looks down on the open-air food court, its stage, the string lights and the people below. Treat it as something to look for nearby rather than a fixed spot. The same guide suggests around 8 PM, when the live band plays and the lights come on.

If you want a second angle, walk to Changklan Road, where a long lane of stalls sits under strung lights and neon signs. Another option is the Iron Bridge, for a quieter riverside view toward the market's night lights; check the angle on the map before you head there. Photography is generally fine, but ask before taking close-ups of stallholders. Keep your phone strap on and a hand free in the crowd.

Where it is: The Night Bazaar is in central Chiang Mai, directly east of the city moat and west of the Ping River. It runs along Chang Khlan Road between Tha Phae Road and Sridonchai Road.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) to capture the stalls, lights and crowd in one frame, or the food court, stage and crowd if you find the walkway. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) in the narrow stall lane so the lights overhead fit in. Use telephoto (2x or more, depending on your phone) for details such as lanterns or the band on stage.

Where to stand: Stand at Kalare Night Bazaar (shoot from the elevated walkway beside the building). An elevated look down onto the open-air food court and its stage, with string lights and the crowd below. It is not on the Kalare walkway. One visitor guide suggests an elevated walkway beside Kalare Night Bazaar with a view down onto the open-air food court and its stage. Look for it nearby, and check on the day for its exact location.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the busiest part of the scene, such as the stage or food court if you can see it, on one third of the frame, with the string lights across the top. Keep any railing out of the corners. For a different view, try Changklan Road, with the stalls leading away from you under the lights, or the Iron Bridge for a quieter view over the river.

Settings tip: Tap on the brightest part of the scene, then slide the exposure down a little so the lights keep their colour and do not turn white. Hold the phone steady against a solid surface if there is one. If night mode is on, wait for it to finish, as moving people may blur. Live Photos can capture the band and crowd atmosphere.

Best time: Around 8 PM, when the live band plays and the string lights are on.

4Wat Phra Singh

Wat Phra Singh is one of the best-known temples in Chiang Mai's Old City, and it rewards a slow wander with a phone in hand. King Phayu of Lan Na founded it in 1345 to hold his father's ashes, and it later became the home of the revered Phra Buddha Sihing image. The compound has several buildings worth framing. The main hall, Viharn Luang, dates from 1924 and is 56 metres long, with a layered Lanna-style teak roof. Viharn Lai Kham, built between 1815 and 1821, is smaller and has gilded decoration. The photo most people come for is the golden bell-shaped chedi (Chedi Than Chang Lom), with elephant reliefs around its base. Gold looks warmest when the sun is low, so late afternoon suits it best, and the white and gold shapes stand out against a blue sky. From the phone icon on the map, aim north at the chedi and let the sky fill the top of the frame. Then walk round for two other exterior shots: the gilded stucco panels on the lower facade of Viharn Lai Kham, and the raised wooden scripture library, Ho Trai, on its stone plinth. Photography inside Viharn Lai Kham is restricted and flash is banned, and Ho Trai is kept locked, so plan on exterior shots only.

Where it is: Wat Phra Singh is in the western part of Chiang Mai's Old City, inside the moat and city walls. The temple compound is large, about 300 metres across.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the whole chedi. Switch to telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the elephant reliefs and the gold detail. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only for the long temple roofs or Ho Trai, and keep the phone level so the lines don't lean.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. From here you see the golden bell-shaped Chedi Than Chang Lom with the elephant reliefs at its base, lit by low sun.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the chedi on one side so there is room for sky. Keep the elephants in the lower third. For a second angle, try the gilded panels on the lower facade of Viharn Lai Kham in direct light, or Ho Trai, where the pond beside it may give a reflection (check on arrival).

Settings tip: Tap the gold part of the chedi to set focus, then pull the exposure slider down a little so the gold keeps its colour and detail instead of going pale. Turn flash off.

Best time: Late afternoon, about 4:30 to 5:30 pm, when the low sun warms the gold. Some visitors prefer early morning (about 7:30 to 9:00), which is also worth trying. Songkran in April makes the grounds very crowded.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Chiang Mai?

The first three on this map are Doi Suthep, Night Bazaar and Wat Chedi Luang. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 18.

Where should I stand to photograph Doi Suthep?

Stand about 10 m south-west of Doi Suthep, at the phone icon on the map, and face north-east. Use the default lens (1x) for the golden chedi and the main buildings.

When is the best time to photograph Night Bazaar?

Around 8 PM, when the live band plays and the string lights are on.

How do I get to Wat Phra Singh?

No station or tram stop serves this part of the Old City, so plan to reach the west side of the Old City, inside the moat, on foot or by local road transport.
