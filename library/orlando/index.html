The 3 free photo spots

1Pulse (nightclub)

Pulse was a nightclub at 1912 South Orange Avenue, opened on 2 July 2004 by Barbara Poma and Ron Legler. Poma named it in memory of her brother John, who died from AIDS in 1991. On 12 June 2016 the club was the scene of a mass shooting in which 49 people were killed and 58 injured. It remains the deadliest incident of violence against LGBTQ people in US history. The site is now a place of remembrance, and visitors come to pay their respects as much as to take photographs.

The best-known frame has been the building wrapped in a wall of photographs, with the illuminated Pulse road sign close by. A quieter frame shows the names wall and the club's original exterior walls. The side-street corner described below gives a more open view than the Orange Avenue frontage, which helps on a phone, where you can't step back far.

Please note that the City of Orlando bought the property in October 2023 and plans a permanent memorial. Demolition of the building began on 18 March 2026. What you can see, and which parts of the site are open, may therefore differ from this description. Check the official site before you go. Keep your photography simple and respectful, and leave other visitors out of your frame where you can.

Where it is: Pulse stands at 1912 South Orange Avenue in Orlando, at the corner with Esther Street.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the building and wall of photographs. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if you need to fit in more of the frontage, and keep the building away from the edges of the frame, where lines can bend. Telephoto (2x-5x) is good for a close, quiet frame of the names wall without walking up to it.

Where to stand: Stand at the corner of South Orange Avenue and West Esther Street (Southwest corner of the site, at the Esther Street entrance). The building and wall of photographs seen from the side street, with a more open angle than the Orange Avenue frontage. From here the building and its wall of photographs are seen from the side street, with a more open angle than the Orange Avenue frontage. The old building lies to the north-west of you.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the building on one of the lines so the wall of photographs leads the eye along the frame. For an alternative, go to the Orange Avenue side to take in the building wrapped by the photo ribbon wall with the illuminated Pulse sign nearby. For a close frame, use the names wall and the original exterior walls.

Settings tip: Tap the building to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky and the lit sign don't wash out. After dark, switch on night mode and hold the phone steady against your body or a rail. Skip portrait mode and Live Photos here; a plain, still photograph suits the place better.

Best time: Daylight gives the clearest view of the wall of photographs and the names. Late evening suits the illuminated Pulse sign. Whenever you go, check the official site first, because the site is changing.

2Camping World Stadium

Camping World Stadium is Orlando's big bowl-shaped football ground, in the West Lakes part of Downtown Orlando. It began life in 1936 as Orlando Stadium, built as a Depression-era public works project, and went on to be known as the Tangerine Bowl and the Florida Citrus Bowl. It has since grown a long way beyond its first 8,900 seats, and by 1989 it could hold 65,438. It still hosts the Citrus Bowl, and it was one of the venues for the 1994 FIFA World Cup. A good place to photograph the stadium from is Lake Lorna Doone Park, just north of it. From the park's lakeside, the stadium rises across the water, with the trail and greenery as foreground. That foreground gives the picture some depth, so it looks like a place rather than just a big wall of seating. If the lake is calm, you may also get some reflection. A second option is Church Street Overlook, which has a lake-level view across Lake Lorna Doone and a citrus-tree design to frame the shot. The stadium is being renovated, with building work focused on the north end until summer 2027, so expect cranes or hoardings in some views. The park's page has also shown a temporary closure notice, so check the park's current status before you set out.

Where it is: In the West Lakes neighbourhood of Downtown Orlando.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the whole stadium, the lake and the path in one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, less stretched look. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the rim of the stadium and the details of the structure.

Where to stand: Stand in Lake Lorna Doone Park (north end of the stadium, across the lake). The stadium rising across the lake, with the park's lakeside trail and greenery in the foreground. The nearby Lake Lorna Doone Park and its trail give you the view, with the stadium rising across the water and greenery in front of you.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and place the water's edge on the lower line so the stadium sits along the upper third. Keep the lakeside trail or some greenery in the bottom corner for depth. If the north-side building work spoils the wide shot, try a tighter 2x-3x frame on the part of the stadium that looks clear. For another angle, try Church Street Overlook, where the overlook's citrus-tree design can frame the stadium.

Settings tip: Tap the stadium to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky keeps its colour and the stadium doesn't wash out. Hold the phone with both hands and keep it level against the gridlines.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon is usually kinder, because facing south around midday puts the sun in front of you. On a calm day the lake may give a reflection.

3Orlando Science Center

The Orlando Science Center is a hands-on science museum in Loch Haven Park, and its domed, curved front makes a clean, simple photo. The museum began life as the Central Florida Museum, which opened in the park in 1960. The building you see today opened on 1 February 1997 and is about six times larger than the original. Inside are a giant-screen CineDome theatre and an observatory on the sixth floor with Florida's largest public refractor telescope, so the building is bigger than it looks from the lawn.

The best view is from the park lawn opposite the domed front. Large oak trees stand in the foreground, which gives your photo depth and a sense of scale, and the building sits neatly in the middle of the frame. Stand square-on and the symmetry does most of the work for you. If you want a second angle, the curved side of the building catches morning light when seen from the parking area side. The outdoor Finfrock Terrace is said to have views of the downtown skyline behind the building, but it may only be reachable through the museum or at private events, so check access before you go.

Keep the phone level, use the gridlines to line up the dome, and let the oak branches frame the top of the picture.

Where it is: The Orlando Science Center stands in Loch Haven Park in Orlando, Florida. The lawn opposite its domed front is the place to take the photo.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) so the dome and the oak trees both fit naturally. If the building does not fit, step back a few paces before you use the ultra-wide (0.5x), which can stretch the edges. Use 2x if you want a tighter shot of the dome itself.

Where to stand: Stand in Loch Haven Park (lawn across from the domed front facade). The domed facade of the science center framed with large oak trees in the foreground for depth and scale. The building is directly to the east. From here you see the domed facade framed by large oak trees in the foreground.

Framing: Centre the dome and switch on the gridlines so the building sits symmetrically. Let the oak branches fill the top or the sides of the frame to add depth and scale. For a different view, try the curved side of the building from the parking area side, or the skyline from Finfrock Terrace if you can get access.

Settings tip: Tap the dome to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little if the sky is bright, so the building does not turn dark against it.

Best time: Morning light suits the curved facade when seen from the parking area side. The lawn view is worth trying at any time of day when the building is evenly lit.
