The 3 free photo spots

1Marine Drive

Marine Drive is the 3-kilometre curved promenade along Mumbai's bay, known as the Queen's Necklace. The name comes from the streetlights, which look like a string of pearls when seen from above at night. The road and promenade were built in 1940, and the sweep of the bay and the palm-lined walkway make it easy to photograph. The wide concrete road follows the shoreline in a gentle banana shape. Behind it stand pale art deco apartment blocks. Some of the earliest, such as Kapur Mahal, Zaver Mahal and Keval Mahal, were built between 1937 and 1939 and belong to the UNESCO-listed Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensembles of Mumbai.

The best way to see the whole curve is from above. Pherozeshah Mehta Gardens, also called the Hanging Gardens, on Malabar Hill are the recommended elevated viewpoint. From the terraces you look back across the bay and see the full C-shaped arc, with the Arabian Sea beside it and the south Mumbai skyline behind. Go around sunset, when the streetlights start to come on and the curve begins to glow. If you would like a different angle, Kamala Nehru Park nearby gives a similar high view, and Girgaon Chowpatty shows the curve running away from the beach.

Keep your phone steady against a wall or railing, and take a few frames as the light changes.

Where it is: Marine Drive runs along the bay in south Mumbai, between Nariman Point at the southern tip and Girgaon Chowpatty and Malabar Hill at the northern end.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to take in the whole curve of the bay and the sky. Then try the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pull the lines of streetlights and buildings closer together. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) only if a terrace railing or trees block the view, because it makes the distant curve look small.

Where to stand: Stand in Pherozeshah Mehta Gardens (Hanging Gardens terraces on the western ridge of Malabar Hill, looking back toward the bay). The whole C-shaped Marine Drive curve along the bay, with the Arabian Sea and the south Mumbai skyline behind it, often seen best at sunset. Stand on a terrace and look back across the bay towards Marine Drive. From here the photo shows the whole C-shaped curve, with the Arabian Sea and the south Mumbai skyline behind it.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the curve of the road along one of the lower lines, with the sea and sky filling the rest. Keep the horizon level. If you want a different view, Kamala Nehru Park has a high view with the Old Woman's Shoe structure in the foreground, and Girgaon Chowpatty shows the curve from beach level.

Settings tip: Tap on the brightest part of the sky to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the lights stay sharp and don't glare. Once the streetlights are on, try night mode. Rest your elbows on a wall and hold still while it works. Take a few shots, because moving people and traffic can blur single frames.

Best time: Sunset, as the streetlights come on. The sun's position along Marine Drive shifts through the year, so arrive early and watch how the light falls.

2Gateway of India

The Gateway of India is a tall basalt arch on Mumbai's waterfront, finished in 1924 and standing about 26 metres high. It was built to mark the 1911 landing of King George V, the first British monarch to visit India, and it later became the ceremonial exit for the last British troops in 1948. The design by George Wittet mixes Indo-Saracenic style with Gujarati details, so look for the fine stone latticework around the smaller arches and the four turrets above the central arch.

This avoids the dead-centre selfie view. The arch sits against the red dome of the Taj Mahal Palace hotel, and the statue on the plaza gives you something in the foreground. Guides say the raised plaza gives a slightly higher angle.

The area is busy with locals, vendors and other photographers, so be patient and wait for a gap. Security is tight here. Hold your phone in your hand rather than bringing a tripod, and don't fly a drone. If you want the arch framed symmetrically against the harbour, walk down to the Gateway itself. One guide suggests Apollo Bunder gives a wider harbour view that takes in the arch, the hotel and the boats, but verify this on the ground.

Where it is: The Gateway of India stands on the Apollo Bunder waterfront in Mumbai, overlooking the Arabian Sea and Mumbai Harbour. It stands opposite the Taj Mahal Palace hotel.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to get the arch and the hotel dome together. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) if the crowds push you close, but keep the arch away from the edges of the frame, where it can look stretched. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for the latticework and the turrets.

Where to stand: Stand in Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Statue Plaza (left of the Gateway when facing the sea). The arch with the Taj Mahal Palace red dome beside it, with the statue adding a foreground element. On the left of the Gateway when you face the sea. Look south-east and you will see the arch with the red dome of the Taj Mahal Palace beside it. The statue can sit in the foreground.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the arch on one third of the frame and the hotel dome on the other, with the statue low down as a foreground element. Keep the phone level so the arch doesn't lean. For a symmetrical view of the arch against the water, walk down to the Gateway itself. One guide suggests Apollo Bunder gives a wider harbour panorama, but verify this on the ground.

Settings tip: Tap the arch to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky and the hotel's red dome don't wash out. Take a few frames, because people keep walking through.

Best time: Sunset, when the hotel facade catches warm light.

3Taj Mahal Palace Hotel

The Taj Mahal Palace is the grand old hotel beside the Gateway of India in Colaba. It opened on 16 December 1903 and was commissioned by Jamsetji Tata. Its Indo-Saracenic style, with a Moorish-looking dome and a long, detailed façade, gives it plenty to photograph. The best-known frame is from the harbour-facing plaza in front of the Gateway of India. From there the arch sits in the foreground, boats and open water lie beyond it, and the hotel dome rises beyond it to the west. The arch and the dome balance each other, so one picture shows two landmarks and the harbour setting.

The building has two parts: the original Palace of 1903 and the separate Tower, which opened in 1972. The Palace was once entered from the land-facing side, where the pool now is, so the harbour side you see from the plaza was not originally the main front. A second option is the Apollo Bandar Promenade, which shows the façade and domes straight on, with the Gateway also in the frame from the east. Check that name on the map before you rely on it.

The plaza is a security site, so keep your phone in your hand and work quickly and politely. Photos inside the hotel are reportedly limited to guests, so plan on exterior shots only.

Where it is: In Colaba, at the southern end of Mumbai, right next to the Gateway of India and facing the harbour.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the arch, the water and the hotel dome in one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, more natural view. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for details of the dome and façade.

Where to stand: Stand in Gateway of India (harbour-facing plaza in front of the arch, looking south-west toward the hotel). The arch in the foreground with harbour boats and water beyond, with the hotel's Moorish dome rising to the south. From here you get the arch in the foreground, harbour boats and water beyond, and the Moorish dome rising beyond it to the west. Moving a little nearer the jetty brings both landmarks into one shot.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the arch on one side and the hotel dome on the other, so they balance. Keep some water and boats in the lower part of the frame. For a straight-on view of the façade and domes, try the Apollo Bandar Promenade as an alternative, where the Gateway also appears in the frame.

Settings tip: Tap the hotel to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky and floodlights keep their colour. After dark, use night mode and hold your phone steady against a wall or railing. Wide shots work better handheld than zoomed in.

Best time: Sunrise, for a quieter plaza and warm light on the arch. Blue hour is the other good moment, when the arch and hotel are both floodlit.
