The 3 free photo spots

1Fort Napoléon des Saintes

Fort Napoléon sits on a bluff called the Mire, 119 m above sea level, on Terre-de-Haut in the Îles des Saintes. That height is the reason to come. The fort looks out over the bay and the neighbouring islands, and its high walls and bastions give you strong shapes to frame a view with. A first fort, Fort Louis, was built here between 1777 and 1779. British forces destroyed it in 1809, and the present fortress was built on its ruins. Work started in 1816, and the bastioned design was finished in 1867. It never saw battle and was used as a penitentiary instead. Today it holds a museum about the history, culture and environment of the Saintes, and a botanical garden of local succulent plants and iguanas. For photos, the northern bastion is the best place to start, with a wide view over the bay and the islands around it. The walls and the cactus garden also make a good foreground, with the sea behind. Take a few wide shots for the view, then a few close ones of the plants and stonework. Check which parts of the grounds are open to visitors when you arrive.

Where it is: The fort stands on the Mire bluff on Terre-de-Haut, in the Îles des Saintes, Guadeloupe. It looks over the bay and the approach to the roadstead.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the bay and the sweep of the walls. Use the default lens (1x) for the bastion and the cactus garden. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the islands in the distance.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. For the wide view across the bay and the surrounding islands, walk to the northern bastion, which is the best vantage point within the fort.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the horizon on the top or bottom third line, keeping it level. Use a bit of the bastion wall at the edge of the frame to add depth. For a second picture, point down at the fort's walls and cactus garden in the foreground, with the open sea and bay behind them.

Settings tip: Tap on the far islands to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the sky stays blue. Try panorama mode along the bastion, moving slowly and keeping the phone level. Hold your phone away from the wall edge when you lean out.

Best time: Morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and clearer colour on the sea. Midday sun can wash out the view.

2Notre Dame de l'Assomption, Les Saintes

Notre-Dame de l'Assomption is the parish church of Terre-de-Haut, an island of Les Saintes. It stands in the village a short walk from the harbour, which makes it an easy first photo after you step off the ferry. Check on the ground that you are in the right spot. From there you can photograph the church front with some of the village around it, so the building has context.

The parish was dedicated to the Virgin of the Assumption to mark the French victory over English troops on 15 August 1666, and Our Lady of the Assumption became the patron saint of Terre-de-Haut. The church has been listed as a national heritage monument (Monument historique) since 31 December 1979. Its bell tower was destroyed in the 2004 earthquake and later rebuilt. The fourth of its four bells was installed on 23 April 2006 at the end of that work.

For your phone, keep the whole front in one frame and watch for people and scooters passing in the foreground. If you want a different angle, the Route de Marigot shows the church front and side from the road. Once you have the wide view, step closer for details of the tower and the entrance.

Where it is: The church is in rue Jean Calot, in the Fond-du-Curé quarter of Terre-de-Haut, in the village close to the harbour. Check on the ground.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the church front. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if people or parked vehicles stop you stepping back far enough. Use the telephoto (2x to 5x, depending on your phone) for the bell tower and the entrance.

Where to stand: Stand in Place du Gouverneur Lion. A view of the church's front from the ferry-landing square, based on a photo filed under that square's name. Place du Gouverneur Lion is the likely nearby square, but check on the ground. If the spot is busy, the Route de Marigot is a second position, showing the front and one side from the road.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the church on one of the vertical lines, with the tower upright and not leaning. Keep a little street or square in the bottom of the frame to show how close the church is to the harbour. Take one wide shot, then one zoomed shot of the tower. If you try the Route de Marigot, use the angle to include some of the side of the building.

Settings tip: Tap on the church wall to focus, then slide the exposure down a little if the sky is bright, so the tower does not turn white. Check the verticals in the preview before you move on.

Best time: Morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer harsh shadows on the front. Midday sun can be flat and bright, so check the result on your screen.

3Pompierre Beach

Pompierre Beach is a long curve of pale sand on the north-east side of Terre-de-Haut, in the Îles des Saintes. It runs for about 800 metres along Pompierre Bay. It is the beach most sought after by tourists, and it is planted with coconut trees. A small islet, Les Roches percées, sits offshore and shelters the bay from the trade winds. The rocks also give your photos something to look at beyond the water. The bay is a classified natural site under a French law of 2 May 1930, and motor boats and sailing boats may not enter or anchor in it. That keeps the water free of boats, so your frame stays clean and uncluttered. For a phone, the best picture is simple: palms and carbets (the small shelters) in the foreground, pale sand leading the eye, then the turquoise cove with the pierced rocks behind. Walk a little way along the sand until a palm trunk or a carbet sits on one side of the frame, then shoot out to sea. If you are up for a hike, the Batterie Caroline gives a higher view down over the bay and its beach, with Dominica and Marie-Galante in the distance. The trail is on foot only and you will want sturdy shoes and water. Check locally for any closures or restrictions before you go.

Where it is: Pompierre Beach is on the north-east coast of Terre-de-Haut, along Pompierre Bay, in the Pompierre area. The islet of Les Roches percées lies just offshore.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the sweep of sand, palms and bay in one frame. Switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer view of the cove with the rocks behind. Use the telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out the pierced rocks and compress the distance.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject (on the sand, facing out toward the bay and the offshore rocks). The white sand, coconut palms and carbets in the foreground, with the turquoise cove and the pierced rocks behind them. From here you see white sand, coconut palms and carbets in the foreground, with the turquoise cove and the pierced rocks behind them.

Framing: Switch on the gridlines and place the horizon on the upper third line, so the sand and palms get most of the frame. Put a palm trunk or a carbet at one edge to give depth, and let the line of the shore lead towards the rocks. As an alternative, the Batterie Caroline gives an elevated view down over the bay and its beach.

Settings tip: Tap on the bright sea or sand to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a touch so the white sand does not wash out. Keep the horizon level using the gridlines.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon usually gives softer light and fewer people on the sand, though the beach is described as fairly uncrowded in general.
