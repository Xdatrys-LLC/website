The 3 free photo spots

1Narikala Fortress

Narikala is an old hilltop fortress above Tbilisi, looking down on the Mtkvari (Kura) River. It stands on a steep hill between the sulphur baths and the botanical gardens, and it has two walled sections. Most of the stonework you see today dates from the 16th and 17th centuries, though a fortress has stood here since about the year 364. Parts were damaged by an earthquake in 1827 and later pulled down. In the lower court is the church of St Nicholas, built in 1996-1997 to replace a 13th-century church lost to fire.

It photographs well because the weathered walls give you strong foreground shapes, with the city and river spread out behind them. A single wall or tower with open sky above it makes a simple, clear picture, and a wider shot of the walls climbing the slope shows the scale of the place.

For a phone, start with the default lens (1x) and use gridlines to keep the walls straight. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the full sweep of masonry, and use the telephoto or zoom for details such as the church or a single tower. Take a few steps between shots, as the view changes quickly on this slope.

Where it is: Narikala sits on a steep hill above the old part of Tbilisi, between the sulphur baths and the botanical gardens, overlooking the Mtkvari (Kura) River.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the walls and the church. Use the ultra-wide (0.5x) for the whole sweep of fortifications, and the telephoto/zoom (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for towers and rooftops in the city below.

Where to stand: Stand on Metekhi Bridge (middle of the bridge, looking upstream toward the fortress). Narikala rising over the Old Town rooftops and the river, with Metekhi Church in the foreground. From here the main body of the walls lies to the south-west, so face that way for your main shot.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and line a wall or tower up with one of them so the stonework stays straight. Keep some sky above the walls, and for a wider shot use the ultra-wide (0.5x) with the near wall low in the frame. Stay clear of the edges of walls and steep drops when stepping back.

Settings tip: Tap on the stonework to focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. If you want a wide view along the walls, try panorama and move slowly.

Best time: Early morning or the hour before sunset, when the light is softer and the stone looks warmer. Midday sun can make the sky look washed out.

2Metekhi Church

Metekhi Church stands right on the edge of a cliff above the Kura river, looking across to the Old Town. It was built to look like a continuation of the rock beneath it, and that is why it shows up so clearly in city views. The church you see today mostly dates from between 1278 and 1289, in the reign of King Demetrius II. Oral tradition traces the site back to the 5th century, though there is no firm evidence for that. The building is small, about 20 by 16 metres, and slightly tall for its footprint. Its history is a varied one: a prison in the Russian Empire period, a store for museum collections, and a youth theatre in the 1970s.

The churchyard terrace next to the King Vakhtang Gorgasali statue is the classic free photo spot. From the cliff edge you look down over the Old Town rooftops, the river and the domed sulphur baths, with the statue as a strong foreground. Remember that the courtyard views are mostly of the city rather than of the church itself. For a shot of the church on its cliff, use Metekhi Bridge, where the Old Town and Narikala Fortress rise behind it, or the Bridge of Peace. On the terrace, keep the statue on one side of the frame and let the river and rooftops fill the rest. Late afternoon gives warm light, and early morning is quieter.

Where it is: Metekhi Church is in Tbilisi on the Metekhi Cliff, on the left bank of the Kura (Mtkvari) river. It faces the Old Town from across the water.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the terrace view. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) to fit the statue and the rooftops in one frame. If your phone has a telephoto (2x-5x), use it to pick out the domed sulphur baths or the Old Town roofs.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. For the view, walk to the churchyard terrace on the cliff edge beside the King Vakhtang Gorgasali statue. From the terrace the photo shows the Old Town rooftops, the river, the domed sulphur baths and the horse-and-rider statue. The church itself is best photographed from Metekhi Bridge.

Framing: Switch on gridlines and put the statue on one third of the frame, with the river and rooftops filling the rest. Hold the phone low and close to the statue for a stronger foreground. For a picture of the church itself, walk to Metekhi Bridge, where the church sits in front of the Old Town and Narikala Fortress. The Bridge of Peace also gives a framed view, but check the angle when you get there.

Settings tip: Tap on the rooftops to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down a little so the sky keeps its colour. Wind can shake the phone on the exposed terrace, so hold it with both hands and take a couple of frames.

Best time: Late afternoon or sunset for warm light. Early morning is quieter.

3Freedom Square, Tbilisi

Freedom Square sits at the eastern end of Rustaveli Avenue, the main street of Tbilisi, and the gilded figure of St George slaying the dragon stands in the middle of it. For a second view, walk to Rustaveli Avenue and look east, where the square and the St George column are framed at the end of a tree-lined street, so you see the monument as the city's main street leads to it. The monument is a recent arrival. A large statue of Lenin stood here from 1956 until it was symbolically pulled down in August 1991. The St George monument by Zurab Tsereteli was unveiled in the same place on 23 November 2006. The place was first called Freedom Square in 1918, and six streets branch out from it, including Rustaveli Avenue and Pushkin Street. The square is a busy roundabout, so take your photos from the pavements and keep your phone and bag close, as guides warn about pickpockets. Look for a clear gap between passers-by and keep the column in the centre of the frame. If you shoot from the avenue, let the line of trees lead the eye towards it. If you want a quieter, higher view, the top of the monument appears level with the old city's rooftops from the Betlemi Street stairs. Confirm the exact steps on the map once you are there.

Where it is: In the centre of Tbilisi, Georgia, at the eastern end of Rustaveli Avenue. The Tbilisi City Assembly and the Courtyard by Marriott Tbilisi are among the buildings on the square.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) to show the monument in its setting. Switch to the ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want more of the buildings and trees, but keep the column away from the edges, where it can look stretched. Try telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for a tighter shot of the monument.

Where to stand: Stand on Shota Rustaveli Avenue (looking back east along the avenue toward the square). The square and the St. George column framed at the end of the tree-lined avenue, with the square seen from the avenue's length. From there you look south-west towards the St George column. For the separate avenue view, stand on Rustaveli Avenue, on the pavement, and look east towards the square, where the column is framed at the end of the tree-lined street.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and put the column on the central vertical line. If you are on the avenue, let the trees run down both sides to lead the eye. Wait for a gap in the traffic and passers-by. For a different angle, the Betlemi Street stairs show the top of the monument level with the old city's rooftops.

Settings tip: Tap on the column to set focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the gold doesn't wash out against the sky. After dark, use night mode and hold the phone steady against a wall or railing.

Best time: After dark, when the column is lit, is the most striking time. Early morning is likely to be quieter for clear views along the avenue.

Questions

What are the top photo spots in Tbilisi?

The first three on this map are Narikala Fortress, Metekhi Church and Freedom Square. They are ranked by how often people search for them. The full map has 18.

Where should I stand to photograph Narikala Fortress?

Stand on Metekhi Bridge (middle of the bridge, looking upstream toward the fortress). Start with the default lens (1x) for the walls and the church.

When is the best time to photograph Metekhi Church?

Late afternoon or sunset for warm light. Early morning is quieter.

How do I get to Freedom Square?

Start from the centre of Tbilisi and head for Freedom Square at the eastern end of Rustaveli Avenue.
