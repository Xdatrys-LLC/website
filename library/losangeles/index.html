The 3 free photo spots

1Venice Beach

Venice is a seaside neighbourhood of Los Angeles, and the neon-style Venice sign on Windward Avenue is the picture most visitors come away with. Seen from across the street, the sign sits in front of an arched colonnade, with the neighbourhood entrance behind it. The arches give the shot a clear frame and a bit of old-resort character, which is why it works so well on a phone.

A little history helps explain the mood. Developer Abbot Kinney founded Venice in 1905 as a beach resort modelled on the Italian city, with canals, a pier and an arcaded business street in Venetian style. It was an independent city until 1926, when Los Angeles annexed it. Today the long Ocean Front Walk promenade, about 2.5 miles (4 km), is lined with performers, fortune-tellers and vendors.

For the photo, cross Windward Avenue so the sign and arches fill the background and the crowd stays behind you. The junction is busy, so keep an eye on the cars and don't step back into the road while framing. If you'd like a wide beach view with the coastline and palm trees as well, Venice Pier is another standard view. Muscle Beach, with its outdoor gym, is another popular picture.

Where it is: In Venice, a coastal neighbourhood in the Westside region of Los Angeles. The sign marks the entrance to the neighbourhood at Windward Avenue, and the subject is near the boardwalk.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Use the default lens (1x) so the sign and arches look natural. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) only if the pavement is too narrow to step back, and keep the sign away from the edges of the frame, where it can stretch.

Where to stand: Stand at the corner of Pacific Avenue and Windward Avenue (stand across Windward Avenue, facing inland). The neon-style Venice sign framed against the arched colonnade, with the neighborhood entrance behind it. Cross Windward Avenue at the sign, so the neon-style Venice sign is framed against the arched colonnade, with the neighbourhood entrance behind it.

Framing: Turn on the gridlines and put the sign on one of the lines, with the arches running behind it. Keep the crowd behind you rather than in the picture. For a wide beach view with palm trees, try Venice Pier as an alternative.

Settings tip: Tap the sign to focus, then slide the exposure down a little so the neon colours stay rich and the sky doesn't wash out. Take a few frames between passing cars.

Best time: Early morning, when the light comes from the east and the midday glare hasn't yet set in.

2Marina del Rey Harbor

Marina del Rey is a huge, calm harbour on the Los Angeles coast, just south-east of Venice. It is North America's largest man-made small-craft harbour, with slips for roughly 5,000 boats. Before it was built, the land was a salt marsh fed by Ballona Creek. The harbour was formally dedicated on 10 April 1965. That history is hard to imagine today, when masts and piers fill the water in every direction.

It photographs well because the scene is wide and layered. Rows of sailboats and piers sit in the foreground, flat water reflects the sky, and the Palos Verdes hills are often visible in the distance. The best easy view from land is the waterfront lawn at Burton W. Chace Park, where you get open views across the water and boats.

For phone photos, think of it as a landscape with a lot of repeating shapes. Boats and piers make natural lines that lead the eye across the picture. Early morning gives soft light. Sunset gives a warm sky that reflects in the harbour. If you want a second angle, Fisherman's Village has brightly painted buildings and a lighthouse beside the boats, and Marina Beach has very still water that mirrors the sky. Parking at the park and at Fisherman's Village is paid and tends to fill up at weekends, so an earlier start helps.

Where it is: Marina del Rey Harbor lies south-east of Venice and north of Playa del Rey, close to the mouth of Ballona Creek. The best land-based view is from the waterfront lawn of Burton W. Chace Park on the marina's edge.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the ultra-wide (0.5x) to take in the whole harbour, then switch to the default lens (1x) for a calmer, more natural view. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) to pick out a single boat, pier or the distant hills.

Where to stand: Stand in Burton W. Chace Park (waterfront lawn along the marina edge). Sailboats and piers spread across the harbor, with the Palos Verdes hills often visible in the background. Chace Park, on the waterfront lawn along the marina edge. From here you look out over sailboats and piers spread across the harbour, with the Palos Verdes hills often visible behind. The harbour area marked on the map is to the north-west of you.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the waterline on the lower or upper third, depending on whether the water or the sky is more interesting. Use a pier or a row of boats as a leading line from the bottom of the frame into the distance. For a different view, Fisherman's Village shows painted buildings and a lighthouse against the boats, and Marina Beach gives a quiet, mirror-like water scene.

Settings tip: Tap on the boats to focus, then slide the exposure down a little at sunset so the sky keeps its colour. Try a panorama along the waterline, moving slowly and keeping the phone level. Keep an eye out for moving boats, which can blur in a panorama.

Best time: Early morning for soft light, or sunset for a warm evening sky over the water.

10Venice Beach Basketball Courts

The Venice Beach Basketball Courts are open-air courts beside Ocean Front Walk, the 2.5-mile (4 km) pedestrian promenade where performers, fortune-tellers and vendors set up. They are widely described as the most photographed outdoor court in the world. The reason is simple: you get a game in progress, palm trees, a big Californian sky and the beach all in one frame. Venice itself was founded in 1905 by Abbot Kinney as a seaside resort and became part of Los Angeles in 1926. The promenade still keeps some of that holiday-crowd atmosphere.

The classic picture is taken from on the courts, looking south toward the handball courts. The boardwalk and the ocean are behind you, so what you see is palm trees, open sky, the players, and the handball courts beyond. Sometimes a helicopter drifts across the distance. Keep to the side of the playing area and let the game carry on around you.

There are no lights on the courts, so plan to shoot before sunset. At weekend afternoons people gather several deep along the fence, so arriving early gives you a clearer view. Golden hour gives warm, dramatic light, while midday gives bright, clean colour. Take a few wide scene-setting shots first, then move in on the action.

Where it is: In the Venice Beach area of Los Angeles, beside Ocean Front Walk, the promenade, near 18th Avenue. The handball courts are just to the south.

Shooting it on your phone

Lens: Start with the default lens (1x) for the classic view of the courts, palms and sky. Switch to ultra-wide (0.5x) if you want the whole scene including the handball courts. Use telephoto (2x-5x, depending on your phone) for close shots of players from the edge.

Where to stand: Stand right at the subject: the phone icon on the map marks the spot. For the classic view, shoot from the courts looking south toward the handball courts, so the boardwalk and the ocean are behind you. The shot shows the courts, palm trees and clear sky, with the handball courts beyond.

Framing: Turn on gridlines and place the far hoop or the line of palm trees on a third. Keep the horizon level and leave plenty of sky. Hold the phone at chest height for a natural view, or crouch a little to make the palms look taller. If a helicopter or a dunk appears, you will get it in the frame without needing to recompose.

Settings tip: Tap on the players to set focus, then drag the exposure slider down slightly so the bright sky keeps its colour. Try Live Photos or motion to catch a jump shot, and pick the best frame afterwards.

Best time: Golden hour for warm, dramatic light, or midday for bright, clear shots. The courts have no lights, so shoot before sunset, and arrive early at weekends to beat the crowds.
