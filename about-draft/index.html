About Xdatrys

You came a long way to see it. Come home with a photo that shows how it felt.

You saved for the trip. You waited in the queue. Now you are standing in front of the Colosseum, the Eiffel Tower or Shibuya Crossing with your phone in your hand, and so is everyone else.

Xdatrys helps you get the photo you came for. Not just "I was here", but "this is what it felt like to be here".

What we believe

A great travel photo starts with where you stand. Most disappointing holiday photos are taken from the wrong spot: too close, too far, or straight on from the same place as everyone else. A few steps can change everything.

Take the photo with intent, the first time. Look at the scene before you tap the screen. Decide what you want the picture to say, then frame it.

You should not need editing skills. Few travellers have the time or the wish to dig through layers of editing menus. At most, straighten the picture or crop it. That's it.

Look, see, understand, take the photo

We call it photographing with intent. It takes seconds, and it works with any phone.

  • Look. Notice what ordinary sightseeing misses: doors, streets, signs, people, textures, small moments.
  • See. Spot what makes the scene interesting: a pattern, a contrast, a gesture, a natural frame.
  • Understand. Ask what the place is really about: its culture, its pace, its history, its atmosphere.
  • Take the photo with intent. Decide what the picture should say (wonder, calm, welcome, the buzz of a crowd) and take it.

A famous landmark is more than an object to tick off. A doorway, a market stall, an alley or a café window can say as much about a place as the monument itself.

What we make for you

Photo maps of the places travellers want to see. Each map in our Map Library shows the top sights of a city or region, with where to stand for the best photo or selfie, simple tips for your smartphone, and directions to get there. 3 spots on every map are free, with no sign-up.

The Xdatrys Maps app. Members get every spot on every map on their phone, and maps they save keep working without signal, wherever the trip takes them.

Pocket Lens. Our free newsletter, Monday to Friday, follows a real traveller to a destination: where to stand at a little-known photo spot, a tour and a hotel, and travel news worth knowing.

How we choose the spots

We start with what travellers actually look for. The sights on each map are ranked by how often people from other countries search for them, so spot 1 is the sight most visitors want to see.

We check every spot's position against OpenStreetMap, the open map of the world. Where we know exactly where the best photo is taken from, the map shows a phone icon on that spot. Many of the photos on our maps were taken by us, at the spot.

Places change. If something on a map is wrong, tell us at info@xdatrys.com and we will check it.

Made for your phone, not a big camera

Xdatrys is for travellers who take their photos on a smartphone. No tripod, no extra lenses, no editing software. Just you, your phone, and the right place to stand.

"Travel photography is the art of looking closely enough to see what a place is really saying, understanding its atmosphere and character, and photographing it with clear intention so the image carries meaning."
