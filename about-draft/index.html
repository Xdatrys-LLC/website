About Xdatrys

Better photos start before you shoot.

Capture more, miss less.

You came a long way to see it. You saved for the trip and waited in the queue, and now you are standing in front of the Colosseum, the Eiffel Tower or Shibuya Crossing with your phone in your hand ... and so is everyone else.

Xdatrys helps you with the two things that make or break a sightseeing day: knowing what to see and how to get there, and knowing where to stand when you arrive. So you come home with the photo you came for: not just "I was here", but "this is what it felt like to be here".

Plan your day around the sights that matter

Every map in our Map Library shows the top sights of a city or region, in the order travellers most want to see them. For each spot you get:

  • What it is and why it is worth your time, in a few short lines.
  • How to get there: walking directions, the nearest station, and one tap to open the route on your phone.
  • The best time to go for the best light.

Members also get ready-made day tours that link the spots into a walk, with train or taxi hops where a walk is too long.

Then get the photo you came for

When you arrive, the map shows you where to stand. On spots where we know exactly where the best photo is taken from, a blue phone icon marks the place, with an arrow showing which way to face. Every spot also has:

  • Shooting it on your phone: which lens to use (0.5x, 1x or zoom), how to frame it, and a simple settings tip.
  • A selfie spot, where there is a good one.

Our philosophy: look before you shoot

Most disappointing holiday photos are taken from the wrong spot: too close, too far, or straight on from the same place as everyone else. A few steps can change everything. That is why better photos start before you shoot.

We call it photographing with intent. It takes seconds, and it works with any phone:

  • Look. Notice what ordinary sightseeing misses: doors, streets, signs, people, textures, small moments.
  • See. Spot what makes the scene interesting: a pattern, a contrast, a gesture, a natural frame.
  • Understand. Ask what the place is really about: its culture, its pace, its history, its atmosphere.
  • Take the photo with intent. Decide what the picture should say (wonder, calm, welcome, the buzz of a crowd) and take it, the first time.

A famous landmark is more than an object to tick off. A doorway, a market stall, an alley or a café window can say as much about a place as the monument itself. Capture more, miss less.

And you should not need editing skills. At most, straighten the picture or crop it. That's it.

"Travel photography is the art of looking closely enough to see what a place is really saying, understanding its atmosphere and character, and photographing it with clear intention so the image carries meaning."

Made for your phone, not a big camera

Xdatrys is for travellers who take their photos on a smartphone. No tripod, no extra lenses, no editing software. Just you, your phone, and the right place to stand.

How we choose and check the spots

We start with what travellers actually look for. The sights on each map are ranked by how often people from other countries search for them, so spot 1 is the sight most visitors want to see.

We check every spot's position against OpenStreetMap, the open map of the world, and many of the photos on our maps were taken by us, at the spot. Places change: if something on a map is wrong, tell us at info@xdatrys.com and we will check it.

What you can use

  • The Map Library: free to browse. 3 spots on every map are open to everyone, with no sign-up.
  • The Xdatrys Maps app, for members: every spot on every map, day tours, and maps saved on your phone that keep working without signal.
  • Pocket Lens: our free newsletter, Monday to Friday. It follows a real traveller to a destination: where to stand at a photo spot, a tour and a place to stay, and travel news worth knowing.
