THROUGH THE LENS

Crowds spill onto a city street, banners overhead, dozens of strangers moving in every direction, and your phone tries to focus on all of it at once. Lock your focus manually by tapping one face on your screen and holding your finger there until a small square or circle locks in place. That stops the camera from chasing movement and keeps your subject sharp while everything else blurs naturally around them.

Street tension also compresses the frame: people stand closer, gestures get bigger, expressions sharpen. We find the wide lens (usually the shortest focal length your phone offers) handles this better than zooming in, because it pulls more of the surrounding scene into the shot and gives the picture real context.

Today's small challenge: tap to lock focus on one person in a busy place and take three frames without moving the phone.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Juan Garré, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Fortaleza General Artigas
Cerro, Montevideo

Fortaleza General Artigas sits on top of Cerro de Montevideo, a hill 135 metres above the bay, and the English-language source describes it as a fortress with elevated views over both the water and the city below. The footprint is compact, with the longest side measuring about 50 metres, so you're never far from an edge with a wide-open view.

Point your phone outward from the walls and let the bay fill the frame. The height does the compositional work for you: position the horizon line low in the frame to emphasise the spread of the city, or high to bring the water forward. A place this small rewards patience, so stay in one spot, wait for a boat or a cloud to move into the frame, and then shoot.

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints sits about 500 metres away, close enough to visit on foot before or after the fortress. Save this for a day when you want two very different architectural scales in your camera roll.

Getting there: Fortaleza General Artigas is in the Cerro area, about 500 m south of La Iglesia de Jesucristo de los Santos de los Últimos Días. Walk south from La Iglesia de Jesucristo de los Santos de los Últimos Días to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Fog and Mist

Point your camera at a solid shape close to you, a fence post, a bare branch, a parked bicycle, and let whatever sits behind it dissolve into grey. Fog flattens depth, so fill the bottom third of your frame with something sharp and concrete, then let the rest go soft. The contrast between that crisp foreground edge and the blurred middle distance does all the work.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
