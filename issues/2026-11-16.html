THROUGH THE LENS

Cultural festivals pack a crowd, a costume, a drumbeat, and a food stall into one small square, and your phone will try to freeze all of it at once. That's where Portrait mode earns its keep: it blurs the background behind your subject so the face or the object in the foreground stays sharp, even when twenty other things compete for attention behind it.

The trick is distance. Stand about one metre from your subject, tap their face on your screen to lock focus, then shoot. The closer you are, the more convincingly your phone separates them from the crowd behind.

Today's small challenge: find one person or object at least two metres in front of a busy background, tap to focus, and take the shot.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Big Ben in Japan, Flickr, CC BY-SA 2.0

Lake Shoji
Fujikawaguchiko

Lake Shoji is the smallest of the Fuji Five Lakes, tucked into forest surroundings near Fujikawaguchiko, and most visitors rush past it on the way to more-photographed spots. The guide 5 Hidden Mount Fuji Viewpoints Beyond Chureito Pagoda: A Photo Guide singles it out for the "Kodaki Fuji" composition, where Mount Fuji appears to cradle a smaller peak in front of it, a framing you will not find at the busier lakes.

That Kodaki Fuji geometry is your starting point: position yourself so the smaller peak sits visibly distinct against Fuji's slope, then use the calm water as a reflection plane to double the layered peaks in one frame. Because the lake surface does the heavy compositional work, cropping to a wide horizontal makes the most of the water-to-mountain ratio your phone can capture.

While you're there, walk the forest edge along the shore and look for a gap in the trees where both peaks appear unobstructed. Save that spot for yourself before the frame fills with anyone else.

Getting there: Lake Shoji is about 14.4 km west of the centre of Fujikawaguchiko.

Photoshop can now relight your finished photos with AI
Adobe's new Relight tool lets you drop fresh light sources into a photo after the shot, adjusting position, colour, and brightness without touching the original. Handy for phone photographers who can't reshoot a scene just because the lighting was off. View ->

INDUSTRY
Metalenz buys startup whose tech lets sensors absorb 2.5x more light
The Bayer filter inside your phone's camera absorbs around 60% of incoming light, and PxE Imaging's Holocoder layer bends light instead of swallowing it, delivering 2.5x more to the sensor. Richer colour and depth data, less computational heavy-lifting to fake what the hardware already missed. View ->

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Fog and Mist

Point your camera at a subject with a strong, solid outline, a bare tree, a fence post, a parked car, and let the grey-white haze swallow everything behind it. Crank your exposure compensation up by one stop so the fog reads as pale grey rather than muddy brown. The gap between subject and background is where the picture lives.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
