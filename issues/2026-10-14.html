THROUGH THE LENS

One neighbourhood, shot properly, beats ten neighbourhoods shot in a rush. When city breaks put you on dense, walkable streets, your phone's wide-angle lens (the shortest, widest camera on the back) can pull in a whole market stall or cobbled lane in a single frame.

Tap your subject on screen before you press the shutter. That tells your phone exactly where to focus and where to set the exposure, so the detail you actually care about stays sharp.

Pick one street today, shoot only from ground level, and see how different the same scene looks when you crouch down.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Guillaume Capron, Flickr, CC BY-SA 2.0. Cropped and divided into three panels.

Mårten Trotzigs Gränd
Gamla stan, Stockholm

Mårten Trotzigs Gränd, in Stockholm's Gamla stan district, squeezes down to about 90 cm at its narrowest point. That's narrower than most front doors. Stockholm's Top Photo Spots: Best Places for Perfect Pictures calls it a stepped lane whose stairs and enclosing walls make it a distinctive photographic subject, and at just 35 metres long, the whole thing fits in a short walk between Västerlånggatan and Järntorget.

Shoot vertical every time. The walls close in on both sides, so a portrait-orientation frame uses that compression rather than fighting it. The steps give you a natural leading line straight through the frame, so position yourself at the top or bottom and let the stairs pull the viewer's eye into the shot.

Once you've worked the lane, walk roughly 140 metres to the German Church for a contrast in scale. Save this for later: Tessin Palace sits about 290 metres away and gives you an entirely different kind of subject to round out your Gamla stan session.

Getting there: Mårten Trotzigs Gränd is in the Gamla stan area, about 140 m southeast of German Church and 290 m south of Tessin Palace. It meets Västerlånggatan and Järntorget. Walk southeast from German Church to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Silhouette

Tap your subject on screen to lock focus, then slide the exposure down (that's the sun icon on your phone's camera) until your subject goes completely dark against the background. Pick a subject with a strong, readable outline, like a bicycle, a lamppost, or a person mid-stride. Keep the shape bold and simple.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
