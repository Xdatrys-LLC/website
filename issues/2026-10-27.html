THROUGH THE LENS

Short video feeds pull crowds to the same ten spots at every destination, and a crowded spot is a harder shot: other phones poke into your frame, and the angle everyone copies rarely has much depth to it. The fix costs nothing. Step away from the cluster of people and look for a second frame, a doorway, an arch, a receding row of columns, something that puts distance between you and your subject.

Your phone's wide lens, the shortest focal length in your camera app, pulls that depth into the shot and makes a tight space read as grand. We find it works especially well where geometry repeats, a tiled floor, a colonnade, a line of identical shuttered windows.

Pick one doorway or arch near you today and shoot through it rather than past it.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: simon, Unsplash

Bab al-Futuh
El-Gamaliyya, Cairo

Bab al-Futuh has stood at the northern edge of El-Gamaliyya since 1087, and that location quietly works in your favour. Cairo Photography 2026: Every Shot You Need & Where to Find It notes that this end of the district draws far fewer people than the southern stretch leading to Khan El-Khalili, so you get the gate without the crowd.

Frame the gate straight on and let the arch fill the upper half of your phone's screen, then step back until the street's depth pulls the eye through to whatever is beyond. Wikala Qaitbey sits just 190 metres away, which means a short walk gives you a second strong subject without losing your bearings.

Beshtak Palace is 580 metres from the gate. Head there after you've worked the gate, and you've got two monuments within easy reach of each other, which is a solid way to use one stretch of time in this neighbourhood.

Getting there: Bab al-Futuh is in the El-Gamaliyya area, about 580 m north of Beshtak Palace and 190 m northwest of Wikala Qaitbey. Walk north from Beshtak Palace to find it.

That means the lens physically opens or closes to control light, switching between f/1.48 for low-light shots and f/4.0 when you need sharper depth across a scene. A real mechanical aperture on a phone main camera is a first for Apple. View ->

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Architecture Looking Up

Point your phone straight up at any ceiling, arch, or tower and let the geometry fill the frame edge to edge. Shoot in portrait orientation so vertical lines pull the eye deep into the shot, and tap the screen on a mid-tone area to lock your exposure before you capture.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
