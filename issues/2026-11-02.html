THROUGH THE LENS

A long delay turns a departure gate into one of the most photogenic places you'll find anywhere. People slump across seats, stare at boards, lean on bags: all of it is unguarded and real, and your phone can catch it from two metres away without anyone noticing a thing.

Keep your phone's wide lens on, not the zoomed-in one, so you pull the whole gate scene into frame, the grey floor, the rows of chairs, the distant windows. Wide means the lens your phone uses for normal shots, roughly equivalent to what your eye sees. It holds the scale of the space and stops faces from dominating.

Try this today: photograph one person from behind, with the departures board in the frame above them.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Epp, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Aida
City center, Tallinn

Aida is a 130-metre pedestrian street in Tallinn's city centre, and it takes some looking to find. Epic Streets in Tallinn describes it as largely untouched and medieval, the kind of place that makes you feel like the last few centuries didn't happen, with a view toward the city theatre's summer stage.

Aida runs between Kooli and Laboratooriumi, so you can shoot from either end and frame the street against what's ahead. It's a straight run, which means the buildings on both sides pull the eye forward naturally: plant yourself at one junction, keep the phone level, and let that compression do the work. A vertical crop fits the scale of a narrow medieval street better than a wide one.

The Green Market sits about 170 metres away, and Three Handed Mother of God Church is only 150 metres from the street. Work Aida into a short loop and you've got three very different subjects on one walk.

Getting there: Aida is in the City center area, about 150 m southwest of Three Handed Mother of God Church and 170 m northwest of Green Market. It meets Kooli and Laboratooriumi. Walk southwest from Three Handed Mother of God Church to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Water in Motion

Point your phone at any moving water and tap the screen to lock focus on the spot where the water breaks or curves, not where it settles. Shoot in burst mode to freeze individual droplets mid-air, then swipe through and pick the frame where the shape holds its tension. White foam against dark rock or wet stone sharpens the contrast your phone needs to separate the water from its background.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
