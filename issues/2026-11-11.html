TODAY IN FOCUS

Overcrowded hotspots are finally getting a smarter fix
Photographing a famous site with hundreds of other visitors in every frame is genuinely one of the hardest problems in travel photography. Spain, Italy, Greece, and others are now pushing formal visitor management plans after rising tourist numbers strained infrastructure and housing in places like Barcelona, Venice, and Santorini, according to Travel And Tour World. Spread your visits across less obvious spots in a destination and you'll find emptier foregrounds and more room to actually frame a shot. View ->


Photo by doit_viaggi on Pixabay

Greece wants you to travel differently, and your camera will thank you
Greece launched a digital card scheme called Tourism for All, letting selected residents book accommodation across the country, with the first phase running through 30 June 2027. The push sits inside a broader European plan calling for visitors to spread across more places and seasons, via Travel And Tour World. For photographers, that pressure toward less-visited spots and off-peak timing is exactly the kind of condition that produces images nobody else has. View ->

Mongolia and Japan are turning fossil digs into a new kind of destination
Mongolia's Ministry of Culture, Sports, Tourism and Youth announced a cooperation with Japan to build visitor experiences around dinosaur fossils and palaeontological heritage in the Gobi Desert. The scale of that landscape, with its natural scenery and scientifically significant fossil sites, gives a photographer something genuinely rare to point a smartphone at. Travel And Tour World covered the announcement, which was made on 9 November 2026. View ->

THROUGH THE LENS

A crowd of fifty strangers in your frame isn't bad luck, it's geometry. Your phone's wide lens pulls in everything in front of you, so the fix is simple: raise your phone above your head and point it slightly downward, cutting the crowd to a strip of colour at the bottom edge rather than the whole foreground.

Your phone's 2x zoom lens (a tighter focal length built into most recent phones) also helps here. Step back, switch to 2x, and the frame narrows, letting you isolate a doorway, a stone staircase, or a single figure against a wall.

Try it today: find one busy spot, switch to 2x, and crop the crowd to a sliver by shooting from shoulder height with the phone tilted five degrees down.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Carlos Adampol, Flickr, CC BY-SA 2.0

Plaza de San Blas
San Cristóbal, Cusco

Plaza de San Blas is a courtyard in Cusco's San Cristóbal area, ringed by artisan workshops and galleries, with a small artificial waterfall and a staircase as its two main photographic subjects. Wanderlog's list of the 29 best photo spots in Cusco flags it specifically for those two features, which puts it a step outside the crowds that circle the Cuzco Cathedral, just 110 metres away.

Point your phone straight at the waterfall and let the staircase lead the eye into the frame from one side. Shooting vertical here makes sense: the stairs give you a natural diagonal to anchor the composition, and the waterfall gives you a vertical drop to follow. We'd pair those two elements in a single frame rather than shoot them separately.

While you're there, step into one of the artisan workshops that line the courtyard. Save this for later: the detail work you see on the shelves inside can double as a close-up subject if the courtyard itself gets busy.

Getting there: Plaza de San Blas is in the San Cristóbal area, about 110 m southwest of Cuzco Cathedral and 130 m northwest of Church of the Society of Jesus. Walk southwest from Cuzco Cathedral to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Staircases

Point your phone's camera straight up or straight down a flight of stairs and shoot from the top or bottom step, letting the repeating lines pull the eye through the frame. Get low, press the shutter, then check whether the handrail or the edge of each step creates a stronger diagonal. Try both and compare.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
