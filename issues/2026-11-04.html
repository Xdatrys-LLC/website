THROUGH THE LENS

Fewer tourists at a crowded site sounds like a logistical footnote, but for your phone's camera it's a real shift. When a plaza or a staircase sits mostly empty, you can hold your phone steady at arm's length, line up the actual geometry of the space, and take the frame the guidebook photo always promised but rarely delivered. No elbows in your shot.

Your phone's wide-angle lens (the one that takes in the most of a scene) needs clear foreground to anchor a composition. Pack that foreground with strangers, and the lens has nowhere to settle.

Next time you reach a quieter spot, crouch low, point the wide-angle lens toward a tiled floor or a stone step in the foreground, and shoot toward the far wall to pull the whole space into one frame.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Kevin Serech, Unsplash

5a Avenida Norte
El Manchén, Antigua Guatemala

5a Avenida Norte, Antigua Guatemala

The Antigua Guatemala Travel Guide calls this 515-metre one-way street a photogenic route connecting the central fountain to the Santa Catalina Arch, with mustard-yellow façades and hidden courtyards tucked along the way. It runs through the El Manchén area, and because traffic moves in one direction only, you can walk the full length without stepping around oncoming cars.

The street runs straight, so line up your shot along the axis of the road and let the façades pull the eye toward the arch ahead. Shoot portrait orientation to stack the yellow walls against the sky, and duck into any open courtyard gate for a compressed, frame-within-a-frame shot.

Plaza Mayor sits about 190 metres away, and the Convento de Santa Catalina is roughly 160 metres from the street, so you can pair both stops with this route in a single short walk. Save the arch shot for your last frame before you turn back.

Getting there: 5a Avenida Norte is in the El Manchén area, about 160 m south of Convento de Santa Catalina and 190 m north of Plaza Mayor. It meets 5a Calle Poniente and 5th Avenue North. Walk south from Convento de Santa Catalina to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Transport and Motion

Pick a spot where vehicles pass close and frame tight, filling the shot with wheels, windows, or the blurred streak of a passing door. Keep your shutter speed slow enough to smear the motion across the frame while holding one sharp, still edge, like a painted line or a concrete kerb, as your anchor.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
