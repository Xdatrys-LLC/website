THROUGH THE LENS

Quieter places reward slower shooting. When a square or street isn't packed with other tourists, you can actually stop, reframe, and try the same shot from three different distances without anyone elbowing past your phone.

The trick at a genuinely calm location is to use your phone's tap-to-focus lock, which holds focus on your chosen point while you reposition. Walk toward your subject while keeping that lock on, and notice how the background compresses and simplifies behind it.

Try it today: find one textured surface, a stone wall, a tiled floor, a weathered door, tap to lock focus on the roughest part, then step back two paces and shoot.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: José Artur Leitão Bárcia, Wikimedia Commons, Public domain

Miradouro da Senhora do Monte
Graça, Lisbon

Miradouro da Senhora do Monte sits high above Lisbon's Graça district, and it's one of those viewpoints that rewards anyone who climbs past the more-visited spots lower down. The source "Best Instagram Spots in Lisbon: A Picture-Perfect Guide to..." puts it plainly: from up here you get a sweeping view of terracotta rooftops, winding streets, and São Jorge Castle in the distance. That combination in a single frame is rare.

Your phone's wide-angle lens handles the depth of the scene well, so use the castle as a fixed anchor point and let the rooftops and winding streets carry the eye toward it. Shoot vertical to pull in the foreground below and the skyline above without cropping either out. One strong composition: place the castle at a third of the frame, off-center, and let the roofscape fill the rest.

Graça Church sits 340 metres away, and Palacete Sommer is at 330 metres, so after you've got your shots, a short walk in either direction gives you something completely different to photograph. Save the church exterior for a close-up contrast to the wide view you just captured.

Getting there: Miradouro da Senhora do Monte is in the Graça area, about 330 m south of Palacete Sommer and 340 m northwest of Graça Church. Walk south from Palacete Sommer to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Shadow Play

Point your phone's camera at any hard-edged shadow falling across a plain surface, like a tiled floor or a whitewashed wall, and frame it so the shadow fills at least half the shot. Crop out whatever's casting it, so the shape does the talking on its own.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
