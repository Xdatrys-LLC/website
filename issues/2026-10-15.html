THROUGH THE LENS

Hotel lobbies built for atmosphere are also built for your phone camera. Tiled floors, layered textures, arched doorways, these interiors give your phone's wide-angle lens (the shortest, broadest view your camera offers) a natural frame to work with.

Get close to one repeating pattern, a geometric floor or a carved wall, and let it fill the frame completely. Forget the whole room. One detail, sharp and centred, tells the story better than a wide shot of everything at once.

Point your phone at a single decorative tile today and tap it on screen to lock the focus there.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Toygar Par, Pexels. Cropped and divided into three panels.

Yavuz Selim Caddesi
PTT Evleri Mahallesi, Istanbul

A single frame on Yavuz Selim Caddesi, near Fener Rum Lisesi in Istanbul's PTT Evleri Mahallesi, gives you a red-brick school, a sloping street, and layered Golden Horn rooflines all at once. TurkoLoco's Secret Streets in Istanbul for Photography 2026 singles out this exact section as the spot, and the street itself runs 720 metres, two-way, through a neighbourhood that most visitors never reach.

Because the street slopes, let the downhill line pull your eye toward the school and the rooflines stacking up beyond it. A frame that includes both the foreground pavement and the distant skyline rewards a wide shot, so try a horizontal composition and let the depth do the work. A short, patient wait for the street to clear gives you a cleaner shot than any filter will.

After you shoot, PTT Evleri Garipler Camii sits about 80 metres away, and a Çaykur tea shop is a little further along at around 170 metres. Save this for later: buy a cup of tea and review your frames before you move on.

Getting there: Yavuz Selim Caddesi is in the PTT Evleri Mahallesi area, about 80 m northwest of PTT Evleri Garipler Camii and 170 m south of Çaykur Çay Satış Mağazası. It meets Okul Caddesi. Walk northwest from PTT Evleri Garipler Camii to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Rain on Glass

Press your smartphone lens close to a rain-covered window and let the droplets do the work: each one bends and distorts whatever sits behind the glass into tiny, curved shapes. Tap your screen on one single droplet to lock focus there, and let the background blur into soft, unrecognisable colour. One droplet. One tap. That's the whole shot.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
