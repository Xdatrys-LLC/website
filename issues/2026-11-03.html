THROUGH THE LENS

Fewer people at a site like Machu Picchu means one thing for your phone camera: you can actually wait for a clean frame. Most phones default to locking focus and exposure the moment you tap the screen, but if you hold your tap for a second, you trigger AE/AF lock, which keeps both the focus point and the brightness fixed even if someone walks through your shot.

That matters when you're framing a stone structure against a sky with strong contrast. Your phone wants to average everything out; AE/AF lock tells it to stop guessing and commit.

Pick one fixed subject today, hold your tap until you see the lock indicator appear, then reframe slightly before you shoot.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: moniko moniko, Flickr, CC BY-SA 2.0

Giardino Bardini
Quartiere 1, Florence

Giardino Bardini sits on a terraced hillside in Florence's Quartiere 1, and its longest side stretches roughly 300 metres, enough slope to give you several distinct vantage points over the city. "10 Aesthetic Spots in Florence for Beautiful Photos" singles it out for exactly that terraced structure, which stacks multiple angles on the Florentine skyline one above the other. For about three weeks in April, a wisteria pergola runs through the garden and frames the Duomo behind it.

Shoot upward along the pergola to pull the Duomo into the far end of the frame, and let the repeating arches act as a natural tunnel that draws the eye straight to the cathedral. The terraced layout means you can move a few steps up or down to change how much sky or roofline appears above the dome, so try at least two or three levels before you settle on a composition.

While you're there, note that Torre dei Fifanti and Torre degli Alberti both sit within 440 metres, so a short walk after your visit puts two medieval towers in reach for a different kind of Florentine skyline frame.

Getting there: Giardino Bardini is in the Quartiere 1 area, about 440 m southeast of Torre dei Fifanti and 440 m southwest of Torre degli Alberti. Walk southeast from Torre dei Fifanti to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Window Light

Position your subject, a hand, a cup, a face, just inside a window frame, then tap your phone screen on the shadowed side of that subject so the camera exposes for the darker area and the bright rectangle behind goes white. That hard contrast between the pale window and the dark surround is the whole shot.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
