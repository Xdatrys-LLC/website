STAY

Hotel Acadia
Hotel in Quebec City, CAD 252 per night (about US$177)

It's a three-star hotel at 43 Rue Sainte-Ursule in Old Quebec. Guests give its location 9.7 out of 10 on Booking.com, and the Plains of Abraham, the Citadel and the Parliament Building are all a few steps away.

That makes it an easy base for photographers: you walk out and you're shooting. Some rooms have brick walls, hardwood floors and an electric fireplace, and breakfast is a buffet.

THROUGH THE LENS

Slow travel suits a phone camera. Stay in one place longer and you can wait for the right moment, steady the phone against a wall or a railing, and let the camera take its time.

That's what "software tuned for stillness rather than speed" would mean in plain terms. A phone that rewards patience with sharper, steadier pictures of a scene that isn't moving, instead of rapid bursts aimed at one that is.

Try it today. Lean on a wall, hold your breath and take one slow picture.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Cephas, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0. Cropped and divided into three panels.

Rue Couillard
Old Québec, Quebec City

Quebec City's tourism office lists Rue Couillard as a quiet street off the beaten track in Old Québec. It's short, it curves, and OpenStreetMap maps it as one-way with a 20 km/h limit, so you can stand in it and take your time.

Start with the street sign at the corner: a white plate with a red border that reads "Rue Couillard." Then shoot along the curve in a vertical frame and let the street run away from you. Step in close and take one house at a time. Painted stone, brick, a lantern and hanging shop signs all sit within a few steps of each other.

Turn around before you leave. The materials change every few metres, and each change is a new picture.

Getting there: Rue Couillard is in the upper town, about 220 m northwest of the Notre-Dame de Québec Basilica-Cathedral and 490 m northwest of the Château Frontenac. It meets Côte de la Fabrique. Walk northwest from the basilica and look for the white street sign. It's a public street and free to visit.
