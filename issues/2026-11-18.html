SHOT SCHOOL

Mount Fuji, Japan (Fujisan): Best Photo Spot and Where to Stand

Watch the video

To photograph Mount Fuji, use your standard lens or the 2x zoom rather than zooming in further from a distance. Zooming in too far produces a grainy image and removes the shore and trees that give the mountain its context. Keeping to the standard lens or 2x lets the lake and trees show the mountain at its true size and keeps the image sharp.

THROUGH THE LENS

A delay at a station or a diverted route hands you something most travellers miss: unplanned stillness. Put your phone's camera on its widest lens, crouch low on the platform, and frame the yellow edge-line running straight toward the far end of the tunnel. That compressed diagonal does more for a photo than any posed shot from the concourse.

If a crowd gathers and you want to separate one person or object from the blur around them, tap that subject on your screen and hold your finger there for a second. That locks both focus and exposure, so the camera stops hunting for something else to fix on.

Try it on your next stop: tap and hold on a tiled wall sign, then reframe so the sign sits in one corner while the platform stretches behind it.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Sandor_Somkuti, Flickr, CC BY-SA 2.0

Heiligenkreuzerhof
Innere Stadt, Vienna

Heiligenkreuzerhof is a historic courtyard complex in Vienna's Innere Stadt, threading between Schönlaterngasse and Grashofgasse. "Instagrammable Vienna: the best photo hotspots in the city" features it as a photo destination, and the tucked-away courtyard setting means you're unlikely to share it with a crowd.

The passage connects two streets, so you can shoot toward either entrance and use the passage as a natural frame within your frame. Try a portrait orientation to pull those converging walls into a strong vertical composition. A second angle: stand near one entry and let the depth of the courtyard pull your eye through the shot.

Palais Pergen sits about 170 metres away and St. Barbara Greek Catholic Church about 180 metres, so once you've worked the courtyard from both ends, you have two more stops within a short walk.

Getting there: Heiligenkreuzerhof is in the Innere Stadt area, about 170 m northeast of Palais Pergen and 180 m west of St. Barbara Greek Catholic Church. Walk northeast from Palais Pergen to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Patterns and Repetition

Point your lens at any surface where a shape multiplies: roof tiles, stacked chairs, a tiled floor, iron railings. Fill the entire frame so the edges of the shot cut through the pattern rather than contain it. We find the strongest entries come from angles that flatten depth, so try shooting straight on or directly overhead.
