THROUGH THE LENS

Pop culture tourism fills famous spots with crowds, which means other visitors and their phones appear in almost every frame. That's your biggest obstacle.

The fix is to shoot from closer than feels natural. Walk right up to a wall, a doorway, or a floor pattern and fill the frame with texture and shape. We'd call this "going abstract," but the practical version is simple: get close enough that the background disappears entirely.

Pick one recognisable surface near you today and photograph just a small square of it.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: jayKayEss, Flickr, CC BY 2.0

Brunkebergstunneln
Norrmalm, Stockholm

Brunkebergstunneln runs for 230 metres under the Norrmalm district of Stockholm, connecting David Bagares Gata and Tunnelgatan at street level. Visit Stockholm's official guide, Best Instagram Spots & Photo Locations, calls it a "uniquely photogenic location," and it's the kind of place most visitors walk straight past without knowing it's there. Traffic moves in both directions through it, so you'll have the tunnel to yourself on foot while the city carries on around you.

Stand at one entrance and shoot straight through toward the far opening: that compression of the full 230-metre length into a single frame is where the geometry does the work. Keep your phone low and centred to hold the symmetry, and bracket a few shots at different distances from the mouth so you can compare how the scale shifts. The two-way traffic means you can wait for a clear gap and shoot without rushing.

St. John's Church sits 250 metres from the tunnel entrance, and Adolf Fredrik Church is 340 metres away: a short loop takes in both, so you've got a natural route to walk once you've finished shooting.

Getting there: Brunkebergstunneln is in the Norrmalm area, about 250 m south of St. John's Church and 340 m east of Adolf Fredrik Church. It meets David Bagares Gata and Tunnelgatan. Walk south from St. John's Church to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Children at Play

Get low, squat to their eye level, and keep your finger hovering over the shutter so you catch the split second a face creases into a laugh or a hand reaches for something just out of frame. Lock your focus on the nearest eye and let the background blur behind them. Loose, unposed, caught-in-the-act frames win every time.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
