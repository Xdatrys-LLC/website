TOUR

WalknTours: Jackson Historic Downtown Walking Tour
an upcoming date, Yellowstone National Park, USA

Jackson, Wyoming sits at the edge of Grand Teton country, and WalknTours has a self-guided walking tour of its historic downtown that you can pick up on an upcoming date whenever you're ready to go. For $9.99, the app-based tour covers around ten stops across a compact, walkable core, from the elk antler arches on Town Square to the century-old Jackson Drug counter, St. John's Episcopal Church, and the Wort Hotel's Silver Dollar Bar.


Pictured: Jackson, Wyoming. Photo: Thank You (25 Millions ) views, CC BY 2.0, via Flickr

You set the pace, pause when something catches your eye, and the audio works offline. Save this one for a scout walk before you shoot: note the antler arch geometry, the angles off Town Square, and the older facade lines on the Van Vleck House, then come back knowing exactly where you want to stand. THROUGH THE LENS

Before you point your phone at any recognisable filming location, check the site's access rules first. Some spots restrict photography outright, and finding that out on arrival wastes your whole visit.

Your phone's camera can still work for you while you're doing that research. Screenshot the location's official page and use it to plan your exact shooting position, so you arrive knowing which angle you want and where to stand.

Before your next outing, look up the photography policy for one specific place on your list and note the nearest alternative spot in case you need it.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: ChillyBlanket, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Snow King Mountain
Jackson, Wyoming

Snow King Mountain sits right on the edge of Jackson, Wyoming, and the summit gives you a wide spread of the town below, the National Elk Refuge, the Snake River, and the Teton range beyond. Top Photo Opportunities and Selfie Spots in Jackson Hole WY flags this viewpoint as one of the area's most rewarding, and the Summit Gondola sits about 580 metres away if you want to vary your approach.

Face outward from the summit and frame the town small against the refuge and the river behind it: that layered depth, with Jackson in the foreground and the Tetons pushing up at the back, is exactly what a wide phone lens handles well. Try a portrait-orientation shot to stack those layers from bottom to top, letting the river draw the eye through the middle of the frame.

The Summit Gondola nearby makes a natural anchor for a second composition once you've worked the view, so save a few frames for it before you head back down.

Getting there: Snow King Mountain is about 580 m northeast of Summit Gondola. Walk northeast from Summit Gondola to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Children at Play

Get low, shoot at a child's eye level, and let the action fill the frame rather than shrinking it to a background detail. A tight crop on hands gripping a rope or feet mid-jump tells the story more sharply than a wide shot. Keep your finger ready on the shutter and shoot in bursts to catch the split second of peak movement.
