THROUGH THE LENS

More destinations dropping visa barriers means more of us landing somewhere new with a full phone battery and no plan. That's actually fine. The one setting worth checking before you step off any plane is your camera's aspect ratio, which is the shape of the frame your phone records. Most phones default to a wide, rectangular shape, but switching to the square 1:1 ratio or the classic 4:3 keeps more detail in the frame and gives you more to work with when you crop later.

New place, unfamiliar streets, no sense yet of what looks good where. Shoot extra frames, and vary your distance to the same subject rather than moving on too quickly.

Before your next outing, open your camera settings and check which aspect ratio your phone is currently set to.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Salem State Archives and Special Collections, Flickr, CC BY 2.0

Prison Lane
Nassau

Prison Lane runs about 220 metres from East Street to Greenwich Street North, cutting Nassau's street grid in a straight line that most Bay Street visitors never find. The Ultimate Nassau Walking Tour for Cruisers: Safety Included flags it as a named historic lane and a deliberate detour away from the main tourist drag.

Because the lane runs straight, use that geometry. Point your phone down the length of it and let the converging edges of the walls pull the viewer's eye toward the far end. A vertical crop suits a narrow street, so shoot portrait orientation rather than landscape, and move close to one side to make the corridor shape work for you.

The Water Tower sits 130 metres away, so walk over once you've worked the lane. Frame it from different distances to see how the scale shifts.

Getting there: Prison Lane is about 130 m west of The Water Tower. It meets East Street and Greenwich Street North. Walk west from The Water Tower to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Empty Spaces

Point your lens at a scene where absence does the heavy lifting: a bare bench, an unmarked stretch of floor, a doorway with nothing behind it. Frame that emptiness deliberately, pushing it to at least two-thirds of the shot, so the small detail you keep in the corner carries real weight.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
