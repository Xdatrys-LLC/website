THROUGH THE LENS

Dense red and orange foliage packs tightly together in a park like Expo '70, and that much saturated colour in a small frame can confuse your phone's auto-exposure, leaving the whole shot looking washed out. The fix is simple: tap the brightest leaf cluster on your screen to lock focus there, then slide the exposure slider down until the reds look as rich as they do to your eye.

Your phone's wide lens, usually the standard one-times view, pulls in too much sky and path and dilutes the colour. Get closer, fill the frame with just two or three branches, and the contrast between deep red and dark bark does the work for you.

Today's challenge: find any patch of colour outside, fill the frame completely with it, and slide your exposure down one notch before you tap the shutter.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Jeangagnon, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Rue du Sault-au-Matelot
Vieux-Québec, Quebec City

Rue du Sault-au-Matelot, a 265-metre street in Vieux-Québec, sits off the main tourist circuit in a way that rewards the curious. Quebec City's tourism office, in its guide "10 Spots for a Unique Photo Op of Old Québec," singles it out for its Old Europe-style architecture and calls it a destination specifically for distinctive photographs. It runs two-way traffic at a 20 km/h limit, with a pedestrian and living-street designation that keeps things calm enough to shoot without rushing.

The street runs straight, so use that geometry deliberately: plant yourself at one end and let the facades compress toward a single vanishing point in your frame. At 265 metres you have room to step back and vary your focal length by walking, rather than zooming, which keeps detail sharp. A vertical crop suits the building scale relative to the street width.

Place des Canotiers sits 170 metres away, and the Musée de la place Royale is 200 metres out, so fold both into a short walk once you've worked the street.

Getting there: Rue du Sault-au-Matelot is in the Vieux-Québec area, about 170 m west of Place des Canotiers and 200 m north of Musée de la place Royale. It meets Côte de la Montagne and Rue Saint-Paul. Walk west from Place des Canotiers to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Reflections

Point your lens at any still or slow-moving water, a shop window, or a polished floor, then drop your phone low so the reflected image fills at least half the frame. Lock focus on the reflection itself rather than the surface, and crop out the horizon line to let the flipped version stand on its own.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
