THROUGH THE LENS

Crowded public spaces make people look away from your phone, which works in your favour. When a square fills with noise and movement, switch your camera to the widest lens available (the one with the smallest number, which means it captures more of the scene in one frame) and get close to the action rather than stepping back.

Keep burst mode on, where your phone takes several frames in quick succession while you hold the shutter down. You get more frames to choose from, and the best one usually has sharper faces and a cleaner background.

Today's small challenge: find one busy doorway or archway, hold your phone at hip height pointing straight ahead, and take five frames without looking at the screen.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Kameron Kincade, Unsplash

Anafiotika
Πετράλωνα, Athens

Anafiotika sits above the old city in Athens, Greece, a neighbourhood of narrow lanes and whitewashed cottages built by Cycladic masons who brought their island craft with them. The source "Hot Spots for Instagrammers in Athens" describes its labyrinthine streets and lopsided cottages as island-style architecture planted in the shadow of the Acropolis. That contrast, a Cycladic village appearing suddenly inside a European capital, is exactly what puts it off the beaten path.

Shoot vertical: the lanes are narrow, and a portrait-frame fills the shot with architecture rather than sky. The Old Acropolis Museum sits 120 metres away, so you can frame a lane with the museum's outline just visible at the end for a sense of scale. Let the curve of a labyrinthine street pull the eye through the frame rather than centering your subject flat against a wall.

The Athens University History Museum is 140 metres from Anafiotika, an easy walk if you want context for what the masons built and why they built it here. Save the coordinates before you go.

Getting there: Anafiotika is in the Πετράλωνα area, about 120 m north of Old Acropolis Museum and 140 m southeast of Athens University History Museum. Walk north from Old Acropolis Museum to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Umbrellas

Point your camera straight up through an open umbrella's canopy and shoot the ribs fanning out from the central ferrule against a plain sky, filling the frame with repeating lines and colour. Get close enough that the fabric curves past the edges of your shot. Keep the frame pure geometry.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
