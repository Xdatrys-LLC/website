THROUGH THE LENS

Crowds at a protest or a busy square share one tricky quality: your phone's auto-exposure reads the whole frame and picks a middle value, so bright banners wash out and shadowed faces go dark. Lock the exposure manually by tapping and holding on the most important subject until a small yellow square appears and stays fixed. That one tap keeps the brightness steady even when people move across the frame.

Chaotic scenes also tend to flatten into a mess of overlapping bodies unless you find a fixed anchor, a red placard, a concrete pillar, a cobblestone foreground, anything that gives the eye somewhere to land first.

Try it today: tap and hold on a single face or object in a busy spot, then slowly pan the phone across the scene and watch how the exposure holds.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Claudio Schwarz, Unsplash

Aussersihler Viadukt
Industriequartier, Zurich

A railway viaduct running through Zurich's Industriequartier, the Aussersihler Viadukt sits far from the old-town postcard circuit. Dein Street Photography Guide für Zürich (2025) names it the most exciting shopping mile in Zurich and a prime spot for street photography.

The arches repeat as you walk along the viaduct, so use that repetition to build depth: frame yourself at one end and let the structure pull the eye through the shot. The Prime Tower sits 320 metres away, close enough to bring into a wide frame if you want to contrast the industrial foreground with the tower behind.

While you're there, walk the 580 metres toward the Office Tower and see how the view shifts with each block. Save this one for a slow wander rather than a quick stop.

Getting there: Aussersihler Viadukt is in the Industriequartier area, about 320 m east of Prime Tower and 580 m south of Office Tower. Walk east from Prime Tower to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Motion Blur

Set your phone's shutter speed to 1/15s or slower (look for it in Pro or Manual mode), point at anything moving across your frame, and keep the camera completely still while you shoot. The sharp background and streaked subject do all the work. Try a few frames and delete the ones that feel too chaotic.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
