SHOT SCHOOL

Mount Fuji, Japan (Fujisan): Best Photo Spot and Where to Stand

Watch the video

To photograph Mount Fuji, use the standard lens or the 2x lens rather than zooming in too far from a distance. Zooming in too far makes the photo grainy and removes the shore and trees that give the mountain context. Using the standard or 2x lens keeps the lake and trees in the frame and shows the mountain at its true size and sharp.

TOUR

The historical and cultural tour of Montevideo - Old City
Uruguay

Curioso Free Tour PRO runs a walking tour through Montevideo's Old City, covering around two and a half hours on foot through the historic centre. Local guides work the route, and recent reviewers single out the storytelling and the mix of history, architecture, and street-level detail as the real draw.

STAY

Hotel America
Hotel in Montevideo, UYU 2,884 per night (about US$71)

A three-star hotel on Rio Negro 1330 Bis in Montevideo Centro, Hotel America sits five blocks from the historic Ciudad Vieja district. Guests rate the location 9.1 on Booking.com, and Pocitos Beach is about a 15-minute drive away , making it easy to explore both on the same trip.

The hotel's tour desk can help you plan your time around the city, and there are even walking tours, bike tours, and bicycle rental available on site. Rates shown are Booking.com rates for 22 to 24 December 2026 and change by date. View this stay

THROUGH THE LENS

A crowd of a hundred phones raised at once captures everything and somehow nothing. Research cited by PetaPixel points to a real pattern: when your phone records a moment, your brain tends to offload the memory to the device rather than holding it yourself. The photo exists. The experience, less so.

The fix is simple and costs you nothing. Watch the whole scene first, phone in your pocket. Then pick one specific detail, a single face in a crowd, the reflection of coloured bulbs in a wet cobblestone, the gap between two buildings framing the action, and shoot just that.

Today, choose one moment and watch it fully before you raise your phone.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Shirley Olivera, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Mercado Agrícola de Montevideo
Villa Muñoz, Montevideo

Mercado Agrícola de Montevideo, in the Villa Muñoz district, is a working produce market that earns barely a footnote in most Uruguay itineraries. The blog Where in the World is Riccardo? flags it as a documentary photographer's subject, with its large historic iron structure sheltering produce stalls, plant vendors, and food sellers across a footprint roughly 130 metres on its longest side.

The iron structure gives you strong repeating geometry overhead, so look up and use those lines to pull the eye through the frame. Stall vendors and flower sellers sit at a human scale inside a large industrial shell, which means you can play the small figure against the tall architecture without moving more than a step or two. Shoot vertical for the full height of the structure, then switch to horizontal for the row of stalls stretching into the distance.

Plaza 1º de Mayo is about 440 metres away, an easy walk once you've worked the market. Save it as a spot to review your shots before deciding which stall scenes to go back and reshoot.

Getting there: Mercado Agrícola de Montevideo is in the Villa Muñoz area, about 440 m northeast of Plaza 1º De Mayo and 90 m east of Prodhin. Walk northeast from Plaza 1º De Mayo to find it.

Metalenz acquired Israeli startup PxE Imaging, whose Holocoder layer bends light instead of absorbing it, delivering 2.5x more light to a sensor while capturing colour and depth together. FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Leading Lines

Point your phone's camera along any rail, fence, road, or staircase edge that runs away from you into the frame, then position yourself so the line enters from a corner rather than the centre. Keep the horizon level and let the line carry the eye deep into the shot. That pull of depth is what you are after.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
