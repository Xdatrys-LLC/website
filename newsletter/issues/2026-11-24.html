HOT LOCATION

Jardins de Laribal, Sants-Montjuïc, Barcelona: where to stand for the photo


Photo: wallygrom, Flickr, CC BY-SA 2.0

Jardins de Laribal
Sants-Montjuïc, Barcelona

Tucked into the slopes of Montjuïc in the Sants-Montjuïc area of Barcelona, Jardins de Laribal is a terraced garden that stretches about 330 metres at its longest. Tourism of Barcelona describes it as a place with ornamental details, fountains, vegetation, and panoramic views over the city, which tells you this isn't a manicured square you'll wander through in five minutes.

Those terraces are your best compositional tool: stand at a lower level and shoot upward to stack the layers, or frame a fountain against the city spread below it. Ornamental details reward a close crop, so tap to focus on one element and let the background fall soft behind it. Vary your position as you move between levels, each terrace gives you a different relationship between foreground and sky.

The Juan Antonio Samaranch Olympic and Sports Museum is 250 metres away, and the National Art Museum of Catalonia is about 360 metres from the garden, so you can string all three into one walk across the hill.


Map: 1 Jardins de Laribal. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

Getting there: Jardins de Laribal is in the Sants-Montjuïc area, about 250 m north of Juan Antonio Samaranch Olympic and Sports Museum and 360 m east of National Art Museum of Catalonia. Walk north from Juan Antonio Samaranch Olympic and Sports Museum to find it.

At the Thames foreshore in London (Bankside), you can find a flat view of the river and the City skyline with nothing in front of it. At low tide on the Bankside foreshore, move your feet to find the old timber piles. Use the timber piles as the foreground.

THROUGH THE LENS

Phone tip: crouch low and point upward at a tower

The world's most photographed heritage sites share one thing: crowds of people all pointing their phones at the same spot, from the same angle. The busiest angle gets the most copies.

Your phone's wide lens, the shortest focal length in your camera app, pulls in more of a building's height and the foreground stone at your feet than the standard view does. Crouch low, point upward at a tower or a carved arch, and that geometry fills the frame differently from every shot taken at standing height by the person next to you.

Today, pick one detail smaller than your hand and photograph only that.

Phone camera news: Xiaomi 18 Pro and VWFNDR

HARDWARE
Xiaomi 18 Pro puts two 200MP Leica cameras at your selfie service
Both the Xiaomi 18 Pro and Pro Max carry a 2.7- or 2.9-inch rear AMOLED screen built into the camera island, so you can frame yourself using the main 200MP Leica lenses instead of the weaker front camera. That's a genuine upgrade for anyone shooting content, group photos, or video. View ->

SOFTWARE
VWFNDR adds colour grades and lands on iPhone
The Android camera app known for clean, unprocessed DNG files hits version 2.0 with three film-inspired colour grades (NATURAL, NIHON, B+W) that adapt across the frame rather than applying a fixed look. It's on iPhone now too, with variable aperture support on iPhone 18 Pro. View ->

INDUSTRY
Your phone's camera was originally built for NASA spacecraft
Engineers at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory invented the sensor inside your phone in the early 1990s to shrink cameras down for spacecraft. That origin explains why your phone handles extreme contrast and scale so well, both daily challenges when you're shooting big architecture or wide open spaces. View ->

FRAME THE MOMENT

Photo challenge: bicycles

Today's theme: Bicycles

Get low and frame the rear wheel against something with strong geometry, a fence, a wall of repeating tiles, a row of parked frames. Fill the shot with the spokes, the chain, the handlebar shadow on concrete. We'd love to see what you find when you stop treating the whole bike as the subject and start treating its parts as the picture.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
