HOT LOCATION

Brooklyn Bridge Park, Brooklyn, New York City: where to stand for the photo


Photo: Christian David, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Brooklyn Bridge Park
Brooklyn, New York City

Brooklyn Bridge Park runs along the Brooklyn waterfront for about 1,480 metres, and NYC Tourism + Conventions flags it as a place that goes well beyond the standard bridge viewpoint, mixing nature and cultural spaces into one long stretch of waterfront.

Point your phone toward Manhattan and use the shoreline itself as your leading line, letting the eye travel from the foreground water to the skyline. The park's length gives you room to shift your position and change what lands in the background, so try walking a good distance before you commit to a frame.

A short walk from the park's edge sits Plymouth Church House, about 270 metres away, worth adding to your route if you want to end your visit somewhere with a bit of local history.


Map: 1 Brooklyn Bridge Park. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

Getting there: Brooklyn Bridge Park is in the Brooklyn area, about 130 m southeast of 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge and 270 m north of Plymouth Church House. Walk southeast from 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge to find it.

THROUGH THE LENS

Phone tip: tap the moon and drag the exposure slider down

Thousands of people pointed their phones at the supermoon this week, and most of those shots came back as a tiny white dot on a black sky. That happens because your phone's auto-exposure reads the whole dark frame and overbrightens the moon itself. Tap directly on the moon on your screen, then drag the exposure slider down until the grey craters actually appear.

A bright full moon against a deep sky is high contrast, meaning the gap between the brightest and darkest parts of the scene is wide. Your phone handles that better if you lock the exposure on the brightest object rather than letting it average everything out.

Try it on any bright object against a dark background today, a streetlamp, a lit window, anything, and watch how much detail comes back the moment you tap and drag down.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Photo challenge: cafe life

Today's theme: Cafe Life

Pull your chair back from the table and shoot across it, letting cups, saucers, and crumpled napkins fill the foreground while a person or window sits sharp in the distance. Switch your phone to portrait mode (which blurs the background to separate your subject from the clutter behind it) and get your lens low, level with the tabletop.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
