Things to Do in Beijing: Great Wall of China and Nanluoguxiang Photo Spots

Where to stand at Nanluoguxiang, a free walking tour of Beijing and a courtyard hotel in Beijing's Dongcheng district

Friday, November 27, 2026

Welcome back, Pocket Lenser. Today's Pocket Lenser, Sabine, 49, is heading to Great Wall of China in Beijing. Also inside: monarch butterfly migration across North America, and where to stand at The Little Mermaid.

Places: Nanluoguxiang, Dongcheng District, Forbidden City, Great Wall of China and Wangfujing Church, Beijing, China; Curaçao; The Little Mermaid, Denmark; Mexico.

Today's challenge: Pick one moving thing, follow it with your lens for thirty seconds, then press the shutter once.

HOT LOCATION

Nanluoguxiang, Jiaodaokou Subdistrict, Beijing: where to stand for the photo


Photo: Uuganbayar Otgonbayar, Pexels

Nanluoguxiang
Jiaodaokou Subdistrict, Beijing

Nanluoguxiang is an 800-metre hutong lane in Beijing's Dongcheng District, and according to Nanluoguxiang & Hutong Alleys, Beijing [2026], it sits inside one of the best-preserved grids of Ming and Qing-dynasty alleyways in central Beijing. That history surrounds you on both sides as you walk, which is exactly what makes it less obvious than the city's headline sights.

A lane this long gives you room to slow down and frame deliberately. Shoot vertical to emphasize the alley's depth and let the walls and rooflines pull the eye toward a vanishing point in the distance. We'd also suggest looking for doorways and gate openings as natural frames within a frame, since the architecture repeats in a rhythm you can use to your advantage.

The Experimental Theatre (Dongcheng Campus of the Central Academy of Drama) sits about 280 metres away, so once you've walked the lane, head over and photograph the contrast between the dynasty-era grid you just left and an active performance building.


Map: 1 Nanluoguxiang. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

Getting there: Nanluoguxiang is in the Jiaodaokou Subdistrict area, about 280 m south of Experimental Theatre (Dongcheng Campus of the Central Academy of Drama) and 340 m south of South Luogu Alley. Walk south from Experimental Theatre (Dongcheng Campus of the Central Academy of Drama) to find it.

TOUR and STAY

A free walking tour of Beijing and a courtyard hotel in Beijing's Dongcheng district


Photo: shankar s., Flickr, CC BY 2.0

TOUR

Beijing Imperial City Free Walking Tour
Great Wall of China, Beijing, China

Seven free walking tours run through Beijing on the GuruWalk platform, led by local guides including Roy, Echo, April, and Wolfie. The options range from a two-hour Imperial City circuit to a four-hour deep read of the Forbidden City, plus a street food route through the hutongs and a session that includes hands-on calligraphy.


Pictured: Beijing. Photo: shankar s., CC BY 2.0, via Flickr

Book one as a first pass through the streets before you shoot. You'll clock the archways, courtyard angles, and tiled rooftops you want to return to with your phone, and the guides point out details most people walk straight past. View this trip

STAY


Pictured: Beijing. Photo: Balon Greyjoy, CC0 public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Beijing Jingyuan Courtyard Hotel
Hotel in Beijing, CNY 1,080 per night (about US$162)

A four-star courtyard hotel on Wangfujing Xitangzi Hutong in Beijing's Dongcheng district, the Beijing Jingyuan Courtyard Hotel sits right next to Wangfujing Church and a two-minute stroll from the Wangfujing Shopping area. Guests rate its location 9.8 out of 10 on Booking.com. Hard to beat for where it puts you.

Wangfujing Church and the Wangfujing Shopping area are practically at the door, so you walk out and start shooting straight away. Rates shown are Booking.com rates for 1 to 3 January 2027 and change by date. View this stay

A photographer spent months chasing monarchs across 3,000 miles
Your next trip could use this same instinct: pick one subject, follow it, and let the journey build the story. CNN Travel covers nature photographer Jaime Rojo, whose book Monarchs documents the wonder and fragility of the monarch butterfly's seasonal migration , a journey that can span 3,000 miles between North America and the mountain forests of central Mexico. It's a reminder that the strongest phone galleries often come from one clear intention rather than shooting everything in sight. View ->

THROUGH THE LENS

Phone tip: pick one thing and shoot only that

The discipline does something practical. When you know your subject in advance, you stop scanning and start looking, and your eye finds angles, distances, and details it would otherwise skip.

Today, pick one thing to photograph repeatedly: a colour, a texture, a repeated shape, and shoot only that for the next hour.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Photo challenge: shopfronts

Today's theme: Shopfronts

Stand close enough to fill your frame with one door, one sign, or one window display, then lock your focus on the nearest edge of the glass or painted lettering. Shoot straight on to keep the lines parallel, or crouch slightly to place the base of the frame against the pavement and let the facade rise above you.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
