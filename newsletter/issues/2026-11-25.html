Things to Do in Paris: Louvre and Passage du Cheval Blanc Photo Spots

Passage du Cheval Blanc
11th Arrondissement, Paris

Passage du Cheval Blanc sits in Paris's 11th Arrondissement, tucked between Rue de la Roquette and Rue du Faubourg Saint-Antoine. Discovering the Centre of Paris describes it as a series of courtyards built around 19th-century workshops, and the layout bears that out: a curved, pedestrian-only passage that runs about 185 metres, branching off into Cour Sainte-Marguerite and Cour de Juin as you go. That mix of courtyards and workshop buildings puts it firmly off the tourist radar.

Because the passage curves, let it pull your eye through the frame rather than shooting straight ahead. Try positioning yourself low and angling toward one of the side courts, Cour Sainte-Marguerite or Cour de Juin, to give the shot two layers of depth. A vertical crop works well here, since the passage is long and the courtyards create natural frames within frames.

Chapelle Saint-Rémi sits 210 metres away, and Église Notre-Dame d'Espérance is around 360 metres out, so you can easily fold a short walk around both into the same visit.


Map: 1 Passage du Cheval Blanc. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

Getting there: Passage du Cheval Blanc is in the 11th Arrondissement area, about 210 m north of Chapelle Saint-Rémi and 360 m southwest of Église Notre-Dame d'Espérance. It meets Cour Sainte-Marguerite and Cour de Juin. Walk north from Chapelle Saint-Rémi to find it. It is a public street and free to visit.

TOUR

StellarTours: Free Walking Tours in Paris

StellarTours runs free walking tours across Paris's most photogenic districts, covering Montmartre, the Latin Quarter, Saint-Germain, and the city centre. STAY

9Confidentiel
Hotel in Paris, €632 per night (about US$692)

9Confidentiel is a 4-star hotel at 58 Rue du Roi de Sicile in Paris's 4th arrondissement, and guests on Booking.com give its location a 9.8 out of 10. Sainte-Chapelle is a 13-minute walk away, and Place des Vosges sits less than 1 km from the door.

For photographers, that score means Sainte-Chapelle and Place des Vosges are close enough to reach on foot and start shooting straight away. Rates shown are Booking.com rates for 30 December to 1 January 2027 and change by date. View this stay

THROUGH THE LENS

Phone tip for your trip

Illuminated streets and lit-up market stalls give your phone camera two things in the same frame: a bright foreground subject and a row of glowing bulbs stretching back behind it. That gap between near and far is what photographers call depth, and your phone reads it well when you get physically close to the nearest stall and let the rest of the street blur into the distance behind it.

Tap the screen on the nearest object to lock focus there. The background stays soft, the foreground stays sharp, and the whole frame looks intentional rather than accidental.

Today's small challenge: find any lit-up window or lamp post, crouch so it sits at eye level, and tap to focus on something between you and it.
