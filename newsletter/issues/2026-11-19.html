THROUGH THE LENS

Strikes and delays hand you something most travellers miss: unplanned waiting time in stations, on platforms, on steps outside a closed ticket hall. Your phone's wide lens (the shortest focal length on your camera app) picks up the full geometry of a crowded concourse, the arch of a vaulted ceiling, the contrast between a stationary crowd and a departing train.

Waiting also strips away the pressure to rush a shot. You can hold the frame still, tap to focus on a specific face or tile or shadow, and actually look at what's in front of you.

Next time you're stuck somewhere in transit, open your camera, switch to the widest lens setting, and take one shot looking straight up at the ceiling above you.

HOT LOCATION


Photo: Giuseppe Guida, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Palazzo dello Spagnuolo
Stella, Naples

Tucked into the Stella district of Naples, Palazzo dello Spagnuolo holds one of the city's most spectacular staircases: a double Baroque falcon-wing design by Ferdinando Sanfelice, built in 1738. The building sits off the main tourist trail, and "Instagrammable and Unusual Spots in Naples: 20 Spots to Photograph" calls it out as a place most visitors walk straight past.

Point your phone straight up from the base of the staircase and shoot vertical. The symmetry of the two mirrored wings gives your frame a natural centre line, so place yourself directly beneath it and let the geometry do the composition work. A wide-angle setting will pull in both arches at once without you needing to step back.

Palazzo del Monastero dei Santi Severino e Sossio is about 180 metres away, so it makes a natural second stop once you've worked the staircase from every angle. Two architectural subjects, one short walk. Save this for later.

Getting there: Palazzo dello Spagnuolo is in the Stella area, about 180 m northwest of Palazzo del Monastero dei Santi Severino e Sossio and 360 m north of Università "SUN" - Dipartimento di Salute Mentale, Fisica e Medicina Preventiva. Walk northwest from Palazzo del Monastero dei Santi Severino e Sossio to find it.

FRAME THE MOMENT

Today's theme: Weathered Surfaces

Get close, close enough that texture fills the frame and context disappears. Lock focus on a single crack, flaking patch, or rust streak, then shoot in portrait orientation to drag that detail from top to bottom of the shot. The tighter you frame, the stronger the story the surface tells on its own.

Shoot it anywhere in the world. The theme is all that matters.
